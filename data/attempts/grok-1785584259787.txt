The Riemann Hypothesis (RH) asserts that every non-trivial zero of \(\zeta(s)\) satisfies \(\operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2\). Equivalently, the completed function
\[
\xi(s)=\frac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)
\]
is entire of order 1 and all its zeros lie on the critical line. Write the Hadamard product
\[
\xi(s)=\xi(0)\prod_{\rho}\Bigl(1-\frac{s}{\rho}\Bigr)
\]
(convergent after grouping \(\rho\) and \(1-\rho\)). Taking the logarithmic derivative yields the explicit formula
\[
\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)=\sum_{\rho}\frac1{s-\rho}.
\]
A standard contour-integral argument then converts this into the von Mangoldt explicit formula
\[
\psi(x)=x-\sum_{\rho}\frac{x^{\rho}}{\rho}-\log(2\pi)-\frac12\log(1-x^{-2}),
\]
where the sum is over non-trivial zeros (taken in conjugate pairs and with increasing \(|\operatorname{Im}\rho|\)).

Assume, for contradiction, that there exists a zero \(\rho_0=\beta+i\gamma\) with \(\beta>1/2\). Then the term \(x^{\rho_0}/\rho_0\) dominates the oscillatory sum for a positive-density set of \(x\) (by the classical zero-density estimate \(N(\sigma,T)\ll T^{c(1-\sigma)}\log T\)). More precisely, the contribution of the single zero already forces
\[
\bigl|\psi(x)-x\bigr|\gg x^{\beta-\varepsilon}
\]
on a set of logarithmic density bounded below by a positive constant depending only on \(\beta\). This contradicts the elementary bound \(\psi(x)=x+O(x\exp(-c\sqrt{\log x}))\) that follows from the classical zero-free region \(\sigma\ge1-c/\log(|t|+2)\). The same lower bound also violates the known mean-square estimate
\[
\int_1^X\bigl(\psi(x)-x\bigr)^2\frac{dx}{x^2}\ll\log X.
\]
Hence no such \(\rho_0\) can exist, provided the zero-density exponent remains strictly less than 1. The argument therefore reduces RH to the (still open) assertion that
\[
N(\sigma,T)=o\bigl(T\bigr)\qquad\text{for every fixed }\sigma>1/2.
\]
All currently available zero-density theorems fall short of this threshold by a positive power of \(T\), so the reduction, while rigorous, does not yet prove RH.
