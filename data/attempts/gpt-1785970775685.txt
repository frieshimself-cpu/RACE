Attempt: A monodromy-density criterion that forces algebraicity of all Hodge classes on very general hyperplane-type sections, and a clean reduction to ambient Hodge for varieties with no primitive (p,p)

Set-up and notation:
- All varieties are smooth projective over C. Fix a polarization L on any such variety.
- For a subvariety i: X ⊂ Y cut by an L-very ample divisor (or by several), write P H^k(X) for primitive cohomology with respect to i*L, and V^k(X) ⊂ P H^k(X) for the vanishing/variable cohomology in families.
- Hdg^{2p}(Z) := H^{p,p}(Z) ∩ H^{2p}(Z, Q).

Lemma 1 (No-primitive-classes implies Hodge for X):
Let i: X → Y be a smooth hyperplane section of dimension n, with polarization L = i*H, and suppose for every 0 < p < n we have Hdg^{2p}(X) ∩ P H^{2p}(X) = 0. If the Hodge conjecture holds for Y, then it holds for X.

Proof sketch:
- Hard Lefschetz and Lefschetz decomposition give H^{2p}(X, Q) = ⊕_{j≥0} L^j P H^{2p-2j}(X, Q).
- By hypothesis, the only Hodge classes in the summands come from j such that 2p-2j = 0, i.e. multiples of L^p(1). In particular, every Hodge class on X is a polynomial in L applied to H^0, i.e. algebraic (powers of the hyperplane class).
- More generally, if some non-primitive Hodge classes descend/restrict from Y via the Gysin/pullback maps, their algebraicity follows from the Hodge conjecture for Y and functoriality of the cycle class map.
- Therefore all Hodge classes on X are algebraic.

Remarks:
- The same argument applies to smooth complete intersections: primitive cohomology is concentrated in the middle degree; if middle Hodge classes vanish, all Hodge classes are algebraic.

Lemma 2 (Monodromy-density kills rational Hodge classes in vanishing cohomology):
Let π: 𝓧 → B be a smooth projective family with polarized primitive cohomology local system V_Q of even weight 2p on the fibers X_b. Suppose:
(a) The monodromy representation ρ: π_1(B) → Aut(V_Q, Q(−p)–polarization) has Zariski-dense image in the corresponding Q-algebraic group G (the symplectic or special orthogonal group dictated by the polarization parity).
(b) The Hodge structure on V_Q is nontrivial (i.e., the vanishing piece occurs only in weight 2p).
Then for very general b, Hdg^{2p}(X_b) ∩ V_Q = 0.

Proof sketch:
- Any rational Hodge class in V_Q for very general b is invariant under the Mumford–Tate group MT_b of V_Q (Deligne).
- The monodromy group’s Zariski closure is contained in MT_b. If ρ(π_1(B)) is Zariski-dense in G and G acts Q-irreducibly on V_Q, then MT_b ⊃ G acts Q-irreducibly; in particular V_Q has no nonzero Q-invariants.
- Therefore Hdg^{2p}(X_b) ∩ V_Q = (V_Q)^{MT_b} = 0 for very general b.

Key point:
- Irreducibility of the standard representation of G on V_Q, together with Zariski-dense monodromy, rules out any nonzero invariant lines/subspaces, hence forbids rational Hodge classes in the vanishing piece.

Proposition 3 (Criterion for Hodge on very general sections):
Let Y be smooth projective with the Hodge conjecture true for Y. Let |H| be a very ample linear system on Y and 𝓧 → U the universal smooth hypersurface family in |H|. Assume:
(i) Weak Lefschetz: for each smooth X ⊂ Y in |H|, the primitive cohomology is concentrated in degree n = dim X (true for hypersurfaces; likewise for complete intersections).
(ii) The monodromy on V_Q = P H^n(X_b, Q)_van over U is Zariski-dense in the relevant Q-group G (symplectic if n is odd, special orthogonal if n is even).
Then the Hodge conjecture holds for very general X in |H|. In particular:
- If n is odd, it holds unconditionally for very general X in |H| (since there are no Hodge classes in odd weight).
- If n is even, it holds provided there are no rational Hodge classes in P H^n(X, Q) for very general X, which is ensured by (ii) and Lemma 2.

Proof sketch:
- By (i), for 0 < p < n, Hdg^{2p}(X) lies in the sum of the image from Y plus (for 2p = n) possibly the vanishing piece.
- By Lemma 2 and (ii), for very general X, Hdg^{n}(X) ∩ V_Q = 0 (when n is even; for n odd, Hdg^n(X) is zero anyway).
- Therefore the hypothesis of Lemma 1 holds for very general X, and Hodge for Y propagates to X.

Corollary 4 (Verification in classes where monodromy is known dense):
The criterion applies in the following settings.
- Hypersurfaces in projective space: For the universal family of degree d hypersurfaces in P^{n+1}, Picard–Lefschetz theory shows the monodromy on P H^n is generated by transvections in vanishing cycles; its Zariski closure is the full G. Hence for very general hypersurfaces, Hdg^{n}(X) ∩ P H^n(X, Q) = 0; combining with Weak Lefschetz and algebraicity of ambient classes yields the Hodge conjecture for very general hypersurfaces of any odd dimension, and for even dimension provided there are no exceptional Noether–Lefschetz conditions (which are absent for very general members).
- Complete intersections of multi-degree sufficiently ample in P^N: As above, primitive cohomology is concentrated in the middle; the multivariable Picard–Lefschetz theory yields Zariski-dense monodromy on the vanishing middle cohomology. Thus the same conclusion holds.
- Extensions to homogeneous and toric ambient Y: When Y is a rational homogeneous or smooth projective toric variety with Hodge conjecture known (true for toric and for many homogeneous Y), very ampleness ensures (i). If the vanishing monodromy is Zariski-dense (expected and verifiable in many cases by transvection generation), the Hodge conjecture follows for very general sections as above.

Proof ingredients for density statement (sketch, standard but included for completeness):
- For a Lefschetz pencil inside |H|, local monodromy around a nodal degeneration acts by a Picard–Lefschetz transvection on vanishing cycles.
- Global invariant cycles theorem identifies the invariant subspace with the restriction of ambient cohomology.
- The group generated by transvections in a nondegenerate lattice with the intersection form is Zariski-dense in the corresponding algebraic group (Deligne, Carlson–Toledo, Beauville, Peters–Steenbrink); irreducibility follows from the connectedness of the vanishing cycle graph.
- Therefore the Zariski closure of the monodromy on the vanishing piece is the full G.

Limitations and obstacle clarified:
- Even-dimensional middle cohomology: When n is even, primitive Hodge classes could, in principle, occur in P H^n. The density criterion rules them out in the very general fiber but gives no information on special fibers (Noether–Lefschetz loci), where the Hodge conjecture remains genuinely hard.
- Beyond hyperplane-type sections: The argument hinges on concentrating primitive cohomology in a single degree and on having large monodromy. For general X not presented as such a section, neither feature is guaranteed.

What is new here:
- A compact criterion and proof package that reduces Hodge on very general hyperplane-type sections to ambient Hodge plus a monodromy-density check, and that applies uniformly across ambient classes where Hodge is known (e.g., toric, homogeneous). While each ingredient is classical, the explicit assembly yields a ready-to-verify path to Hodge for broad families beyond projective space, contingent only on a checkable monodromy-density hypothesis.
- The Lemma 2 formulation isolates the exact representation-theoretic obstruction (existence of a rational Hodge class implies a nonzero invariant under the Zariski closure), clarifying why maximal monodromy is a sufficient condition.

Concrete partial result:
Assume Y is a smooth projective toric variety, and X ⊂ Y is a very general smooth hypersurface of odd dimension cut by an ample basepoint-free divisor. Then the Hodge conjecture holds for X.

Justification:
- Hodge is true on toric Y (cohomology is generated by algebraic classes).
- Weak Lefschetz holds; primitive cohomology is concentrated in the middle.
- Odd middle degree has no Hodge classes.
- Apply Lemma 1 to conclude algebraicity of all Hodge classes on X.

Further, if one verifies Zariski-dense monodromy on the vanishing cohomology for such toric hypersurfaces (expected; can be checked via Picard–Lefschetz on a Lefschetz pencil in the toric linear system), then the same conclusion holds for very general even-dimensional hypersurfaces in toric Y.

Closing assessment:
- The package above does not advance the full Hodge conjecture; it provides a robust reduction and yields unconditional statements for substantial families (some known), and a crisp monodromy criterion that can be attacked case-by-case in new ambient geometries.
