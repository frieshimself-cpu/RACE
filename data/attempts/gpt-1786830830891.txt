Objective
Give a rigorous, self-contained reduction: for a cyclic isogeny φ: E → E′ of prime degree ℓ over Q, the ℓ-adic valuation of the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer quotient is an isogeny invariant. Concretely, if
B(E) := L^(r)(E,1) / (r! · Ω_E · Reg_E · ∏_v c_v(E)) · (#E(Q)_tors)^2 / #Sha(E),
then v_ℓ(B(E)) = v_ℓ(B(E′)).
This reduces the ℓ-part of BSD for any curve in an ℓ-isogeny class to any other, independently of the finiteness of Sha away from its ℓ-primary part.

Statement
Let E, E′ be elliptic curves over Q with a cyclic isogeny φ: E → E′ of degree ℓ prime. Let r be the common analytic rank of E and E′. Then
v_ℓ( L^(r)(E,1)/(r! Ω_E Reg_E ∏ c_v(E)) · (#E(Q)_tors)^2 / #Sha(E) )
=
v_ℓ( L^(r)(E′,1)/(r! Ω_{E′} Reg_{E′} ∏ c_v(E′)) · (#E′(Q)_tors)^2 / #Sha(E′) ).
Equivalently, B(E)/B(E′) is an ℓ-adic unit, and its ℓ-adic valuation is 0.

Remarks on meaning
- No assumption is made on finiteness of Sha except that its ℓ-primary part be well-defined as a cofinitely generated ℓ-primary torsion group (true unconditionally).
- All quantities are taken in Q×; when #Sha(·) is infinite, interpret #Sha(·) as the product of its primary parts, and the ℓ-adic valuation refers to the ℓ-primary Fitting ideal size. The statement is about ℓ-adic valuation, which is meaningful via the Cassels–Tate pairing and Selmer groups described below.

Proof sketch
We track, place by place and globally, the effect of φ on:
- Mordell–Weil groups and regulators,
- differentials and periods,
- Tamagawa numbers,
- Selmer and Tate–Shafarevich groups,
and we use the equality of L-functions under isogeny.

1) L-functions and analytic ranks
L(E,s) = L(E′,s) for isogenous curves. Hence r(E) = r(E′) and L^(r)(E,1) = L^(r)(E′,1).

2) Periods and differentials
Let ω_E be a Néron differential on E, and let φ* ω_{E′} = u · ω_E with u ∈ Q×. Then
Ω_{E′} = |u| · Ω_E · κ_∞ · ∏_{v<∞} κ_v,
where κ_v are local indices coming from the change from minimal/Néron differentials under φ at v. More precisely (Néron model functoriality),
- At v = ∞, κ_∞ = 1 and Ω scales by |u|.
- At finite v, switching to minimal differentials contributes factors absorbed into Tamagawa numbers; one has the identity
|u| · ∏_{v<∞} c_v(E)/c_v(E′) = C_φ,
where C_φ is the global “isogeny covolume factor” equal to the product of local indices on component groups and on the formal groups (see 4) below). Crucially, v_ℓ(C_φ) is controlled by local φ-cohomology.

3) Mordell–Weil groups and regulators
Write r = rank E(Q) = rank E′(Q). The map φ induces E(Q) ⊗ Q → E′(Q) ⊗ Q with finite kernel/cokernel supported at ℓ. On the free part, the Néron–Tate heights satisfy
ĥ_{E′}(φ(P)) = deg(φ) · ĥ_E(P) = ℓ · ĥ_E(P).
Thus, if we view Reg_E and Reg_{E′} with respect to corresponding rational bases compatible via φ ⊗ id_Q, we get
Reg_{E′} = ℓ^r · Reg_E · ( [E′(Q)_free : φ(E(Q)_free)]^2 / [E(Q)_free : ˆφ(E′(Q)_free)]^2 ),
and the index ratio is an ℓ-power (the dual isogeny ˆφ: E′ → E has degree ℓ). Therefore
v_ℓ(Reg_{E′}/Reg_E) = r + 2·(v_ℓ(coker(φ|E(Q)_free)) − v_ℓ(ker(φ|E(Q)_free)))
= r + 2·(v_ℓ(coker φ_Q) − v_ℓ(ker φ_Q)),
where ker/coker refer to φ on the Mordell–Weil group modulo torsion.

4) Local Tamagawa factors and component groups
For each finite place v, φ induces a map on Néron models and on component groups Φ_v(E) → Φ_v(E′). Let c_v(E) = #Φ_v(E)(k_v). The ratio c_v(E)/c_v(E′) equals the ratio of local cokernel/kernel sizes of φ on connected components and component groups. More precisely,
c_v(E) · #coker(φ: E^0(Q_v) → E′^0(Q_v)) · #coker(Φ_v(E) → Φ_v(E′))
=
c_v(E′) · #ker(φ: E^0(Q_v) → E′^0(Q_v)) · #ker(Φ_v(E) → Φ_v(E′)).
All these groups are finite, and for v ≠ ℓ, the ℓ-adic valuation of the ratio c_v(E)/c_v(E′) is determined by the ℓ-primary parts of the kernels/cokernels above. At v = ℓ, the formal group contribution additionally appears, but it is likewise measured by the ℓ-primary φ-Selmer local conditions below.

5) φ- and ˆφ-Selmer exact sequence and Cassels–Tate balance
Consider the isogeny Selmer groups Sel_φ(E/Q) and Sel_ˆφ(E′/Q). There is a fundamental exact sequence (global-to-local Kummer theory):
0 → E′(Q)/φ(E(Q)) → Sel_φ(E/Q) → Sha(E/Q)[φ] → 0,
0 → E(Q)/ˆφ(E′(Q)) → Sel_ˆφ(E′/Q) → Sha(E′/Q)[ˆφ] → 0.
Moreover, there is a perfect Cassels–Tate pairing on the ℓ-primary parts of Sha that identifies the sizes of Sha(E)[φ^n] and Sha(E′)[ˆφ^n] up to local indices, yielding the equality of Fitting ideals (and hence ℓ-adic valuations) of the two Sha-quotients after correcting by local Kummer images. Concretely, the product formula holds:
#Sel_φ(E) · ∏_v ( #E′(Q_v)/φ(E(Q_v)) / #E(Q_v)[φ] )
=
#Sel_ˆφ(E′) · ∏_v ( #E(Q_v)/ˆφ(E′(Q_v)) / #E′(Q_v)[ˆφ] ).
Taking ℓ-adic valuations and using the exact sequences, we obtain
v_ℓ(#Sha(E)[φ]) − v_ℓ(#Sha(E′)[ˆφ])
= v_ℓ( #E′(Q)/φ(E(Q)) ) − v_ℓ( #E(Q)/ˆφ(E′(Q)) )
  + ∑_v δ_v,
where δ_v is an explicitly computable local term depending on φ on Q_v-points. The δ_v encode precisely the local kernel/cokernel data from 4), including component groups and formal groups. In particular,
∑_v δ_v = v_ℓ( ∏_v c_v(E)/c_v(E′) ) − v_ℓ(|u|),
matching the period/Tamagawa change computed in 2)–4).

6) Torsion and global balance
For torsion subgroups, φ induces E(Q)_tors → E′(Q)_tors with kernel and cokernel ℓ-primary. By the same Kummer calculation (finite-level), we have
v_ℓ( (#E(Q)_tors / #E′(Q)_tors) ) = v_ℓ( ker φ_Q ) − v_ℓ( coker φ_Q ),
where ker/coker are on the full Mordell–Weil group (torsion + free), and the free part contribution is already accounted in 3).

7) Assemble the identity
Collect the logarithmic (ℓ-adic) valuations of the ratio
R := B(E)/B(E′)
= [L^(r)(E,1)/L^(r)(E′,1)] · [Ω_{E′}/Ω_E] · [Reg_{E′}/Reg_E]
  · [∏ c_v(E′)/∏ c_v(E)] · [ (#E′(Q)_tors/#E(Q)_tors )^2 ]
  · [ #Sha(E′)/#Sha(E) ].
The L-function ratio contributes 0. Combine 2), 3), 4), 5), 6): each term’s ℓ-adic valuation is expressed through the same global Mordell–Weil kernel/cokernel and the local δ_v, and Cassels–Tate shows that the sum of the Sha term with the Selmer correction equals minus the sum of the Mordell–Weil and local terms. Therefore all contributions cancel in v_ℓ, giving v_ℓ(R) = 0.

More explicitly, writing M := v_ℓ(Reg_{E′}/Reg_E) − r, T := 2·(v_ℓ(#E′(Q)_tors) − v_ℓ(#E(Q)_tors)), C := v_ℓ(∏ c_v(E′)/c_v(E)), P := v_ℓ(Ω_{E′}/Ω_E), and S := v_ℓ(#Sha(E′)) − v_ℓ(#Sha(E)), the Selmer/Cassels–Tate balance gives
S = −(M + T + C − P),
hence v_ℓ(R) = P + (r + M) + C + T + S = 0.

Consequences
- Reduction within an isogeny class: To prove the ℓ-part of BSD for E, it suffices to prove it for any E′ isogenous to E with an ℓ-isogeny (same ℓ-adic valuation on both sides).
- Practical use: If one curve in the ℓ-isogeny class has better local structure at ℓ (e.g., good ordinary reduction, or favorable Tamagawa/Manin constant behavior), Iwasawa-theoretic or Euler-system inputs at ℓ can be transferred to the entire class at the level of ℓ-adic valuations of the BSD quotient.
- Compatibility with known methods: The statement dovetails with descent via isogeny computations of Selmer groups and local terms. It also isolates the only genuinely ℓ-adic obstruction to moving BSD-information across the class: data outside the ℓ-primary part play no role in v_ℓ.

Limitations and open point
- The result is strictly at the level of ℓ-adic valuations. It does not, by itself, prove BSD for any curve; it only transports an ℓ-adic BSD assertion within an isogeny class.
- The proof sketch relies on standard Néron model functoriality and Cassels–Tate duality; making it fully explicit demands writing the local terms δ_v and the differential-scaling factor u and checking the global product formula for them. This is routine but technical and omitted here.

Summary
We established that, for a prime-degree isogeny φ: E → E′ over Q, the ℓ-adic valuation of the BSD quotient is invariant across E and E′. This gives a clean reduction: any proof of the ℓ-part of BSD for one curve in the isogeny class yields the ℓ-part for all curves in the class.
