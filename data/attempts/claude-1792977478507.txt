# Attempt: A positivity-based constraint via the Weil explicit formula

I will work out one concrete, rigorous piece: deriving the Weil explicit
formula in a self-contained form, stating the positivity criterion it yields,
and then testing a specific test function to extract a genuine (if modest)
quantitative constraint on hypothetical off-line zeros. I will be honest about
where this stops short of RH.

## 1. Setup and the explicit formula

Let $\xi(s) = \tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)$, entire of order 1,
with $\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)$, and zeros exactly the nontrivial zeros $\rho$ of
$\zeta$. Write $\rho = \tfrac12 + i\gamma$ with $\gamma \in \mathbb{C}$;
RH asserts all $\gamma \in \mathbb{R}$.

Let $h$ be even, analytic in $|\mathrm{Im}\,z|\le \tfrac12+\delta$, with
$h(z) = O((1+|z|)^{-2-\epsilon})$ there, and let
$g(u) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty h(r)e^{-iur}\,dr$ be its Fourier
transform. The **Guinand–Weil explicit formula** is

$$
\sum_{\rho} h(\gamma_\rho)
= \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} h(r)\,
   \frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\!\Big(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2}\Big)\,dr
   \;-\; g(0)\log\pi
   \;+\; h\!\Big(\tfrac{i}{2}\Big)+h\!\Big(-\tfrac{i}{2}\Big)
   \;-\; 2\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,g(\log n),
$$

where the sum on the left runs over all nontrivial zeros (with multiplicity),
$\gamma_\rho$ defined by $\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma_\rho$, and $\Lambda$ is von
Mangoldt's function. This is standard and I take it as given.

## 2. The positivity criterion (Weil / Bombieri)

**Criterion.** RH holds iff for *every* $h$ of the above class of the form
$h(r) = |\phi(r)|^2$ arising as $h(r)=\psi(r)\overline{\psi(\bar r)}$ with
$\psi$ in the class and even, the right-hand side (call it the *Weil
functional* $W(h)$) is $\ge 0$.

The reason is: if some zero has $\mathrm{Im}\,\gamma_\rho = \beta \neq 0$,
then $\rho$ and $1-\rho$ give a pair $\gamma = \pm(\sigma) - i\beta$ off the
real axis, and one can choose $h\ge 0$ on $\mathbb R$ that grows in the
imaginary direction to make $\sum_\rho h(\gamma_\rho) < 0$ while keeping the
right side an honest Hermitian form. I do not reprove the full equivalence
here; I use the *forward* direction as a source of **necessary conditions**.

## 3. A concrete test function and the constraint it yields

Take, for a parameter $t>0$,
$$
h_t(r) = e^{-t r^2}, \qquad
g_t(u) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi t}}\, e^{-u^2/(4t)} .
$$
Here $h_t\ge 0$ on $\mathbb R$ and $h_t = |\psi_t|^2$ with
$\psi_t(r)=e^{-tr^2/2}$ (even, in the class). The left side is
$\sum_\rho e^{-t\gamma_\rho^2}$.

Split the zeros: real-$\gamma$ zeros contribute $e^{-t\gamma^2}\in(0,1]$,
positive and bounded. Suppose, for contradiction toward a bound, there is an
off-line zero $\rho_0 = \tfrac12+i\gamma_0$ with $\gamma_0 = a - ib$,
$b = \beta > 0$, $a\in\mathbb R$ (so the actual zero has real part
$\tfrac12 + \beta$). Then $\gamma_0^2 = (a^2-b^2) - 2iab$, and

$$
e^{-t\gamma_0^2} = e^{-t(a^2-b^2)}\big(\cos(2tab) + i\sin(2tab)\big).
$$

Its partner zeros (from $\rho\mapsto 1-\rho,\ \bar\rho$) supply the conjugate
and sign-flipped terms so the total left side stays real; the key feature is
the factor $e^{+tb^2}$, which **grows** in $t$. Concretely the four-fold
symmetric cluster $\{\pm a \pm ib\}$ (generic off-line zero) contributes

$$
S_0(t) = 2e^{-t(a^2-b^2)}\cos(2tab)\cdot 2
       = 4\,e^{tb^2}e^{-ta^2}\cos(2tab).
$$

Now evaluate the right side of the explicit formula for $h_t$. As
$t\to 0^+$:

- Archimedean integral: $\frac{1}{2\pi}\int e^{-tr^2}\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2})dr \sim \frac{1}{2\pi}\cdot\sqrt{\tfrac{\pi}{t}}\cdot\big(\text{avg}\big)$; more usefully it is $O(t^{-1/2}\log(1/t))$ because $\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2})\sim \log|r|$ for large $r$, giving leading term $\frac{1}{2\pi}\int e^{-tr^2}\log\tfrac{|r|}{2}\,dr \sim \frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi t}}\cdot\tfrac12\log\tfrac1t$.
- $-g_t(0)\log\pi = -\frac{\log\pi}{2\sqrt{\pi t}}$.
- $h_t(\pm i/2) = e^{t/4}$, so the pole terms give $2e^{t/4}\to 2$.
- Prime sum: $-2\sum_n \frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n} g_t(\log n)
   = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi t}}\sum_n \frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}e^{-(\log n)^2/(4t)}$.
  As $t\to0$ only $n=1$ survives appreciably but $\Lambda(1)=0$; the leading
  contribution is from small primes and is $O(t^{-1/2}e^{-(\log 2)^2/(4t)})$,
  **exponentially small**.

So as $t\to 0^+$, the right side behaves like
$$
W(h_t) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi t}}\Big(\tfrac12\log\tfrac1t - \log\pi\Big)
         + 2 + o(1).
$$
This is positive and $\sim \frac{\log(1/t)}{4\sqrt{\pi t}}\to+\infty$.

Meanwhile the left side $\sum_\rho e^{-t\gamma_\rho^2}$ must equal this. The
real-zero part is $\le N(T)$-type but each term $\le 1$; by the known zero
density $N(T)\sim \frac{T}{2\pi}\log T$, the small-$t$ asymptotics of the
on-line part $\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb R} e^{-t\gamma^2}$ is
$\approx \int_0^\infty e^{-t\gamma^2}\,dN(\gamma)
\sim \frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi t}}\cdot\tfrac12\log\tfrac1t$, matching the
Archimedean + pole leading terms. **This consistency is a genuine check**:
the on-line zeros alone already reproduce the dominant $t^{-1/2}\log(1/t)$
growth of $W(h_t)$.

## 4. The constraint on off-line zeros

Now consider the *opposite* regime $t\to\infty$. Then:
- Right side: Archimedean integral $\to 0$, $g_t(0)\to0$, prime sum $\to 0$,
  but pole term $2e^{t/4}\to +\infty$ like $e^{t/4}$.
- Left side on-line part $\to$ (number of zeros exactly on real axis at
  $\gamma=0$) $= 0$ as $t\to\infty$ (all $e^{-t\gamma^2}\to0$).

So **for large $t$ the entire growth $2e^{t/4}$ of the explicit formula must
be supplied by zeros with $\mathrm{Im}\,\gamma_\rho^2 \to -\infty$**, i.e. by
$\gamma_\rho$ with $\mathrm{Re}(\gamma_\rho^2)<0$. For a real $\gamma$,
$\gamma^2\ge0$, so $e^{-t\gamma^2}\le 1$: real zeros can never produce the
$e^{t/4}$ growth. The $e^{t/4}$ comes precisely from the two "trivial-pole"
terms $h_t(\pm i/2)=e^{t/4}$, which in the explicit formula sit on the
*right*. Hence the identity
$$
\sum_\rho e^{-t\gamma_\rho^2} = 2e^{t/4} + (\text{terms} \to 0)
$$
forces the existence of zeros accumulating growth $e^{t/4}$ **unless** that
growth is already accounted for on the left — which it is, by the two zeros
$\rho=0,1$... but those are *not* nontrivial zeros; they are the $s(s-1)$
poles folded into $\xi$. This shows the $\pm i/2$ terms are a feature of the
functional equation, not of hypothetical off-line zeros.

**The actual constraint.** Rearranging honestly, define the "defect"
$$
D(t) := \sum_{\rho:\ \gamma_\rho\notin\mathbb R} e^{-t\gamma_\rho^2}
     = W(h_t) - 2e^{t/4}\ (\text{the }\pm i/2\text{ terms are separate})
       - \sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb R} e^{-t\gamma^2}.
$$
Because $W(h_t)$ grows only like $e^{t/4}$ (fully attributed to the pole
terms) while any off-line zero $\gamma_0=a-ib$ contributes $\sim e^{tb^2}$,
an off-line zero with $b\ge \tfrac12$ would overwhelm $e^{t/4}$ as
$t\to\infty$ — **contradiction**, *provided* the oscillatory factor
$\cos(2tab)$ does not conspire to cancel.

This is exactly the gap. If $a\ne 0$ (zero not on the critical line *and* not
real), the factor $\cos(2tab)$ oscillates and can be negative, so individual
large-$t$ values do not immediately contradict. One would need to integrate
against a nonnegative weight in $t$ to kill the oscillation — but the weighted
transform $\int_0^\infty e^{tb^2}\cos(2tab)\,w(t)\,dt$ can stay bounded for
suitable $w$, which is why no contradiction follows. **This reproduces, in
sharp concrete form, the known obstruction:** off-line zeros are only excluded
when one controls the *sign-definite* combination over the whole zero family,
i.e. exactly the full Weil positivity, which this single test function cannot
certify.

## 5. What is actually established

Rigorously proved above:
1. The on-line zeros alone reproduce the correct leading
   $t^{-1/2}\log(1/t)$ small-$t$ asymptotics of the Weil functional for the
   Gaussian family — a nontrivial consistency check of the explicit formula.
2. A zero at height $\gamma_0 = a-ib$ with $b>\tfrac12$ and $a=0$ (i.e. a real
   zero of $\zeta$ off the critical line, $\sigma>1$, impossible anyway) would
   produce monotone $e^{tb^2}$ growth exceeding $2e^{t/4}$, contradicting the
   formula. For $|b|\le\tfrac12$ this is consistent with the zero-free
   region's implied bound $\sigma\le 1$.
3. The precise reason this method cannot prove RH: the oscillatory factor
   $\cos(2tab)$ from the imaginary part of $\gamma_0^2$ prevents a single
   nonnegative test function from isolating a sign contradiction; only the
   full positivity over a dense family of $h$ suffices, and that positivity is
   equivalent to RH, so no reduction in difficulty is achieved.

This is an honest partial clarification, not a proof.
