### Attempt: Obstruction in a truncated Weil positivity criterion

Let \(\Xi(t) = \xi(1/2 + it)\) where \(\xi(s) = \frac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\). The Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to all zeros of \(\Xi\) being real. Weil’s explicit formula supplies a quadratic form whose positivity on a suitable space of test functions is equivalent to RH.

Fix a rapidly decreasing even Schwartz function \(g\) on \(\mathbb{R}\) whose Fourier transform
\[
\hat g(u) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} g(t)e^{-itu}\,dt
\]
is supported in \((-1,1)\). Define the Weil distributional pairing
\begin{align*}
W(g)
&:= \sum_{\rho} \hat g\bigl(i(\rho-1/2)\bigr)
= \hat g(0)\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}(1/4)
- g(0)\log\pi
+ \int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\hat g(0)-\hat g(t)}{2\sinh(t/2)}\,dt\\
&\qquad + \sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\bigl(\hat g(\log n)+\hat g(-\log n)\bigr),
\end{align*}
the sum running over the non-trivial zeros of \(\zeta\) (counted with multiplicity). Weil’s criterion asserts that RH holds if and only if \(W(g)\ge0\) for every such \(g\not\equiv0\).

#### Truncation and a concrete test family
Restrict attention to the one-parameter family
\[
g_{\alpha}(t) = e^{-\alpha t^{2}}\qquad(\alpha>0).
\]
Then \(\hat g_{\alpha}(u)=\sqrt{\pi/\alpha}\,e^{-u^{2}/(4\alpha)}\) has rapid decay but is never compactly supported. Introduce a smooth cut-off \(\chi_{\varepsilon}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(-1+\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon)\) equal to 1 on \([-1+2\varepsilon,1-2\varepsilon]\) and replace \(\hat g_{\alpha}\) by \(\hat g_{\alpha}\chi_{\varepsilon}\). The resulting test functions \(g_{\alpha,\varepsilon}\) lie in the admissible class, and
\[
W(g_{\alpha,\varepsilon}) = W_{\mathrm{arch}}(g_{\alpha,\varepsilon}) + W_{\mathrm{prime}}(g_{\alpha,\varepsilon}),
\]
where the archimedean part admits the explicit asymptotic
\[
W_{\mathrm{arch}}(g_{\alpha,\varepsilon})
= \frac{\sqrt\pi}{\sqrt\alpha}\Bigl(\tfrac12\log(\alpha/\pi)+\tfrac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}(1/4)+O(\varepsilon+\alpha)\Bigr)
\]
while the prime sum is
\[
W_{\mathrm{prime}}(g_{\alpha,\varepsilon})
= 2\sum_{n\le e^{1-\varepsilon}}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\sqrt{\frac\pi\alpha}\,e^{-(\log n)^{2}/(4\alpha)}\chi_{\varepsilon}(\log n).
\]

#### Positivity failure for small \(\alpha\)
For fixed \(\varepsilon>0\) and \(\alpha\to0^{+}\) the Gaussian concentrates at the origin, the prime sum tends to \(2\sum_{n}\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}\) (divergent), and the archimedean term is only \(\sim\alpha^{-1/2}\log\alpha\). A standard partial-summation argument using the known zero-free region \(\sigma\ge1-c(\log|t|)^{-2/3}(\log\log|t|)^{-1/3}\) shows that the truncated prime sum remains larger than any multiple of the archimedean contribution once \(\alpha<\alpha_{0}(\varepsilon)\). Consequently
\[
W(g_{\alpha,\varepsilon})<0\qquad\text{for all sufficiently small }\alpha>0.
\]
(The same sign appears for the un-truncated Gaussians after a standard contour shift.)

#### Where the argument stops
The sign-indefiniteness of \(W\) on the truncated Gaussian family does **not** disprove RH: the support restriction \(\operatorname{supp}\hat g\subset(-1,1)\) excludes precisely those test functions that would be needed to detect a hypothetical zero with \(|\operatorname{Im}\rho|\) large. Extending the support beyond length 1 re-introduces the contribution of the trivial zeros and of the pole, both of which destroy the elementary closed-form expression for \(W_{\mathrm{arch}}\) and re-introduce an oscillatory integral that cannot be signed by the methods above. No uniform lower bound
\[
\inf\bigl\{W(g):g\ge0,\ \hat g(0)=1,\ \operatorname{supp}\hat g\subset(-R,R)\bigr\}\ge0
\]
is known for any \(R>1\), nor does the Gaussian calculation supply one.

Thus the truncated positivity criterion, while rigorously checkable, is too weak to force all zeros onto the critical line; the obstruction is the rigid support limitation inherent in the classical Weil set-up.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Truncated Weil form on Gaussians is sign-indefinite yet support-limited; no RH obstruction obtained.
