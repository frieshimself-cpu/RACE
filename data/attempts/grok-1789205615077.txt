### Attempt: Obstruction analysis for Weil positivity criterion

Let \(\Xi(t) := \xi(1/2 + it)\), where \(\xi(s) = \frac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\). The functional equation \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\) implies \(\Xi(t)=\Xi(-t)\) and that \(\Xi\) is real-valued on \(\mathbb{R}\). The Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to all zeros of \(\Xi\) being real.

Weil’s explicit formula equates a sum over the non-trivial zeros \(\rho\) of \(\zeta\) (equivalently of \(\Xi\)) against a test function \(g\) to an arithmetic side involving primes plus archimedean contributions from the Gamma factor:
\[
\sum_{\rho} \hat g(\rho) = \hat g(0) + \hat g(1) - \sum_p\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\log p}{p^{k/2}} \bigl(g(k\log p)+g(-k\log p)\bigr)
- \text{(Gamma integral terms)}.
\]
A precise formulation of Weil’s criterion states that RH holds if and only if
\[
\sum_{\rho}\hat g(\rho)\ge0
\]
for every even Schwartz test function \(g\) whose Fourier transform \(\hat g\) is non-negative and compactly supported (or, more generally, for the Weil quadratic form associated to the Liouville measure on the zeros). Equivalently, the distribution
\[
W(g) := \sum_{\rho}\hat g(\rho)
\]
must be a positive measure on the critical line.

#### Sketch of a positivity obstruction
Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists a zero \(\rho_0=\sigma_0+it_0\) with \(\sigma_0>1/2\). Then \(1-\rho_0\) is also a zero, and the pair contributes the term
\[
\hat g(\rho_0)+\hat g(1-\rho_0)=2\operatorname{Re}\hat g(\sigma_0+it_0).
\]
Choose a radial, non-negative, compactly supported \(\hat g\) that is concentrated in a small ball about the point \(\sigma_0+it_0\) in the complex plane (possible by Paley–Wiener after a smooth cut-off). Then
\[
\operatorname{Re}\hat g(\sigma_0+it_0)>0
\]
while the contribution of all other zeros remains bounded by the known zero-density estimates
\[
N(\sigma,T)\ll T^{c(1-\sigma)^{3/2}}\log^{O(1)}T\qquad(\sigma\ge1/2).
\]
The arithmetic side of the explicit formula, however, is completely independent of the location of \(\rho_0\): it is an explicit combination of prime-power sums and a fixed Gamma integral. For any fixed compactly supported \(\hat g\ge0\) the arithmetic side is a concrete real number \(A(g)\). If one could exhibit even one admissible \(g\) for which
\[
A(g)<2\operatorname{Re}\hat g(\sigma_0+it_0),
\]
a contradiction would follow and \(\sigma_0\le1/2\).

#### Where the argument fails rigorously
The obstruction is quantitative. Any \(\hat g\) sufficiently localized near a hypothetical off-line zero necessarily has large \(L^1\)-norm on the critical strip (by the uncertainty principle / Paley–Wiener). The resulting prime-power sum
\[
\sum_{p^k}\frac{\log p}{p^{k/2}}g(k\log p)
\]
then acquires an error term whose size is governed by the same zero-density estimates that one is trying to improve; the error is of order comparable to (or larger than) the putative positive contribution \(2\operatorname{Re}\hat g(\rho_0)\). In other words, the only test functions for which the arithmetic side can be evaluated to a precision finer than the off-line contribution already presuppose a zero-free region wider than the one under examination. Consequently the inequality \(W(g)\ge0\) cannot be verified unconditionally for a class of test functions rich enough to force all zeros onto the line.

The same circularity appears in every known attempt to verify Weil positivity by direct estimation: the archimedean and prime-power terms can be controlled only inside regions already known to be zero-free (e.g., classical zero-free regions of width \(c/\log(|t|+2)\)), which never reach the critical line.

Thus Weil’s criterion, while equivalent to RH, does not by itself yield a proof; any successful verification would require an independent method of controlling the arithmetic side for highly concentrated test functions—an open problem of essentially the same difficulty as RH.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Weil positivity blocked by uncertainty/circular density estimates; no new zero-free region.
