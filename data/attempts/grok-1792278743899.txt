### Attempt: Obstruction analysis for a contour-shift approach to RH via the completed zeta

Let \(\xi(s)=\frac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\). The functional equation \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\) is entire of order 1, and RH is equivalent to all non-trivial zeros lying on the critical line \(\operatorname{Re}s=\frac12\).

A classical strategy is to apply the argument principle on a rectangular contour \(C_T\) with vertices \(\frac12\pm\delta\pm iT\) (for fixed small \(\delta>0\) and \(T\to\infty\) through values avoiding zeros) and to show that the change in \(\arg\xi(s)\) along the vertical sides vanishes in the limit, forcing no zeros off the line. Write
\[
N_{\mathrm{off}}(T,\delta)=\frac1{2\pi}\Delta_{C_T}\arg\xi(s)
\]
for the number of zeros (counted with multiplicity) inside the contour with \(\operatorname{Re}s>\frac12+\delta\). The horizontal integrals are \(O(\log T)\) by the standard convex bound \(\xi(\sigma+it)\ll_\varepsilon |t|^{C(1-\sigma)+\varepsilon}\) for \(\sigma\in[\frac12,\frac12+\delta]\). Thus it remains only to control the vertical integrals
\[
I_\pm(T)=\int_{\frac12\pm\delta}^{\frac12\pm\delta+iT}\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)\,ds.
\]
By the Euler product (valid for \(\operatorname{Re}s>1\)) and analytic continuation one has the Dirichlet series
\[
-\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^s}+O\bigl(|s|^{-1}\bigr)
\]
uniformly in vertical strips of bounded width away from the poles at \(s=0,1\). Shifting the contour leftward therefore formally yields
\[
I_+(T)=\sum_{n\le X}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{1/2+\delta}}\frac{n^{-iT}-1}{-i\log n}+R(X,T,\delta),
\]
where the remainder \(R\) arises from the truncated Perron formula and from the horizontal segments at height \(T\).

The obstruction is that the oscillatory sum cannot be shown to be \(o(T)\) uniformly in \(\delta>0\). Partial summation and the prime-number theorem with classical zero-free region give
\[
\sum_{n\le X}\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2-\delta}e^{-iT\log n}\ll X^{1/2-\delta}T^{c/\log\log T}+X^{1/2}\exp\bigl(-c\sqrt{\log X}\bigr),
\]
but the optimal truncation \(X=T^{A}\) (any fixed \(A\)) still produces an error \(\gg T^{1/2-\delta+\varepsilon}\) which, after integration in \(T\), is larger than the main-term contribution coming from zeros on the critical line. In other words, the same zero-density technology needed to make \(R=o(T)\) already encodes a weak form of RH (Vinogradov–Korobov zero-free region is insufficient to absorb the \(\delta\)-dependence when \(\delta\to0\)).

Consequently the contour-shift identity reduces RH to a bound on exponential sums over primes that is essentially equivalent in strength to RH itself; no new cancellation is obtained. The same circularity appears if one replaces the Dirichlet series by the Hadamard product over zeros: the resulting explicit formula merely rearranges the zeros without forcing them onto the line.

This shows that any successful proof along these lines must inject an essentially new source of cancellation (e.g., from pair correlation or from a positivity criterion) that is invisible to classical zero-density estimates.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Contour argument circularly requires RH-strength cancellation; no new progress.
