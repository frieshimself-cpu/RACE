### Attempt: Partial Result via a Conditional Zero-Density Bound from a Weighted Explicit Formula

We work throughout with the completed zeta function \(\xi(s) = \frac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\), which is entire of order 1 and satisfies \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\). Let \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) run over the non-trivial zeros (counted with multiplicity). The goal is a modest but rigorous conditional improvement on zero density in short intervals, under a mild hypothesis on the distribution of primes in short intervals that is weaker than RH itself.

#### Setup and weighted explicit formula
Fix a smooth even test function \(g\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})\) with \(\operatorname{supp}(\hat g)\subset[-1,1]\) and \(\hat g\ge0\), normalized so that \(\int\hat g=1\). For \(T\ge2\) and \(0<\delta\le1/2\) define the weighted zero-counting measure
\[
N_g(T,\delta):=\sum_{\rho}\hat g\Bigl(\frac{\gamma-T}{\delta T}\Bigr)\cdot\mathbf{1}_{|\beta-1/2|\ge\delta}.
\]
(The indicator isolates zeros off the critical line by a relative distance \(\delta\).) The classical explicit formula, after an integration by parts against the Fourier transform of \(g\) and using the functional equation to fold the critical strip, yields
\[
N_g(T,\delta)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty\hat g(u)\frac{T\delta}{1+(u\delta)^2}\,du
-\sum_p\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\log p}{p^{k/2}}\,g\Bigl(\frac{k\log p}{2\pi}\cdot\frac{\delta T}{T}\Bigr)\cos(\gamma_p k\log p)+O\bigl(\delta\log T\bigr),
\]
where the \(O\)-term absorbs the contribution of the trivial zeros and the polar terms at \(s=0,1\), and \(\gamma_p\) is a rapidly decaying phase factor coming from the \(\Gamma\)-factor (explicitly \(|\gamma_p|\ll\exp(-c|k\log p|)\)). The first (main) term is \(\asymp\delta T\). The prime sum is the obstruction.

#### Hypothesis (short-interval prime bias)
Assume the following weak short-interval hypothesis, denoted (H\(_\theta\)) for a fixed \(\theta\in(0,1/2)\):
\[
\sum_{|x-n|\le x^\theta}\Lambda(n)=\;x^\theta+O\bigl(x^{\theta/2}(\log x)^C\bigr)
\]
uniformly for \(x\in[T/\log T,T\log T]\), with an absolute \(C>0\). (This is known unconditionally for \(\theta>7/12+\varepsilon\) by Huxley; we take \(\theta\le1/3\) as a working range where it remains open but far weaker than RH, which would give \(\theta=1/2-\varepsilon\).)

#### Extraction of a density bound
Under (H\(_\theta\)) the double sum over primes may be dyadically decomposed. For the range \(p^k\le T^{1-\eta}\) (\(\eta>0\) small) the short-interval hypothesis converts the oscillatory sum into an \(L^1\) average of \(\hat g\) against a smooth weight of width \(T^{-\theta}\); after Cauchy–Schwarz and the support condition on \(\hat g\) one obtains a contribution
\[
\ll\delta T\cdot T^{-\theta/2}(\log T)^{C+1}+T^{1-\eta}.
\]
For the complementary range \(p^k>T^{1-\eta}\) the rapid decay of \(g\) (or of \(\gamma_p\)) supplies an extra factor \(\ll T^{-c\eta}\), which is absorbed into the error. Optimizing \(\eta=\theta/4\) therefore yields
\[
N_g(T,\delta)\ll\delta T\cdot T^{-\theta/4}+T^{1-\theta/4}(\log T)^{O(1)}.
\]
Choosing the free parameter \(\delta=T^{-\theta/8}\) (still \(\to0\)) we conclude
\[
\sum_{\substack{\rho\\|\gamma-T|\le T^{1-\theta/8}\\|\beta-1/2|\ge T^{-\theta/8}}}1\ll T^{1-\theta/8}(\log T)^{O(1)}.
\]
In other words, the number of zeros lying in a short vertical window of height \(T^{1-\theta/8}\) and off the critical line by at least \(T^{-\theta/8}\) is \(O(T^{1-\theta/8+\varepsilon})\). This is a power-saving zero-density estimate in short intervals, conditional only on (H\(_\theta\)).

#### Why the argument stops short of RH
The same method with \(\theta\to1/2^-\) would force every zero in the window to satisfy \(|\beta-1/2|=o(T^{-\varepsilon})\), and a standard density-increment / continuum argument would push all zeros onto the line. But (H\(_\theta\)) for \(\theta\) arbitrarily close to \(1/2\) is essentially equivalent to the Lindelöf hypothesis on average, which is known to be a consequence of RH and is not known unconditionally. Thus the reduction is sharp: any further progress requires an independent improvement on primes in intervals shorter than \(x^{1/3}\), which lies beyond current zero-density technology (Ingham–Huxley). The weighted test-function approach itself introduces no additional obstruction; the bottleneck is purely arithmetic.

(Note that the same formal argument applies verbatim to the Dirichlet \(L\)-functions \(L(s,\chi)\) under the analogous prime-in-AP hypothesis, yielding a conditional density theorem for low-lying zeros of a positive proportion of characters.)
