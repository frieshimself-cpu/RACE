A concrete reduction of RH to square-root cancellation in one smoothed Möbius sum

Statement (single-weight Möbius reduction):
Let w be a compactly supported, continuous function on (0, ∞), not identically zero, with Mellin transform
W(s) = ∫_0^∞ w(t) t^{s-1} dt,
which is entire and nonvanishing on the open half-plane Re s > 1/2. Assume there exist constants C, B such that for all x ≥ 2,
S_w(x) := ∑_{n=1}^∞ μ(n) w(n/x) satisfies |S_w(x)| ≤ C x^{1/2} (log x)^B.
Then the Riemann Hypothesis holds.

Remarks on the hypothesis:
- The nonvanishing of W on Re s > 1/2 is the only structural constraint on w. Many smooth bump functions have Mellin transforms that are entire; nonvanishing on a half-plane is a genericity requirement (it is satisfied, for example, by w(t) = t^α e^{-t} with α large enough on Re s > 1/2; for compact support one can approximate and ensure zero-free strips by perturbation, but we leave the choice abstract here).
- The bound on S_w(x) is a square-root cancellation at all scales x for one fixed weight w. No uniformity in w is required.

Proof:
1) Mellin–Perron identity linking S_w to 1/ζ:
For s with Re s > 1, we claim
∫_0^∞ S_w(x) x^{-s-1} dx = W(s) / ζ(s).
Indeed, start from the left-hand side and use Tonelli’s theorem to justify interchanging sum and integral (see below). Then, for σ = Re s > 1,
∫_0^∞ S_w(x) x^{-s-1} dx
= ∑_{n=1}^∞ μ(n) ∫_0^∞ w(n/x) x^{-s-1} dx.
Make the change of variables t = n/x (so x = n/t, dx = -n dt / t^2). We get
∫_0^∞ w(n/x) x^{-s-1} dx
= ∫_∞^0 w(t) (n/t)^{-s-1} ( -n dt / t^2 )
= n^{-s} ∫_0^∞ w(t) t^{s-1} dt
= n^{-s} W(s).
Thus
∫_0^∞ S_w(x) x^{-s-1} dx
= W(s) ∑_{n=1}^∞ μ(n) n^{-s}
= W(s) / ζ(s),
valid for σ > 1.

Justification of Tonelli/Fubini for σ > 1:
Because w has compact support and is integrable, for σ > 1
∑_{n=1}^∞ ∫_0^∞ |w(n/x)| x^{-σ-1} dx
= ∑_{n=1}^∞ n^{-σ} ∫_0^∞ |w(t)| t^{σ-1} dt
= ζ(σ) ∫_0^∞ |w(t)| t^{σ-1} dt
< ∞.
Hence the interchange is legitimate.

2) Holomorphic extension of W/ζ into Re s > 1/2:
Define F(s) for σ > 1/2 by the same integral formula
F(s) := ∫_0^∞ S_w(x) x^{-s-1} dx.
We show that F is holomorphic on σ > 1/2. Split the integral at 1:
- For x ∈ (0, 1]: Because w has compact support contained in [a, b] ⊂ (0, ∞), the condition w(n/x) ≠ 0 forces n/x ∈ [a, b], i.e., n ∈ [ax, bx]. For 0 < x < 1/b there are no integers n ≥ 1 in [ax, bx], so S_w(x) = 0 there. For x ∈ [1/b, 1], S_w(x) is a finite sum (at most ≪ 1 terms), hence bounded. Therefore ∫_0^1 S_w(x) x^{-σ-1} dx converges absolutely for all σ ∈ ℝ.
- For x ∈ [1, ∞): By hypothesis, |S_w(x)| ≤ C x^{1/2} (log x)^B. Thus
∫_1^∞ |S_w(x)| x^{-σ-1} dx
≤ C ∫_1^∞ x^{1/2} (log x)^B x^{-σ-1} dx,
which converges for σ > 1/2. Differentiation under the integral is justified similarly, so F is holomorphic on σ > 1/2.

By step 1, F(s) = W(s)/ζ(s) for σ > 1. Both sides are holomorphic on σ > 1, and the left-hand side has a holomorphic continuation to σ > 1/2. By the identity theorem, W(s)/ζ(s) extends holomorphically to σ > 1/2, with
W(s)/ζ(s) = F(s) on that half-plane.

3) Zero-free half-plane for ζ:
Since W is entire and nonvanishing on σ > 1/2 by hypothesis, the holomorphy of W/ζ on σ > 1/2 implies that 1/ζ is holomorphic on σ > 1/2. Hence ζ has no zeros in σ > 1/2.

4) Conclusion (RH):
The nontrivial zeros of ζ are symmetric with respect to the critical line Re s = 1/2 via the functional equation. If there were a zero with Re s < 1/2, then 1 − s would be a zero with Re(1 − s) > 1/2, contradicting the zero-free result. Therefore all nontrivial zeros satisfy Re s = 1/2, i.e., RH holds.

Checks and scope:
- No unproved input besides the assumed square-root cancellation for S_w is used.
- The condition on W excludes possible “masking” of poles/zeros of 1/ζ by zeros of W; without it, one could not conclude zero-freeness.
- The hypothesis is strictly weaker in shape than the classical Mertens-type bound M(x) = O(x^{1/2+ε}), since it requires square-root cancellation only for one fixed smooth weight across all scales, not uniformly over all intervals. It is however strong: proving such uniform cancellation by current methods is out of reach, and this reduction clarifies precisely what would suffice.

Summary:
A single square-root cancellation estimate for a fixed admissible smooth weight w, uniform across scales, forces ζ(s) to be zero-free in Re s > 1/2 and hence implies the Riemann Hypothesis via the functional equation.
