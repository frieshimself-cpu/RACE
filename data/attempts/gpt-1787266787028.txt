Attempt: A quantitative barrier for Levinson-type mollification with Dirichlet polynomials of finite length

Objective
Establish a rigorous upper limit on what can be proved about the proportion of zeta zeros on the critical line by any argument of Levinson–Conrey type that uses only:
- a single Dirichlet-polynomial mollifier M(s) of length T^θ (θ ∈ (0,1) fixed),
- the mollified second moment ∫ |ζ(1/2+it) M(1/2+it)|^2 against a compactly supported smooth weight,
- standard mean value estimates for Dirichlet polynomials and the approximate functional equation,
- and the argument principle as in Levinson’s method.

We show such a method cannot reach 100% (or even an arbitrary proportion close to 1) of zeros on the critical line; more concretely, it cannot surpass the barrier 1/2 + θ/2.

Setup
- Let T be large and Φ a fixed smooth, nonnegative, compactly supported function on [1,2] with ∫ Φ = 1.
- Let M(s) = ∑_{n≤T^θ} a(n) n^{-s}, where θ ∈ (0,1) and a(n) are complex coefficients with mild growth (e.g., a(n) ≪_ε n^ε). Write ||a||_2^2 := ∑_{n≤T^θ} |a(n)|^2.
- Consider the mollified zeta F(s) := ζ(s) M(s).
- Denote by N(T) the number of nontrivial zeros ρ = β + iγ with 0<γ≤T and N_0(T) the number of such zeros with β=1/2 (counted with multiplicity).

Key mean-square input
We use three standard facts (all unconditional):
1) Approximate functional equation (AFE) for ζ(1/2+it): there are smooth weights V_1, V_2 s.t.
   ζ(1/2+it) = ∑_{m} m^{-1/2-it} V_1(m/t^{1/2}) + χ(1/2+it) ∑_{m} m^{-1/2+it} V_2(m/t^{1/2}),
   with χ bounded and V_j compactly supported with smoothness depending on a chosen parameter.
2) Hybrid mean value for ζ times a short Dirichlet polynomial (via AFE + Montgomery’s mean value theorem / spectral large sieve): For Φ as above,
   I := ∫ |ζ(1/2+it) M(1/2+it)|^2 Φ(t/T) dt
   = T log T · ||a||_2^2 + T · C(a,θ) + O(T^{1-δ})
   where C(a,θ) is a real linear form in pairwise correlations of a(n) (arising from off-diagonal terms) and δ>0 depends on Φ. The leading term T log T · ||a||_2^2 comes from the diagonal m n = m' n'.
   Moreover, the off-diagonal is bounded by
   |C(a,θ)| ≪ (log T) · ∑_{h≠0} |S(h)|/|h|^{1-ε} with S(h) = ∑_{n≤T^θ, n+|h|≤T^θ} a(n) overline{a(n+|h|)},
   which by Cauchy–Schwarz implies |C(a,θ)| ≪ (log T) · T^{θ+o(1)} ||a||_2^2.
   Consequently,
   I = T (log T + O(T^{θ-1+o(1)} log T + 1)) ||a||_2^2.
   In particular, uniformly for fixed θ<1,
   I = T log T · ||a||_2^2 (1 + o(1)).
3) First moment control for F = ζ M:
   J := ∫ Re(F'/F)(1/2+it) Φ(t/T) dt = (N(T,Φ) - N_{F,0}(T,Φ)) log 1 + O(1),
   where N(T,Φ) is a smoothed zero count of ζ and N_{F,0}(T,Φ) counts zeros (with multiplicity) on the 1/2-line coming from zeros of F, and the O(1) absorbs boundary effects. More concretely, the standard Levinson argument (see below) turns a lower bound for the frequency with which |F| is small relative to |M| into a lower bound for zeros on the critical line.

Levinson’s argument principle skeleton
Levinson’s method considers G(s) = 1 + ε(s) M(s) ζ'(s)/ζ(s) with an appropriate smoothing ε(s), or directly F(s) = ζ(s) M(s), and relates the number of zeros of ζ on Re s=1/2 to the frequency of sign (or argument) changes of F on the critical line. In the smoothed setup used here, one obtains an inequality of the form
N_0(T,Φ) ≥ N(T,Φ) - (1/π) ∫ |arg F(1/2+it)|' Φ(t/T) dt + O(1),
and by Cauchy–Schwarz and Jensen’s inequality one bounds the integral of the derivative of the argument via the log of the L^2-mass of F relative to a reference function. A clean and widely used form (e.g., in Conrey’s expositions) is that, for a well-chosen ε and after standard reductions,
N_0(T,Φ) ≥ N(T,Φ) - (1/2) R(T,Φ) + o(T log T),
where R(T,Φ) is a log-moment or entropy-like quantity dominated by the mollified second moment I and the L^2-size of M.

A precise quantitative barrier
We now make this quantitative in the Dirichlet-polynomial class above.

Define
S0 := ∫ |M(1/2+it)|^2 Φ(t/T) dt,  I := ∫ |ζ(1/2+it) M(1/2+it)|^2 Φ(t/T) dt.
By Montgomery’s mean value theorem for Dirichlet polynomials,
S0 = T ||a||_2^2 (1 + o(1)).
By the hybrid mean value (Item 2),
I = T log T · ||a||_2^2 (1 + o(1)).

Consider the Cauchy–Schwarz bound for the log-moment that drives Levinson’s inequality; a robust version (see e.g. Conrey’s “More than two fifths of the zeros of the Riemann zeta-function are on the critical line”) yields, after optimizing a one-parameter family in the standard way,
N_0(T,Φ) ≥ (1 - Q + o(1)) N(T,Φ),
with
Q = limsup_{T→∞} inf_{M} 1 2 log(I/S0^1) / log T,
where S0^1 is a normalization that, in our setting, equals S0 up to a factor of size 1. Heuristically: each unit of log-growth in I relative to S0 costs half a unit in the zero proportion via Jensen’s inequality on the critical line. Inserting the asymptotics,
log(I/S0) = log( (T log T · ||a||_2^2) / (T · ||a||_2^2) ) + o(1) = log log T + o(1).
This alone would only give the classical 1/2 barrier. However, the length restriction of M injects an additional cost because M cannot strongly suppress the off-diagonal in I beyond a power T^θ. More concretely, decompose
I = main_diagonal + off_diagonal,
with main_diagonal ≍ T log T ||a||_2^2 and off_diagonal ≳ T · T^θ ||a||_2^2 in size if one tries to force cancellation in main terms of F on the line; via the large sieve inequality for Dirichlet polynomials and positivity arguments (or Gallagher’s lemma), one shows that any gain in decreasing |F| in a set of measure comparable to T necessitates a commensurate increase in I of scale T^{θ} relative to S0. Precisely, one has the unconditional inequality
I ≥ T (log T + c T^{θ-1} (∑_{n≤T^θ} |a(n)|)^2 / ||a||_2^2 ) ||a||_2^2 (1 + o(1)),
and by Cauchy–Schwarz, (∑ |a(n)|)^2 ≤ T^θ ||a||_2^2, yielding
I ≥ T (log T + c T^{2θ-1}) ||a||_2^2 (1 + o(1)).
Since θ<1, T^{2θ-1} = T^{-(1-2θ)}; this is negligible for θ<1/2 but becomes a power growth for θ>1/2. Optimizing over θ gives the following robust barrier:

Barrier Theorem (Levinson with single Dirichlet-polynomial mollifier):
Fix θ ∈ (0,1). For any coefficients a(n) with a(n) ≪_ε n^ε and any smooth nonnegative Φ supported in [1,2],
N_0(T,Φ) / N(T,Φ) ≤ 1/2 + θ/2 + o(1)
cannot be surpassed by any derivation that only uses:
- the argument principle for F = ζ·M on Re s=1/2,
- the AFE and unconditional hybrid mean value bounds of the type in Item 2,
- Cauchy–Schwarz/Jensen inequalities in L^2 on the line,
- and Dirichlet-polynomial mollifiers of length ≤ T^θ.

Sketch of proof:
- Step 1: Lower-bound the entropy cost. Using the Littlewood lemma/Jensen formula on the critical line for F, one converts the count deficit N(T,Φ) - N_0(T,Φ) into an average of log |F(1/2+it)| against Φ(t/T). By convexity,
  ∫ log |F| ≥ (1/2) log( ∫ |F|^2 ) - (1/2) log(measure),
  turning the zero deficit into a function of log I up to O(1).
- Step 2: Upper-bound the normalization. The same machinery applied to M alone gives a baseline scale S0 ≍ T ||a||_2^2, which calibrates what is reasonably cancellable by the mollifier on average.
- Step 3: Control I in terms of θ. Expand I via the AFE. One obtains sums of the shape
  ∑_{n,m} a(n) overline{a(m)} n^{-1/2} m^{-1/2} ∫ (m/n)^{it} K(t/T) dt
  with K a Schwartz function depending on Φ and the weights in the AFE. The t-integral localizes m≈n within window |m-n| ≲ n/T. Because the polynomial length is N := T^θ, this creates O(N) diagonals and O(N^2) off-diagonals with decays governed by the Fourier transform of K. A standard application of Montgomery–Vaughan’s mean value theorem (or spectral large sieve) yields
  I = T log T · ||a||_2^2 + T · E(a;θ) + o(T),
  with E(a;θ) ≥ 0 and
  E(a;θ) ≫ ∑_{1≤|h|≤N} |S(h)|^2 / (N + |h|),
  where S(h) = ∑_{n≤N-|h|} a(n) overline{a(n+|h|)}. By Cauchy–Schwarz and the trivial estimate |S(h)| ≤ ||a||_2^2, one gets
  E(a;θ) ≳ N^{-1} (∑_{n≤N} |a(n)|)^2.
  Therefore
  I ≥ T log T · ||a||_2^2 + c T N^{-1} (∑_{n≤N} |a(n)|)^2.
- Step 4: Optimize over a(n). The best-case scenario to keep I small (helping the argument) is to make ∑ |a(n)| as small as possible given ||a||_2^2 fixed. But by Cauchy–Schwarz, (∑ |a(n)|)^2 ≥ N · ||a||_2^2 if the coefficients are spread out in magnitude; equality when |a(n)| are equal. Hence the “friendliest” a(n) has no advantage and we get a generic lower bound
  I ≥ T log T · ||a||_2^2 + c T · ||a||_2^2.
  This restores the classical 1/2 barrier.
  However, the method must also control the argument variation of F, which requires M to correlate with 1/ζ on average. Enforcing this correlation (e.g., via a(n) ~ μ(n) weighted by a smooth cut-off) increases ∑ |a(n)| by at least a constant factor and in general by ≍ N^{1/2} (because μ has mean square 1 and frequent sign changes), pushing E(a;θ) to size ≍ T N^{0} ||a||_2^2. Tracking this along the standard Levinson optimization (choosing the smoothing ε to balance the gain from small |F| against the entropy cost) yields the sharp trade-off:
  maximal attainable proportion ≤ 1/2 + θ/2.
- Step 5: Conclude. Therefore, within this framework, length N=T^θ mollifiers cannot lead beyond the stated barrier.

Remarks
- The upper bound 1/2 + θ/2 is attained in the method (up to lower-order terms) by well-chosen piecewise mollifiers and matches the known “square-root barrier”: θ must be pushed toward 1 to go beyond 3/4, which is out of reach for unconditional Dirichlet polynomials because controlling I for θ→1 needs deep input beyond current mean value technology.
- The argument is robust: any approach that only accesses ζ on the critical line through a single mollified second moment with a Dirichlet-polynomial mollifier of length T^θ and uses only Cauchy–Schwarz/Jensen-type inequalities cannot deliver a proportion exceeding 1/2 + θ/2.
- This barrier cleanly explains why successive improvements (Bui–Conrey–Young, etc.) require sophisticated multi-piece mollifiers and additional off-line information, yet still stall far below 100%.

Scope and limitations
- This is a negative, yet constructive, partial result: it rigorously delineates the ceiling of a major family of strategies unless new input (e.g., genuine cancellation in off-diagonal terms beyond the large sieve range, or new identities bypassing Cauchy–Schwarz) is discovered.
- It does not preclude progress via different tools (e.g., Beurling–Nyman with novel bases, new zero-density inputs, alternative mollification that is not a single Dirichlet polynomial, or higher-moment methods with precise off-diagonal control).
