### Partial result: The Hodge conjecture holds in dimension \(\leq 3\)

Let \(X\) be a smooth complex projective variety of dimension \(n\leq 3\). Write \(H^{k}(X,\mathbb{Q})\) for singular cohomology with rational coefficients, and let
\[
\operatorname{Hdg}^{p}(X):=H^{2p}(X,\mathbb{Q})\cap H^{p,p}(X)
\]
be the space of rational Hodge classes of type \((p,p)\). The cycle class map
\[
\operatorname{cl}:\operatorname{CH}^{p}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\operatorname{Hdg}^{p}(X)
\]
is known to be surjective when \(p=0\) or \(p=n\) (points and the fundamental class). It remains to treat the intermediate degrees. We use the Lefschetz theorem on \((1,1)\)-classes, the hard Lefschetz theorem, and the Lefschetz hyperplane theorem.

#### Case \(n=1\)
Only \(p=0,1\) arise; both are immediate.

#### Case \(n=2\)
The only nontrivial group is \(\operatorname{Hdg}^{1}(X)\). By the Lefschetz theorem on \((1,1)\)-classes every class in
\[
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})\cap H^{1,1}(X)
\]
is the class of a divisor, so \(\operatorname{cl}\) is surjective.

#### Case \(n=3\)
The groups \(\operatorname{Hdg}^{0}\) and \(\operatorname{Hdg}^{3}\) are trivial as above. For \(\operatorname{Hdg}^{1}(X)\) the Lefschetz \((1,1)\)-theorem again gives surjectivity of \(\operatorname{cl}\). It remains to treat \(\operatorname{Hdg}^{2}(X)\).

Hard Lefschetz supplies an isomorphism
\[
L:H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{4}(X,\mathbb{Q}),\qquad\alpha\mapsto\alpha\cup\omega,
\]
where \(\omega=c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(1))\) is the class of a hyperplane section. This isomorphism is of pure Hodge type: it sends \(H^{p,q}\) onto \(H^{p+1,q+1}\). In particular it restricts to an isomorphism of rational Hodge structures
\[
L:\operatorname{Hdg}^{1}(X)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Hdg}^{2}(X).
\]
Any class \(\beta\in\operatorname{Hdg}^{2}(X)\) may therefore be written \(\beta=L(\alpha)\) for a unique \(\alpha\in\operatorname{Hdg}^{1}(X)\). By the already-established case \(p=1\), \(\alpha=\operatorname{cl}(D)\) for some divisor \(D\). Then
\[
\beta=\operatorname{cl}(D\cdot H),
\]
where \(H\) is a hyperplane section; the intersection product \(D\cdot H\) is an algebraic 1-cycle (codimension 2). Hence \(\operatorname{cl}\) is surjective on \(\operatorname{Hdg}^{2}(X)\).

(The same argument with Poincaré duality shows that the dual statement for homology classes holds equally.)

Thus the Hodge conjecture is true for every smooth complex projective variety of dimension at most 3.

(Note that the argument uses projectivity in an essential way via the existence of an ample class \(\omega\) that realises hard Lefschetz; it does not extend to arbitrary Kähler threefolds.)

REASON: Rigorous proof that HC holds for all smooth projective complex varieties of dim ≤3 (standard but self-contained).
