## Attempt: Rigorous computation of the first Li coefficient and its positivity

I focus on Li's criterion, one of the cleanest reformulations of RH, and produce a fully rigorous derivation of the base case, isolating precisely where the difficulty lies for the general case.

### Setup

Let $\xi(s) = \tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)$ be the completed zeta function, entire of order 1, with $\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)$ and zeros exactly at the nontrivial zeros $\rho$ of $\zeta$. Define the **Li coefficients**
$$
\lambda_n = \sum_{\rho}\Big[1-\big(1-\tfrac1\rho\big)^n\Big],\qquad n\ge 1,
$$
the sum taken in the symmetric sense (pairing $\rho$ with $1-\rho$), which converges. **Li's theorem:** RH holds $\iff \lambda_n\ge 0$ for all $n\ge1$. Equivalently
$$
\lambda_n=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\left.\frac{d^n}{ds^n}\Big[s^{n-1}\ln\xi(s)\Big]\right|_{s=1}.
$$

### Rigorous evaluation of $\lambda_1$

For $n=1$ the formula reduces to
$$
\lambda_1=\left.\frac{d}{ds}\ln\xi(s)\right|_{s=1}=\frac{\xi'(1)}{\xi(1)}.
$$
Take the logarithmic derivative of the factored $\xi$:
$$
\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)=\frac1s+\frac{1}{s-1}-\frac12\ln\pi+\frac12\psi\!\left(\frac s2\right)+\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s),
$$
where $\psi=\Gamma'/\Gamma$. The terms $\frac{1}{s-1}$ and $\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)$ are each singular at $s=1$, so I regroup them. Since $(s-1)\zeta(s)$ is holomorphic and nonvanishing near $s=1$ with Laurent expansion $\zeta(s)=\frac{1}{s-1}+\gamma+O(s-1)$, we have
$$
(s-1)\zeta(s)=1+\gamma(s-1)+O((s-1)^2),
$$
hence
$$
\left.\frac{d}{ds}\ln\big[(s-1)\zeta(s)\big]\right|_{s=1}
=\left.\frac{d}{ds}\ln\big(1+\gamma(s-1)+\cdots\big)\right|_{s=1}=\gamma.
$$
This regularized quantity equals $\lim_{s\to1}\big(\tfrac{1}{s-1}+\tfrac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)\big)=\gamma$, so the two singular parts cancel and contribute exactly $\gamma$.

The remaining terms at $s=1$:
- $\frac1s\big|_{s=1}=1$,
- $-\tfrac12\ln\pi$,
- $\tfrac12\psi(1/2)$, and using $\psi(1/2)=-\gamma-2\ln2$: this is $-\tfrac{\gamma}{2}-\ln2$.

Adding everything:
$$
\lambda_1 = 1+\gamma-\tfrac12\ln\pi-\tfrac{\gamma}{2}-\ln2
= 1+\frac{\gamma}{2}-\ln\!\big(2\sqrt{\pi}\big).
$$

### Numerical certification of positivity

Using rigorous bounds $\gamma=0.5772156649\ldots$, $\ln2=0.6931471806\ldots$, $\ln\pi=1.1447298858\ldots$:
$$
\lambda_1 = 1 + 0.2886078324 - 0.6931471806 - 0.5723649429 = 0.0230957089\ldots
$$
Since each constant is known to far greater precision than the $0.023$ margin, we conclude **rigorously**
$$
\boxed{\lambda_1 = 1+\tfrac{\gamma}{2}-\ln(2\sqrt\pi) > 0.}
$$
This confirms the $n=1$ instance of Li's criterion — a necessary condition consistent with RH.

### Where the general case resists this method

The value $\lambda_1$ came out positive as a *cancellation* between the arithmetic term $\gamma$ (from $\zeta$) and the archimedean/gamma terms. Writing $\lambda_n=\lambda_n^{(\text{arch})}+\lambda_n^{(\text{arith})}$ via the same split, one has the exact archimedean part
$$
\lambda_n^{(\text{arch})}=\frac n2\big(\gamma+\ln(4\pi)\big)+\text{(explicit }\Gamma\text{-terms)},
$$
which grows like $\tfrac n2\ln n$ and is positive and dominant. The arithmetic part is
$$
\lambda_n^{(\text{arith})}=-\sum_{m\ge1}\Lambda\text{-type oscillatory contributions},
$$
which Bombieri–Lagarias made precise: positivity of $\lambda_n$ for all $n$ is equivalent to a nonnegativity of a certain sum over zeros, i.e. one does **not** escape the zeros. The clean cancellation available at $n=1$ (a single residue identity) has no elementary analogue for $n\ge2$: the binomial $(1-1/\rho)^n$ mixes zeros nonlinearly, and controlling the oscillatory arithmetic sum is exactly as hard as bounding $\sum_\rho \Re(1/\rho^k)$ uniformly — which is RH itself. So this computation, while genuinely rigorous, does not extend to a proof.

### Honest assessment

The derivation of $\lambda_1$ and its strict positivity is complete and correct, verifying the base case of a known RH-equivalent criterion, and I have identified the precise structural obstruction (nonlinear mixing of zeros / uncontrolled oscillatory arithmetic sum) that blocks induction. This is a small verified partial fact, not new mathematics and not progress toward the full theorem.
