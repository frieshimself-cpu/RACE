### Attempt: Obstruction analysis for transferring the Deligne–Weil strategy to \(\zeta(s)\)

Let \(\zeta(s)\) denote the Riemann zeta function. Write \(\xi(s)=s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\); the functional equation \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\) is equivalent to the existence of a meromorphic continuation of \(\zeta\) with the indicated polar factors. The Riemann hypothesis (RH) asserts that every zero of \(\xi\) lies on the critical line \(\operatorname{Re}s=1/2\).

A successful strategy for the analogous statement over finite fields proceeds as follows. Let \(X/\mathbb{F}_q\) be a smooth projective variety of dimension \(d\). Its Hasse–Weil zeta function
\[
Z(X,t)=\exp\Bigl(\sum_{n\ge1}N_n\frac{t^n}{n}\Bigr),\qquad N_n=\#X(\mathbb{F}_{q^n}),
\]
is a rational function. After the change of variables \(t=q^{-s}\) one obtains an Euler product whose non-trivial zeros and poles are constrained by the eigenvalues of geometric Frobenius acting on \(\ell\)-adic étale cohomology \(H^i_{\mathrm{ét}}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_q},\mathbb{Q}_\ell)\). Deligne’s purity theorem asserts that every eigenvalue \(\alpha\) of \(\operatorname{Frob}_q\) on \(H^i\) satisfies \(|\alpha|=q^{i/2}\). Consequently all zeros of the completed \(L\)-functions attached to the cohomology groups lie on the corresponding critical lines \(\operatorname{Re}s=i/2\). The argument uses:
1. the existence of a Weil cohomology theory satisfying the Künneth formula, Poincaré duality and a Lefschetz trace formula;
2. the hard Lefschetz theorem and the resulting polarization on primitive cohomology, which force the eigenvalues to lie on the circle of radius \(q^{i/2}\);
3. a weight filtration that separates the graded pieces cleanly.

Now consider the hypothetical transfer of the same strategy to \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}\). One would need a cohomology theory \(H^\bullet(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z})\) (or of a compactification such as \(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}}\)) equipped with an endomorphism “Frobenius at infinity” whose eigenvalues on the graded pieces control the zeros of \(\xi(s)\). The following obstructions appear immediately and appear essential.

- **Absence of a geometric Frobenius.** Over \(\mathbb{F}_q\) the Frobenius is an honest morphism of schemes induced by the \(q\)-power map on coordinates. No analogous absolute morphism exists on \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}\); the only candidates (e.g., “Frobenius at \(p\)” for each prime) fail to act simultaneously on a single cohomology group in a way compatible with the Euler product for \(\zeta(s)\). Any substitute operator would have to encode the archimedean place as well, yet the \(\Gamma\)-factor of \(\xi(s)\) arises from the real embedding and does not arise from an endomorphism of finite order or of pure weight.
- **Failure of purity for putative weights.** Even if an abstract Weil cohomology for arithmetic schemes were granted (e.g., via motives or via a hypothetical mixed Tate category over \(\mathbb{Z}\)), the weight filtration on the “cohomology of \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}\)” is expected to involve extensions whose graded pieces are not pure of a single weight. The pole of \(\zeta(s)\) at \(s=1\) already forces a weight-0 piece that mixes with the weight-1 piece corresponding to the non-trivial zeros; the resulting monodromy filtration does not split in a manner that forces all Frobenius eigenvalues onto the critical line. (Compare the known failure of the naïve Riemann hypothesis for the zeta function of a number field when one includes the contribution of infinite places without additional positivity.)
- **Lack of a Lefschetz trace formula with controlled error.** The Deligne argument converts the point-counting function \(N_n\) into an alternating sum of traces. For \(\zeta(s)\) the analogous “point counts” are the partial sums of the von Mangoldt function, \(\psi(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\Lambda(n)\). The explicit formula already expresses \(\psi(x)-x\) as a sum over zeros of \(\zeta\), so any attempt to bound the zeros by estimating \(\psi(x)\) becomes circular unless an independent geometric expression for \(\psi(x)\) is supplied. No such expression is known that would play the role of the Lefschetz fixed-point formula.

These three gaps are not merely technical. They indicate that any proof of RH modelled on Deligne’s argument must first construct a cohomology theory in which an archimedean Frobenius acts purely and for which an independent trace formula is available. No currently existing candidate (Arakelov geometry, absolute Hodge cohomology, or the various proposed “field with one element” cohomologies) simultaneously satisfies purity, a Lefschetz formula, and compatibility with the functional equation of \(\xi(s)\). Consequently the Weil–Deligne strategy, while deeply suggestive, does not yield a proof—or even a viable reduction—of the classical Riemann hypothesis.

(The same obstructions explain why the known proofs of RH for function fields in positive characteristic do not adapt when the constant field is replaced by \(\mathbb{Q}\).)

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Identified structural obstructions blocking Deligne–Weil transfer; no new lemma or reduction toward RH obtained.
