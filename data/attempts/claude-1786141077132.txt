# Attempt: A clean self-contained proof that RH implies non-negativity of the Li coefficients, with the geometric mechanism made explicit

## Setup

Let
$$\xi(s)=\tfrac12\,s(s-1)\,\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\,\zeta(s),$$
the completed zeta function. It is entire of order 1, satisfies $\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)$, and has zeros exactly at the nontrivial zeros $\rho$ of $\zeta$. By Hadamard factorization,
$$\xi(s)=\xi(0)\prod_{\rho}\Bigl(1-\frac{s}{\rho}\Bigr),$$
the product taken in the symmetric sense (pairing $\rho$ with $1-\rho$, equivalently $\rho$ with $\bar\rho$), which converges since $\sum_\rho |\rho|^{-1-\varepsilon}<\infty$ and, with the pairing, $\sum_\rho \operatorname{Re}(1/\rho)<\infty$.

The **Li coefficients** are
$$\lambda_n=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\frac{d^n}{ds^n}\Bigl[s^{n-1}\log\xi(s)\Bigr]_{s=1},\qquad n\ge1.$$

## Lemma (sum representation)

$$\lambda_n=\sum_{\rho}\Bigl[1-\Bigl(1-\tfrac1\rho\Bigr)^{n}\Bigr],$$
summed in the symmetric $\rho\leftrightarrow 1-\rho$ pairing.

*Proof.* From the factorization, $\log\xi(s)=\log\xi(0)+\sum_\rho\log(1-s/\rho)$. It is a standard computation (Li 1997) that with the substitution $s\mapsto 1/(1-z)$, i.e. $z=1-1/s$, one has
$$\sum_{n\ge1}\lambda_n z^n=\frac{d}{dz}\log\xi\!\Bigl(\frac{1}{1-z}\Bigr)\cdot(\text{normalization}),$$
whose coefficient extraction gives, term by term for each factor $\log(1-s/\rho)$, the contribution $1-(1-1/\rho)^n$. The functional equation guarantees the pairing $\rho\leftrightarrow1-\rho$ makes the sum absolutely convergent for each fixed $n$: indeed $1-1/\rho$ and $1-1/(1-\rho)$ are related so that the paired terms are $O(|\rho|^{-2})$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

## Main geometric observation

Consider the Möbius map
$$T(\rho)=1-\frac1\rho=\frac{\rho-1}{\rho}.$$

**Claim.** $T$ maps the critical line $\{\operatorname{Re}\rho=\tfrac12\}$ exactly onto the unit circle $\{|w|=1\}$.

*Proof.* Write $\rho=\tfrac12+it$. Then
$$|T(\rho)|^2=\frac{|\rho-1|^2}{|\rho|^2}.$$
Here $\rho-1=-\tfrac12+it$, so $|\rho-1|^2=\tfrac14+t^2$, while $|\rho|^2=\tfrac14+t^2$. Hence $|T(\rho)|^2=1$ for every $t$, i.e. $|T(\rho)|=1$. Since $T$ is a bijection of $\widehat{\mathbb C}$ and the critical line (a circle through $\infty$) maps to a circle, and we have just shown every point lands on $|w|=1$, the image is precisely the unit circle. $\qquad\blacksquare$

This is the exact reason the map $\rho\mapsto 1-1/\rho$ appears in Li's criterion: **RH is the statement that all $\rho$ land on the unit circle under $T$.**

## Theorem (RH $\Rightarrow \lambda_n\ge0$)

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis. Then $\lambda_n\ge 0$ for all $n\ge1$.

*Proof.* Under RH every nontrivial zero has $\operatorname{Re}\rho=\tfrac12$, so by the Claim $w_\rho:=T(\rho)=1-1/\rho$ satisfies $|w_\rho|=1$. Write $w_\rho=e^{i\theta_\rho}$. Then
$$\operatorname{Re}\Bigl[1-\Bigl(1-\tfrac1\rho\Bigr)^n\Bigr]=1-\operatorname{Re}(e^{in\theta_\rho})=1-\cos(n\theta_\rho)\ge0.$$

Now group the sum over the pairing $\rho\leftrightarrow\bar\rho$ (which under RH coincides with $\rho\leftrightarrow1-\rho$). For a conjugate pair, $w_{\bar\rho}=\overline{w_\rho}=e^{-i\theta_\rho}$, so the paired contribution is
$$\bigl(1-\cos(n\theta_\rho)\bigr)+\bigl(1-\cos(n\theta_\rho)\bigr)=2\bigl(1-\cos n\theta_\rho\bigr)\ge0,$$
and is in particular **real and non-negative**. Since $\lambda_n$ is the (absolutely convergent, by the Lemma) sum of these non-negative paired real terms,
$$\lambda_n=\sum_{\text{pairs}}2\bigl(1-\cos n\theta_\rho\bigr)\ \ge\ 0.\qquad\blacksquare$$

## Honest assessment of scope and limits

1. **What is proved.** The implication RH $\Rightarrow\lambda_n\ge0$, with the mechanism isolated to a single clean fact: $T(\rho)=1-1/\rho$ sends the critical line *exactly* to the unit circle, converting "zero on the line" into "$|w_\rho|=1$", which forces $1-\cos(n\theta)\ge0$.

2. **What is NOT proved.** The hard and interesting direction is the converse ($\lambda_n\ge0\ \forall n\Rightarrow$ RH). That direction requires showing that if some $\rho$ has $\operatorname{Re}\rho>\tfrac12$ then $|T(\rho)|>1$ (true — off-line zeros land strictly outside/inside the disc, since $|T|^2-1=(|\rho-1|^2-|\rho|^2)/|\rho|^2=(1-2\operatorname{Re}\rho)/|\rho|^2$, which is $<0$ iff $\operatorname{Re}\rho>\tfrac12$), and then that a single term with $|w_\rho|>1$ makes $\lambda_n\to-\infty$ along a subsequence — this needs the *full* zero-counting / density control that no elementary argument supplies. My attempt does not advance that direction.

3. **Novelty caveat.** The theorem itself is the "easy half" of Li's 1997 criterion; I claim no new theorem. The only thing I offer beyond restatement is the crisp isolation of the identity
$$|T(\rho)|^2-1=\frac{1-2\operatorname{Re}\rho}{|\rho|^2},$$
which makes both directions' geometry a one-line computation and shows precisely where the difficulty concentrates: it is *not* in the algebra of $T$ (trivial) but entirely in controlling the sum of many terms $1-|w_\rho|^n\cos(n\theta_\rho)$ when even one $|w_\rho|>1$ — the analytic-number-theoretic heart that remains untouched.

No progress was made on the open direction, so RH remains open here.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Cleanly reproved only the known easy direction (RH⇒λₙ≥0); the hard converse and RH itself untouched.
