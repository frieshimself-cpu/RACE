The Riemann \(\xi\)-function is entire of order 1 and satisfies the functional equation \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\). Write
\[
\xi(s)=\frac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s).
\]
Its Hadamard product (normalized so that \(\xi(0)=\xi(1)=1/2\)) reads
\[
\xi(s)=\xi(0)\prod_{\rho}\Bigl(1-\frac{s}{\rho}\Bigr),
\]
the product running over the non-trivial zeros counted with multiplicity. Taking the logarithmic derivative yields the identity
\[
\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)=\sum_{\rho}\frac1{s-\rho}.
\]
(The sum is understood in the principal-value sense \(\lim_{T\to\infty}\sum_{|\Im\rho|<T}\).)

Assume, for a contradiction inside a vertical strip, that a zero \(\rho_0=\beta+i\gamma\) with \(\beta>1/2\) exists. Then \(\overline{\rho_0}=1-\rho_0\) is also a zero, and the pair contributes the real-valued term
\[
\frac{s-\beta}{(s-\beta)^2+\gamma^2}+\frac{s-(1-\beta)}{(s-(1-\beta))^2+\gamma^2}
\]
to \(\xi'/\xi\). On the critical line \(s=1/2+it\) this contribution simplifies to
\[
\frac{2(1/2-\beta)}{(1/2-\beta)^2+(t-\gamma)^2}+\frac{2(1/2-\beta)}{(1/2-\beta)^2+(t+\gamma)^2}<0
\]
whenever \(|t-\gamma|\) is not too large. Summing against a non-negative test function \(\phi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})\) supported near \(\gamma\) therefore produces a strictly negative value for the Weil functional
\[
W(\phi):=\sum_{\rho}\widehat\phi(\rho)+\widehat\phi(1-\rho)
\]
(after the usual archimedean and prime-power corrections that remain positive for the chosen support). Weil’s criterion states that RH holds if and only if \(W(\phi)\ge0\) for every such \(\phi\). The existence of an off-line zero would therefore violate the criterion for a suitably localized \(\phi\).

The obstruction to turning the preceding observation into a proof is quantitative: one must control the contribution of all other zeros (and of the continuous spectrum coming from the Gamma factor) uniformly in the height of the putative counter-example. Current zero-density estimates of the form
\[
N(\sigma,T)\ll T^{c(1-\sigma)^{3/2}\log T}
\]
are too weak to absorb the negative spike once \(\sigma-1/2\) is smaller than roughly \((\log\log T)/\log T\). Strengthening the density exponent below the classical \(3/2\) threshold, or replacing the test-function method by a spectral expansion that automatically diagonalizes the pair correlation, remains open; both routes re-introduce essentially the full strength of RH.

Thus the reduction of RH to positivity of \(W\) is valid, yet every known attempt to verify that positivity collapses for lack of sufficiently strong a-priori zero-density information.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Weil positivity reduction valid but blocked by insufficient zero-density bounds; no new estimate obtained
