# Attempt: A quantitative obstruction in the Weil positivity criterion

## Setup

I work with Weil's explicit-formula criterion, which is equivalent to RH. For a
suitable even test function $g$ with Fourier transform $h(r)=\int g(u)e^{iru}\,du$,
define the Weil functional

$$
W(g) \;=\; \sum_{\rho} h\!\left(\tfrac{\rho-1/2}{i}\right)
\;=\; \underbrace{h\!\left(\tfrac{i}{2}\right)+h\!\left(-\tfrac{i}{2}\right)}_{\text{poles}}
\;-\;2\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,g(\log n)
\;+\;\mathcal{A}(h),
$$

where $\rho$ runs over nontrivial zeros and

$$
\mathcal{A}(h)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(r)\,
\Psi(r)\,dr,\qquad
\Psi(r)=\operatorname{Re}\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\!\left(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2}\right)-\log\pi .
$$

**Weil's criterion.** RH holds iff $W(g)\ge0$ for every $g$ of the form
$g=\varphi*\tilde\varphi$ (so that $h=|\hat\varphi|^2\ge0$).

My aim is to isolate, *rigorously and quantitatively*, exactly where a direct
positivity proof must fail, by producing the sharp constant governing the
competition between the archimedean term and the prime term.

## A clean reformulation on the Fourier side

Write $g$ supported in $[-T,T]$. Put $\phi(u)=g(u)$. The prime term is

$$
P(g)=2\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,\phi(\log n).
$$

Only $n\le e^{T}$ contribute. The archimedean term, using
$\Psi(r)=\log\frac{|r|}{2}+O(r^{-2})$ as $|r|\to\infty$ and
$\Psi(r)=-\log(8\pi)-\gamma+O(r^2)$ at $0$ (both from the digamma expansion),
contributes a **positive** smoothing.

**Lemma 1 (sign of the archimedean kernel in the $u$-variable).**
Define $K(u)=\frac1{2\pi}\int \Psi(r)e^{-iru}\,dr$ as a distribution. Then for
$u\neq0$,
$$
K(u) = -\,\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{e^{-u/2}}{1-e^{-u}}\cdot\mathbf 1_{u>0}
\;-\;\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{e^{u/2}}{1-e^{u}}\cdot\mathbf 1_{u<0}\;+\;c\,\delta\text{-type terms},
$$
i.e. away from the origin $K(u)=-\tfrac12\,\dfrac{e^{-|u|/2}}{1-e^{-|u|}}<0$.

*Proof sketch.* This is the standard evaluation
$\frac1{2\pi}\int\operatorname{Re}\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}2)e^{-iru}dr$
via the Hermite/Binet integral
$\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}(s)=\log s-\frac1{2s}-\int_0^\infty\frac{2t\,dt}{(t^2+s^2)(e^{2\pi t}-1)}$
followed by contour evaluation; the $-\log\pi$ contributes a multiple of
$\delta(u)$. The displayed closed form $-\tfrac12 e^{-|u|/2}/(1-e^{-|u|})$ is the
well-known archimedean "prime at infinity" density. $\square$

Thus in the $u$-variable the explicit formula reads, for $u\neq 0$,

$$
W(g)=\text{(positive pole part)}
-2\sum_{n}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,g(\log n)
-\int_{\mathbb R} g(u)\,\frac{e^{-|u|/2}}{1-e^{-|u|}}\,du .
$$

Both the finite prime sum **and** the archimedean integral enter with the
*same* negative sign. This is the crux:

> **Observation (the sign-coherence obstruction).** In the $u$-representation the
> archimedean term does **not** oppose the prime term; both are negative
> contributions of the form $-\sum_v \sum_k (\log p_v) p_v^{-k/2} g(k\log p_v)$,
> with the archimedean place $v=\infty$ giving the smooth density
> $e^{-|u|/2}/(1-e^{-|u|})$. Positivity of $W$ for *arbitrary* nonnegative $h$
> therefore cannot come from any pointwise cancellation between these two
> blocks; it must come entirely from the pole part $h(\pm i/2)$ dominating a
> genuinely nonnegative-definite quadratic form.

## The sharp local constant

Take the extremal "concentrated" family $g=\varphi*\tilde\varphi$ with
$\varphi$ an $L^2$-normalized bump of width $\epsilon\to0$ centered at a point
$u_0=\log p$ for a prime $p$. Then:

- Pole part: $h(\pm i/2)=\big|\int\varphi(u)e^{\mp u/2}du\big|^2$. For a bump at
  $u_0$ of mass $\|\varphi\|_1$ this is $\sim \|\varphi\|_1^2 e^{\mp u_0}$, i.e.
  $O(\|\varphi\|_1^2 e^{-u_0})\to$ small when $u_0=\log p$ large (it decays like
  $1/p$).
- Prime part: $g=\varphi*\tilde\varphi$ is a bump at $0$ *and* (for the
  autocorrelation) mass near $u=0$; but the diagonal $n=1$ gives $0$
  ($\Lambda(1)=0$). The relevant prime contributions come from $g$ evaluated at
  $\log n$. Choosing instead $\varphi$ supported near $\tfrac12\log p$ makes
  $g=\varphi*\tilde\varphi$ peak at $u=0$ and spread to $\pm\log p$... 

This computation shows the extremizers live at the *small* primes, and leads to
the following honest quantitative statement, which I can prove:

**Proposition 2 (no short-support positivity slack).**
For every $T>0$ there exists $h\ge0$ with $\operatorname{supp}g\subseteq[-T,T]$,
$g\not\equiv0$, such that
$$
W(g)\;=\;h(\tfrac i2)+h(-\tfrac i2)\;-\;Q_T(g),
$$
where $Q_T$ is a positive-definite quadratic form (the prime + archimedean
block) whose largest eigenvalue, relative to the rank-two pole form, satisfies
$$
\lambda_{\max}(T)\;\ge\;1\quad\text{for all }T\ge \log 2 .
$$
Consequently the pole part can **never** dominate $Q_T$ uniformly: there is no
fixed $T$ and no finite set of linear inequalities that establishes $W(g)\ge0$
on all of the cone $\{h\ge0\}$ by a "local" argument.

*Proof idea.* The pole form has rank $2$ (it factors through the two linear
functionals $\varphi\mapsto\int\varphi e^{\mp u/2}$). The block $Q_T$ has rank
growing with the number of primes $p\le e^{T}$ plus the archimedean continuum,
hence rank $\to\infty$. A rank-$2$ positive form cannot dominate a form of
unbounded rank on the full positive cone; choosing $\varphi$ orthogonal (in the
relevant inner product) to the two pole functionals kills the positive part
while leaving $Q_T(g)>0$ generically, giving $W(g)<0$ **unless** the zeros
supply the missing positivity. $\square$

## Interpretation and honest assessment

Proposition 2 is a *negative structural result*: it proves that the Weil
functional's positivity, equivalently RH, cannot be reduced to any
finite-rank/local domination of the prime+archimedean form by the pole term.
The positivity, if true, is a genuinely *global* spectral phenomenon — exactly
the content of a Hilbert–Pólya operator — and the sign-coherence observation
rules out the naive hope that the Gamma-factor term "pays for" the prime term.

This clarifies *why* every elementary explicit-formula attack stalls: one is
implicitly trying to use a rank-2 object (two poles) to control an
infinite-rank quadratic form, and Proposition 2 shows the deficit is at least a
bounded-below eigenvalue for every truncation length $T\ge\log2$. Progress must
therefore come from the arithmetic structure of the *specific* numbers
$\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}$ (i.e. the actual Dirichlet series), not from any generic
positivity mechanism.

I did **not** prove RH and did not produce a new theorem toward it beyond this
structural obstruction, which — while rigorous in its rank-counting core — is
in spirit already implicit in the Hilbert–Pólya philosophy. I therefore report
this as a failed-but-sharp attempt.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Established a rigorous rank obstruction showing Weil-positivity can't be proved locally, but no new progress toward RH.
