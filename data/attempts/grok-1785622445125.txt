### Attempt: Obstruction analysis for Euler-product/approximate-functional-equation approaches to RH

Let \(\zeta(s)\) denote the Riemann zeta function. For \(\operatorname{Re}(s)>1\) one has the absolutely convergent Euler product
\[
\zeta(s)=\prod_p(1-p^{-s})^{-1},
\]
which is visibly zero-free in that half-plane. The functional equation
\[
\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)=\pi^{-(1-s)/2}\Gamma((1-s)/2)\zeta(1-s)
\]
supplies the meromorphic continuation, and the completed function
\[
\xi(s)=\tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)
\]
is entire of order one. The non-trivial zeros of \(\zeta\) are precisely the zeros of \(\xi\), and RH asserts that they all lie on the critical line \(\operatorname{Re}(s)=\tfrac12\).

A natural family of attacks proceeds by replacing the Euler product by a truncated product (or Dirichlet polynomial) and controlling the error via an approximate functional equation. Write, for \(s=\sigma+it\) with \(0<\sigma<1\) and \(t>0\) large,
\[
\zeta(s)=\sum_{n\le x}n^{-s}+\chi(s)\sum_{n\le y}n^{s-1}+R(x,y;s),
\]
where \(\chi(s)=\pi^{s-1/2}\Gamma((1-s)/2)/\Gamma(s/2)\) and the remainder \(R\) admits the standard bound
\[
R(x,y;s)\ll x^{-\sigma}+|t|^{1/2-\sigma}y^{\sigma-1}
\]
(under the usual balancing \(xy\asymp|t|\)). The first sum is essentially a partial Euler product; the second is its functional-equation dual. One hopes to show that the two main terms cannot cancel when \(\sigma\neq\tfrac12\), or that any zero off the line would force an implausibly large remainder.

Suppose, for contradiction, that \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) is a zero with \(\beta>\tfrac12\) and \(\gamma\) large. Substituting into the approximate equation and using \(|\chi(\rho)|\asymp|\gamma|^{1/2-\beta}\) yields
\[
\Bigl|\sum_{n\le x}n^{-\rho}\Bigr|\asymp|\gamma|^{1/2-\beta}\Bigl|\sum_{n\le y}n^{\rho-1}\Bigr|+O\bigl(x^{-\beta}+|\gamma|^{1/2-\beta}y^{\beta-1}\bigr).
\]
Choosing \(x=y=|\gamma|^{1/2}\) makes the error \(O(|\gamma|^{-\beta/2}+|\gamma|^{\beta/2-1})\). The left-hand side is then a Dirichlet polynomial of length \(|\gamma|^{1/2}\). By the classical mean-value estimate
\[
\int_T^{2T}\Bigl|\sum_{n\le\sqrt{t}}n^{-\sigma-it}\Bigr|^2\,dt\ll T(\log T)^C
\]
(for any fixed \(\sigma\in(1/2,1)\)), such polynomials are typically of size \((\log|\gamma|)^{O(1)}\). The right-hand side, however, is of size \(|\gamma|^{1/2-\beta}\) times a dual polynomial of comparable length. For \(\beta-\tfrac12\gg(\log\log|\gamma|)/\log|\gamma|\) the factor \(|\gamma|^{1/2-\beta}\) is smaller than any negative power of \(\log|\gamma|\), forcing both Dirichlet polynomials to be abnormally small simultaneously. 

The obstruction is that the required simultaneous smallness lies beyond the reach of existing large-value or mean-value technology for Dirichlet polynomials. The best available zero-density estimates (e.g., Ingham–Huxley) only recover a zero-free region of the shape
\[
\beta\le 1-c(\log|\gamma|)^{-2/3}(\log\log|\gamma|)^{-1/3},
\]
which is far weaker than the line \(\beta=\tfrac12\). Any improvement that would push the density exponent all the way to the critical line re-encounters precisely the same hybrid large-value problem for a pair of dual Dirichlet polynomials linked by \(\chi(s)\). In other words, the Euler-product intuition, once made quantitative via the approximate functional equation, reduces RH to a statement about the joint distribution of two dual Dirichlet polynomials that is of comparable arithmetic depth to RH itself; no cancellation or positivity mechanism is known that separates the \(\beta=\tfrac12\) case from the off-line case at the necessary level of precision.

Thus the approach, while suggestive, contains a circular obstruction: the error terms and mean-value estimates that would be needed to rule out off-line zeros are essentially equivalent in strength to the Riemann hypothesis.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Euler-product/AFE method reduces to unsolved hybrid large-value problem for dual Dirichlet polynomials
