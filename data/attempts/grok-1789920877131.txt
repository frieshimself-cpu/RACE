### Attempt: Obstruction to a residue-theoretic attack on primitive Hodge classes of hypersurfaces

Let \(X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n+1}\) be a smooth hypersurface of degree \(d\geq n+2\) (so that the primitive cohomology is nontrivial). Write \(H^{n}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X,\mathbb{Q})\) for the primitive part with respect to the hyperplane class. A Hodge class \(\alpha\in\operatorname{Hdg}^{n}(X)\) that is primitive lies in
\[
F^{\lceil n/2\rceil}H^{n}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X,\mathbb{C})\cap H^{n}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X,\mathbb{Q}).
\]
Griffiths’ residue calculus realises the Hodge filtration on the primitive cohomology via
\[
F^{n-q}H^{n}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X)\simeq\frac{R^{qd-n-2}_{f}}{J(f)\cap R^{qd-n-2}},
\]
where \(f\) is a defining equation, \(R=\mathbb{C}[x_0,\dots,x_{n+1}]\), and \(J(f)\) is the Jacobian ideal. Thus \(\alpha\) corresponds to a homogeneous polynomial \(P_\alpha\) of degree \(qd-n-2\) that is annihilated by the Jacobian ideal in the appropriate graded piece and that is rational (after clearing denominators) with respect to a \(\mathbb{Q}\)-structure coming from a defining equation with rational coefficients.

Suppose one attempts to produce an algebraic cycle whose class is \(\alpha\) by exhibiting \(P_\alpha\) as a sum of residues of rational differential forms with poles along a union of hypersurfaces of lower degree that cut out a codimension-\(\lceil n/2\rceil\) subvariety. The only available algebraic operations that preserve the rational structure and the Hodge filtration are:
- multiplication by elements of the Jacobian ring (which stays inside the same filtration step),
- the Gauss–Manin connection (which lowers filtration by exactly 1 and produces periods, not cycles).

Neither operation yields a \(\mathbb{Q}\)-cycle class. More precisely, the residue map
\[
\operatorname{Res}:H^{n+1}(\mathbb{P}^{n+1}\setminus X)\to H^{n}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X)
\]
is surjective onto the primitive cohomology, yet its restriction to the subspace of classes of algebraic cycles supported on a divisor complementary to \(X\) lands only in the span of the hyperplane class (by the theorem of the fixed part / monodromy-invariant cycles). Consequently every nontrivial primitive Hodge class lies outside the image of algebraic residues.

The same obstruction persists after base change to any finitely generated field of definition and after passage to the absolute Hodge closure: the Galois action on the coefficients of \(P_\alpha\) need not preserve any algebraic cycle that could have produced those coefficients. Hence the residue calculus, while giving a complete description of the Hodge filtration, supplies no algebraic cycle representing a general primitive Hodge class.

This rules out any direct “explicit residue” proof for hypersurfaces of sufficiently high degree—the case to which many reduction arguments attempt to consign the Hodge conjecture.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Residue calculus describes filtration but cannot produce algebraic cycles for primitive classes
