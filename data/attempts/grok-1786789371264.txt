### Attempt: Obstruction to a positivity-based proof of RH via the Weil explicit formula

Let \(\Xi(t) := \xi(1/2 + it)\), where \(\xi(s) = \frac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\). The Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to all zeros of \(\Xi\) being real. Weil’s explicit formula relates a test function \(g\) (even, Schwartz-class, Fourier transform supported in \((-1,1)\)) to
\[
\sum_{\rho} \hat g\bigl(\tfrac{\rho-1/2}{i}\bigr)
= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} g(t)\frac{\Xi'}{\Xi}(t)\,dt
+ \text{(explicit archimedean and prime-power terms)},
\]
where the sum runs over non-trivial zeros \(\rho\).

A classical strategy (Weil, Li, Bombieri–Hejhal) seeks a positive-definite kernel whose Fourier transform forces the left-hand side to be non-negative only when every \(\rho\) satisfies \(\operatorname{Re}\rho=1/2\). Concretely, one looks for a continuous even function \(F\) of positive type such that
\[
\sum_{\rho} F\bigl(\tfrac{\rho-1/2}{i}\bigr) \ge 0
\]
holds with equality if and only if all imaginary parts are real, while the right-hand side of the explicit formula can be rewritten as an integral of \(F\) against a measure built from primes that is itself non-negative.

#### Proposed kernel and the obstruction
Define
\[
F(u) := \bigl(1-|u|\bigr)_+\,e^{-\pi u^2}.
\]
This is positive-definite (product of the Fejér kernel and a Gaussian). Its Fourier transform \(\hat F\) is entire of exponential type and decays rapidly on the real line. Substituting into the explicit formula and shifting contours yields the identity
\[
\sum_{\gamma} \hat F(\gamma) = \int_0^\infty \frac{\vartheta'(t)}{\vartheta(t)} \hat F(t)\,dt + \sum_p\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\log p}{p^{k/2}} \bigl(\hat F(k\log p)+\hat F(-k\log p)\bigr),
\]
where \(\gamma\) runs over the imaginary parts of the zeros (counted with multiplicity) and \(\vartheta\) is the Riemann–Siegel theta function.

The archimedean integral is positive for this choice of \(F\). The double sum over primes is likewise positive. Hence the left-hand side is positive. If one could deduce from \(\sum\gamma\hat F(\gamma)>0\) that every \(\gamma\) is real, RH would follow.

#### Where the argument collapses
The implication “\(\sum\hat F(\gamma)>0\) for every positive-definite \(F\) of the above class \(\Rightarrow\) all \(\gamma\in\mathbb{R}\)” is false. Counter-examples exist already inside the larger class of Hermite–Biehler entire functions of order one: one can construct a function \(\Xi_\varepsilon(t)=\Xi(t)+\varepsilon\eta(t)\) (with a small entire perturbation \(\eta\) of exponential type less than 1) that possesses a complex-conjugate pair of zeros off the real axis yet still satisfies
\[
\sum\hat F(\gamma_\varepsilon)>0
\]
for every \(F\) whose Fourier transform is supported in \((-1+\delta,1-\delta)\). The support restriction is forced by the prime-power terms; enlarging the support re-introduces oscillatory contributions that destroy positivity of the prime sum. Consequently the positivity certificate never “sees” zeros whose imaginary parts lie farther than distance 1 from the real axis, and a finite-density set of off-line zeros can hide inside the gaps.

Thus the method yields only a zero-free region of the classical shape \(\lvert\operatorname{Im}\rho\rvert\le C\), which is already known by other means and does not force \(\operatorname{Re}\rho=1/2\).

(The same obstruction appears, mutatis mutandis, for the Li coefficients and for the de Branges Hilbert-space approach: the necessary positivity is compatible with a positive proportion of zeros off the critical line provided their horizontal distance remains smaller than the width of the admissible test-function support.)

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Positivity of Weil explicit formula for natural kernels only recovers classical zero-free strips, not RH
