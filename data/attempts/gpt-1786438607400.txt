Attempt: Hodge classes of degree 4 and coniveau ≥1 are algebraic

Statement (Theorem).
Let X be a smooth projective complex variety. Let α ∈ H^4(X, Q) be a rational Hodge class of type (2,2). If α has coniveau ≥1 in the sense that α ∈ N^1 H^4(X, Q), i.e., α lies in the subgroup generated by Gysin images i_*: H^2(D, Q)(-1) → H^4(X, Q) from smooth divisors D ⊂ X, then α is algebraic (a Q-linear combination of classes of codimension-2 algebraic cycles on X).

Remarks on the hypothesis.
- I use the standard coniveau filtration N^1 defined via images of Gysin maps from divisors (equivalently: the first step of the Bloch–Ogus coniveau filtration). This is stronger than the naive “vanishes on the complement of some closed subset of codimension ≥1” but agrees with it after passing through the coniveau spectral sequence and resolution in degree 4 in the birational class; the argument below only needs the Gysin-generator definition.
- The conclusion gives a concrete partial case of the Hodge conjecture in codimension 2: every rational (2,2)-class with coniveau ≥1 is algebraic.

Proof.
- Setup of Hodge structures and Gysin maps.
  For a smooth divisor i: D ↪ X, the Gysin map i_*: H^2(D, Q)(-1) → H^4(X, Q) is a morphism of rational Hodge structures. The Hodge types shift by (+1,+1) across i_* because of the Tate twist (−1) on the source.

- Coniveau hypothesis expressed via divisors.
  By assumption α ∈ N^1 H^4(X, Q). Thus there exist smooth divisors D_1, …, D_m ⊂ X and classes β_j ∈ H^2(D_j, Q) such that
    α = ∑_{j=1}^m i_{j*}(β_j)  in H^4(X, Q),
  where i_j: D_j ↪ X are the inclusions (we have absorbed the Tate twist into the notation of Hodge type tracking below).

- Hodge-theoretic refinement (strictness).
  Each i_{j*} is a morphism of rational Hodge structures. Hence:
  • The (2,2)-component of i_{j*}(β_j) depends only on the (1,1)-component of β_j.
  • More precisely, i_{j*}(F^1 H^2(D_j, C)) ⊂ F^2 H^4(X, C), and by strictness of morphisms of Hodge structures, any element of type (2,2) in Im(i_{j*}) comes from an element of type (1,1) in the source.
  Applying this to the sum, since α is of type (2,2), there exist classes γ_j ∈ H^2(D_j, Q) ∩ H^{1,1}(D_j) such that
    α = ∑_{j=1}^m i_{j*}(γ_j)
  in H^4(X, Q). (This uses that the maps are defined over Q, so we can choose preimages in the Q-structure intersected with the Hodge (1,1)-part.)

- Algebraicity on divisors via Lefschetz (1,1).
  For each smooth projective divisor D_j, Lefschetz’s (1,1)-theorem implies
    H^2(D_j, Q) ∩ H^{1,1}(D_j) = NS(D_j)_Q,
  the Q-span of divisor classes on D_j. Thus each γ_j is a Q-linear combination of classes of algebraic divisors on D_j.

- Pushforward preserves algebraicity of cycle classes.
  For each algebraic divisor Z_j ⊂ D_j, its cohomology class [Z_j] ∈ H^2(D_j, Q) maps under i_{j*} to the cohomology class of the codimension-2 algebraic cycle i_{j*}(Z_j) ⊂ X. Therefore each i_{j*}(γ_j) is a Q-linear combination of classes of codimension-2 algebraic cycles on X, and so is their sum α.

This completes the proof.

Corollary (birational variant).
If μ: X' → X is a birational morphism of smooth projective varieties and α' = μ^*α ∈ H^4(X', Q) has coniveau ≥1 (in the divisor-generated sense) and is of type (2,2), then α is algebraic on X. Indeed, α' is algebraic by the theorem, hence pushforward μ_* of the corresponding algebraic codimension-2 cycles on X' yields an algebraic cycle whose class is α on X by functoriality.

Discussion of scope.
- This establishes the Hodge conjecture for all rational (2,2)-classes in H^4 of coniveau ≥1. It is sharp in that the argument fundamentally uses (i) functoriality and strictness of Hodge structures for Gysin maps from divisors, and (ii) the Lefschetz (1,1)-theorem on the divisors. The method is specific to codimension 2; in higher codimension p one would require Hodge-theoretic control on H^{2p-2} of the supporting subvarieties, where no analogue of Lefschetz (1,1) is available in general.

- The hypothesis α ∈ N^1 H^4(X, Q) is strictly weaker than “α is already known algebraic,” but it is still a meaningful geometric condition: it asserts that α is supported in codimension ≥1 at the level of coniveau, i.e., it is assembled via divisor-level data. This is the exact regime where codimension 2 can be attacked using only the (1,1) case on hypersurfaces.

- In many geometrically relevant situations (e.g., fourfolds with H^4 of level 1, or varieties where the coniveau spectral sequence degenerates suitably in low degrees), natural Hodge classes lie in N^1 and are thus covered by the theorem.
