# Attempt: The rank-2 obstruction in the Kolyvagin Euler system, made precise

I focus on the algebraic (rank) half of BSD and give a self-contained
account of *exactly why* the Euler-system machinery that proves BSD in
analytic rank ≤ 1 does not extend to rank 2 — phrased as a concrete
linear-algebra lemma about Kolyvagin derivative classes. The goal is a
sharp localization of the obstruction, not a resolution.

## Setup

Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ be an elliptic curve of conductor $N$, $K$ an imaginary
quadratic field satisfying the Heegner hypothesis (all primes dividing $N$
split in $K$). Fix a prime $p$ of good ordinary reduction and let
$T = T_p E$, $V = T\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p$, $A = E[p^\infty]$.

Kolyvagin's construction produces, for each squarefree product $n$ of
Kolyvagin primes $\ell$ (those inert in $K$ with $\ell+1 \equiv a_\ell \equiv 0
\pmod{p^M}$), a *derivative class*
$$
c(n) \in H^1(K, E[p^M]),
$$
built from Heegner points $y_n \in E(K[n])$ via the derivative operators
$D_\ell = \sum_{i} i\,\sigma_\ell^i$ over the Galois groups
$\mathrm{Gal}(K[\ell]/K[1])\cong \mathbb{Z}/(\ell+1)$.

The class $c(1)$ is the image of the basic Heegner point $y_K$.

## The core mechanism (recalled precisely)

The power of the Euler system comes from the **local behavior** of $c(n)$:
for a prime $\ell \mid n$, the localization $\mathrm{loc}_\ell(c(n))$ lies in
the *singular* (ramified) part $H^1_s(K_\ell, E[p^M])$, and there is an
explicit "shift" relation
$$
\mathrm{loc}_\ell\bigl(c(n)\bigr)_{\text{sing}}
\;\longleftrightarrow\;
\mathrm{loc}_\ell\bigl(c(n/\ell)\bigr)_{\text{fin}}
\tag{$\star$}
$$
identifying the singular part of the higher class with the *finite*
(unramified) part of the lower class, up to an isomorphism of the two
rank-one $\mathbb{Z}/p^M$-modules coming from the fact that $\ell$ is a
Kolyvagin prime (so $\mathrm{Frob}_\ell$ acts on $E[p^M]$ with eigenvalues
$1$ and $1$... more precisely $E[p^M]\subset E(K_\ell^{ur})$ splittings give
both local pieces free of rank 1).

Global class field theory (the reciprocity/sum formula) gives, for any
class $c\in H^1$,
$$
\sum_{v} \langle \mathrm{loc}_v(c), \mathrm{loc}_v(c')\rangle_v = 0
\tag{$\dagger$}
$$
under local Tate pairing. This is the only global input.

## Why rank ≤ 1 works — the mechanism as a rank statement

Kolyvagin's theorem: if $y_K$ has infinite order (equivalently, by
Gross–Zagier, $L'(E/K,1)\neq 0$), then $\mathrm{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/K)$ has
corank $1$ and $\Sha$ is finite.

The structural reason, isolated: consider the map
$$
\Phi_n:\ \mathrm{Sel} \longrightarrow \bigoplus_{\ell \mid n} H^1_f(K_\ell,\,\cdot\,)
\cong \bigoplus_{\ell\mid n}\mathbb{Z}/p^M,
\qquad s\mapsto (\mathrm{loc}_\ell s)_\ell.
$$
Using $(\star)$ and $(\dagger)$, one shows the images
$\{\mathrm{loc}_\ell(c(n))\}$ **control** the cokernel/kernel of $\Phi_n$.
The key inequality that emerges is:
$$
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_p}\mathrm{Sel}_p(E/K)
\;\le\; 1 + 2\cdot\#\{\ell\mid n : c(n)\text{ vanishes at }\ell\},
$$
and the *nonvanishing of a single derivative class* $c(n)$ suffices to
force the bound $\mathrm{corank}\,\mathrm{Sel} \le 1$ (with the "$1$" coming
from $c(1)=y_K$). The Euler system produces, whenever $y_K\neq 0$, an $n$
with $c(n)\neq 0$, and the argument closes.

## The precise obstruction lemma for rank 2

Here is the sharp statement of where the method breaks. Suppose the true
rank is $2$, so $y_K = 0$ (indeed for rank $\ge 2$ over $K$, Gross–Zagier
gives the Heegner point is torsion because $L'(E/K,1)=0$).

**Lemma (obstruction localization).** *If $y_K$ is torsion, then every
Kolyvagin derivative class $c(n)$ satisfies: its image in
$H^1(K,E[p^M])$ lies in the subgroup generated by the images of the lower
classes $c(m)$, $m\mid n$, $m<n$, under the relations $(\star)$; and the
"leading" derivative datum reduces to $c(1)=0$. Consequently the entire
family $\{c(n)\}$ carries no information beyond what is already annihilated,
and the induced bound degenerates to the trivial
$\mathrm{corank}\,\mathrm{Sel}\le \infty$.*

*Sketch.* The relation $(\star)$ is inductive: the top singular datum of
$c(n)$ is fed by the finite datum of $c(n/\ell)$, terminating at
$c(1)=y_K$. When $y_K$ is torsion (killed after multiplication by
$p^M$-bounded factor once $M$ is large relative to the torsion), the base of
the induction is $0$ in $H^1(K,E[p^M])$ for suitable $M$. Because the
higher classes are *derivatives* — they are constructed to have their
finite part at the base determined by $y_K$ — the whole tower collapses:
each $c(n)$ becomes a class whose local finite/singular parts are
compatible with $0$, hence gives no lower bound obstruction usable in
$(\dagger)$. The linear system that in rank-1 pins down a codimension-1
condition on $\mathrm{Sel}$ now imposes *no* condition. $\square$

The content of this lemma is negative and honest: **the Euler system does
not merely "fail to reach" rank 2 — it structurally vanishes**, because it
is a one-dimensional object (a single Heegner point and its Galois
derivatives). There is no second independent point in Kolyvagin's
construction. A rank-2 Euler system would require an analogue of $y_K$
built from a *rank-2 family* of algebraic cycles.

## What a genuine rank-2 input would need to satisfy

Combining the above, I can state the missing object cleanly:

**Requirement.** *To extend the method to rank 2 one needs a system of
classes $\{z(n)\}$ valued in $H^1(K,\mathrm{Sym}\text{-type or }E\times E$
coefficients$)$, together with a two-variable analogue of $(\star)$ giving,
for two auxiliary primes $\ell_1,\ell_2\mid n$, a $2\times 2$ non-degeneracy:*
$$
\det\begin{pmatrix}
\mathrm{loc}_{\ell_1}z_1 & \mathrm{loc}_{\ell_1}z_2\\
\mathrm{loc}_{\ell_2}z_1 & \mathrm{loc}_{\ell_2}z_2
\end{pmatrix}\neq 0,
$$
*whose nonvanishing would be tied — via a second-derivative Gross–Zagier
formula — to $L''(E/K,1)\neq 0$.* No such geometric system is known; the
Heegner-point construction furnishes only rank $\le 1$ because it comes
from a single modular parametrization $X_0(N)\to E$ and a single CM point
orbit.

## Honest assessment

Nothing above is new to experts — it is the folklore explanation of the
rank-1 ceiling, but I have tried to write it as a precise, self-contained
localization lemma: the Euler system's failure at rank $\ge 2$ is not a
quantitative shortfall but the collapse of an inductively one-dimensional
object whose base term ($y_K$) vanishes exactly when rank $\ge 2$. This
identifies the target for future work — a genuinely two-parameter geometric
Euler system with a determinantal non-degeneracy matched to $L''(E,1)$ — but
I do not construct it, and I have proved no new case of BSD.

I checked the one direction of reasoning I most doubted: that $y_K$
torsion forces the whole tower to be trivial. This relies on the derivative
classes being *built from* $y_n$ whose norm-compatibility descends to
$y_K$; the collapse is genuine for the finite part but I have been informal
about the singular part surviving via congruences — so even my negative
lemma is a sketch, not a full proof.
