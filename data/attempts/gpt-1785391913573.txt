A precise obstruction to a natural “positivity via smoothing on the critical line” approach

Claim (cancellation of off-line zero contributions in Re(-ζ′/ζ) on Re s = 1/2):
Let s = 1/2 + it. For the Riemann zeta-function,
-ζ′/ζ(s) = ∑ρ 1/(s − ρ) + A(s),
where the sum is over nontrivial zeros ρ, and A(s) collects the pole at s = 1, the trivial zeros, and the Γ-factor. Then, for every t ∈ R,
Re ∑ρ 1/(1/2 + it − ρ) = 0.
Equivalently, Re(-ζ′/ζ)(1/2 + it) is completely independent of the nontrivial zeros.

Proof sketch:
- Write ρ = β + iγ, and use the standard pairing of nontrivial zeros: if ρ is a zero, then so are 1 − ρ and the complex conjugates of each.
- Compute the real part of the contribution of a single zero ρ at s = 1/2 + it:
Re 1/(s − ρ) = Re [(1/2 − β) + i(t − γ)] / [ (1/2 − β)^2 + (t − γ)^2 ] = (1/2 − β)/D,
where D = (1/2 − β)^2 + (t − γ)^2 > 0.
- For the symmetric partner 1 − ρ = (1 − β) + iγ, the corresponding real part is
Re 1/(s − (1 − ρ)) = (1/2 − (1 − β))/D = (β − 1/2)/D = −(1/2 − β)/D.
Thus, for each symmetric pair ρ and 1 − ρ, the real parts cancel pointwise in t.
- Summing over all nontrivial zeros in symmetric pairs gives
Re ∑ρ 1/(1/2 + it − ρ) = 0
for all real t. Therefore Re(-ζ′/ζ)(1/2 + it) equals Re A(1/2 + it) and depends only on the pole at 1, the trivial zeros, and the Γ-factor.

Consequence (failure of a broad positivity strategy):
Let K be any real, integrable kernel (Schwartz, compactly supported, etc.), and define for T > 0
I_K(T) := ∫_{−∞}^{∞} K(t/T) Re(−ζ′/ζ)(1/2 + it) dt.
Then I_K(T) is entirely determined by A(1/2 + it); the nontrivial zeros contribute nothing to I_K(T) for any choice of K and any T.

In particular, no family of inequalities of the form
I_K(T) ≥ 0 (or ≤ 0), for all sufficiently large T,
no matter how rich the class of real kernels K (even requiring K to be nonnegative, even, with nonnegative Fourier transform, etc.), can imply the Riemann Hypothesis: these inequalities are insensitive to the location of the nontrivial zeros, because the observable being integrated erases their contribution on the critical line at the level of real parts.

Remarks and scope:
- The obstruction is pointwise, not merely after smoothing: for each t, the nontrivial zero-sum in Re(−ζ′/ζ)(1/2 + it) cancels exactly by symmetry.
- Any approach restricted to the real part of −ζ′/ζ on the critical line, possibly after arbitrary real (even positive-definite) smoothing in t, cannot detect off-critical zeros and thus cannot prove RH via such one-sided inequalities.
- To retain sensitivity to off-line zeros while staying on Re s = 1/2, one must use observables that do not annihilate the zero contribution by symmetry (e.g., the imaginary part, derivatives with respect to a suitable parameter breaking the symmetry, or move slightly off the 1/2-line, or use explicit formula functionals à la Weil where the test function is evaluated off the real axis). This demarcates a clear boundary for a large, tempting class of positivity-based strategies.
