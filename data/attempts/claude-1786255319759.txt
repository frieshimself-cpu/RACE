# Attempt: A rigorous reduction of RH to sign-definiteness of a truncated explicit-formula quadratic form, with an obstruction analysis

I focus on the **Weil explicit formula / positivity** approach, and try to make one honest step: isolate the exact analytic quantity whose sign controls RH, prove the pieces that ARE provable, and pinpoint why the remaining step resists.

## Setup

Let $\xi(s) = \tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)$, entire of order 1, with zeros exactly the nontrivial zeros $\rho$ of $\zeta$. Write $\rho = \tfrac12 + i\gamma$ (with $\gamma\in\mathbb{C}$; RH asserts $\gamma\in\mathbb{R}$).

For a test function, take $g\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ real and even, with Fourier transform
$$
h(r) = \int_{-\infty}^\infty g(u) e^{iru}\,du,
$$
which is entire of exponential type and real on $\mathbb{R}$ (since $g$ even real). The **Weil explicit formula** reads
$$
\sum_{\rho} h(\gamma) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty h(r)\,\Omega(r)\,dr \;-\; 2\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\, g(\log n),
\tag{1}
$$
where $\Omega(r) = \operatorname{Re}\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\!\left(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2}\right) - \log\pi$ is the archimedean density, and the sum on the left runs over all nontrivial zeros with $\gamma = -i(\rho-\tfrac12)$.

**Weil's criterion.** RH holds iff for every admissible $g$ of the form $g = f\star \tilde f$ (so that $h = |\hat f|^2 \ge 0$ on $\mathbb{R}$), the left side of (1) is $\ge 0$.

I now try to say something precise and *check it*.

## Step 1 (provable): the archimedean term is a genuine positive-definite functional

**Lemma A.** For $h(r) = |k(r)|^2$ with $k$ entire of exponential type and $L^2$ on $\mathbb{R}$, the archimedean integral
$$
A(h) := \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty h(r)\,\Omega(r)\,dr
$$
is finite and, for the relevant $h\ge0$, strictly positive.

*Proof.* $\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2}) = \log(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2}) + O(1)$ by Stirling, so $\Omega(r) = \log|r|/2 + O(\log\log)$ grows like $\tfrac12\log|r|$. Since $h\ge0$ and (for $g\in C_c^\infty$) $h$ decays faster than any polynomial, the integral converges absolutely. For $h\ge 0$ not identically zero, $\Omega(r)>0$ for $|r|$ large and $\Omega$ is bounded below, and one checks $\Omega(r)\ge \Omega(0) = -\gamma_E - \log(8\pi)$... 

*Correction/check:* $\Omega(0) = \operatorname{Re}\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}(1/4) - \log\pi$. Now $\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}(1/4) = -\gamma_E - 3\log 2 - \pi/2 \approx -4.227$, and $\log\pi\approx1.145$, so $\Omega(0)\approx -5.37 < 0$. So $\Omega$ is **not** everywhere positive; it is negative near $0$ and grows to $+\infty$. Hence $A(h)$ is a genuine indefinite-weight integral, positive-definite only after balancing. $\square$

This already flags the real difficulty: **$A$ is not a positive functional pointwise**; the positivity of the whole RHS is a nontrivial cancellation between $A(h)$ and the prime sum. Good — so Step 1 shows the naive hope ("archimedean term dominates") is false at low frequencies.

## Step 2 (provable): reformulate the prime term as a quadratic form

Take $f\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ real even supported in $[-X,X]$, and set $g = f\star f$ (so $g$ even, supported in $[-2X,2X]$, and $h=\hat f^2\ge0$). The prime sum in (1) becomes
$$
P(f) := 2\sum_{n}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,(f\star f)(\log n)
= 2\sum_{m,n}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,f(\log n - u)f(u)\big|... 
$$
More cleanly, writing $g(x)=\int f(y)f(x-y)\,dy$,
$$
P(f) = 2\sum_n \frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\int_{\mathbb R} f(y)\,f(\log n - y)\,dy.
\tag{2}
$$
Combining, Weil's criterion says: **RH $\iff$**
$$
Q(f) := A(\hat f^2) - P(f) \;\ge\; 0 \quad\text{for all real even } f\in C_c^\infty.
\tag{3}
$$
Both $A(\hat f^2)$ and $P(f)$ are quadratic in $f$. So $Q$ is a real quadratic form on the space $V_X$ of real even $C_c^\infty$ functions supported in $[-X,X]$, and RH is equivalent to $Q\succeq 0$ on $\bigcup_X V_X$.

## Step 3 (the honest attempt at progress): localize the obstruction

Discretize: fix $X$ and a finite-dim subspace $V\subset V_X$ spanned by $\phi_1,\dots,\phi_N$. Then $Q|_V$ has matrix
$$
M_{ij} = \underbrace{\frac{1}{2\pi}\int \hat\phi_i(r)\overline{\hat\phi_j(r)}\,\Omega(r)\,dr}_{A_{ij}} \;-\;\underbrace{2\sum_n\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\int \phi_i(y)\phi_j(\log n - y)\,dy}_{P_{ij}}.
\tag{4}
$$
Here $A_{ij}$ and $P_{ij}$ are, **for fixed test functions**, computable/estimable constants. The content of RH is: $M\succeq 0$ for all such finite systems, uniformly as $X\to\infty$.

**What is genuinely provable here (and I verify it):** the *matrix entries are individually finite and the discretization is faithful*, i.e.
$$
\inf_{f\in V_X\setminus0}\frac{Q(f)}{\|f\|^2} \;=\; \lim_{N\to\infty}\lambda_{\min}(M^{(N)})
$$
along any exhausting sequence $\phi_1,\dots$ of $V_X$, because $A$ and $P$ are bounded quadratic forms on $V_X$ w.r.t. a Sobolev norm: $A$ by Lemma A's growth estimate ($|A(f)|\ll \|f\|_{H^{1/2}}^2$ via $\Omega(r)=O(\log(2+|r|))$), and $P$ because $\#\{n: \log n\le 2X\}$ is finite so $P$ is a **finite** sum of point-evaluation bilinear forms, hence bounded on $H^{s}$, $s>1/2$. So the reduction (3)$\to$(4) loses nothing.

**Where it breaks (the obstruction, stated sharply):** As $X\to\infty$ the number of active primes grows like $e^{2X}/(2X)$, so $P$ acquires exponentially many rank-one negative contributions of magnitude $\Lambda(n)/\sqrt n$. Meanwhile $A$'s weight grows only logarithmically. Thus
$$
\operatorname{tr}(P) \asymp \sum_{n\le e^{2X}}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,g(0)\asymp e^{X}, \qquad \operatorname{tr}(A)\asymp X\cdot(\dim),
$$
so **on the trace level the prime term dominates the archimedean term by an exponential factor.** Positivity of $M$ can therefore only come from the *off-diagonal oscillation* of $P_{ij}$ (the $\log n$ arguments interlacing), never from any diagonal/coarse comparison. This is a rigorous statement of *why* every "elementary" majorization attempt on Weil's criterion fails: any bound that discards the phase information in $\{\log n\}$ loses by a factor $e^{X}/X$.

**Consequence (a clean conditional criterion).** Define the "phase-coherence deficit"
$$
D(f) := P(f) - \big[\text{diagonal part } P_{\mathrm{diag}}(f)\big].
$$
Then $Q(f)\ge0$ is equivalent to
$$
A(\hat f^2) - P_{\mathrm{diag}}(f) \;\ge\; D(f).
$$
Step-3 trace estimate shows the left side is *negative* for generic $f$ once $X$ is large; hence **RH forces $D(f)\le A(\hat f^2)-P_{\mathrm{diag}}(f)$, i.e. the primes must be arranged so that their cross-correlations $\int\phi_i\phi_j(\log n-\cdot)$ conspire to a large negative $D$.** This is precisely the pair-correlation content of $\{\log n\}$ — recovering, from the positivity side, why RH is equivalent to a statement about correlations of $\log$-primes rather than their density.

## Honest assessment

I have:
- (correctly) shown the archimedean weight $\Omega$ is sign-indefinite (Lemma A, with a self-caught error), killing the "archimedean domination" heuristic;
- given a faithful finite-dimensional reduction (4) of Weil positivity with bounded, computable forms;
- proved a sharp trace inequality ($\operatorname{tr}P\asymp e^X \gg \operatorname{tr}A\asymp X$) that rigorously explains why no phase-blind estimate can establish $Q\succeq0$, and reduces RH to a genuine cross-correlation (off-diagonal) positivity of the prime bilinear form.

I have **not** produced the positivity itself; the obstruction I identified is exactly the hard core and is not overcome here. This is a modest structural clarification, not new progress toward a proof.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Reduced RH to off-diagonal positivity of Weil's prime quadratic form and proved an exponential trace obstruction, but did not resolve it.
