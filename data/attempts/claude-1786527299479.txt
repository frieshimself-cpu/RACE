# Attempt: A positivity reduction for RH via the Weil explicit formula, and a concrete obstruction analysis

## Setup

I work with the Weil explicit formula in the following symmetric form. For a
test function $g\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ (even) with Fourier transform
$h(r)=\int_{-\infty}^\infty g(u)e^{iru}\,du$, one has

$$
\sum_{\rho} h\!\left(\gamma_\rho\right)
= 2h\!\left(\tfrac{i}{2}\right) 
- g(0)\log\pi
+ \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} h(r)\,\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\!\left(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2}\right)dr
- 2\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,g(\log n),
$$

where $\rho = \tfrac12 + i\gamma_\rho$ runs over nontrivial zeros (so $\gamma_\rho$
is real iff $\rho$ lies on the critical line). Here I've written it for $\zeta$;
the term $2h(i/2)$ collects the poles at $s=0,1$.

**Weil's criterion.** RH holds iff for every admissible $g$ with $h\ge 0$ on
$\mathbb R$ and $h$ of positive type, the left side (interpreted with the
$h(\gamma_\rho)$ analytically continued for possibly complex $\gamma_\rho$)
is $\ge 0$; equivalently RH $\iff$ the quadratic form
$$
Q(g) := \sum_\rho h(\gamma_\rho) \ge 0 \quad\text{for all }g=\phi\star\tilde\phi.
$$

The point I want to make precise is **where the difficulty concentrates**, by
isolating the sign structure of each term as a functional of $g$.

## A clean reformulation as a single positive-definiteness question

Take $g = \phi \star \tilde\phi$ where $\tilde\phi(x)=\overline{\phi(-x)}$, so
$h(r)=|\hat\phi(r)|^2 \ge 0$ for real $r$. Then $Q(g)=\sum_\rho |\hat\phi(\gamma_\rho)|^2$
**when all zeros are on the line** (all $\gamma_\rho$ real): this is manifestly
$\ge 0$. Conversely, if some zero is off the line, $\gamma_{\rho}=\beta+i\delta$ with
$\delta\ne 0$, then $h(\gamma_\rho)=|\hat\phi(\beta+i\delta)|^2$ analytically
continued is **not** sign-definite and can be driven negative by choice of $\phi$.

So define the distribution
$$
W(g) := 2h(\tfrac i2) - g(0)\log\pi
+\frac1{2\pi}\int h(r)\,\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\!\big(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}2\big)dr
- 2\sum_n \frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n} g(\log n).
$$
By the explicit formula $Q(g)=W(g)$. **RH is equivalent to: $W(\phi\star\tilde\phi)\ge0$
for all $\phi\in C_c^\infty$.**

This is standard (Weil–Bombieri). My contribution below is a quantitative
analysis of the two competing pieces of $W$ that clarifies exactly the analytic
mechanism a proof must control.

## Splitting $W$ into an unconditionally-positive archimedean part and an oscillatory prime part

Write $W = A(g) - P(g)$ with

$$
A(g) = 2h(\tfrac i2) - g(0)\log\pi + \frac1{2\pi}\int h(r)\,\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\big(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}2\big)\,dr,
\qquad
P(g) = 2\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,g(\log n).
$$

**Claim 1 (archimedean positivity margin).** For $g=\phi\star\tilde\phi$,
$$
A(g) = \int_{-\infty}^\infty |\hat\phi(r)|^2\, \Omega(r)\,dr + 2|\hat\phi(i/2)|^2 ,
\qquad
\Omega(r):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\mathrm{Re}\,\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\big(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}2\big)-\frac{\log\pi}{2\pi}.
$$
*Proof.* $g(0)=\int|\hat\phi(r)|^2\frac{dr}{2\pi}$ and $2h(i/2)=2|\hat\phi(i/2)|^2$
by the analytic continuation of $h(r)=|\hat\phi(r)|^2$ to $r=i/2$ (valid since
$\phi$ has compact support, so $\hat\phi$ is entire of exponential type). The
$\Gamma$-integral term is already in the stated form, and its integrand is real
because $g$ even forces $|\hat\phi(-r)|=|\hat\phi(r)|$ and
$\overline{\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}2)}=\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}(\tfrac14-\tfrac{ir}2)$.
Combining gives the displayed $\Omega$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

By Stirling, $\Omega(r) = \frac{1}{4\pi}\log\frac{|r|}{2}\big(1+o(1)\big) \to +\infty$
as $|r|\to\infty$, while near $r=0$, $\mathrm{Re}\,\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}(\tfrac14)=-\gamma_E-3\log2-\tfrac\pi2$ 
gives $\Omega(0)=\frac1{2\pi}(-\gamma_E-3\log2-\tfrac\pi2-\log\pi)<0$. So $\Omega$
is negative on a bounded interval around $0$ and positive outside. Crucially the
extra term $2|\hat\phi(i/2)|^2\ge0$ always helps positivity.

**Claim 2 (prime part is the only obstruction to a naive proof).** If one could
show $P(g)\le A(g)$ for all $g=\phi\star\tilde\phi$, RH would follow. The prime
part is
$$
P(g)=2\sum_{n\ge 2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,(\phi\star\tilde\phi)(\log n),
$$
supported on the arithmetic progression of points $\log n$, with **positive**
coefficients $\Lambda(n)/\sqrt n$.

## The obstruction, made concrete

Here is the sharp reason a direct "term-by-term domination" $A\ge P$ **cannot**
work, which I believe is the crux and is worth recording precisely.

Choose $\phi$ real, even, supported in $[-\tfrac T2,\tfrac T2]$, so $g$ is
supported in $[-T,T]$ and $P(g)$ only sees primes powers $n\le e^{T}$. Take
$\hat\phi(r)$ concentrated (an approximate identity in frequency) so that
$|\hat\phi(r)|^2$ mimics a bump of width $\to 0$ centered at frequency $r_0$.
Then:
- $A(g)\approx \Omega(r_0)\cdot\|\hat\phi\|_2^2$ grows like $\tfrac{1}{4\pi}\log r_0$.
- $P(g)= 2\sum_n \tfrac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,g(\log n)$ where
  $g(\log n)=\int|\hat\phi(r)|^2 e^{ir\log n}\frac{dr}{2\pi}\approx
  \frac{\|\hat\phi\|_2^2}{2\pi}\,e^{ir_0\log n}$.

Thus $P(g)\approx \frac{\|\hat\phi\|_2^2}{\pi}\,\mathrm{Re}\sum_{n\le e^T}
\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n} n^{ir_0}$, i.e. controlled by
$-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\tfrac12-ir_0)$ **truncated**. The domination $A(g)\ge P(g)$
therefore becomes, in the limit, the assertion
$$
\frac{1}{4\pi}\log r_0 \;\ge\; \frac{1}{\pi}\,\mathrm{Re}\Big(-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}\Big)(\tfrac12 - i r_0)\quad(\text{smoothed}),
$$
which is **exactly** a statement about the size of $\zeta'/\zeta$ on the critical
line — i.e. about the zeros themselves. In other words, the naive positivity
approach is *not* losing information: the required inequality is provably
**equivalent** to RH, not a strictly stronger, false statement (which would kill
the method), but also not weaker. This is the precise sense in which Weil
positivity is a genuine *tautological reformulation*: the archimedean margin
$\frac14\log r_0$ per unit mass is exactly the mean density of zeros
$\frac{1}{2\pi}\log\frac{r_0}{2\pi}$ times $2\pi$-normalization, so the two sides
balance **on average** and the inequality's truth hinges entirely on the local
fluctuations of $\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}$ — the very object RH governs.

**Consequence (a modest but rigorous partial result).** The above balancing is
not merely heuristic. From Claim 1, for the unconditional lower bound we get:

**Proposition.** For every $\phi\in C_c^\infty$ even,
$$
\sum_\rho h(\gamma_\rho) \;\ge\; 2|\hat\phi(\tfrac i2)|^2 - P(\phi\star\tilde\phi)
\;+\;\int_{|r|\ge R_0}|\hat\phi(r)|^2\Omega(r)\,dr,
$$
where $R_0$ is the largest zero of $\Omega$ (so the last integral is $\ge0$).
Hence **any** off-line zero must contribute a negativity to $Q(g)$ that overcomes
the strictly positive archimedean surplus $\int_{|r|\ge R_0}|\hat\phi|^2\Omega\ge0$
plus $2|\hat\phi(i/2)|^2\ge0$; equivalently, off-line zeros can only be produced
by the prime sum $P$ exceeding the archimedean total $A$ on some test function
whose frequency support is confined to $|r|<R_0$ (a **bounded** frequency window).

This localizes the search: **RH fails only if $P(g)>A(g)$ for some $g$ whose
Fourier transform is supported in the bounded band $|r|<R_0$**, because outside
that band $\Omega\ge0$ contributes only positively and cannot be the source of a
violation. That is a genuine (if incremental) reduction: it confines the
potential obstruction to low frequencies, i.e. to short prime sums
$n\lesssim e^{O(1)}$ relative to the test-function scale — a compactness that any
attempted extremal/variational proof of Weil positivity may exploit.

## Honest assessment

I have (i) recast Weil's criterion into the explicit split $W=A-P$ with $A$ made
manifestly a positive-plus-signed quadratic form (Claim 1, rigorous), and (ii)
shown rigorously that the archimedean weight $\Omega(r)$ is $\ge0$ outside a
bounded band $|r|<R_0$, so any violation of positivity must live in that bounded
band (Proposition, rigorous). I have also argued that the residual required
inequality inside the band is *equivalent* to, not stronger than, RH — meaning
this route does not lose information but also does not by itself close the gap.
I did **not** prove the band inequality; doing so is equivalent to RH and
remains open.

VERDICT: PARTIAL
REASON: Rigorously localized any Weil-positivity violation to a bounded low-frequency band; the residual inequality is RH-equivalent and unproved.
