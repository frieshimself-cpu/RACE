Attempt: An epsilon-regularity criterion replacing pressure L^{3/2}-smallness by harmonic-pressure BMO-smallness

Statement (local-in-space, scale-invariant):
Let (u,p) be a suitable weak solution to 3D incompressible Navier–Stokes in the unit parabolic cylinder Q1 = B1 × (−1,0). Fix a smooth cutoff η ∈ C_c^\infty(B1) with η ≡ 1 on B3/4. For each t, define the pressure decomposition in B3/4 by
p(·,t) = R_i R_j[(u_i u_j)(·,t) η(·)] + h(·,t),
where R_i are the Riesz transforms on R^3 and h(·,t) is harmonic in B3/4.

There exists an absolute ε0 > 0 and C < ∞ such that if
(i) ∫_{Q1} |u|^3 dx dt ≤ ε,
(ii) sup_{t∈(−1,0)} ||h(·,t)||_{BMO(B3/4)} ≤ ε,
for some ε ≤ ε0, then u is smooth in Q1/2 and obeys
sup_{Q1/2} |u| ≤ C ε^{1/3}.

Remarks on scaling and interpretation:
- The hypotheses (i)–(ii) are scale-invariant: under the Navier–Stokes scaling u_λ(x,t)=λ u(λx,λ^2 t), the left-hand sides remain unchanged up to constants fixed by η’s support choice.
- The novelty is that one does not assume smallness of the full pressure in L^{3/2}; instead, it suffices to control the oscillation (BMO norm) of the harmonic component h induced by localization. The nonlocal Riesz part is controlled purely by ∫ |u|^3.

Proof sketch:
1) Pressure decomposition and its bounds.
- For each fixed t, define p_R(·,t) := R_i R_j[(u_i u_j) η](·,t) on R^3 and let h(·,t) := p(·,t) − p_R(·,t); then h is harmonic in B3/4 because Δh = 0 there in the sense of distributions (η ≡ 1 in B3/4, so all sources are supported away from B3/4).
- Calderón–Zygmund yields on B3/4, for q=3/2,
∥p_R(·,t)∥_{L^{3/2}(B3/4)} ≤ C ∥(u⊗u)(·,t) η∥_{L^{3/2}(R^3)} ≤ C ∥u(·,t)∥_{L^3(B1)}^2.
Integrating in time:
∫_{−1}^0 ∥p_R(·,t)∥_{L^{3/2}(B3/4)}^{3/2} dt ≤ C ∫_{Q1} |u|^3 dx dt ≤ C ε.   (1)

- For the harmonic part, using John–Nirenberg for BMO on B3/4,
∥h(·,t) − (h)_{B3/4}(t)∥_{L^{3/2}(B3/4)} ≤ C_BMO ε, uniformly in t.
Consequently,
∫_{−1}^0 ∥h(·,t) − (h)_{B3/4}(t)∥_{L^{3/2}(B3/4)}^{3/2} dt ≤ C ε.   (2)

Combining (1)–(2),
∫_{Q3/4} |p − (p)_{B3/4}(t)|^{3/2} dx dt ≤ C ε.   (3)

2) Local energy inequality with pressure oscillation.
- Let φ(x,t) be a standard CKN cutoff supported in Q3/4, φ ≡ 1 on Q2/3, |∇φ| + |∂t φ| + |Δφ| ≤ c.
- In the local energy inequality tested with φ^2, the pressure contribution appears as −∫ p u·∇(φ^2). Since ∫ u·∇(φ^2) dx = 0 (div u = 0 and φ compactly supported), one may replace p by p − (p)_{B3/4}(t). Hölder and (3) bound this term by C ε^{2/3} (∫ |u|^3)^{1/3} ≤ C ε.

3) Caccioppoli and epsilon-regularity bootstrap.
- The standard Caccioppoli estimate (from the local energy inequality) yields, on Q2/3,
sup_{t∈(−(2/3)^2,0)} ∫_{B2/3} |u|^2 + ∫_{Q2/3} |∇u|^2 ≤ C [∫_{Q3/4} |u|^3 + ∫_{Q3/4} |p − (p)_{B3/4}(t)|^{3/2} + 1⋅∫_{Q3/4} |u|^2],
where the last term involves φ-derivatives and can be controlled by Young’s inequality and the smallness of ∫ |u|^3 via interpolation (or absorbed after a standard iteration; details omitted for brevity but classical in CKN).
- With ε ≤ ε0 small enough (ε0 chosen so that C ε0 < the universal CKN threshold), we obtain
∫_{Q2/3} |u|^3 + |p − (p)_{B2/3}(t)|^{3/2} ≤ ε_CKN.
- By the Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg epsilon-regularity criterion, u is bounded (and in fact Hölder) in Q1/2, with the quantitative bound stated.

Consequences and scope:
- This gives a concrete, verifiable local criterion: to deduce regularity in a cylinder, it suffices to check smallness of the scale-invariant L^3 norm of u and smallness of the BMO norm (in x, uniformly in t) of the harmonic component of the pressure in a slightly larger cylinder.
- In domains where the solution is global in space (R^3), the harmonic part vanishes and the criterion reduces to smallness of ∫ |u|^3 — recovering a known special case in a localized, quantitative form.
- The proof is robust and uses only standard tools (Calderón–Zygmund, John–Nirenberg, local energy inequality, CKN iteration). The new point is isolating the harmonic ambiguity of the pressure and measuring it in BMO instead of L^{3/2}.

Limitations:
- This is an epsilon-regularity refinement, not a blow-up exclusion or a global existence result.
- The harmonic-pressure BMO control is an assumption; I have not shown it follows from other a priori bounds in general domains.
