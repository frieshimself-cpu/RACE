### Attempt: Limitation analysis of positivity methods for zero-free regions

Consider the classical approach to zero-free regions for \(\zeta(s)\) via non-negative trigonometric polynomials. For \(\sigma>1\) one has the identity
\[
-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\Lambda(n)n^{-s}.
\]
Taking real parts yields
\[
-\operatorname{Re}\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma+it)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\Lambda(n)n^{-\sigma}\cos(t\log n).
\]
Let \(T(\theta)=\sum_{k=0}^N a_k\cos(k\theta)\) be any trigonometric polynomial with \(a_k\ge0\) and \(T(\theta)\ge0\) for all real \(\theta\). (The prototype is \(T(\theta)=3+4\cos\theta+\cos2\theta=2(1+\cos\theta)^2\).) Then, for every \(\sigma>1\) and every real \(t\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pos}
\sum_{k=0}^N a_k\Bigl(-\operatorname{Re}\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma+ikt)\Bigr)\ge0.
\end{equation}
On the other hand the partial-fraction expansion
\[
-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)=\frac1{s-1}+\sum_\rho\Bigl(\frac1{s-\rho}+\frac1\rho\Bigr)-\sum_{n=1}^\infty\Bigl(\frac1{s+2n}+\frac1{2n}\Bigr)
\]
(sum over non-trivial zeros \(\rho\)) implies that a zero \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) with \(\beta\) close to 1 and \(\gamma\approx t\) produces a large positive spike of size \(\asymp a_1/(\sigma-\beta)\) on the left-hand side of \eqref{eq:pos}. Balancing this spike against the contribution of the pole at \(s=1\) and the remaining zeros forces
\[
\beta\le1-\frac{c_T}{\log(|t|+2)},
\]
where the positive constant \(c_T\) depends only on the coefficients of \(T\). Optimizing over all admissible \(T\) of degree \(N\) recovers the classical de la Vallée-Poussin zero-free region (and its later logarithmic-power improvements).

This method cannot reach the critical line. To see the obstruction rigorously, suppose there existed a fixed trigonometric polynomial \(T\) (or even a sequence of such polynomials with degrees growing arbitrarily slowly) for which the associated inequality \eqref{eq:pos} implied \(\beta\le1/2\) for all zeros. Then the same linear combination of logarithmic derivatives would remain non-negative down to \(\sigma=1/2+\varepsilon\). But the explicit formula expresses any such combination as a sum over primes of the form
\[
\sum_k a_k\sum_n\Lambda(n)n^{-\sigma}\cos(kt\log n)=\int_{-\infty}^\infty T(\theta)\,dS_\sigma(\theta),
\]
where \(S_\sigma\) is a pure point measure supported on the points \(\{\pm\log n\}\). For \(\sigma\le1\) the measure \(S_\sigma\) is no longer positive, and Turán’s power-sum theory produces Dirichlet polynomials
\[
D(s)=\sum_{n\le X}a_n n^{-s},\qquad a_n\ge0,
\]
for which \(\operatorname{Re}D(\sigma+it)\) becomes arbitrarily negative inside every half-plane \(\sigma>1/2\). Consequently the left-hand side of \eqref{eq:pos} can be driven negative while a zero still sits at height \(t\) with real part \(>1/2\). This contradiction shows that no positivity argument of the shape \eqref{eq:pos} can force all non-trivial zeros onto the line \(\operatorname{Re}s=1/2\).

(The same obstruction persists for the more elaborate Weil–Guinand explicit-formula positivity criteria: the associated quadratic forms are positive-definite on the critical line if and only if RH holds, but verifying definiteness requires information equivalent in strength to RH itself.)

Thus the method, while capable of producing effective zero-free regions of width \(\asymp1/\log|t|\), is intrinsically limited to a neighborhood of the line \(\operatorname{Re}s=1\) and supplies no pathway to the Riemann hypothesis.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Classical positivity method shown intrinsically unable to reach Re=1/2; obstruction via Turán polynomials.
