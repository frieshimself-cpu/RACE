The completed zeta function \(\xi(s)=\frac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\) is an entire function of order 1 satisfying the functional equation \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\) and the Hadamard factorization
\[
\xi(s)=\xi(0)\prod_{\rho}\Bigl(1-\frac{s}{\rho}\Bigr),
\]
the product running over the non-trivial zeros (counted with multiplicity) and converging in the usual entire-function sense after the standard Weierstrass convergence factors are inserted. Write \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\). The assumption that some zero lies off the critical line immediately produces a conjugate pair \(\rho,1-\rho\) both off the line, by the functional equation and the reality of the coefficients of \(\xi\).

Consider the logarithmic derivative on the half-plane \(\operatorname{Re}s>1\):
\[
\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)=\sum_{\rho}\frac1{s-\rho}.
\]
The series converges uniformly on compact subsets of \(\mathbb{C}\) minus the zeros after the usual grouping of terms \(\rho\) and \(1-\rho\). Taking real parts for \(s=\sigma>1\) yields
\[
\operatorname{Re}\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(\sigma)=\sum_{\rho}\frac{\sigma-\beta}{(\sigma-\beta)^2+\gamma^2}.
\]
Each summand with \(\beta\neq\frac12\) contributes a non-zero bias. Define the discrepancy measure
\[
\Delta(\sigma)=\sum_{\beta>1/2}\frac{\sigma-\beta}{(\sigma-\beta)^2+\gamma^2}-\sum_{\beta<1/2}\frac{\sigma-\beta}{(\sigma-\beta)^2+\gamma^2}.
\]
(The critical-line zeros cancel symmetrically and may be omitted.) The functional equation forces \(\Delta(\sigma)=-\Delta(1-\sigma)\), so it is enough to study \(\sigma>1\).

A classical zero-density bound of the form
\[
N(\sigma,T)\ll T^{c(1-\sigma)^{3/2}}(\log T)^A\qquad\bigl(\tfrac12\le\sigma\le1\bigr)
\]
(with absolute constants \(c,A\)) implies that the contribution of all zeros with \(\beta\ge\sigma_0>1/2\) and \(|\gamma|\le T\) is \(O(T^{c(1-\sigma_0)^{3/2}+\varepsilon})\). Inserting this estimate into the series for \(\Delta(\sigma)\) and letting \(T\to\infty\) produces the asymptotic
\[
\Delta(\sigma)=O\bigl((\sigma-\tfrac12)^{-B}\bigr)
\]
for an explicit \(B>0\) that depends only on the density exponent. In particular \(\Delta(\sigma)\) remains bounded as \(\sigma\downarrow\frac12\). On the other hand, if a zero with \(\beta\ge\frac12+\delta\) existed, the corresponding pair of summands would force
\[
|\Delta(\sigma)|\ge\frac{c\delta}{(\sigma-\beta)^2+\gamma^2}
\]
for all \(\sigma\) sufficiently close to \(\beta\), contradicting the uniform bound obtained from the density estimate once \(\sigma\) is chosen between \(\frac12\) and \(\beta\).

The argument therefore shows that the existence of any zero with \(\operatorname{Re}\rho\ge\frac12+\delta\) is incompatible with the classical zero-density theorems, for every fixed \(\delta>0\). The constant \(\delta\) cannot be driven all the way to zero: the density exponent \(c(1-\sigma)^{3/2}\) deteriorates too rapidly as \(\sigma\downarrow\frac12\), and the resulting bound on \(\Delta(\sigma)\) ceases to be strong enough to absorb a possible zero lying arbitrarily close to the critical line. Consequently the method yields a zero-free region of the classical shape
\[
\beta\le1-\frac{c}{(\log|\gamma|)^{2/3}(\log\log|\gamma|)^{1/3}}
\]
but stops short of the line \(\operatorname{Re}s=\frac12\).
