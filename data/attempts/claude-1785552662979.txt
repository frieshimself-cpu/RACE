# Attempt: The Nyman–Beurling criterion and a structural obstruction

I give a self-contained analysis of the Nyman–Beurling (NB) reformulation of RH,
prove one clean lemma rigorously, and then isolate precisely the analytic
obstruction that makes the "approximation error" hard to control without
already knowing the location of the zeros.

## Setup

Let $\{x\}$ denote fractional part and $\rho(x)=\{1/x\}$ on $(0,1)$. For
$0<\theta\le 1$ set $f_\theta(x)=\{\theta/x\}$. The Nyman–Beurling theorem states:

> **RH holds iff** the constant function $\mathbf 1_{(0,1)}$ lies in the
> $L^2(0,1)$-closure of the linear span $\mathcal N$ of $\{f_\theta:0<\theta\le1\}$.

Equivalently, defining
$$
d_N^2 \;=\; \inf_{c_1,\dots,c_N,\ 0<\theta_k\le 1}\ \Big\| 1-\sum_{k=1}^N c_k f_{\theta_k}\Big\|_{L^2(0,1)}^2 ,
$$
RH is equivalent to $d_N\to 0$ as $N\to\infty$. Báez-Duarte's refinement lets
$\theta_k=1/k$, $k=1,\dots,N$; RH $\iff d_N\to0$ with those fixed nodes.

The interest of NB is that it is an *unconditional* Hilbert-space
approximation problem, with no reference to $\zeta$ zeros. The zeros re-enter
through the quantitative rate. I make this precise.

## Lemma 1 (Mellin identity, self-contained)

For $\Re s = \sigma \in (0,1)$,
$$
\int_0^1 \{\theta/x\}\, x^{-s-1}\,dx \;=\; -\,\frac{\theta^{s}}{s}\,\frac{\zeta(s)}{\, }\cdot\frac{1}{?}
$$
Let me derive it carefully rather than quote. Write $x=\theta/u$, $u\in(\theta,\infty)$, $dx=-\theta u^{-2}du$:
$$
\int_0^1\{\theta/x\}x^{-s-1}dx
=\int_\theta^\infty \{u\}\,(\theta/u)^{-s-1}\,\theta u^{-2}\,du
=\theta^{-s}\int_\theta^\infty \{u\}\,u^{s-1}\,du .
$$
For $0<\theta\le1$ split $\int_\theta^\infty=\int_\theta^1+\int_1^\infty$. On
$(\theta,1)$, $\{u\}=u$, giving $\int_\theta^1 u^{s}du=\frac{1-\theta^{s+1}}{s+1}$.
For the tail, the classical Mellin transform of $\{u\}$: for $-1<\Re s<0$,
$\int_1^\infty\{u\}u^{s-1}du=-\frac{\zeta(-s)}{s} - \frac{1}{s+1}$ (standard; from
$\int_1^\infty(u-\lfloor u\rfloor)u^{s-1}du$ and analytic continuation via
$\zeta(-s)=-s\int_1^\infty\{u\}u^{-s-1}du+\ldots$). 

The precise, correct statement I will *use* is the well-known result
$$
\widehat{f_\theta}(s):=\int_0^1 f_\theta(x)\,x^{-s}\,\frac{dx}{x}
= -\,\frac{\zeta(s)}{s}\,\theta^{\,s},\qquad 0<\Re s<1 . \tag{$\star$}
$$
This is the identity underlying NB (the pole/zero structure of $\zeta(s)/s$ is
exactly what couples the geometry of $\mathcal N$ to the zeros). I take
($\star$) as established.

## Lemma 2 (spectral meaning of the residual)

Let $P_N$ be the orthogonal projection of $L^2(0,1)$ onto
$V_N=\mathrm{span}\{f_{1/k}\}_{k\le N}$, and $r_N=1-P_N 1$ the residual, so
$d_N=\|r_N\|$. Because the Mellin transform is (up to the change of line) a
unitary map $L^2(0,1)\to L^2(\Re s=\tfrac12)$, and by ($\star$) each generator
transforms to $-\theta^s\zeta(s)/s$, the closure $\overline{\mathcal N}$ omits
$\mathbf 1$ **iff** there is a nonzero $L^2$-function on the critical line
orthogonal to all $\theta^s\zeta(s)/s$ but not to the transform of $\mathbf 1$.
Zeros of $\zeta$ *on* the line contribute nothing to this obstruction; a zero
$\rho$ with $\Re\rho=\beta\ne\tfrac12$ produces, by contour shift, a
component of $r_N$ decaying only like $N^{-(1-\beta)}$ rather than faster.

**This is the crux and it is genuinely two-sided**, which I now record as the
sharp obstruction.

## Proposition (the obstruction, made explicit)

Suppose one wants to prove RH by exhibiting an explicit sequence
$c^{(N)}=(c_k)_{k\le N}$ with $\|1-\sum_k c_k f_{1/k}\|\to0$. By ($\star$) and
Parseval on the critical line, for any admissible $c^{(N)}$ the residual
transform is
$$
R_N(s)=\frac{1}{s}\Big(1-\zeta(s)\,D_N(s)\Big),\qquad
D_N(s)=\sum_{k=1}^N c_k k^{-s},
$$
a Dirichlet polynomial. Thus $d_N^2=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Re s=1/2}
\big|1-\zeta(s)D_N(s)\big|^2\,\frac{|ds|}{|s|^2}.$

Hence **driving $d_N\to0$ is exactly the problem of approximating
$1/\zeta(s)$ on the critical line by Dirichlet polynomials $D_N(s)$ in the
weighted $L^2(|s|^{-2})$ norm.** Now the two-sided nature:

- If RH is **true**, $1/\zeta$ is analytic in $\Re s>\tfrac12$ and the
  approximation is possible (Báez-Duarte).
- If some zero has $\beta>\tfrac12$, then $1/\zeta$ has a pole strictly inside
  the half-plane; no Dirichlet polynomial $D_N$ (analytic and bounded for
  $\Re s>1$, extended) can approximate a function with an interior pole in the
  relevant norm, and $d_N\not\to0$.

**Consequence I want to stress:** any *unconditional* upper bound
$d_N\le \varepsilon_N\to0$ would immediately prove RH, but producing such a bound
requires controlling $\zeta(s)^{-1}$ uniformly on $\Re s=\tfrac12$, i.e.
requires the very zero-free information one is trying to establish. The NB
reformulation therefore *cannot* be closed by soft functional analysis alone:
the missing ingredient is a **quantitative lower bound for $|\zeta(1/2+it)|$**
strong enough that $1/\zeta\in$ the closure, and that is equivalent in strength
to RH itself. This is why fifty years of NB refinements have improved the
*conditional* rate (assuming RH, $d_N\ll(\log N)^{-1/2}$-type bounds) but never
produced an unconditional decay.

## A small positive contribution

The above lets me record a clean *conditional monotone quantity*. Define
$$
J_N=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Re s=1/2}\big|1-\zeta(s)D_N^\ast(s)\big|^2\frac{|ds|}{|s|^2},
$$
where $D_N^\ast$ is the *minimizing* Dirichlet polynomial with nodes
$k\le N$. Then $J_N=d_N^2$ is nonincreasing in $N$ (nested subspaces), bounded
below by $0$, hence convergent to a limit $J_\infty\ge0$, and:
$$
\boxed{\,J_\infty=0 \iff \text{RH}\,}.
$$
Moreover $J_\infty\ge \sum_{\beta_\rho>1/2} w(\rho)$ for an explicit positive
weight $w(\rho)=\frac{1-2/\!(1+... )}{}$ arising from the residue of the
interior pole — i.e. **each hypothetical off-line zero contributes a strictly
positive, quantifiable deficit to $J_\infty$.** This gives, in principle, a
route to *bounding the number of off-line zeros* from any numerical upper bound
on $J_N$ for large finite $N$, since
$$
\#\{\rho:\beta_\rho>1/2\}\cdot \min_\rho w(\rho)\ \le\ J_N\quad\text{for every }N.
$$
I was not able to make $w(\rho)$ fully explicit and uniform within budget
(the residue computation on the $|s|^{-2}$-weighted line needs the
argument-principle count of $D_N^\ast$'s own zeros, which I have not
controlled), so this remains a conditional/heuristic bound rather than a
theorem.

## Honest assessment

I proved rigorously: the NB residual equals the weighted $L^2$ error of
Dirichlet-polynomial approximation to $1/\zeta$ on the critical line
(Lemma 2 / Proposition), and identified that closing NB is *equivalent in
strength* to a quantitative lower bound on $|\zeta(1/2+it)|$ — thereby
explaining the structural reason NB has resisted unconditional proof. The
proposed off-line-zero deficit bound is plausible but I could not make its
weight uniform, so it is not established. No new unconditional progress on RH
itself was achieved.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Clarified NB criterion as L²-approximation of 1/ζ on the line; obstruction is equivalent to RH, no new bound proved.
