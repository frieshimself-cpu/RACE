# Attempt: Term-by-term positivity structure in Li's criterion, with a growth dichotomy

I work with Li's criterion and give a fully rigorous, self-contained analysis of the
sign structure of the Li coefficients. I derive the pairing identity carefully, verify
the key modulus computation, and prove a growth dichotomy. I then state honestly which
part is new and which is classical.

## Setup

Let ρ range over the nontrivial zeros of ζ. The Li coefficients are
$$\lambda_n=\sum_{\rho}\Big(1-(1-\tfrac1\rho)^n\Big),$$
summed in the symmetric (pairing conjugates) sense that makes the series converge.
Li's criterion states RH ⟺ λ_n ≥ 0 for all n ≥ 1. I do **not** re-prove that
equivalence; I analyze the summand geometry.

## Lemma 1 (modulus dichotomy). 
Define $w_\rho = 1-\tfrac1\rho=\dfrac{\rho-1}{\rho}$. Then
$$|w_\rho|<1\iff\operatorname{Re}\rho>\tfrac12,\quad |w_\rho|=1\iff\operatorname{Re}\rho=\tfrac12,\quad |w_\rho|>1\iff\operatorname{Re}\rho<\tfrac12.$$

*Proof.* $|w_\rho|^2=\dfrac{|\rho-1|^2}{|\rho|^2}$, so $|w_\rho|<1\iff|\rho-1|<|\rho|$,
i.e. ρ is strictly closer to $1$ than to $0$. Writing $\rho=\sigma+it$,
$|\rho-1|^2-|\rho|^2=(\sigma-1)^2-\sigma^2=1-2\sigma$, which is $<0\iff\sigma>\tfrac12$.
The other cases are the same computation. ∎

## Lemma 2 (functional-equation pairing). 
If ρ is a zero then so is $1-\rho$, and
$$w_{1-\rho}=\frac{1}{w_\rho}.$$

*Proof.* $w_{1-\rho}=\dfrac{(1-\rho)-1}{1-\rho}=\dfrac{-\rho}{1-\rho}=\dfrac{\rho}{\rho-1}
=\dfrac{1}{w_\rho}.$ ∎

So the zeros' images under $\rho\mapsto w_\rho$ are closed under $w\mapsto 1/w$
(functional equation) and under $w\mapsto\bar w$ (complex conjugation). A zero off the
critical line thus produces a genuine quadruple with moduli $r,\,r,\,1/r,\,1/r$ for some
$r\neq1$; a zero on the line produces points on the unit circle.

## Lemma 3 (contribution of one symmetric quadruple).
Group the four zeros $\{\rho,\bar\rho,1-\rho,1-\bar\rho\}$. Writing $w_\rho=re^{i\theta}$
($r=|w_\rho|$), their combined contribution to $\lambda_n$ is real and equals
$$C_n(r,\theta)=4-2\big(r^{n}+r^{-n}\big)\cos(n\theta).$$

*Proof.* The four $w$-values are $re^{i\theta},\,re^{-i\theta},\,r^{-1}e^{-i\theta},\,
r^{-1}e^{i\theta}$ (using Lemma 2 for the reflection and conjugation for the bar).
Each contributes $1-w^n$. Summing:
$$4-\big(r^ne^{in\theta}+r^ne^{-in\theta}+r^{-n}e^{-in\theta}+r^{-n}e^{in\theta}\big)
=4-2(r^n+r^{-n})\cos(n\theta).$$
This is manifestly real. ∎

(For a zero exactly on the line, $r=1$ and the same formula with a doubled pair gives
$C_n=2(1-\cos n\theta)\ge0$, matching the classical nonnegativity of on-line
contributions.)

## Consequence A (on-line ⇒ nonnegative each block).
If $r=1$ then $C_n(1,\theta)=4-4\cos(n\theta)=4(1-\cos n\theta)\ge0$ for every $n$.
Thus **every** individual quadruple of critical-line zeros contributes nonnegatively to
every λ_n. Non-positivity can only be injected by off-line zeros. This is the precise
"where could negativity come from" statement.

## Consequence B (growth dichotomy — the analytic pressure).
Suppose there exists an off-line zero, so $r=r_0>1$ for some quadruple with angle
$\theta_0$. Its contribution is
$$C_n=4-2(r_0^n+r_0^{-n})\cos(n\theta_0).$$
Consider two cases.

- If $\theta_0/\pi$ is irrational, $\{n\theta_0 \bmod 2\pi\}$ is equidistributed, so there
is a subsequence $n_k$ with $\cos(n_k\theta_0)\ge \tfrac12$. Then
$C_{n_k}\le 4-r_0^{n_k}\to-\infty$ **exponentially**.
- If $\theta_0/\pi=p/q$ rational, then along $n=qm$ we have $\cos(n\theta_0)=1$, giving
$C_{qm}=4-2(r_0^{qm}+r_0^{-qm})\to-\infty$ exponentially.

In both cases a single off-line quadruple drives its own contribution to $-\infty$ at an
exponential rate $r_0^{n}$.

Now compare with the total size of λ_n. It is classical (Bombieri–Lagarias) that
unconditionally $|\lambda_n|=O(n\log n)$ **is not** available without RH; what is
unconditional is the arithmetic formula
$$\lambda_n=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(-1)^{j-1}\,\eta_{j}\quad\text{(secondary terms)}$$
with the remaining zero-sum controlled only polynomially *under* suitable hypotheses.
Here is the honest gap: to convert Consequence B into a contradiction one must bound the
**combined** contribution of all other zeros from below by something growing slower than
$r_0^n$. The exponential growth $r_0^n$ from one off-line pair does dominate any single
competing pair with modulus $r_1<r_0$, but off-line zeros with $r$ arbitrarily close to
the *supremal* modulus could, a priori, conspire so that partial-sum cancellation keeps
$\lambda_n$ bounded. Controlling that conspiracy is exactly the analytic difficulty and I
cannot close it here.

## What is genuinely established vs. classical
- Lemmas 1–3 and Consequence A give a clean, correct, self-contained derivation of the
  sign geometry: **only off-line zeros can produce negative λ_n, and each off-line
  quadruple contributes an oscillating exponentially-growing term $-2(r^n+r^{-n})\cos n\theta$.**
- Consequence B is essentially the mechanism behind Li's theorem (that a *single* off-line
  zero forces λ_n → −∞ along a subsequence *if* one controls the remainder). My
  contribution is the explicit, verified closed form $C_n(r,\theta)$ for a full symmetric
  quadruple and the clean equidistribution/rational dichotomy for the subsequence.
- I did **not** prove the required uniform lower bound on the aggregate of the remaining
  zeros, so this does not yield RH nor an unconditional new inequality.
