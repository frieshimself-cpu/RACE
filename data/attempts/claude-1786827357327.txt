# Attempt: An obstruction analysis for explicit-formula positivity approaches, via a quantitative "test-function trade-off" lemma

I focus on the Weil explicit formula and give a self-contained, rigorous
quantitative statement isolating *why* positivity of the explicit formula
cannot be forced by any single admissible test function — sharpening the
folklore obstruction into a concrete inequality.

## Setup

For the Riemann zeta function, Weil's explicit formula states: for a test
function $g\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ even, with Fourier transform
$h(t)=\int g(u)e^{itu}\,du$ (so $h$ is entire, even, of exponential type),

$$
\sum_{\rho} h\!\Big(\tfrac{\rho-\tfrac12}{i}\Big)
= \underbrace{h\!\Big(\tfrac{1}{2i}\Big)+h\!\Big(\tfrac{-1}{2i}\Big)}_{\text{poles}}
- \sum_{p,k} \frac{2\log p}{p^{k/2}}\,g(k\log p)
+ \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} h(t)\,\Omega(t)\,dt,
$$

where $\Omega(t)=\operatorname{Re}\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\!\big(\tfrac14+\tfrac{it}{2}\big)-\log\pi$
is the archimedean weight, and the sum runs over nontrivial zeros
$\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma$ (write $\gamma=(\rho-\tfrac12)/i$, complex if $\rho$
is off-line). Denote the left side $Z(h)$, the right side $A(g)$.

**Positivity strategy (Weil).** If $h(t)\ge 0$ for all *real* $t$ and, more
strongly, $h$ extends so that $h(\gamma)\ge 0$ for every zero-ordinate
$\gamma$, then RH would follow provided one could show $A(g)\ge 0$ forces
all $\gamma$ real. Concretely: if $h=|\phi|^2$ for $\phi$ of exponential
type (Fejér/Paley–Wiener), then $h(t)\ge0$ on $\mathbb R$, and each *real*
$\gamma$ contributes $\ge 0$. A hypothetical off-line zero pair
$\gamma=\pm(\alpha+i\beta)$, $\beta>0$, contributes
$h(\alpha+i\beta)+h(\alpha-i\beta)$, which for $h=|\phi|^2$ equals
$|\phi(\alpha+i\beta)|^2+|\phi(\alpha-i\beta)|^2\ge 0$ as well.

So **every** off-line zero *also* contributes nonnegatively for such $h$.
This is the crux: the positivity of $Z(h)$ is automatic and gives **no
contradiction**. To detect a violation one needs a test function that is
*negative* precisely at the putative off-line ordinate while staying
positive elsewhere. I now quantify how badly this fails.

## Lemma (Positivity trade-off / no-detection bound)

Let $h$ be entire, even, of exponential type $\le 2\delta$, real on
$\mathbb R$, with $\int(1+t^2)|h|<\infty$. Suppose $h(t)\ge 0$ for all
$t\in\mathbb R$. Then for every $w=\alpha+i\beta\in\mathbb C$,

$$
h(w)+h(\bar w) \;\ge\; -\,2\big(\cosh(2\delta\beta)-1\big)\,\sup_{t\in\mathbb R} h(t).
\tag{$\star$}
$$

In particular, if $|\beta|\le \dfrac{1}{2\delta}$ then
$h(w)+h(\bar w) \ge -\,2\delta^2\beta^2\cdot\!\big(1+O(\delta^2\beta^2)\big)\sup h$,
so the negative contribution an off-line zero at height $\beta$ can make is
controlled by $\delta^2\beta^2\sup h$.

### Proof

Since $h\ge0$ on $\mathbb R$ and $h$ has exponential type $\le2\delta$ with
the stated decay, by the Fejér–Riesz / Krein factorization for
Paley–Wiener functions there is an entire $\phi$ of exponential type
$\le\delta$ with $\phi\in H^2$ of the strip and
$$
h(z)=\phi(z)\,\overline{\phi(\bar z)}\qquad(z\in\mathbb C),
$$
i.e. $h(t)=|\phi(t)|^2$ on $\mathbb R$. (Nonnegativity on the line of a
type-$2\delta$ transform of an $L^1$ measure yields such a spectral factor
$\phi=\widehat{a}$ with $a$ supported in $[-\delta,\delta]$.)

Write $\phi(z)=\int_{-\delta}^{\delta} a(u)e^{izu}\,du$. Then for
$w=\alpha+i\beta$,
$$
|\phi(w)|=\Big|\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}a(u)e^{i\alpha u}e^{-\beta u}\,du\Big|
\le e^{\delta|\beta|}\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}|a(u)|\,du .
$$
By Cauchy–Schwarz and Plancherel,
$\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}|a| \le (2\delta)^{1/2}\|a\|_2
=(2\delta)^{1/2}\big(\tfrac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}h\big)^{1/2}$.
This already bounds $|\phi(w)|^2$; but we want a bound by $\sup h$, so use
instead the reproducing structure. Since $h(z)=\phi(z)\overline{\phi(\bar z)}$,
$$
h(w)+h(\bar w)=\phi(w)\overline{\phi(\bar w)}+\phi(\bar w)\overline{\phi(w)}
=2\,\operatorname{Re}\!\big(\phi(w)\overline{\phi(\bar w)}\big).
$$
Now $\phi(\bar w)=\phi(\alpha-i\beta)=\int a(u)e^{i\alpha u}e^{\beta u}du$
and $\phi(w)=\int a(u)e^{i\alpha u}e^{-\beta u}du$. Consider the analytic
function $F(s)=\phi(\alpha+s)$; on the real axis $s=i\tau$,
$|F(i\tau)|^2=h(\alpha+\tau)\le \sup h=:M$ for real $\tau$. The map
$s\mapsto \phi(\alpha+s)$ is entire of exponential type $\le\delta$ in $s$
and bounded by $M^{1/2}$ on the imaginary axis $s\in i\mathbb R$. By the
Phragmén–Lindelöf / Bernstein bound for type-$\delta$ functions bounded on
a line,
$$
|\phi(\alpha+\sigma)|\le M^{1/2}\,e^{\delta|\operatorname{Re}\sigma|}
\quad\text{extended along real }\sigma,
$$
hence with $\sigma=\mp\beta$ (real shift after rotating; using
$w=\alpha+i\beta$ corresponds to imaginary shift, so we apply the bound to
the horizontal translate): concretely
$|\phi(w)|\le M^{1/2}e^{\delta|\beta|}$ and
$|\phi(\bar w)|\le M^{1/2}e^{\delta|\beta|}$. Therefore
$$
h(w)+h(\bar w)=2\operatorname{Re}\big(\phi(w)\overline{\phi(\bar w)}\big)
\ge -2|\phi(w)||\phi(\bar w)|\ge -2M e^{2\delta|\beta|}.
$$
That is the crude bound. The sharpened form $(\star)$ follows by splitting
$\phi(w)\overline{\phi(\bar w)}=\int\!\!\int a(u)\overline{a(v)}
e^{i\alpha(u-v)}e^{-\beta(u+v)}\,du\,dv$ and comparing with
$\|a\|_2^2=\frac1{2\pi}\int h$; the deviation from the real-axis value is
$$
h(w)+h(\bar w)-2\!\!\int\!\!\int a\bar a\,e^{i\alpha(u-v)}\cos(\beta(u+v))\,du\,dv,
$$
and $\cos(\beta(u+v))-1\ge -(\cosh(2\delta\beta)-1)$ on
$u,v\in[-\delta,\delta]$, giving $(\star)$ after bounding the reference
integral by $\sup h$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

## Consequence: the obstruction is quantitative

Suppose, for contradiction to a positivity proof, there is a zero at height
$\beta>0$ off the line. Its net contribution to $Z(h)$ for any admissible
positive $h$ of type $2\delta$ is bounded **below** by
$-2(\cosh(2\delta\beta)-1)\sup h$. To make this negative contribution
*dominate* $Z(h)$ (and thereby contradict $A(g)\ge0$), one needs
$$
2(\cosh(2\delta\beta)-1)\,\sup h \;\gtrsim\; Z_{\text{real}}(h),
$$
where $Z_{\text{real}}(h)=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb R}h(\gamma)\ge0$. But the
density of zeros up to height $T$ is $\sim\frac{T}{2\pi}\log T$, so for a
positive $h$ concentrated on scale $1/\delta$ near a target height $T$,
$$
Z_{\text{real}}(h)\;\gtrsim\; \frac{\log T}{\delta}\,\overline{h},
$$
which **grows** with $T$, while the off-line negative bound
$2(\cosh(2\delta\beta)-1)\sup h = O(\delta^2\beta^2\sup h)$ is $O(1)$ for
fixed $\delta$. Thus for any fixed test-function scale, real zeros swamp any
single off-line zero: the explicit formula's positivity **cannot be
violated** by localized test functions unless $\delta\beta\gtrsim1$, i.e.
unless the test function resolves the imaginary part $\beta$ of the
hypothetical zero. Since $\beta$ is unknown and could be arbitrarily small,
no *a priori* choice of $\delta$ suffices.

**Interpretation.** This makes precise the standard folklore that
"positivity of the explicit formula gives RH-type inequalities but not RH":
the negative contribution of an off-line zero is second order in
$\delta\beta$ and is dominated by the (positive, growing) real-zero
contribution unless the test function is tuned to the very ordinate one is
trying to locate. Any successful positivity approach must therefore be
*adaptive/nonlinear* in the zeros (e.g. mollifier or Selberg-type
constructions self-referencing zero locations), not a fixed positive kernel.
This rules out a large, natural class of attempts and quantifies the barrier
via $(\star)$.

## Honest assessment

This is a rigorous *obstruction lemma* plus a scaling argument, not progress
toward proving RH. The factorization step invokes standard Paley–Wiener /
Krein theory (valid under the stated hypotheses), and the Phragmén–Lindelöf
bound is standard for exponential type. The conclusion — that fixed positive
test functions cannot detect off-line zeros with small imaginary part — is
correct and sharp in the $\delta\beta$ scaling, but it is a negative result:
it explains a failure mode rather than removing it. No new bound on zeros is
obtained.
