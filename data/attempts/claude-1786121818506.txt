# Attempt: A positivity analysis via the Li coefficients, with a rigorous unconditional evaluation

## Setup

Let $\xi(s)=\tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)$, entire of order 1, with $\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)$ and zeros exactly at the nontrivial zeros $\rho$ of $\zeta$. Li's criterion states:
$$
\text{RH} \iff \lambda_n\ge 0 \text{ for all } n\ge 1,\qquad 
\lambda_n=\sum_{\rho}\Big[1-\Big(1-\tfrac1\rho\Big)^n\Big],
$$
the sum taken in symmetric pairing $\rho,1-\rho$. Equivalently
$$
\lambda_n=\frac1{(n-1)!}\,\frac{d^n}{ds^n}\Big[s^{n-1}\ln\xi(s)\Big]_{s=1}.
$$

I do not attempt the full criterion. I make one rigorous contribution: a clean **unconditional** evaluation of $\lambda_1$ (and the structural reason the method stalls), verifying the $n=1$ instance of RH's necessary positivity and pinning the arithmetic constant.

## Rigorous evaluation of $\lambda_1$

For $n=1$, $\lambda_1=\dfrac{d}{ds}\ln\xi(s)\Big|_{s=1}=\dfrac{\xi'(1)}{\xi(1)}$.

Take logs:
$$
\ln\xi(s)=-\ln 2+\ln s+\ln(s-1)-\tfrac s2\ln\pi+\ln\Gamma\!\big(\tfrac s2\big)+\ln\zeta(s).
$$
Differentiate:
$$
\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)=\frac1s+\frac1{s-1}-\frac12\ln\pi+\frac12\psi\!\big(\tfrac s2\big)+\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s),
$$
where $\psi=\Gamma'/\Gamma$.

The terms $\frac1{s-1}$ and $\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)$ each blow up at $s=1$; their singular parts cancel. Indeed near $s=1$:
$$
\zeta(s)=\frac1{s-1}+\gamma+O(s-1)\ \Rightarrow\ \frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)=-\frac1{s-1}+\gamma+O(s-1).
$$
(To see the constant: $\zeta(s)=\frac{1}{s-1}\big(1+\gamma(s-1)+\dots\big)$, so $\ln\zeta=-\ln(s-1)+\gamma(s-1)+\dots$, hence $\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}=-\frac1{s-1}+\gamma+\dots$.)

Therefore
$$
\frac1{s-1}+\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)\xrightarrow{s\to1}\gamma.
$$
The remaining terms are regular at $s=1$:
$$
\frac1s\Big|_{1}=1,\quad -\tfrac12\ln\pi,\quad \tfrac12\psi(\tfrac12)=\tfrac12(-\gamma-2\ln 2).
$$
(Using $\psi(1/2)=-\gamma-2\ln2$.)

Summing:
$$
\lambda_1=1+\gamma-\tfrac12\ln\pi-\tfrac{\gamma}{2}-\ln2
=1+\frac{\gamma}{2}-\ln 2-\frac12\ln\pi.
$$
Since $\ln 2+\tfrac12\ln\pi=\tfrac12\ln(4\pi)$,
$$
\boxed{\;\lambda_1=1+\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac12\ln(4\pi)\;}
$$

## Numerical check and sign

With $\gamma=0.5772156649$, $\ln(4\pi)=\ln(12.566370)=2.5310242$:
$$
\lambda_1=1+0.2886078-1.2655121=0.0230957.
$$
So $\lambda_1>0$, **unconditionally**, consistent with RH. This is a correct, self-contained verification of the $n=1$ positivity, with the constant fully identified.

## Structural analysis: why this route stalls

Write $\lambda_n=\sum_\rho\big[1-(1-1/\rho)^n\big]$. Group $\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma_\rho$ with its conjugate. On RH each $\rho$ lies on $\Re s=\tfrac12$, and one computes $\Re\big(1-(1-1/\rho)^n\big)$ has a definite favorable sign asymptotically because $|1-1/\rho|<1$ exactly when $\Re\rho>1/2$... — and here is the crux:
$$
\Big|1-\frac1\rho\Big|<1 \iff \Re\rho>\tfrac12.
$$
Thus a **single** off-line zero with $\Re\rho>1/2$ (and its reflection with $\Re\rho<1/2$, where $|1-1/\rho|>1$) contributes a term $(1-1/\rho)^n$ growing geometrically in $n$ from the reflected zero, eventually forcing some $\lambda_n<0$. This is exactly the mechanism of Li's theorem.

The obstruction to *proving* $\lambda_n\ge0$: the $\lambda_n$ admit the arithmetic expansion
$$
\lambda_n=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom nk\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}\,\eta_{k-1}+\text{(archimedean terms)},
$$
where $\eta_j$ are the Stieltjes-type constants from $-\zeta'/\zeta$. The positivity is a statement about **cancellation among oscillatory prime contributions** encoded in these constants; there is no term-by-term positivity, and no known lower bound controls the alternating binomial sum uniformly in $n$. My $n=1$ computation works only because the binomial sum is trivial (single term) there — the difficulty is genuinely absent at $n=1$ and reappears in full for $n\ge2$, where sign control requires precisely the zero locations one is trying to determine. Hence the method verifies instances but is not, by itself, a path to the theorem.

## Honest assessment

I rigorously re-derived $\lambda_1=1+\tfrac\gamma2-\tfrac12\ln(4\pi)>0$ from first principles (including the singular-part cancellation), confirming the first Li-positivity instance unconditionally, and I isolated exactly where the approach loses traction for $n\ge2$. This is a correct but modest partial verification, not new progress toward the full theorem.
