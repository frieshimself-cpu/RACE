# Attempt: Localizing the obstruction in the degree-reduction of the Hodge Conjecture

Throughout, $X$ is smooth projective over $\mathbb{C}$ of dimension $n$, with hyperplane
class $L=c_1(\mathcal O(1))\in H^2(X,\mathbb Q)$, of Hodge type $(1,1)$ and **algebraic**.
Write $\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)=H^{2p}(X,\mathbb Q)\cap H^{p,p}(X)$ and let
$\mathcal A^p(X)\subseteq \mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)$ be the subspace of classes of algebraic
cycles. The Hodge conjecture is $\mathcal A^p=\mathrm{Hdg}^p$ for all $p$.

I isolate exactly which part of the "reduction to middle dimension" is unconditional and
which part is genuinely obstructed, identifying the obstruction with the Lefschetz standard
conjecture. This is meant as a clean, self-contained accounting, not a claim of new
progress on the open core.

## 1. Two elementary closure properties of $\mathcal A^\bullet$

(i) $\mathcal A^\bullet(X)=\bigoplus_p \mathcal A^p(X)$ is a graded $\mathbb Q$-subalgebra of
$H^\bullet(X,\mathbb Q)$ under cup product (intersection of cycles, made transverse), and
$L\in\mathcal A^1$. Hence **multiplication by $L$ maps $\mathcal A^p\to\mathcal A^{p+1}$.**

(ii) The subring $\langle \mathrm{Hdg}^1(X)\rangle$ generated by divisor classes lies in
$\mathcal A^\bullet(X)$: by the Lefschetz $(1,1)$ theorem $\mathrm{Hdg}^1=\mathcal A^1$, and
$\mathcal A^\bullet$ is closed under products. So the *only* Hodge classes that can fail to
be algebraic are those **not** expressible as polynomials in $(1,1)$-Hodge classes. (On
abelian varieties such classes exist — Weil/Mumford–Tate classes — so this closure property
is genuinely insufficient; it does, however, dispose of complete intersections and any $X$
whose rational cohomology ring is generated in degree $2$.)

## 2. Unconditional reduction: high degrees $\Rightarrow$ low degrees

**Lemma A.** Fix $p$ with $2p>n$, and set $q=n-p$ (so $2q<n$). If
$\mathrm{Hdg}^{q}(X)=\mathcal A^{q}(X)$ then $\mathrm{Hdg}^{p}(X)=\mathcal A^{p}(X)$.

*Proof.* Hard Lefschetz gives an isomorphism
$L^{\,2p-n}=L^{\,p-q}:H^{2q}(X,\mathbb Q)\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }H^{2p}(X,\mathbb Q)$.
Since $L$ has type $(1,1)$, this map is a morphism of Hodge structures (of bidegree
$(p-q,p-q)$), hence restricts to an isomorphism
$L^{p-q}:\mathrm{Hdg}^{q}(X)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Hdg}^{p}(X)$.
Given $\alpha\in\mathrm{Hdg}^p$, let $\beta=(L^{p-q})^{-1}\alpha\in\mathrm{Hdg}^q$.
By hypothesis $\beta\in\mathcal A^q$, and by §1(i)
$\alpha=L^{p-q}\cup\beta\in\mathcal A^{p}$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

Thus **the Hodge conjecture on $X$ in all degrees is equivalent to its truth in degrees
$2p\le n$.** The key point is that Lemma A only *multiplies* by the algebraic class $L$;
no division is needed. This is the direction that works for free.

## 3. Where the reduction genuinely breaks: division by $L$

To reduce a low-degree class $2p<n$ *downward* (toward the middle, or onto a hyperplane
section) one is forced to run the argument in reverse and this is where things fail.

**Observation.** Let $\alpha\in\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)$ with $2p<n$, and let $i:Y\hookrightarrow X$
be a smooth hyperplane section, $\dim Y=n-1$. The restriction
$i^*:H^{2p}(X)\to H^{2p}(Y)$ sends Hodge classes to Hodge classes, and by the Lefschetz
hyperplane theorem it is injective for $2p\le n-1$. Suppose inductively that HC holds on
$Y$, so $i^*\alpha=[Z]$ for an algebraic cycle $Z$ on $Y$. The Gysin map gives
$$
i_*[Z]=i_*i^*\alpha=L\cup\alpha\in\mathcal A^{p+1}(X).
$$
So HC on hyperplane sections proves only that $L\cup\alpha$ is algebraic, **not that
$\alpha$ is.** To conclude $\alpha\in\mathcal A^p$ one must invert cup-with-$L$ on the
algebraic level, i.e. know that the class $L\cup\alpha$, which is algebraic, has an
algebraic "$L$-quotient."

This is exactly the content of the **Lefschetz standard conjecture** $B(X)$: that the
operator $\Lambda$ (the inverse of the appropriate power of $L$ in the Lefschetz
decomposition) is induced by an algebraic correspondence. Equivalently:

**Proposition.** Assume $B(X)$. Then HC for all degrees $\le n$ follows from HC for
*primitive middle-dimensional* Hodge classes together with HC on all smooth linear
sections. Concretely: if $L\cup\alpha\in\mathcal A^{p+1}$ and $B(X)$ holds, then
$\alpha=\Lambda(L\cup\alpha)$ lies in $\mathcal A^p$, because $\Lambda$ is then an
algebraic correspondence and $\mathcal A^\bullet$ is stable under algebraic
correspondences.

*Proof.* Algebraic correspondences act on $\mathcal A^\bullet$ (image of a cycle under a
correspondence is a cycle). Under $B(X)$, $\Lambda$ is such a correspondence, and on the
Lefschetz component containing $\alpha$ one has $\Lambda\circ(L\cup-)=\mathrm{id}$. Apply
$\Lambda$ to $L\cup\alpha\in\mathcal A^{p+1}$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

## 4. Summary of the accounting

- The passage $2p>n\rightsquigarrow 2p<n$ is **unconditional** (Lemma A): it uses only
  multiplication by algebraic $L$.
- The passage $2p<n\rightsquigarrow$ hyperplane sections / middle dimension **requires
  dividing by $L$**, and the precise obstruction is the algebraicity of $\Lambda$, i.e. the
  Lefschetz standard conjecture $B(X)$.
- Hence, *modulo* $B(X)$, the full Hodge conjecture for all $X$ is equivalent to the Hodge
  conjecture for primitive Hodge classes in the middle degree $2p=n$ (n even). Since the
  Hodge conjecture itself implies $B(X)$, the two "hard" inputs are not independent, and no
  circularity is smuggled in: what §2–3 show is that the *only* place the elementary
  formalism stalls is the non-multiplicative direction, and it stalls at exactly the
  Λ-algebraicity statement.

I did not resolve either $B(X)$ or the middle-dimensional primitive case; §1(ii) shows the
divisor-generated subring is algebraic but this misses the essential classes (Weil classes
on abelian varieties). So no new ground is gained on the open core; the contribution is a
sharp localization of where the classical reduction is unconditional versus where it is
equivalent to the Lefschetz standard conjecture.
