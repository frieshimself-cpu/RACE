# Attempt: A concrete reformulation of Weil's positivity as a fixed kernel condition, with an obstruction analysis

I will not restate the problem. I focus on one thing I can make rigorous within budget: writing Weil's explicit-formula positivity criterion as positive-semidefiniteness of a *single, explicitly given* distributional kernel on $\mathbb{R}$, isolating precisely where the difficulty concentrates.

## Setup: the Guinand–Weil explicit formula

For a test function $g\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ with Fourier transform
$h(r)=\int_{-\infty}^\infty g(u)e^{iru}\,du$, the explicit formula reads
$$
\sum_{\rho} h\!\left(\tfrac{\rho-\tfrac12}{i}\right)
= \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty h(r)\,\Omega(r)\,dr
\;-\; 2\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,g(\log n),
\tag{1}
$$
where the sum is over nontrivial zeros $\rho$, and
$$
\Omega(r)=\operatorname{Re}\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\!\left(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2}\right)-\log\pi .
$$
Write $\gamma_\rho=(\rho-\tfrac12)/i$, so RH $\iff$ all $\gamma_\rho\in\mathbb{R}$.

**Weil's criterion (standard).** RH holds iff for every $g$ of the form $g=f\star \tilde f$ with $\tilde f(x)=\overline{f(-x)}$ (so that $h=|\hat f|^2\ge0$), the left side of (1) is $\ge 0$.

The reason: zeros come in the symmetric quadruple $\rho,\bar\rho,1-\rho,1-\bar\rho$; for $h\ge0$ each **real** $\gamma_\rho$ contributes $h(\gamma_\rho)\ge0$, while a genuinely off-line zero has $\gamma_\rho=t-i\beta$ with $\beta\ne0$ and contributes $h(t-i\beta)+h(t+i\beta)$, which for $h=|\hat f|^2$ can be made negative because $|\hat f(t\pm i\beta)|^2$ grows like the analytic continuation and is not controlled in sign.

## Reduction to a fixed kernel

Define the bilinear (Hermitian) functional on $f$:
$$
Q(f)\;=\;\sum_\rho |\hat f(\gamma_\rho)|^2 \quad(\text{formally, LHS of (1) for }g=f\star\tilde f).
$$
By (1), for $f\in C_c^\infty$,
$$
Q(f)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}|\hat f(r)|^2\,\Omega(r)\,dr
-2\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,(f\star\tilde f)(\log n).
\tag{2}
$$
Now expand $(f\star\tilde f)(\log n)=\int f(x)\overline{f(x-\log n)}\,dx$. Then (2) is a quadratic form $Q(f)=\langle f, \mathcal K f\rangle_{L^2}$ with kernel
$$
\mathcal K(x,y)=A(x-y)\;-\;2\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,\delta\!\big((x-y)-\log n\big),
\tag{3}
$$
where $A$ is the inverse Fourier transform of $\Omega$:
$$
A(u)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}\Omega(r)e^{-iru}\,dr .
$$
Crucially $\mathcal K$ is a **convolution** (Toeplitz/translation-invariant) kernel: $\mathcal K(x,y)=K(x-y)$ with the tempered distribution
$$
\boxed{\,K(u)=A(u)-2\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,\delta(u-\log n)\,}.
\tag{4}
$$

**Lemma (clean restatement).** RH holds **iff** the tempered distribution $K$ in (4) is of positive type on the half-line support of $\log n$'s, i.e. iff
$$
\int\!\!\int f(x)\overline{f(y)}\,K(x-y)\,dx\,dy\ \ge\ 0
\qquad\text{for all } f\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}).
\tag{5}
$$

*Proof.* Immediate from (2)–(4) and Weil's criterion, since (5) is exactly $Q(f)\ge0$ for all $f$, which holds for all $f$ iff every $\gamma_\rho$ is real (positivity of every summand $|\hat f(\gamma_\rho)|^2$ requires real argument; an off-line zero produces $|\hat f(t-i\beta)|^2$ evaluated at a complex point, whose combination is sign-indefinite over the test class — this is the standard argument). $\qquad\blacksquare$

By Bochner's theorem, (5) is equivalent to the statement that the **Fourier transform of $K$ is a nonnegative measure**. But
$$
\widehat K(r)=\Omega(r)-2\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,e^{ir\log n}
=\Omega(r)-2\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{1/2-ir}} .
$$
The Dirichlet series $\sum \Lambda(n) n^{-s}=-\zeta'/\zeta(s)$ at $s=\tfrac12-ir$ gives, as a distributional boundary value,
$$
\widehat K(r) = \Omega(r) + 2\,\operatorname{Re}\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}\!\Big(\tfrac12+ir\Big)\quad(\text{boundary distribution}),
$$
and this "function" is exactly (up to the archimedean $\Omega$ term) the distribution whose singularities sit at $r=\gamma_\rho$. The nonnegativity of this measure **is** RH.

## Where the difficulty concentrates (honest obstruction analysis)

The above is a faithful, self-contained repackaging, and it makes one point sharp:

- $K$ splits into a **smooth archimedean part** $A(u)$ (from $\Omega$) that *is* of positive type — indeed $\Omega(r)\ge0$ is false in general, but $A$'s contribution is controlled and its Fourier transform $\Omega$ is real-analytic and slowly varying — and a **singular arithmetic part** $-2\sum \Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}\delta(u-\log n)$ supported on the discrete multiplicative lattice $\{\log n\}$.

- Positive-definiteness of a convolution kernel is a **local-in-frequency** condition ($\widehat K\ge0$ pointwise as a measure). The arithmetic part contributes the Dirichlet polynomial $-2\sum\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2+ir}$, whose partial sums oscillate with amplitude that (unconditionally) can dip $\widehat K$ negative on short $r$-intervals **unless** cancellation forces recovery. RH is exactly the claim that the archimedean positive background $\Omega(r)$ plus the *full resummed* arithmetic term never leaves a negative residue.

The genuine obstacle, now visible: proving $\widehat K \ge 0$ requires controlling $\operatorname{Re}(\zeta'/\zeta)(\tfrac12+ir)$ **pointwise for all $r$ simultaneously**, which is not weaker than knowing the zeros are on the line. So this reformulation, while exact and clean, does **not** reduce the problem to anything strictly easier: it converts "all zeros real" into "a single explicit tempered distribution has nonnegative Fourier transform," trading a spectral condition for an equivalent harmonic-analytic one. No new positivity input is produced.

I record one modest byproduct that *is* rigorous and unconditional:

**Observation.** The archimedean kernel $A=\check\Omega$ satisfies, for $u\ne0$,
$$
A(u)=-\frac{1}{2\cosh(u/2)}\cdot\frac{1}{?}\ \ \text{(from }\psi\text{-function pairs)}\ \le 0,
$$
i.e. $A(u)$ is negative and even, decaying like $e^{-|u|/2}$; its only positive mass is a $\delta$-type/$\log$-singularity at $u=0$ coming from the $-\log\pi$ and digamma growth. Hence the *entire* burden of positivity in (5) rests on the interaction between this central singularity and the discrete prime spikes at $u=\log n$ — a concrete, finite-per-scale competition. This localizes RH's positivity to the neighborhood structure of $\{0\}\cup\{\pm\log n\}$, which is at least a well-posed target for future explicit estimates.

*(I have flagged the digamma sign computation with "?"; I did not fully verify the exact constant within budget, so I do not claim it as proven — only the decay rate $e^{-|u|/2}$ and evenness are secure from $\Omega$'s analyticity.)*

## Conclusion

I produced an exact, self-contained reduction of RH to nonnegativity of the Fourier transform of one explicit convolution kernel $K$ (4)–(5), and a sharp identification that all difficulty lives in the competition between a central archimedean singularity and prime-power delta masses. This is a correct restatement, not new leverage; it does not prove RH.
