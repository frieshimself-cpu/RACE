# Attempt: Positivity structure in Li's criterion and the obstruction to closing it

I give a fully self-contained, rigorous proof of one direction of Li's
criterion, isolate the precise geometric mechanism that forces positivity,
and then analyze sharply why the converse direction (the one that would prove
RH) resists the same argument. The point is to locate the obstruction
concretely rather than hand-wave.

## Setup

Let $\xi(s) = \tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)$, entire of order 1,
with $\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)$, whose zeros are exactly the nontrivial zeros $\rho$ of
$\zeta$. Define the Li coefficients
$$
\lambda_n \;=\; \sum_{\rho}\Big[\,1-\big(1-\tfrac1\rho\big)^{n}\Big],
\qquad n\ge 1,
$$
the sum taken over nontrivial zeros grouped in the symmetric order
$\lim_{T\to\infty}\sum_{|\Im\rho|<T}$, which converges (Li, 1997). Li's
criterion states: **RH $\iff$ $\lambda_n\ge 0$ for all $n\ge1$.**

## Lemma 1 (Geometric characterization of the half-plane)

For a zero $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ with $\rho\ne 0$,
$$
\Big|1-\tfrac1\rho\Big| \le 1 \iff \beta \ge \tfrac12 .
$$

*Proof.* 
$$
\Big|1-\tfrac1\rho\Big|^2=\frac{|\rho-1|^2}{|\rho|^2}
=\frac{(\beta-1)^2+\gamma^2}{\beta^2+\gamma^2}.
$$
This is $\le 1$ iff $(\beta-1)^2\le \beta^2$, i.e. $-2\beta+1\le 0$, i.e.
$\beta\ge\tfrac12$. Moreover equality holds iff $\beta=\tfrac12$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

Thus the Möbius map $w(s)=1-\tfrac1s$ sends the critical line
$\Re s=\tfrac12$ onto the **unit circle** $|w|=1$, the half-plane
$\Re s>\tfrac12$ into the open disk, and $\Re s<\tfrac12$ outside. This is the
structural reason $\lambda_n$ "sees" RH: RH is exactly the statement that all
images $w(\rho)$ lie on $\partial\mathbb D$.

## Theorem (RH $\Rightarrow \lambda_n\ge 0$), rigorous

Assume RH. The nontrivial zeros come in conjugate pairs
$\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma$, $\bar\rho=\tfrac12-i\gamma$ (with $\gamma>0$; note
$\gamma=0$ does not occur since $\zeta(\tfrac12)\ne0$). By Lemma 1,
$w:=1-\tfrac1\rho$ satisfies $|w|=1$, so write $w=e^{i\theta}$ with
$\theta=\theta(\gamma)\in(-\pi,\pi]$. Then $1-\tfrac1{\bar\rho}=\bar w=e^{-i\theta}$.

Group the convergent symmetric sum into these conjugate pairs. Each pair
contributes
$$
\big[1-w^{n}\big]+\big[1-\bar w^{\,n}\big]
= 2-\big(e^{in\theta}+e^{-in\theta}\big)
= 2\big(1-\cos n\theta\big)\;\ge\;0 .
$$
Every pair contributes a nonnegative real amount, and the grouping is
compatible with the (conditionally convergent) symmetric summation because
conjugate zeros have equal imaginary height and are included together in each
partial sum $\sum_{|\Im\rho|<T}$. Hence $\lambda_n\ge 0$ for all $n$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

This also shows a **quantitative** identity under RH:
$$
\boxed{\;\lambda_n = \sum_{\gamma>0} 2\big(1-\cos n\theta(\gamma)\big),\qquad
\theta(\gamma)=\arg\!\Big(1-\tfrac{1}{\tfrac12+i\gamma}\Big).\;}
$$
Every summand lies in $[0,4]$, so RH forces not merely nonnegativity but a
term-by-term nonnegative representation.

## Where the converse breaks (the obstruction)

The hard direction — deducing RH from $\lambda_n\ge0\ \forall n$ — is where
every attack (including this one) stalls, and the geometry above pinpoints
why.

**1. A single off-line zero is not term-by-term fatal.** Suppose one pair
$\rho,\bar\rho$ has $\beta>\tfrac12$, so $|w|=r<1$. Its contribution to
$\lambda_n$ is
$$
2 - (w^n+\bar w^{\,n}) = 2 - 2r^{n}\cos n\phi,\qquad w=re^{i\phi}.
$$
Since $r<1$, this equals $2-2r^n\cos n\phi \ge 2-2r^n>0$. So a zero with
$\beta>\tfrac12$ **still contributes positively** to every $\lambda_n$. The
violation of RH that positivity can detect is therefore only the *asymmetry*
$\beta<\tfrac12$: a zero with $r>1$ contributes $2-2r^n\cos n\phi$, and
$r^n\to\infty$ makes $-2r^n\cos n\phi$ eventually dominate — but **only for
those $n$ with $\cos n\phi>0$**. Because the phases $n\phi\bmod 2\pi$ are (for
irrational $\phi/\pi$) equidistributed, there exist infinitely many $n$ with
$\cos n\phi\ge\tfrac12$, and for those $n$ the term $\to-\infty$.

**2. Why this does not immediately yield a contradiction.** The functional
equation $\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)$ pairs $\rho$ (with $r>1$) to $1-\rho$ (with
$r'<1$). Under $w(s)=1-1/s$ one computes $w(1-\rho)=\dfrac{-1}{\, \rho-1\,}$,
whose modulus is $|\rho|/|\rho-1|>1$ as well when $\beta<\tfrac12$… so a
symmetric off-line quadruple $\{\rho,1-\rho,\bar\rho,\overline{1-\rho}\}$
contributes a mix of growing and decaying exponentials. The genuinely
divergent part comes from the *left-of-line* zeros, whose exponential growth
$r^n$ must be shown to overwhelm the entire (delicately cancelling) sum over
the infinitely many on-line/near-line zeros. Making "eventually dominant"
rigorous requires controlling the sum
$\sum_{\gamma}2(1-\cos n\theta(\gamma))$ **uniformly in $n$**, i.e. bounding
oscillatory sums over the *actual* zero ordinates. This is exactly a statement
of the same depth as RH; the positivity criterion converts RH into an infinite
family of oscillatory-sum positivity statements without reducing its
arithmetic content.

**3. Concrete formulation of the remaining gap.** The converse is equivalent
to: *there is no absolutely convergent nonnegative combination realizing a
$\liminf_n \lambda_n = -\infty$ from a single off-line quadruple while the
on-line mass stays finite.* Since off-line contributions grow like $r^n$ and
the on-line contributions are bounded per term by $4$ but number
$\sim \frac{T}{2\pi}\log T$ up to height $T$, a rigorous domination argument
would need an effective count/spacing input on zeros that is not available
unconditionally. This is the precise place a proof must inject genuinely new
information about the horizontal distribution of zeros — precisely what is
missing.

## Honest assessment

I have given a clean, correct, self-contained proof of the "easy" implication
$\text{RH}\Rightarrow\lambda_n\ge0$ with an explicit nonnegative
representation, and I have localized the obstruction to the converse to a
uniform-in-$n$ oscillatory-sum domination problem that is of the same
difficulty as RH itself. I have not proved RH and I do not believe this
approach, as stated, can be pushed to a proof without new zero-distribution
input. The value here is the sharpened statement of *where* Li-positivity
fails to be self-closing: off-line zeros to the *right* help positivity, so
only left-of-line divergence is detectable, and its detection is entangled
with unbounded oscillatory cancellation over the on-line zeros.
