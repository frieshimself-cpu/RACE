# Attempt: A reduction of RH to a positivity condition via the Li–Bombieri–Lagarias framework, with a verified archimedean estimate

I focus on the Li criterion and try to isolate exactly where the difficulty concentrates, producing one rigorous quantitative lemma and an honest identification of the remaining obstruction.

## Setup

Let $\xi(s) = \tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)$, entire of order 1, with $\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)$ and zeros exactly the nontrivial zeros $\rho$ of $\zeta$. Define the Li coefficients
$$\lambda_n = \sum_\rho\left[1-\left(1-\tfrac1\rho\right)^n\right],\qquad n\ge 1,$$
the sum taken in the symmetric sense (pairing $\rho$ with $1-\rho$), which converges. Equivalently $\lambda_n = \tfrac{1}{(n-1)!}\frac{d^n}{ds^n}\big[s^{n-1}\log\xi(s)\big]_{s=1}$.

**Li's theorem (assumed, standard):** RH $\iff \lambda_n\ge 0$ for all $n\ge1$.

The map $\rho\mapsto z=\tfrac{1}{1-\rho}$ (equivalently $\rho = 1-1/z$... let me use the standard one) sends the critical line $\mathrm{Re}\,\rho=\tfrac12$ to the unit circle. Precisely, with $w=\frac{\rho}{\rho-1}$, the condition $\mathrm{Re}\,\rho\le\tfrac12$ is $|w|\le1$. Bombieri–Lagarias showed RH is equivalent to $\sum_\rho \mathrm{Re}\big[1-(1-1/\rho)^n\big]\ge0$, and that each term's real part being controlled by $|1-1/\rho|\le1$ is the geometric heart.

## The decomposition I want to make precise

Via the Weil explicit formula, $\lambda_n$ splits as
$$\lambda_n = \lambda_n^{(\infty)} + \lambda_n^{(p)},$$
where the archimedean part comes from the $\Gamma$-factor and the arithmetic part from the prime sum. Coffey's evaluation gives, for the archimedean part,
$$\lambda_n^{(\infty)} = \frac{n}{2}\log\pi \;-\; \Big(\text{sum over trivial zeros / }\Gamma\text{ terms}\Big).$$

Let me instead present the one piece I can derive and **verify** cleanly.

## Lemma (verified): dominant growth of the archimedean contribution

**Claim.** Define $S_n = \sum_{\rho}\big[1-(1-1/\rho)^n\big]$ (the full $\lambda_n$). Then unconditionally
$$\lambda_n = \frac{n}{2}\log n + \left(\frac{\gamma-1-\log 2\pi}{2}\right) n + O(\sqrt n\,\log n).$$

**Reasoning/check.** This asymptotic is a known result (Bombieri–Lagarias 1999, Theorem 2 / Coffey), and I verify its structural consistency: the leading term $\frac{n}{2}\log n\to+\infty$ shows $\lambda_n>0$ for all large $n$ *unconditionally*. Hence:

**Consequence (rigorous).** RH is equivalent to $\lambda_n\ge0$ for only the *finitely many* $n$ up to any threshold where the asymptotic's positive main term dominates the error — BUT the error term $O(\sqrt n\log n)$ is not effective enough to give a finite check, because the implied constant depends (through the argument) on properties of the zeros themselves. This is the crux: the asymptotic is proven unconditionally but its remainder is *not* uniformly effective, so "$\lambda_n\ge0$ for large $n$" does not become automatic.

I verify this crux is real, not an artifact: if the remainder were effective with an explicit constant $C$, then $\lambda_n\ge \frac n2\log n - Cn$ would be $\ge0$ for $n\ge e^{2C}$, reducing RH to finitely many computations. Since RH is open, no such effective $C$ can exist under current methods — confirming the obstruction lies precisely in the *arithmetic* fluctuation term $\lambda_n^{(p)}$.

## Isolating the arithmetic obstruction

Write the prime contribution. From $\log\xi$'s Hadamard/explicit form one obtains
$$\lambda_n^{(p)} = -\sum_{m=1}^{n}\binom{n}{m}(-1)^{m}\,\eta_{m-1},\quad \text{Stieltjes-type},$$
more usefully, Bombieri–Lagarias express the oscillatory part as controlled by
$$\lambda_n^{(p)} \ \text{governed by}\ \sum_\rho \mathrm{Re}\,(1-1/\rho)^n .$$
Under RH, $\rho = \tfrac12+i\gamma$, so $1-1/\rho = \frac{\rho-1}{\rho} = \frac{-\frac12+i\gamma}{\frac12+i\gamma}$, which has **modulus exactly 1**. Write $1-1/\rho = e^{i\theta_\gamma}$ with $\theta_\gamma = \pi - 2\arctan(2\gamma)$ (for $\gamma>0$). Then
$$\mathrm{Re}(1-1/\rho)^n = \cos(n\theta_\gamma),$$
so the arithmetic oscillation under RH is a sum of pure cosines — bounded, oscillating — and cannot overcome $\frac n2\log n$. **This is a clean, self-contained verification that RH forces $\lambda_n\to+\infty$:** on RH,
$$\lambda_n = \frac n2\log n + O(n) + \underbrace{\sum_{\gamma>0} 2\cos(n\theta_\gamma)\cdot(\text{convergence factor})}_{\text{bounded oscillation}} > 0\ \text{eventually},$$
consistent with Li's theorem but importantly *circular* for proving RH.

If RH is false, some $\rho_0$ has $|1-1/\rho_0|=r>1$, contributing $\mathrm{Re}(1-1/\rho_0)^n \sim r^n\cos(n\phi)$, an **exponentially growing oscillation** that must eventually drive some $\lambda_n$ negative. This recovers Li's criterion geometrically and shows the entire content of RH is:

> **No zero produces an exponentially growing term $r^n$ with $r>1$ in $\sum_\rho(1-1/\rho)^n$.**

## What would close the gap (honest statement)

To prove RH one needs to show the sum $\sum_\rho(1-1/\rho)^n$ has *subexponential* growth. Equivalently, that the sequence $(\lambda_n)$ has generating function $\sum \lambda_n x^n = \frac{x}{1-x}\cdot\frac{d}{dx}\log\xi\!\left(\frac{1}{1-x}\right)$ analytic on $|x|<1$ with no pole penetrating inside — but this analyticity radius is itself equivalent to RH. I do **not** have a non-circular handle on the exponential rate $r=\max_\rho|1-1/\rho|$; controlling it requires a zero-free region better than any known, or a positivity input (Weil's criterion) that I cannot establish here.

## Summary of genuine content produced

1. Clean geometric identity: under RH, $1-1/\rho$ lies on the unit circle with explicit angle $\theta_\gamma=\pi-2\arctan(2\gamma)$, making $\lambda_n$'s arithmetic part a bounded cosine sum.
2. A rigorous argument that the remainder in the $\frac n2\log n$ asymptotic *cannot* be made effective without settling RH — sharpening *why* Li's criterion resists a finite verification.
3. Reduction of the full RH to the single statement $\max_\rho|1-1/\rho|\le1$, i.e. subexponential growth of $\sum_\rho(1-1/\rho)^n$.

None of this breaks the circularity; the exponential-rate control remains exactly as hard as RH.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Reframed RH via Li coefficients to a subexponential-growth condition and verified the archimedean asymptotic, but the core estimate stays circular.
