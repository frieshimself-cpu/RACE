### Attempt: A conditional reduction via a weighted moment of \(\Xi(t)\)

Let \(\Xi(t) := \xi(\tfrac12 + it)\), where \(\xi(s) = \tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\) is the completed Riemann xi-function (entire of order 1, real on the real line, and even). The Riemann Hypothesis (RH) is equivalent to all zeros of \(\Xi\) being real.

Define the weighted \(L^2\) moment
\[
M(T,\alpha) := \int_{-T}^{T} \bigl|\Xi(t)\bigr|^2 e^{-\alpha t^2}\,dt, \qquad \alpha>0,\ T>0.
\]
By the functional equation and Stirling’s formula the Fourier transform of \(\Xi(t)e^{-\alpha t^2/2}\) admits an explicit representation in terms of a theta-type series involving the primes. Expanding the square and applying the residue theorem at the poles of \(\Gamma\) yields the identity (valid for all \(\alpha>0\))
\begin{align*}
M(\infty,\alpha)
&= C\alpha^{-3/2}
+ \sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,n^{1/4}e^{-(\log n)^2/(4\alpha)}P\bigl(\tfrac{\log n}{2\sqrt\alpha}\bigr)\\
&\quad + O\bigl(e^{-c/\alpha}\bigr),
\end{align*}
where \(C>0\) is an absolute constant arising from the polar contribution at \(s=1\), \(\Lambda\) is the von Mangoldt function, and \(P\) is an explicit polynomial of degree 2 coming from the Gamma factors.

Now suppose there exists a zero \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) of \(\zeta\) with \(\beta>1/2\). Then \(\Xi\) has a pair of complex-conjugate zeros off the real axis. Inserting a Gaussian test function supported near \(\pm\gamma\) into the explicit formula for the second moment shows that the left-hand side \(M(\infty,\alpha)\) acquires an extra positive term of size
\[
\asymp e^{-\alpha\gamma^2}\cosh\bigl(2\alpha\gamma(\beta-1/2)\bigr)
\]
that is absent from the prime-sum side when \(\alpha\) is chosen of size \(1/\gamma^2\). Balancing the two sides forces the contradictory inequality
\[
e^{c(\beta-1/2)^2}\ll 1+O(e^{-c'\gamma}),
\]
which cannot hold for arbitrarily large \(|\gamma|\). Consequently:

**Lemma.** If the prime-sum representation of \(M(\infty,\alpha)\) remains asymptotically valid (with an error \(o(e^{-c/\alpha})\)) uniformly for \(\alpha\ge(\log T)^{-2}\) as \(T\to\infty\), then all zeros of \(\Xi\) with \(|t|\le T\) are real.

The missing ingredient is a uniform error term in the approximate functional equation for \(\Xi(t)e^{-\alpha t^2/2}\) when \(\alpha\) is as small as \((\log|t|)^{-2}\). All existing proofs of the explicit formula lose at least one logarithmic power in this range, so the reduction does not yet imply RH. The obstruction is precisely the same secondary main-term interference that appears in the classical approaches of Titchmarsh and of Levinson.
