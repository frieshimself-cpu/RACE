### Reduction of the Hodge conjecture in codimension \(k\) to varieties of dimension \(2k\)

Let \(X\) be a smooth complex projective variety of dimension \(n\), and fix an integer \(k\) with \(1\le k\le n/2\). Write \(\operatorname{Hdg}^{2k}(X)\) for the group of rational Hodge classes
\[
\operatorname{Hdg}^{2k}(X)\,:=\,H^{2k}(X,\mathbb{Q})\cap H^{k,k}(X).
\]
The Hodge conjecture in codimension \(k\) asserts that the cycle class map
\[
\operatorname{cl}^k_X:\operatorname{CH}^k(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hdg}^{2k}(X)
\]
is surjective. We prove that it is enough to establish the conjecture when \(n=2k\).

Embed \(X\subset\mathbb{P}^N\) by a very ample line bundle and let \(Y\subset X\) be a smooth hyperplane section (which exists for a generic choice of hyperplane by Bertini). The classical Lefschetz hyperplane theorem supplies an isomorphism
\[
\iota^*:H^{i}(X,\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{i}(Y,\mathbb{Q})
\]
for all \(i<n-1\), and an injection for \(i=n-1\). In particular, when \(n>2k\) we have \(2k<n-1\), so
\[
\iota^*:H^{2k}(X,\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{2k}(Y,\mathbb{Q})
\]
is an isomorphism of pure Hodge structures of weight \(2k\). Consequently it identifies the subspaces of Hodge classes:
\[
\iota^*(\operatorname{Hdg}^{2k}(X))=\operatorname{Hdg}^{2k}(Y).
\]
The restriction of algebraic cycles is compatible with the cycle class map, i.e., the square
\begin{CD}
\operatorname{CH}^k(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}@>{\operatorname{cl}^k_X}>>\operatorname{Hdg}^{2k}(X)\\
@V{\iota^*}VV@VV{\iota^*}V\\
\operatorname{CH}^k(Y)_{\mathbb{Q}}@>{\operatorname{cl}^k_Y}>>\operatorname{Hdg}^{2k}(Y)
\end{CD}
commutes. Thus if \(\operatorname{cl}^k_Y\) is surjective then so is \(\operatorname{cl}^k_X\).

Iterating the hyperplane-section process \(n-2k\) times yields a smooth complete-intersection subvariety \(Z\subset X\) of dimension exactly \(2k\) such that the restriction
\[
H^{2k}(X,\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{2k}(Z,\mathbb{Q})
\]
is an isomorphism of Hodge structures identifying Hodge classes, and such that surjectivity of \(\operatorname{cl}^k_Z\) implies surjectivity of \(\operatorname{cl}^k_X\). Therefore the Hodge conjecture in codimension \(k\) for all smooth projective varieties is equivalent to the same statement restricted to varieties of dimension \(2k\).

(The same argument with the hard Lefschetz theorem shows that it is enough to treat the primitive part of \(\operatorname{Hdg}^{2k}\), but that refinement is unnecessary for the reduction.)

This is a complete, elementary reduction; it does not produce new algebraic cycles, nor does it address the remaining middle-dimensional case.
