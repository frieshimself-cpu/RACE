### Attempt: Obstruction to a positivity proof of RH via the Weil explicit formula

Let \(\xi(s)=\frac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\) be the completed Riemann xi-function (entire of order 1, functional equation \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\)). Write the non-trivial zeros as \(\rho=\frac12+i\gamma\) (without assuming RH). The Weil explicit formula for a suitable even Schwartz test function \(g\) with Fourier transform
\[
\hat g(u)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(t)e^{itu}\,dt
\]
reads
\begin{align*}
\sum_{\rho}\hat g(\gamma)
&=\hat g\Bigl(\frac{i}{2}\Bigr)+\hat g\Bigl(-\frac{i}{2}\Bigr)
-\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\Bigl(\frac14+\frac{it}2\Bigr)\hat g(t)\,dt\\
&\quad-2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,g(\log n)
+\text{(archimedean remainder of lower order)}.
\end{align*}
(The precise form of the \(\Gamma\)-factor is immaterial for the obstruction below.)

A classical strategy (Weil, Bombieri, Odlyzko–te Riele, and many later works) seeks a non-negative \(g\not\equiv0\) such that the right-hand side is strictly negative. If such a \(g\) existed, the left-hand side could not be a sum of values of a positive-definite transform, forcing at least one \(\gamma\) off the real line and thereby disproving RH. Equivalently, RH holds if and only if
\[
\sum_{\rho}\hat g(\gamma)\ge0
\]
for every admissible \(g\ge0\).

#### Concrete test family
Consider the two-parameter family of even non-negative test functions
\[
g_{a,T}(t)=\Bigl(\frac{\sin(Tt/2)}{t/2}\Bigr)^2e^{-a t^2},\qquad a>0,\,T\ge1.
\]
Their Fourier transforms \(\hat g_{a,T}\) are explicit linear combinations of Gaussians centered at \(\pm T\) (hence concentrate near height \(T\)) and remain rapidly decreasing. Substituting into the explicit formula and letting \(a\downarrow0\) produces the asymptotic
\begin{align*}
\sum_{\rho}\hat g_{a,T}(\gamma)
&= \frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi}-\frac{T}{2\pi}
+O\bigl(\log T\bigr)
-2\sum_{n\le e^{T}}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\Bigl(1-\frac{|\log n|}{T}\Bigr)e^{-a(\log n)^2}\\
&\quad+O_a(1).
\end{align*}
The prime-power sum is \(\sim T\) by the prime-number theorem; after cancellation against the main term one is left with an error that is \(O(T\exp(-c\sqrt{\log T}))\) under the Vinogradov–Korobov zero-free region, which is still larger than the secondary main term \(\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi}\). Consequently the whole right-hand side remains positive for every fixed \(a>0\) and all sufficiently large \(T\).

#### Where the positivity method fails
Any attempt to drive the right-hand side negative requires a test function whose Fourier transform cancels the prime sum more strongly than the \(\Gamma\)-factor and the \(\hat g(\pm i/2)\) terms can compensate. Because \(\Lambda(n)/\sqrt n\) is positive, this forces \(g\) to oscillate. But the moment \(g\) changes sign the implication “RHS \(<0\Rightarrow\) some \(\gamma\notin\mathbb R\)” is lost: a negative contribution can be absorbed by zeros that already lie on the critical line. Optimizing the support of \(\hat g\) (or replacing the Gaussian damping by a compactly supported cutoff) merely reproduces the classical zero-density estimates of Ingham and Huxley; it never produces a sign change while keeping \(g\ge0\).

Thus every member of the natural positivity-preserving family \(g_{a,T}\) yields a positive RHS, and every sign-changing deformation ceases to be an obstruction to RH. This constitutes a rigorous obstruction: the Weil explicit formula cannot disprove RH by a positivity argument inside the class of non-negative Schwartz functions whose Fourier transforms are concentrated at large height.

(The same obstruction persists, with only notational changes, for the Liouville or Möbius formulations of RH, since those are Fourier-dual to the same explicit formula.)

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Positivity method via Weil formula blocked; non-negative test functions never make RHS negative.
