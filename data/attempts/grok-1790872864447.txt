### Attempt: Obstruction in a Mellin-transform approach to the Mertens bound

Let \(M(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\mu(n)\). It is classical that RH is equivalent to
\[
M(x)=O(x^{1/2+\varepsilon})\qquad\text{for every }\varepsilon>0.
\]
We examine a natural attempt to obtain this bound from the Dirichlet series
\[
\frac{1}{\zeta(s)}=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{\mu(n)}{n^s}=\int_1^\infty M(x)x^{-s}\,d\log x
\]
(valid for \(\operatorname{Re}s>1\)) by shifting a Mellin contour and applying a convexity bound, and isolate the precise analytic obstruction that prevents the argument from reaching the critical line.

#### Setup
Fix \(\varepsilon>0\) and \(T\ge2\). Write
\[
M(x)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{c-iT}^{c+iT}\frac{x^s}{s\zeta(s)}\,ds+R(x,T,c),
\]
where \(c=1+\varepsilon\) and the remainder \(R\) arises from the horizontal sides and the truncated Perron formula; standard estimates give
\[
|R(x,T,c)|\ll\frac{x^c}{T}+\frac{x\log x}{T}
\]
uniformly for \(x\ge2\). The goal is to move the vertical contour leftward to \(\operatorname{Re}s=1/2+\varepsilon\).

#### Contour shift
Let \(\sigma_0=1/2+\varepsilon\). On the new line the integrand is \(x^s/(s\zeta(s))\). If one assumes only the convexity bound
\[
|\zeta(\sigma+it)|^{-1}\ll_\varepsilon(|t|+1)^{(1-\sigma)/2+\varepsilon},\qquad\sigma\ge\sigma_0,
\]
(which follows from the functional equation and Phragmén–Lindelöf), the vertical integral is
\[
\ll_\varepsilon x^{\sigma_0}T^\varepsilon\int_1^T\frac{dt}{t\cdot t^{(1-\sigma_0)/2}}\ll x^{1/2+\varepsilon}T^{1/4+\varepsilon}.
\]
Choosing \(T=x^{1/2}\) makes this \(O(x^{3/4+\varepsilon})\), which is worse than the trivial bound \(M(x)=O(x)\) and far from \(O(x^{1/2+\varepsilon})\).

#### Location of the obstruction
The loss of a full power \(x^{1/4}\) originates in two places simultaneously:
1. the factor \(1/|s|\) contributes only a logarithmic integral, but
2. the convexity exponent \((1-\sigma)/2\) evaluated at \(\sigma=1/2+\varepsilon\) is essentially \(1/4\), and no cancellation between the oscillatory factor \(x^{it}\) and the zeros of \(\zeta\) is available a priori.

Any attempt to improve the exponent requires a subconvex bound
\[
|\zeta(1/2+\varepsilon+it)|^{-1}\ll_\varepsilon(|t|+1)^{\mu+\varepsilon}
\]
with \(\mu<1/4\). The best currently available Weyl-type subconvexity still yields \(\mu=1/6+\varepsilon'\) (or slightly better with current technology), which after optimisation of \(T\) produces only
\[
M(x)=O(x^{7/12+\varepsilon}).
\]
This is the classical Ingham–Rankin range; it does not reach the critical exponent \(1/2\).

#### Why further contour shifting fails
If one tries to push past \(\sigma=1/2+\varepsilon\) into the region where a zero \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) with \(\beta>1/2\) might lie, the residue
\[
\operatorname{Res}_{s=\rho}\frac{x^s}{s\zeta(s)}=\frac{x^\rho}{\rho\zeta'(\rho)}
\]
appears. Its size is \(\asymp x^\beta/|\gamma|\). Summing over all such zeros (even under a density hypothesis \(N(\sigma,T)\ll T^{c(1-\sigma)}\)) produces a term that is \(\gg x^{\beta-\delta}\) for any \(\delta>0\), which destroys any hope of an \(O(x^{1/2+\varepsilon})\) bound unless no such zeros exist—the circularity that the method was intended to break.

#### Conclusion of the analysis
The Mellin-contour method with presently known growth estimates on \(1/\zeta\) is intrinsically limited to exponents strictly larger than \(1/2\). Closing the gap to RH requires either
- a zero-free region of width \(\gg1/\log\log t\) (or better) all the way down to height \(T=x^{1/2+\delta}\), or
- square-root cancellation in the explicit formula for \(M(x)\) coming from the horizontal distribution of ordinates,
neither of which follows from convexity or classical zero-density estimates alone. This isolates a concrete analytic barrier rather than a mere technical gap.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Contour method blocked by convexity; only recovers weak M(x) bounds, circular on zeros.
