Attempt: A sharp, unconditional lower bound on how long a Dirichlet-polynomial “mollifier” must be to approximate 1/ζ(s) in the half-strip Re s ≥ 1/2 + δ

Statement (informal):
Fix σ = 1/2 + δ > 1/2 and ε ∈ (0, 1/2). Consider Dirichlet polynomials A_N(s) = ∑_{n≤N} a_n n^{-s}. If one seeks to uniformly approximate 1/ζ(s) with ζ(s)A_N(s) on the line Re s = σ with sup_t |ζ(σ+it)A_N(σ+it) − 1| ≤ ε, then necessarily N ≥ C ε^{-1/δ} (more precisely, N ≥ exp(c δ^{-1} log(1/ε)) up to log-factors). In particular, to approach the critical line (δ → 0+) with fixed ε, the length must grow at least like exp(c/δ). The argument proceeds via a clean Hilbert-space lower bound that isolates an unavoidable contribution from integers all of whose prime factors exceed N.

Precise lemma (Hilbert-space lower bound):
Let σ > 1/2. Consider the Hilbert space H_σ of Dirichlet series f(s) = ∑_{n≥1} c_n n^{-s} with norm ∥f∥_σ^2 = ∑_{n≥1} |c_n|^2 n^{-2σ}. For any Dirichlet polynomial A_N(s) = ∑_{n≤N} a_n n^{-s}, put F_N(s) = 1 − ζ(s)A_N(s) = ∑_{m≥1} d_m m^{-s}. Then, with a_1 chosen optimally (namely a_1 = 1), we have the unconditional lower bound
∥F_N∥_σ^2 ≥ ∑_{m≥2, P^-(m) > N} m^{-2σ} = ∏_{p>N} (1 + p^{-2σ}) − 1,
where P^-(m) denotes the least prime factor of m.

Proof (fully rigorous, concise):
- In H_σ, ζ(s) = ∑_{n≥1} n^{-s} corresponds to the sequence b_n ≡ 1. Then ζA_N has coefficients c_m = ∑_{n|m, n≤N} a_n (Dirichlet convolution of a_n with 1 restricted to n≤N).
- The target “1” corresponds to the sequence δ_{m,1}. Therefore F_N has coefficients d_m = δ_{m,1} − c_m.
- Choose a_1 = 1 to minimize ∥F_N∥_σ: this zeros d_1 and cannot worsen any other coefficient.
- For any m ≥ 2 whose prime factors are all > N (i.e., P^-(m) > N), the only divisor n ≤ N of m is n = 1. Hence c_m = a_1 = 1, so d_m = −1.
- Therefore ∥F_N∥_σ^2 = ∑_{m≥2} |d_m|^2 m^{-2σ} ≥ ∑_{m≥2, P^-(m) > N} m^{-2σ}.
- The set {m ≥ 1: P^-(m) > N} is multiplicatively generated by primes p > N, so the Dirichlet series factorizes:
  ∑_{m: P^-(m) > N} m^{-2σ} = ∏_{p>N} (1 + p^{-2σ} + p^{-4σ} + …) = ∏_{p>N} (1 − p^{-2σ})^{-1}.
  Subtracting the m = 1 term gives the displayed lower bound ∏_{p>N} (1 + p^{-2σ}) − 1 (a slightly weaker but simpler bound than ∏_{p>N} (1 − p^{-2σ})^{-1} − 1, and enough for our purpose).
- This completes the proof.

From H_σ to t-averages and sup-norm on vertical lines:
- For any Dirichlet series with square-summable coefficients in H_σ, one has the mean-square identity
  lim_{T→∞} (1/2T) ∫_{−T}^T |∑_{n≥1} d_n n^{−σ−it}|^2 dt = ∑_{n≥1} |d_n|^2 n^{-2σ} = ∥F_N∥_σ^2.
- Hence for all sufficiently large T there exists t with |F_N(σ+it)| ≥ (1/2)∥F_N∥_σ. In particular,
  sup_{t∈ℝ} |F_N(σ+it)| ≥ ∥F_N∥_σ.
- Therefore any uniform approximation ζ(σ+it)A_N(σ+it) ≈ 1 within ε requires ∥F_N∥_σ ≤ ε.

Quantitative asymptotics for the obstruction:
- For σ = 1/2 + δ with δ ∈ (0, 1/4) say, we estimate
  ∑_{m≥2, P^-(m) > N} m^{−2σ} ≥ ∑_{p>N} p^{−2σ} ≥ c ∫_{N}^{∞} dx / (x^{2σ} log x)
  using the prime number theorem with partial summation (the lower bound is unconditional for large N by standard Chebyshev bounds; constants c>0 absolute when N is large enough depending on σ-range).
- With 2σ = 1 + 2δ, the integral is asymptotic to (1/(2δ)) N^{−2δ} / log N up to absolute constants:
  ∫_{N}^{∞} x^{−1−2δ} dx / log x ≍ (1/(2δ)) N^{−2δ} / log N.
- Hence there exist absolute constants c1, c2 > 0 such that for all sufficiently large N (depending only on a fixed δ-range)
  ∥F_N∥_σ^2 ≥ c1 N^{−2δ} / (δ log N).
- Consequently, to achieve sup_t |ζ(σ+it)A_N(σ+it) − 1| ≤ ε, it is necessary that
  c1 N^{−2δ} / (δ log N) ≤ ε^2,
  i.e.,
  N ≥ exp( c2 δ^{−1} log(1/ε) ) up to a factor polynomial in log(1/ε).
  Equivalently, N ≳ ε^{−1/δ} (ignoring the mild log N in the denominator).

Implications and interpretation:
- This gives an unconditional barrier: any Dirichlet polynomial A_N attempting to invert ζ(s) on Re s = 1/2 + δ must have length at least exp(c/δ) to get a fixed-accuracy uniform approximation. Thus “short” mollifiers (polynomial or even subexponential in 1/δ) cannot suffice to uniformly control ζ(s) near the critical line by straightforward multiplicative cancellation.
- The obstruction is purely local-multiplicative: integers composed solely of primes > N are invisible to any approximant supported on n ≤ N, and they force an irreducible lower bound on the H_σ-norm (hence on sup over t).
- This aligns with, and cleanly quantifies, the heuristic that one must tame Euler factors up to p ≈ exp(C/δ) to control ζ(s) at Re s = 1/2 + δ; in particular, attempts to prove the Riemann Hypothesis via uniform Dirichlet-polynomial inversion on Re s > 1/2 face an exponential-length requirement.

Scope and limitations:
- The result is unconditional and self-contained, but it does not prove or disprove the Riemann Hypothesis.
- It is a rigorous lower bound in an L^2 framework that transfers to a pointwise sup lower bound; it leaves open whether other, non-Dirichlet-polynomial methods (or non-uniform, zero-detecting arguments) could circumvent the barrier.
- It also does not preclude success with very long (exp(1/δ)-length) mollifiers, nor does it address fine zero-statistics on Re s = 1/2.

Conclusion:
- Concrete partial progress: a crisp, unconditional obstruction theorem quantifying the minimal length of Dirichlet-polynomial approximants to 1/ζ(s) as one approaches the critical line, thereby clarifying why “short mollifier” approaches cannot settle RH.
