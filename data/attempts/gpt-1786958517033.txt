Attempt: A clean reduction of the Hodge conjecture in degree 2k on n-folds to the middle-degree case on 2k-fold hyperplane complete intersections, together with a primitive reduction via hard Lefschetz

Setup: Let X be a smooth projective complex variety of dimension n, fix 1 ≤ k ≤ n, and write Hdg^{2k}(X, Q) for the Q-vector space of rational Hodge classes in H^{2k}(X, Q).

Lemma 1 (Weak Lefschetz injection in degree 2k). Let Y ⊂ X be a smooth complete intersection of r = n − 2k very ample divisors (so dim Y = 2k). Then the restriction map res: H^{2k}(X, Q) → H^{2k}(Y, Q) is injective.
Proof. By the weak Lefschetz hyperplane theorem and its iteration for complete intersections, the restriction is an isomorphism for cohomological degrees < dim Y and injective in degree = dim Y. Since 2k = dim Y, res is injective.

Lemma 2 (Reduction to middle dimension on complete intersections). Fix X and k as above, and choose Y as in Lemma 1. Assume the Hodge conjecture holds on Y in degree 2k, i.e., every class in Hdg^{2k}(Y, Q) is algebraic. Then the Hodge conjecture holds for X in degree 2k.
Proof. Let α ∈ Hdg^{2k}(X, Q). Its restriction β := res(α) ∈ Hdg^{2k}(Y, Q) is algebraic by assumption: there exists a codimension-k algebraic cycle Z ⊂ Y with cl_Y(Z) = β. View Z as a codimension-k cycle on X via inclusion i: Y ↪ X. Its cohomology class cl_X(Z) ∈ H^{2k}(X, Q) restricts to cl_Y(Z) = β. Thus res(cl_X(Z) − α) = 0. By Lemma 1, res is injective, hence cl_X(Z) = α. Therefore α is algebraic.

Corollary 3 (Global reduction to middle dimension). The Hodge conjecture on all smooth projective n-folds in degree 2k reduces to the Hodge conjecture for smooth projective varieties of dimension 2k in their middle cohomology.
Proof. Given any X, choose Y as above. Apply Lemma 2. Since any smooth projective 2k-fold occurs (up to birationally very ample embedding and successive general hyperplane sections) as such a Y in some ambient X, this yields a global reduction statement: it suffices to prove the conjecture for all smooth projective varieties of dimension 2k in degree 2k.

Primitive refinement

Let h ∈ H^{2}(X, Q) be the hyperplane class of a very ample embedding. Define the primitive subspace P^{2k}(X, Q) = ker(L^{n−2k+1}: H^{2k}(X, Q) → H^{2n−2k+2}(X, Q)), where L denotes cup product with h.

Lemma 4 (Projection to primitive and inductive reduction in k). Suppose:
- For all smooth projective varieties of dimension 2k, every primitive rational Hodge class in H^{2k} is algebraic (Primitive Hodge in middle degree 2k), and
- For all smooth projective varieties and all j < k, the Hodge conjecture holds in degree 2j.
Then the Hodge conjecture holds in degree 2k for all smooth projective varieties.
Proof. For any X and α ∈ Hdg^{2k}(X, Q), decompose α = ∑_{t≥0} h^{t} ∪ α_t with α_t primitive in H^{2k−2t}(X, Q) (Lefschetz decomposition). By Hodge-Riemann, each α_t is a Hodge class. For t ≥ 1, h^{t}∪α_t is the class of the algebraic cycle obtained by intersecting an algebraic representative of α_t (which exists by the inductive hypothesis for degree 2k−2t) with t general hyperplanes. Thus algebraicity of α reduces to the algebraicity of the primitive part α_0 ∈ P^{2k}(X, Q). By Lemma 2, it suffices to verify algebraicity of primitive middle-degree classes on smooth 2k-fold complete intersections, i.e., the assumed Primitive Hodge in middle degree 2k. Therefore α is algebraic.

Theorem 5 (Complete reduction). To prove the Hodge conjecture in all degrees on all smooth projective varieties, it is enough to prove the following statement for each k ≥ 1:
Every primitive rational Hodge class on every smooth projective 2k-fold is algebraic.
Proof. Combine Lemma 4 (induction on k, anchored at k = 1 by Lefschetz (1,1) for divisors) with Lemma 2 (reduction to middle dimension on complete intersections).

Remarks on scope and sharpness
- Lemma 2 is unconditional and uses only standard Lefschetz theorems; it isolates the essential difficulty to primitive middle cohomology in middle dimension.
- The reduction strictly strengthens the usual folklore “reduce to middle degree” by additionally peeling off nonprimitive contributions via actual algebraic intersections with hyperplanes.
- For k = 1, the base case is settled by Lefschetz (1,1). For k ≥ 2, the reduction pinpoints the remaining obstruction to the primitive part in middle degree, which matches the location where normal function techniques and Noether–Lefschetz methods must engage.

Conclusion
This attempt rigorously establishes a clean, fully self-contained reduction: proving Hodge for primitive middle-degree classes on 2k-folds suffices for the general Hodge conjecture in degree 2k on all n-folds, and, by induction on k, for all degrees. While this does not solve the conjecture, it isolates and simplifies the target in a way directly usable for focused attacks (e.g., via variations of Hodge structure, normal functions, or absolute Hodge classes) on a minimal class of varieties and cohomology.
