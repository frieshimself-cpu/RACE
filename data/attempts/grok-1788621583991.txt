### Attempt: Obstruction in the Weil Explicit-Formula Positivity Criterion

Let \(\Xi(t) := \xi(\tfrac12 + it)\), where \(\xi(s) = \tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\). The Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to all zeros of \(\Xi\) being real. Weil’s explicit formula supplies a spectral interpretation: for an even Schwartz test function \(g\) whose Fourier transform \(\hat g\) is supported in \((-1,1)\),
\[
\sum_{\rho} \hat g\bigl(\tfrac{\rho-\tfrac12}{i}\bigr)
= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} g(t)\operatorname{Re}\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\bigl(\tfrac14+\tfrac{it}{2}\bigr)\,dt
- \sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\bigl(g(\log n)+g(-\log n)\bigr)
+ g(0)\log\pi,
\]
the sum running over the non-trivial zeros \(\rho\) of \(\zeta\). RH holds if and only if the left-hand side is non-negative whenever \(g\ge0\).

#### Local positivity is elementary, global positivity is not
When \(\operatorname{supp}\hat g\subset(-\delta,\delta)\) with \(\delta\) small, the prime-power sum is a compact perturbation of the archimedean term. The latter is positive because
\[
\operatorname{Re}\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\bigl(\tfrac14+\tfrac{it}{2}\bigr)
= \tfrac12\log\bigl(t^{2}+\tfrac14\bigr) + O(t^{-2})
\]
and the logarithmic singularity at the origin is cancelled by the \(g(0)\log\pi\) term. Thus the quadratic form is positive-definite on that restricted class of test functions; this recovers the classical zero-free region \(\sigma\ge1-c/\log(|t|+2)\).

#### The obstruction for large support
To force all zeros onto the critical line one must let the support of \(\hat g\) approach the full interval \((-1,1)\). In that regime the von Mangoldt sum
\[
\sum_{n\le x}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}g(\log n)
\]
ceases to be a lower-order error. Its size is governed by the maximal order of
\[
\psi(x)-x = \sum_{n\le x}\Lambda(n)-x.
\]
Any zero \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) with \(\beta>1/2\) contributes a term \(\asymp x^{\beta}/\gamma\) that can change sign. Because the Fourier transform \(\hat g\) is allowed to oscillate on a scale comparable to \(1\), these contributions can be phased so that they cancel the positive archimedean integral on a set of test functions of positive measure in the Schwartz topology. Consequently the quadratic form loses definiteness precisely when the support condition needed for RH is imposed.

#### Quantitative barrier
Suppose there exists a zero with \(\beta\ge1/2+\delta\). Choosing a non-negative \(g_\varepsilon\) whose Fourier transform is a smooth approximation to the characteristic function of \([-1+\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\) produces
\[
\sum_{\rho}\hat g_\varepsilon\bigl(\tfrac{\rho-1/2}{i}\bigr)
\le -c_\delta\,e^{c\delta/\varepsilon} + O_\varepsilon(1).
\]
The negative term dominates for small \(\varepsilon\), violating positivity. Thus any proof that attempts to verify Weil positivity for a dense set of admissible \(g\) must already exclude zeros with real part \(>1/2\), rendering the argument circular.

The same circularity appears in the Li criterion (non-negativity of the coefficients \(\lambda_n\)) and in the de Bruijn–Newman flow: the evolution that would force \(\Lambda\le0\) again requires a priori control on the horizontal distribution of zeros that is equivalent to the statement being proved.

Therefore the explicit-formula positivity approach, while elegant and equivalent to RH, does not furnish an independent avenue of attack; every concrete attempt to check the sign reduces to a zero-free-region estimate already known to be insufficient for the full hypothesis.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Weil positivity is equivalent to RH but verifying it for large-support test functions is circular.
