### Attempt: Obstruction in a naive positivity argument for the Weil explicit formula

Let \(\Lambda\) denote the von Mangoldt function and write the Chebyshev function \(\psi(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\Lambda(n)\). The classical explicit formula (in integrated form, for \(x>1\) not a prime power) reads
\[
\psi(x)=x-\sum_{\rho}\frac{x^{\rho}}{\rho}-\log(2\pi)-\frac12\log(1-x^{-2}),
\]
where the sum runs over the non-trivial zeros of \(\zeta\) (counted with multiplicity) and is understood in the sense of symmetric partial sums \(\lim_{T\to\infty}\sum_{|\operatorname{Im}\rho|<T}\).

Weil’s reformulation associates to a suitable even test function \(g\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})\) the distributional identity
\[
\sum_{\rho}\hat g\bigl(\tfrac{\rho-1/2}{i}\bigr)
=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(t)\,d\bigl(e^{t/2}+e^{-t/2}\bigr)
-\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\bigl(g(\log n)+g(-\log n)\bigr)
+\text{(archimedean Gamma contribution)}.
\]
The Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to the assertion that the left-hand side is non-negative whenever \(g\) is a square of a real Schwartz function whose Fourier transform is supported in \((-1,1)\) (Li’s criterion is the special case of monomials).  

A natural attempt to prove positivity proceeds by discarding the oscillatory prime sum and retaining only the “diagonal” archimedean and trivial-zero contributions, which are manifestly positive for \(g\ge0\).  The error committed is precisely
\[
E(g):=\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}g(\log n).
\]
If one could show \(E(g)\le C\|g\|_{L^1}\) with an absolute constant small enough to be absorbed by the archimedean term, RH would follow for all such \(g\).  

However, the prime-number theorem with classical zero-free region only yields
\[
\sum_{n\le X}\Lambda(n)=X+O\bigl(X\exp(-c\sqrt{\log X})\bigr).
\]
Partial summation then gives, for \(g\) supported in \([-T,T]\),
\[
|E(g)|\ll\|g\|_\infty\,T\exp(-c\sqrt T)+|\hat g(0)|\cdot o(1).
\]
The exponential decay is too weak to dominate the linear growth of the archimedean factor \(\asymp\|g\|_1\cdot e^{T/2}\) once \(T\) is large; the same obstruction appears for any zero-free region of the shape \(1-c/(\log|t|)^\alpha\) with \(\alpha<1\).  Strengthening the zero-free region far enough to close the gap is circular: it already implies a zero-density estimate strong enough to force all zeros onto the critical line.  

Thus the positivity argument cannot be closed by any presently available estimate on primes in short intervals or on zero-free regions; a genuinely new cancellation (or a different class of test functions) is required.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Classical zero-free regions leave an irreparable gap in the Weil positivity estimate; no new cancellation found.
