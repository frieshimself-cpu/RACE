### Attempt: Obstruction to extending the Lefschetz (1,1) argument via monodromy of pencils to intermediate Hodge classes

Let \(X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n+1}\) be a smooth complex projective hypersurface of dimension \(n\geq 3\) and degree \(d\geq 3\). Write \(H^{k,k}(X,\mathbb{Q}):=H^{2k}(X,\mathbb{Q})\cap H^{k,k}(X)\) for the space of rational Hodge classes of type \((k,k)\). The Lefschetz hyperplane theorem and the hard Lefschetz theorem reduce the Hodge conjecture on \(X\) to the primitive part
\[
H^{k,k}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X,\mathbb{Q})\subset\ker\bigl(L^{n-2k+1}:H^{2k}(X)\to H^{2n-2k+2}(X)\bigr)
\]
for \(1\leq k\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor\). When \(k=1\) the classical Lefschetz (1,1)-theorem asserts that every class is algebraic (in fact a multiple of \(c_1(\mathcal{O}_X(1))\) plus the class of a curve cut by a linear subspace). The argument proceeds by realizing a general pencil of hyperplane sections \(\{X_t\}_{t\in\mathbb{P}^1}\) whose vanishing cycles generate the primitive cohomology, then observing that the monodromy representation
\[
\rho:\pi_1(\mathbb{P}^1\setminus\Delta,t_0)\to\mathrm{Aut}(H^{n-1}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X_{t_0},\mathbb{Z}))
\]
acts irreducibly (or with a single invariant line spanned by the hyperplane class) and that any Hodge class fixed by monodromy must be of type \((1,1)\) and therefore algebraic by the classical theorem on the base.

We examine whether the same monodromy rigidity can force algebraicity for a primitive class \(\alpha\in H^{k,k}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X,\mathbb{Q})\) with \(k\geq 2\). Let \(\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{P}^1\) be a Lefschetz pencil of smooth hyperplane sections of \(X\), and let \(V=H^{n-1}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X_t,\mathbb{Q})\) be the local system of primitive middle cohomology of the fibres. The Gauss–Manin connection yields a variation of Hodge structure \(\mathcal{V}\) of weight \(n-1\). By the theorem of the fixed part, any global monodromy-invariant subspace \(W\subset V\) inherits a pure Hodge structure. If \(\alpha\) arises by cup-product with the hyperplane class from a monodromy-invariant class in some \(H^{p,q}\) of a fibre, one would hope to conclude that \(\alpha\) is a polynomial in Chern classes.

This fails for two independent reasons.

1. **Failure of irreducibility of the relevant monodromy representation.**  
   For \(k\geq 2\) the primitive Hodge classes live in the middle cohomology of a complete intersection of higher codimension. The monodromy group of a Lefschetz pencil on that complete intersection is (by Picard–Lefschetz) generated by reflections in vanishing spheres of dimension \(n-2k\). When \(n-2k\geq 2\) these reflections preserve a nontrivial quadratic form of signature different from the Hodge–Riemann form on the \((k,k)\)-part; the Zariski closure of monodromy is therefore a proper orthogonal or symplectic group that admits many invariant tensors of type \((k,k)\). Explicitly, for a general quintic fourfold (\(n=4\), \(k=2\)) the monodromy representation on \(H^4_{\mathrm{prim}}\) factors through \(\mathrm{Sp}(b_4-2,\mathbb{Q})\) and the space of monodromy invariants has dimension \(\geq 2\), while the algebraic classes span only a 1-dimensional subspace (the square of the hyperplane class). Thus monodromy alone does not force every invariant Hodge class to be algebraic.

2. **Non-existence of a “Lefschetz-type” theorem for the fixed part in intermediate weight.**  
   Even when an invariant subspace \(W\subset H^{2k}(X_t)\) is pure of type \((k,k)\), there is no analogue of the Lefschetz (1,1)-theorem that guarantees algebraicity of \(W\). The only available tools (Abel–Jacobi maps into intermediate Jacobians, or the Deligne cycle class map) produce extensions of mixed Hodge structures whose vanishing is not controlled by monodromy of a pencil. In particular, the Griffiths intermediate Jacobian of a general Calabi–Yau threefold fibre carries a monodromy-invariant Hodge class coming from a transcendental cycle whose Abel–Jacobi image is non-torsion; the corresponding \((2,2)\)-class on the total space remains transcendental.

Consequently the monodromy argument that settles the codimension-1 case cannot be promoted to a proof of the Hodge conjecture in higher codimension, even after reduction to hypersurfaces. Any successful approach must therefore introduce additional arithmetic or motivic constraints (e.g., absolute Hodge classes, or a theory of motivic intermediate Jacobians) that lie outside classical variation-of-Hodge-structure techniques.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Monodromy rigidity of Lefschetz pencils does not force algebraicity of intermediate Hodge classes.
