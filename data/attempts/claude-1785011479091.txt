# Attempt: A structural constraint via the Li–Keiper coefficients

I'll make a self-contained attempt centered on the Li criterion, prove a
correct partial result rigorously, and then isolate precisely where the
argument cannot be closed — which is itself informative.

## Setup

Let ξ(s) = ½ s(s-1)π^{-s/2}Γ(s/2)ζ(s) be the completed zeta function, entire
of order 1, satisfying ξ(s) = ξ(1-s), with zeros exactly the nontrivial zeros
ρ of ζ. Define the Li coefficients

  λ_n = (1/(n-1)!) d^n/ds^n [ s^{n-1} log ξ(s) ] |_{s=1},  n ≥ 1.

**Li's criterion (assumed as our working framework):** RH ⟺ λ_n ≥ 0 for all
n ≥ 1. Equivalently, using the Hadamard product,

  λ_n = Σ_ρ [ 1 − (1 − 1/ρ)^n ],   (∗)

where the sum is over nontrivial zeros paired as ρ with 1−ρ (needed for
conditional convergence).

The pairing gives a useful real form. Write ρ = ½ + it with t possibly
complex (Im part = deviation from the line). Under the map w = 1 − 1/ρ, RH is
the statement Re(ρ) = ½, i.e. |w| = |1 − 1/ρ|. Note the key algebraic fact:

  |1 − 1/ρ| < 1  ⟺  Re(ρ) > ½,  and  |1 − 1/ρ| = 1 ⟺ Re(ρ) = ½.

Proof of this fact: 1 − 1/ρ has modulus < 1 iff |ρ − 1| < |ρ|, i.e. iff
(σ−1)² + t² < σ² + t² with ρ = σ+it, i.e. iff −2σ+1 < 0, i.e. σ > ½. □

So each zero contributes a point w_ρ inside, on, or outside the unit circle
according as Re(ρ) >, =, < ½. By the functional equation zeros come in pairs
(ρ, 1−ρ), and the images satisfy w_{1−ρ} = 1 − 1/(1−ρ) = -ρ/(1-ρ) = ... let
me record: if ρ↦w then 1−ρ ↦ 1 − 1/(1−ρ). One checks w_ρ · (1 − w_{1-ρ})^{-1}
relations, but the clean symmetric statement is:

**Lemma A.** For a conjugate/functional-equation quadruple
{ρ, \barρ, 1−ρ, 1−\barρ}, the four images w lie: all on the unit circle if
that quadruple is on the critical line; otherwise two strictly inside and two
strictly outside the unit circle.

*Proof.* Complex conjugation preserves |w|. The involution ρ↦1−ρ sends σ to
1−σ, flipping the sign of (σ−½), hence by the computation above sends "inside"
to "outside" and fixes the circle. So an off-line quadruple splits 2 inside /
2 outside; an on-line quadruple is entirely on the circle. □

## The partial positivity result

From (∗), pairing ρ with \barρ (a symmetric summation that converges),

  λ_n = Σ_{ρ} Re[ 1 − (1 − 1/ρ)^n ]  = Σ_ρ ( 1 − |w_ρ|^n cos(n θ_ρ) ),

where w_ρ = |w_ρ| e^{iθ_ρ}. Each summand is 1 − Re(w_ρ^n).

**Lemma B (contribution sign for on-line zeros).** If ρ is on the critical
line, |w_ρ| = 1, so the summand equals 1 − cos(nθ_ρ) ≥ 0, and this holds for
*every* n.

This is the crucial structural point: **critical-line zeros contribute
non-negatively to every λ_n.** So RH, if true, is "self-certifying" in Li's
sense term by term — no cancellation is needed. This is a genuine reason the
Li criterion is natural.

**Lemma C (n=1 unconditional positivity).** λ_1 > 0 unconditionally.

*Proof.* From (∗), λ_1 = Σ_ρ Re(1/ρ) (since 1 − (1−1/ρ) = 1/ρ). The known
closed form (differentiating log ξ at s=1, using
ξ'/ξ(1) = Σ 1/ρ symmetrized) gives

  λ_1 = 1 + ½γ − ½ log(4π) ≈ 1 + 0.2886 − 1.2655 = 0.0230 > 0,

where γ is Euler's constant. The value is standard and positive. □

Note λ_1 > 0 is consistent with — but does not imply — RH; it only says the
*first* moment of Re(1/ρ) is positive, i.e. zeros are on average not too far
right, which is automatic from the functional-equation symmetry Re(1/ρ) =
Re(1/(1-\barρ)) already forcing the symmetric average behavior.

## Where the argument cannot close (honest analysis)

The difficulty is entirely in the **off-line contribution**. Suppose, for
contradiction toward RH, there is an off-line quadruple giving images
w, w̄ inside (|w| = r < 1) and w', w̄' outside (|w'| = R > 1), with by Lemma A
a reciprocal-type relation between r and R. Its contribution to λ_n is

  c_n = (1 − Re w^n) + (1 − Re \bar w^n) + (1 − Re w'^n) + (1 − Re \bar w'^n)
      = 4 − 2 r^n cos(nα) − 2 R^n cos(nβ),

for angles α, β. Since R > 1, the term −2R^n cos(nβ) can be as negative as
−2R^n and *grows exponentially in n*. Thus a single off-line quadruple, taken
alone, drives some λ_n to −∞ as n→∞ (choosing n with cos(nβ) near +1, which
exists by equidistribution unless β is a rational multiple of π making it
periodic — and even then infinitely many n give cos(nβ) bounded away from
negative).

This is exactly the mechanism by which Li's criterion detects RH: an off-line
zero forces λ_n < 0 for infinitely many n. **However**, to convert this into a
proof of RH one must control the *sum over all zeros*: the exponentially
negative contribution of one bad quadruple must not be secretly cancelled by
the collective (also unbounded) positive contributions Σ(1 − Re w_ρ^n) of the
infinitely many on/near-line zeros, whose partial sums grow like
(n/2)log n via the density of zeros. Quantitatively:

  Σ_{on-line} (1 − cos nθ_ρ) ~ (main term ~ (n/2)(log n − 1 + log 2π) + ...),

while a single off-line quadruple contributes O(R^n). Because R^n dominates
(n log n) for large n whenever R > 1, **one off-line zero really does win**,
and this would give λ_n < 0. This is a correct sketch of the "easy" direction
(off-line ⇒ some λ_n<0). But it is *not new*, and the reverse — showing
positivity of all λ_n implies no such quadruple, rigorously handling the
conditional convergence and the competition of two unbounded quantities — is
precisely the content of RH and is not achieved here. The exponential-vs-
polynomial heuristic is not a proof because the rearrangement/convergence of
(∗) is only conditional, and one cannot legitimately isolate a single
quadruple's contribution to λ_n from the tail without an unconditional
uniform bound on zero density that is itself equivalent-strength to RH-type
input.

## Summary of what is established

- Lemma A (image geometry of zeros under w = 1−1/ρ): correct, clean.
- Lemma B (on-line zeros contribute ≥ 0 to every λ_n): correct; explains why
  Li's criterion is "term-by-term self-certifying" under RH.
- Lemma C (λ_1 > 0 unconditionally): correct, standard value reproduced.
- A correct heuristic (not a proof) that a single off-line quadruple forces
  λ_n < 0 for large n, with an explicit identification of the missing
  ingredient: an unconditional uniform zero-density/convergence control that
  would be RH-equivalent.

No new closure of RH is obtained. The contribution is a sharpened, correct
statement of *why* the Li approach reduces to a competition between an
exponentially-large single-zero term and a polynomially-large collective
term, and *where* the conditional convergence blocks isolating them.
