Attempt: A sharp reduction from RH to a square-root bound for the Mertens function

Statement (conditional reduction).
Assume there exist constants C>0 and A≥0 such that for all x≥2,
|M(x)| = |∑_{n≤x} μ(n)| ≤ C x^{1/2} (log x)^A.
Then the Riemann Hypothesis holds.

Proof sketch.
- Define for s=σ+it with σ>1/2 the Dirichlet series
  F(s) := ∑_{n=1}^∞ μ(n) n^{-s}.
  We show F(s) converges locally uniformly (hence defines a holomorphic function) on the half-plane σ>1/2 and extends 1/ζ(s) there.

- Convergence and bound via summation by parts.
  For X≥2,
    S(X,s) := ∑_{n≤X} μ(n) n^{-s}.
  By summation by parts,
    S(X,s) = M(X) X^{-s} + s ∫_{1}^{X} M(u) u^{-s-1} du.
  Using the hypothesis |M(u)| ≤ C u^{1/2} (log u)^A and σ>1/2,
    |M(X) X^{-s}| ≤ C X^{1/2-σ} (log X)^A → 0 as X→∞,
  and
    ∫_{1}^{∞} |M(u)| u^{-σ-1} du
      ≤ C ∫_{1}^{∞} u^{1/2-σ-1} (log u)^A du
      = C ∫_{1}^{∞} u^{-(σ+1/2)} (log u)^A du < ∞
    because σ+1/2>1.
  Hence S(X,s) converges as X→∞, uniformly on compact subsets of σ>1/2, to
    F(s) = s ∫_{1}^{∞} M(u) u^{-s-1} du,
  showing F is holomorphic on σ>1/2. Furthermore, the same estimates give a local bound for F(s).

- Identification with 1/ζ(s) and zero-free region.
  For σ>1, the Dirichlet series for 1/ζ(s) converges absolutely and equals ∑ μ(n) n^{-s}. Thus F(s)=1/ζ(s) on σ>1. By the identity theorem, F(s)=1/ζ(s) throughout σ>1/2 (the domain is connected). Therefore 1/ζ(s) is holomorphic on σ>1/2, so ζ(s) has no zeros there.

- Conclusion by symmetry.
  The functional equation and conjugation symmetry of the zeros of ζ(s) imply that zeros are symmetric with respect to the critical line Re s=1/2. Since there are no zeros with Re s>1/2, there are none with Re s<1/2 either. Hence every nontrivial zero has real part exactly 1/2, i.e., the Riemann Hypothesis holds.

Remarks.
- The hypothesis is quantitatively sharp: RH implies M(x)=O(x^{1/2+ε}) for every ε>0; here we assume an explicit bound O(x^{1/2} (log x)^A), which is within that range (the log-power slack does not affect the argument).
- The proof isolates a concrete analytic target: establish square-root cancellation (up to logs) in the Möbius summatory function uniformly for all x, and RH follows.

REASON: Proved a rigorous conditional reduction: M(x)=O(x^{1/2} log^A x) ⇒ RH via analytic continuation of 1/ζ(s)
