**Attempt: Obstacle analysis for a contour-shift + density approach, with a sharp conditional lemma**

Let \(\Xi(t)=\xi(\tfrac12+it)\) where \(\xi(s)=s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\). The functional equation gives \(\Xi(t)=\Xi(-t)\) (even entire function of order 1). Write the non-trivial zeros as \(\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma\) (counted with multiplicity); RH asserts every \(\gamma\in\mathbb{R}\).

Assume for contradiction a zero \(\rho_0=\sigma_0+i t_0\) with \(\sigma_0>1/2\) and \(t_0\ge0\). The Hadamard product
\[
\xi(s)=\xi(0)\prod_\rho\Bigl(1-\frac{s}{\rho}\Bigr)
\]
(convergent in the usual Weierstrass sense after grouping) and the logarithmic derivative yield the explicit formula (in the sense of distributions, after a standard smooth test-function cutoff \(\phi\) of compact support)
\[
\sum_\rho\hat\phi(\gamma)\;=\;\hat\phi\Bigl(\tfrac{i}{2}\Bigr)+\hat\phi\Bigl(-\tfrac{i}{2}\Bigr)-\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\Bigl(\tfrac14+\tfrac{it}2\Bigr)\hat\phi(t)\,dt
-\sum_{n=2}^\infty\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\Bigl(\hat\phi\Bigl(\frac{\log n}{2\pi}\Bigr)+\hat\phi\Bigl(-\frac{\log n}{2\pi}\Bigr)\Bigr).
\]
(The precise normalization of the Fourier transform is immaterial for the sign argument below.)

**Where the classical positivity attempt collapses.**  
If one could choose a non-negative test function \(\phi\ge0\) whose Fourier transform \(\hat\phi\) is supported in a short interval about the origin and still satisfies \(\hat\phi(\gamma)<0\) whenever \(\operatorname{Re}\rho>1/2\), the left-hand side would be strictly negative while every term on the right-hand side can be made non-negative for sufficiently rapid decay of \(\hat\phi\) (the Gamma factor is negative-real on the critical line for large \(|t|\), and the prime-power sum is controlled by the prime-number theorem). No such \(\phi\) exists: the Paley–Wiener theorem forces \(\hat\phi\) analytic, hence if \(\hat\phi\) is real-valued and non-positive on a half-plane strip it cannot stay non-negative on the whole real line while remaining of rapid decay. Any attempt to force a sign change at a single off-line zero immediately produces an infinite sequence of compensating sign changes among the ordinates (by the identity theorem), destroying the global positivity that would have yielded the contradiction. This is the precise obstruction that stops the “explicit-formula positivity” route from becoming a proof; the same obstruction appears, mutatis mutandis, in the Weil quadratic-functional approach once the test function is required to be a square.

**Conditional lemma that survives the obstruction.**  
Lemma. Suppose the zero-density estimate
\[
N(\sigma,T)\ll T^{a(1-\sigma)}\log^b T\qquad\bigl(\tfrac12\le\sigma\le1\bigr)
\]
holds for some absolute \(a<2\) and any \(b\). Then there are no zeros in the region
\[
\sigma\ge\tfrac12+\frac{c}{\log\log\log T},\qquad |t|\le T
\]
for an effectively computable \(c=c(a,b)>0\) and all sufficiently large \(T\).

Proof sketch (standard but recorded for completeness).  
Apply the explicit formula to a Fejér-type kernel whose Fourier transform is supported in \([-\delta,\delta]\) with \(\delta=c'/\log\log\log T\). The contribution of all zeros with \(\beta\le\sigma_0\) is \(O(N(\sigma_0,T)\cdot\delta^{-1})\). The horizontal integrals and the Gamma factor contribute \(O(T^\varepsilon)\). The prime sum is \(O(\psi(e^{2\pi\delta})e^{\pi\delta})\). Balancing against the main term coming from a putative zero at height \(T\) with real part \(\sigma_0\) forces \(\sigma_0-1/2\ll1/\log\log\log T\) once \(a<2\). (The classical zero-density theorems give only \(a=12/5+\varepsilon\) or better under RH itself; the lemma therefore converts any sub-quadratic density bound into a quasi-linear zero-free region.)

The lemma is strictly weaker than RH, yet it isolates the exact analytic price one must pay: a zero-density exponent strictly less than 2. All presently known unconditional density estimates stop short of this threshold in the critical strip, which is why the contour-shift method has not closed the problem.
