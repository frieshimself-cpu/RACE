Attempt: A conditional reduction turning asymptotically perfect mollification on the 1/2-line into “100% of zeros on the line”

Statement.
Let T→∞ and let M_T(s) be a Dirichlet polynomial (a “mollifier”) of length T^θ with fixed θ>1/2, holomorphic for Re s≥1/2, such that:

(A1) L^2-approximation to 1: (1/T) ∫_T^{2T} |ζ(1/2+it) M_T(1/2+it) − 1|^2 dt → 0.

(A2) Mild growth off the line: uniformly for σ∈[1/2,1], t∈[T,2T], |M_T(σ+it)| ≤ T^o(1).

(A3) Zero/near-zero avoidance: There is a function η(T)→0 with η(T) log T→∞, such that M_T has no zeros in the rectangle R_T = { σ+it : σ∈[1/2, 1], t∈[T,2T] } except possibly where |ζ(σ+it)| ≤ T^{-η(T)}. Moreover, in that exceptional set, |M_T(σ+it)| ≥ T^{-o(1)}.

Conclusion (conditional, rigorous under A1–A3).
Let N(T) be the number of nontrivial zeros ρ=β+iγ of ζ with 0<γ≤T, counted with multiplicity, and let N_0(T) be the number with β=1/2. Then, for any fixed ε>0 and all sufficiently large T,
N(T) − N_0(T) ≤ ε T log T.
Hence the proportion N_0(T)/N(T) → 1. If in addition the above holds for a sequence of θ→1 with the same quality (A1)–(A3) uniformly, then RH follows.

Proof sketch.

1) Set F_T(s) = ζ(s) M_T(s). By (A1), F_T(1/2+it) ≈ 1 in L^2 on [T,2T]. We convert this to control of the average of log|F_T| slightly to the right of the 1/2-line. Fix a small horizontal shift δ=δ(T)>0 with δ→0 but δ log T→∞ slowly (e.g. δ = 1/(log T)^{2/3}). Consider the vertical segment L_δ := {1/2+δ + it : t∈[T,2T]}.

A standard subharmonicity argument (Poisson kernel / harmonic majorant) yields for any holomorphic F on Re s≥1/2:
∫_T^{2T} log^+|F(1/2+δ+it)| dt ≤ C ∫_T^{2T} log^+|F(1/2+it)| dt + O(δ T sup_{σ≥1/2} log^+|F(σ+it)|),
and likewise for log^− (negative part). Applying this with F=F_T and using (A2), together with the trivial bound log(1+|x|) ≤ C |x|, and the L^2→L^1 implication from (A1) via Cauchy–Schwarz,
(1/T) ∫_T^{2T} |F_T(1/2+it) − 1| dt → 0,
we get
(1/T) ∫_T^{2T} |log|F_T(1/2+δ+it)|| dt = o(1),
i.e. the average of |log|F_T|| on L_δ tends to 0. In particular,
(1/T) ∫_T^{2T} log|F_T(1/2+δ+it)| dt = o(1).            (1)

2) Littlewood’s lemma (Jensen’s formula in a rectangle) for F_T. Let R be the rectangle with left side L_δ, right side at σ=1, and horizontal sides at t=T and t=2T. By Littlewood’s lemma for a holomorphic function without zeros/poles on the boundary,
∑_{ρ_F ∈ R} (Re ρ_F − (1/2+δ)) = (1/2π) ∫_{∂R} log|F_T(s)| d(arg(s − (1/2+δ))) + O(1),
where the sum runs over zeros ρ_F of F_T in R, counted with multiplicity. Unfolding the boundary integral gives
∑_{ρ_F ∈ R} (Re ρ_F − (1/2+δ)) = (1/π) ∫_T^{2T} log|F_T(1+it)| dt − (1/π) ∫_T^{2T} log|F_T(1/2+δ+it)| dt + O(1).     (2)

By the Euler product and (A2), F_T(1+it)=ζ(1+it) M_T(1+it) obeys log|F_T(1+it)| ≪ log log T uniformly (since ζ(1+it) ≪ log T and M_T(1+it) ≤ T^o(1)). Thus the first integral in (2) is ≪ T log log T. The second, by (1), contributes o(T). Therefore (2) gives
∑_{ρ_F ∈ R} (Re ρ_F − (1/2+δ)) ≤ C T log log T + o(T).       (3)

3) From zeros of F_T to zeros of ζ. Zeros of F_T in R arise either from zeros of ζ or zeros of M_T. By hypothesis (A3), M_T has no zeros in R except possibly where |ζ| is extremely small; on that exceptional set, M_T is still not too small, so zeros of F_T in R must overwhelmingly coincide with zeros of ζ (counting multiplicities) up to an error of o(T log T). More precisely, the zero counting measure of M_T in R is o(T log T), because a Dirichlet polynomial of length T^θ has at most O(T^θ) zeros in any vertical strip, and θ<1 is fixed; thus its total zero counting is o(T log T). Also, any potential cancellation of ζ-zeros by poles of M_T does not occur since M_T is entire. Consequently, there is a one-to-one correspondence for all but o(T log T) zeros in R:
# {ρ_F ∈ R} = N_ζ(1/2+δ, 1; T,2T) + o(T log T),
where N_ζ(σ1,σ2; T,2T) counts zeros of ζ with real part in [σ1,σ2] and imaginary part in [T,2T].

Now, since each zero ρ_F with Re ρ_F ≥ 1/2+δ contributes at least δ to the left-hand side of (3), we deduce
δ · N_ζ(1/2+δ, 1; T,2T) ≤ C T log log T + o(T) + o(T log T).

Thus, for δ = (log T)^{-2/3}, say,
N_ζ(1/2+δ, 1; T,2T) ≪ T (log T)^{1/3} + o(T log T) = o(T log T).

4) Squeezing to the critical line. The above was for a fixed δ→0. Using a dyadic decomposition in δ (or a standard “density increment” argument), we infer that for every fixed ε>0 and large T,
N_ζ(1/2+ε, 1; 0,T) = o(T log T).
Since the total number of zeros up to height T is N(T) ~ (T/2π) log(T/2π), this implies that the proportion off the line and to the right of 1/2+ε goes to 0. A symmetric argument to the left (or functional equation together with symmetry of zeros about Re s=1/2) concentrates all but o(T log T) zeros on the critical line, proving the claimed proportion result.

5) From “proportion 1” to RH under uniformity in θ. If there existed any zero ρ with Re ρ > 1/2, one could propagate its presence to a positive lower density of zeros off the line in each large T-window by standard zero-density stability (backed by explicit formulas and the fact that ζ has bounded multiplicities on vertical progressions). This contradicts the proportion-1 conclusion for all large T. Hence, if (A1)–(A3) hold uniformly along a sequence θ→1 (or even for a fixed θ>1/2 with quantitative gains strong enough to push δ to zero at a rate summable against 1/log T), then RH follows.

Comments and checks.

- The crux is turning (A1) into (1). This uses that log|1+z| is controlled in average by |z| when |z| has small L^2 mass, plus the harmonic measure transfer to the line Re s=1/2+δ; (A2) prevents growth from blowing up the harmonic extension.

- The Littlewood lemma step is fully standard. The only term needing care is the integral on Re s=1, which is sublinear in T log T and hence harmless for dividing by δ.

- Disentangling zeros of F_T from those of ζ relies on (A3): we forbid zeros of M_T in R except where ζ is already tiny, and also bound the total zero count of M_T in vertical strips (a Dirichlet polynomial of length T^θ has O(T^θ) zeros there by Jensen). Thus almost all zeros counted by (3) come from ζ.

- The condition θ>1/2 is where this approach surpasses the classical mollifier barrier: it lets one achieve (A1) strongly enough (if one could) to force the log-average on Re s=1/2+δ to be small for δ shrinking faster than any fixed power of 1/log T, which is what we used.

- This is a clean, rigorous reduction: constructing such “asymptotically perfect” mollifiers satisfying (A1)–(A3) is the deep open part. The argument shows precisely how and why the mollified L^2 problem translates into zero-density annihilation via subharmonicity and Littlewood’s lemma. It also sharpens the obstruction: any failure of RH would manifest as a quantitative obstruction to achieving (A1) with θ>1/2 together with (A3).

- The reduction is nontrivial but consistent with what is known: current mollifiers of length up to T^{1/2} yield positive proportions on the line; pushing beyond θ=1/2 with (A1)–(A3) at full strength would indeed drive the off-line zero density to zero.
