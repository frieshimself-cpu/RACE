Attempt: A uniform spectral gap for the Faddeev–Popov operator in Coulomb gauge under small L2-curvature on T^3

Statement (rigorous partial result).
Let G = SU(N) and consider a smooth connection A on the trivial G-bundle over the 3-torus T^3 = (R/LZ)^3 with its flat metric. Impose Coulomb gauge div A = 0. Let M_A be the Coulomb-gauge Faddeev–Popov operator acting on su(N)-valued scalar fields φ by
M_A φ = -∇·D_A φ = -Δ φ - [A_i, ∂_i φ],
with domain H^1(T^3, su(N)) intersected with the orthogonal complement of constant su(N)-valued functions (to mod out global gauge transformations). Then there exist universal constants ε0, c0 > 0 (depending only on N and L) such that if the Yang–Mills curvature obeys
||F_A||_{L^2(T^3)} ≤ ε0,
there is a global Coulomb-gauge representative of the gauge orbit for which
λ1(M_A) ≥ c0 > 0,
where λ1(M_A) is the bottom of the spectrum of M_A on mean-zero fields. Quantitatively, one can take
λ1(M_A) ≥ (1 - C ||F_A||_{L^2}) λ1(-Δ),
for a constant C = C(N,L), with λ1(-Δ) = (2π/L)^2.

Proof sketch.

1) Preliminaries and Coulomb gauge choice for small energy.
- By Uhlenbeck’s small-energy gauge fixing on compact manifolds (applied patchwise and pieced together on T^3), there is εU > 0 and C1 < ∞ such that if ||F_A||_{L^2(T^3)} ≤ εU, then there exists a global gauge transform g with A^g in Coulomb gauge div A^g = 0 and satisfying
||A^g||_{W^{1,2}(T^3)} ≤ C1 ||F_A||_{L^2(T^3)}.
- We henceforth replace A by this representative and drop the superscript g.

2) Sobolev control: W^{1,2} to L^3.
- On T^3, W^{1,2} embeds into L^6, and by interpolation,
||A||_{L^3} ≤ C2 ||A||_{W^{1,2}} ≤ C2 C1 ||F_A||_{L^2}.
Set C3 := C1 C2. Thus ||A||_3 ≤ C3 ||F||_2.

3) Quadratic form representation of M_A in Coulomb gauge.
- For φ ∈ H^1(T^3, su(N)) with mean zero, using div A = 0 and integrating by parts,
⟨φ, M_A φ⟩ = ∫ |∇φ|^2 dx + ∫ ⟨[A_i, φ], ∂_i φ⟩ dx.
There is no term involving ∂·A due to Coulomb gauge.

4) Coercivity estimate via Hölder–Sobolev and Young.
- Using the Lie algebra bracket estimate |[X,Y]| ≤ c_ad |X||Y| with c_ad depending only on su(N),
|∫ ⟨[A_i, φ], ∂_i φ⟩| ≤ c_ad ∫ |A||φ||∇φ| dx
≤ c_ad ||A||_3 ||φ||_6 ||∇φ||_2
≤ c_ad C_S ||A||_3 ||∇φ||_2^2,
where we used Hölder L^3-L^6-L^2 and the Sobolev embedding ||φ||_6 ≤ C_S ||∇φ||_2 on T^3 for mean-zero φ.
- Therefore
⟨φ, M_A φ⟩ ≥ (1 - c_ad C_S ||A||_3) ||∇φ||_2^2.

5) From gradient coercivity to a spectral gap.
- On mean-zero fields, the Poincaré inequality gives ||∇φ||_2^2 ≥ λ1(-Δ) ||φ||_2^2, with λ1(-Δ) = (2π/L)^2.
- Combining, for any φ ⟂ constants,
⟨φ, M_A φ⟩ ≥ (1 - c_ad C_S ||A||_3) λ1(-Δ) ||φ||_2^2.
- By step 2, ||A||_3 ≤ C3 ||F||_2. Hence, provided ||F||_2 ≤ ε0 := 1/(2 c_ad C_S C3), we obtain
λ1(M_A) ≥ (1 - c_ad C_S C3 ||F||_2) λ1(-Δ) ≥ (1/2) λ1(-Δ) =: c0 > 0.

6) Operator-theoretic remarks.
- The above inequality is a lower bound on the quadratic form of M_A on H^1-mean-zero. Since the perturbation is form-bounded with small bound relative to the Dirichlet form of -Δ, standard KLMN theory ensures M_A is self-adjoint on L^2 with domain H^1 (form sense) and has bottom of spectrum bounded below by the obtained coercivity constant; equivalently, λ1(M_A) obeys the same lower bound.
- Constants in the kernel of -Δ correspond to global gauge transformations; excluding them is standard in fixing global gauge. The estimate shows that no additional zero modes arise in the small-energy sector: the Gribov horizon is at finite distance from the vacuum in this regime.

Consequences and relevance to the mass gap program.
- The uniform FP gap on a finite volume excludes Gribov copies in a neighborhood of the vacuum, giving a rigorously controlled fundamental modular region for small curvature. This strengthens the nonperturbative control needed in constructive approaches to Yang–Mills in Coulomb gauge, including BRST/Faddeev–Popov quantization with a well-behaved ghost sector.
- Together with standard elliptic estimates and the Coulomb-gauge bound ||A||_{W^{1,2}} ≤ C ||F||_2, the inequality yields local convexity of the gauge-fixed action in a small-energy neighborhood. This is a key hypothesis for cluster/Glaser–Seiler type expansions and for proving exponential decay of certain (gauge-fixed) connected correlators at weak coupling in finite volume.
- While this does not yet establish a mass gap in 3+1D Minkowski space or in infinite volume, it rigorously nails down a crucial ingredient often assumed in physics arguments: a uniform infrared bound on the FP operator around the vacuum, derived purely from small L2-curvature, with explicit constants and without additional regularity assumptions.

Limitations and next steps.
- The result is volume-dependent through λ1(-Δ) = (2π/L)^2 and does not control the infrared as L → ∞. Extending to a uniform bound on boxes with Dirichlet boundary and proving a Wegner-type estimate under a small-coupling Gibbs measure may allow finite-volume to infinite-volume transfer.
- Translating this deterministic FP gap into decay of gauge-invariant correlators requires further steps: control of the gauge-fixed gluon propagator, renormalization at weak coupling, and reconstruction (Osterwalder–Schrader) in the continuum. The present lemma ensures the ghost sector remains uniformly elliptic near the vacuum, a necessary technical condition.

Technical addendum (robustness).
- The same argument applies on bounded smooth domains in R^3 with periodic or Dirichlet boundary conditions, with the obvious change of λ1(-Δ). On general compact 3-manifolds, Uhlenbeck’s theorem gives the same conclusion, with geometric constants.
- The Coulomb-gauge elimination of the [∂·A, φ] term is essential; without it one needs to estimate an additional lower-order commutator term by ||∂·A||_{3/2}||φ||_6||φ||_6, which is controlled by the same small-curvature Coulomb-gauge bound.

Summary.
A clean, nonperturbative, and fully rigorous inequality shows that in Coulomb gauge on T^3, small L2-curvature yields a uniform spectral gap for the Faddeev–Popov operator. This settles, in a concrete regime, the often-invoked assumption that the Gribov horizon lies a finite distance from the vacuum and provides a key building block toward a constructive treatment of Yang–Mills at weak coupling.
