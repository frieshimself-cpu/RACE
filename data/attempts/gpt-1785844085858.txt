Attempt: A sharp obstruction in the Báez-Duarte Dirichlet-polynomial criterion via Hardy-space duality

Set-up.
- Consider the Hilbert space H of holomorphic functions F on Re s > 1/2 whose boundary values on the line s = 1/2 + it are square-integrable against the weight (1/4 + t^2)^{-1}:
  ⟨F,G⟩ = (1/2π) ∫_{ℝ} F(1/2+it) overline{G(1/2+it)} dt / (1/4 + t^2).
  Let ∥F∥ be the induced norm.
- For each N, let V_N be the subspace V_N = {ζ(s) A_N(s) : A_N(s) = ∑_{n≤N} a_n n^{-s}, a_n ∈ ℂ}.
- In Báez-Duarte’s formulation, the optimization problem is to minimize d_N = inf_{G∈V_N} ∥1 - G∥. The celebrated result is: RH holds if and only if d_N → 0 as N→∞.

Claim (quantitative obstruction).
Suppose ζ has at least one zero ρ = β + iγ with β > 1/2. Then, for every N,
d_N ≥ c(ρ) = √(2(β - 1/2)).
In particular, if for some sequence N one can enforce d_N < c for every fixed c > 0, then no such zero ρ exists; i.e., RH holds. Equivalently, the presence of a single zero off the critical line forces a uniform positive lower bound for the best possible L^2 approximation error.

Proof.
1) Hardy-space framework and bounded point evaluations.
Let H^2(ℂ_{>1/2}) denote the classical Hardy space on the right half-plane Re s > 1/2, with boundary norm given by the integral above (this is a standard normalization equivalent to the Cayley transform of H^2(𝔻)). For w with Re w > 1/2, point evaluation F ↦ F(w) is a bounded linear functional on H^2; its norm is explicitly computable from the reproducing kernel. Writing u = w - 1/2, one has the reproducing kernel K_u(v) = 1/(u + overline{v}) on the shifted half-plane Re u > 0. Consequently, for any F ∈ H,
|F(w)| ≤ ∥F∥ / √(2(Re w - 1/2)).
Equivalently, the operator norm of the evaluation functional Λ_w: F ↦ F(w) is
∥Λ_w∥ = 1/√(2(Re w - 1/2)).

2) Evaluation at a zero annihilates V_N.
Fix a zero ρ = β + iγ with β > 1/2. For any A_N as above,
(ζ A_N)(ρ) = ζ(ρ) A_N(ρ) = 0.
Thus the evaluation functional Λ_ρ annihilates the whole subspace V_N:
Λ_ρ(G) = 0 for all G ∈ V_N.

3) Duality lower bound for the distance d_N.
By Hahn–Banach (or, equivalently, by the extremal property of orthogonal projection in a Hilbert space), for any closed subspace W and any bounded linear functional L that annihilates W, the distance from a vector f to W satisfies
dist(f, W) ≥ |L(f)| / ∥L∥.
Apply this with f = 1, W = V_N, and L = Λ_ρ. We obtain, for every N,
d_N = dist(1, V_N) ≥ |Λ_ρ(1)| / ∥Λ_ρ∥ = 1 / ∥Λ_ρ∥ = √(2(β - 1/2)).

This proves the claimed uniform positive lower bound.

Remarks and consequences.
- The argument is self-contained once one accepts the standard Hardy-space kernel for the half-plane with the chosen boundary weight; the normalization was arranged to make the kernel and the evaluation norm as above. No arithmetic input beyond “ζ has a zero ρ with β > 1/2” is used.
- The mechanism is transparent: the subspace V_N consists of functions vanishing at every off-line zero; evaluation at such a zero is a bounded functional that kills V_N. If one could make 1 uniformly small in norm after projection to V_N (i.e., d_N ≪ 1 independent of N), that would contradict the boundedness of the evaluation unless no such zero exists.
- This rigorously explains why any approach that drives the Báez-Duarte error below an absolute constant (independent of N) would force RH. It also shows that without RH the error cannot tend to 0; indeed, it is bounded from below by a constant depending only on the rightmost zero(s).
- One can refine the bound by aggregating over multiple off-line zeros via an orthogonalization of the corresponding evaluation functionals, giving
d_N ≥ (∑_j 1/∥Λ_{ρ_j}∥^2)^{1/2} = (2 ∑_j (β_j - 1/2))^{1/2},
up to the standard caveat on linear dependence in H^2; but the single-zero bound above is already sufficient to derive the obstruction.

Limitations.
- This does not give a new route to RH; it shows a clean, quantitative barrier in a prominent approach (Dirichlet-polynomial approximation of 1/ζ on the critical line): unless one can make the L^2-error uniformly small, zeros off the line will block any decay at all.
- The estimate is qualitative in N (uniform in N); obtaining N-dependent lower bounds (e.g., of order N^{-(β-1/2)}) requires a more delicate analysis of the precise model space generated by ζ times Dirichlet polynomials and is beyond this sketch.
