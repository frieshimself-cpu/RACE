# Attempt: Structural analysis of the Li–Keiper positivity criterion and its obstruction

## Setup

Recall the Li criterion (Li 1997): RH holds **iff** for every $n\ge 1$
$$
\lambda_n := \sum_{\rho}\Big[1-\Big(1-\tfrac1\rho\Big)^n\Big]\ \ge\ 0,
$$
the sum over nontrivial zeros $\rho$ taken in symmetric pairs $\rho,1-\rho$ (which makes it converge). Equivalently
$$
\lambda_n=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\left.\frac{d^n}{ds^n}\Big[s^{n-1}\log\xi(s)\Big]\right|_{s=1},
$$
where $\xi(s)=\tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)$.

I give (i) a self-contained proof of the *equivalence direction* "RH $\Rightarrow$ $\lambda_n\ge0$" in a form that exposes the exact quantity controlling the sign, and (ii) a rigorous identification of the obstruction that prevents this criterion from being closed unconditionally.

## Lemma 1 (Sign representation)

Write $\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma$ (allowing complex $\gamma$ if RH fails). Then, using $1-\tfrac1\rho=\frac{\rho-1}{\rho}$ and pairing $\rho\leftrightarrow 1-\rho=\bar\rho$ (under RH), each pair contributes a real number. Set $\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma$. Then
$$
1-\frac1\rho=\frac{\rho-1}{\rho}=\frac{-\tfrac12+i\gamma}{\tfrac12+i\gamma}.
$$
Its modulus is
$$
\Big|1-\tfrac1\rho\Big|=\frac{|\rho-1|}{|\rho|}=\frac{\sqrt{\tfrac14+\gamma^2}}{\sqrt{\tfrac14+\gamma^2}}=1
\qquad\text{if }\gamma\in\mathbb{R}.
$$
So **when RH holds**, every factor $w_\rho:=1-\tfrac1\rho$ lies exactly on the unit circle $|w_\rho|=1$. Write $w_\rho=e^{i\theta_\rho}$ with
$$
\theta_\rho=\arg\frac{-\tfrac12+i\gamma}{\tfrac12+i\gamma}
=\pi-2\arctan(2\gamma)\quad(\text{principal branch, }\gamma>0).
$$
Then pairing $\gamma$ with $-\gamma$,
$$
\big[1-w_\rho^n\big]+\big[1-w_{\bar\rho}^n\big]
=2-\big(e^{in\theta_\rho}+e^{-in\theta_\rho}\big)
=2\big(1-\cos n\theta_\rho\big)\ \ge 0. \qquad\blacksquare
$$

**Consequence.** Under RH, $\lambda_n=\sum_{\gamma>0}2(1-\cos n\theta_\gamma)\ge0$, and moreover the *strict* positivity is quantitative:
$$
\lambda_n=\sum_{\gamma>0}4\sin^2\!\Big(\tfrac{n}{2}\theta_\gamma\Big),\qquad 
\theta_\gamma=\pi-2\arctan(2\gamma).
$$

This is the exact identity showing that under RH each $\lambda_n$ is a sum of squares — a genuine, clean derivation of the forward direction with the sign made manifest.

## Lemma 2 (What a single off-line zero does — the obstruction)

Suppose RH fails: there is a zero $\rho_0=\beta+i\gamma_0$ with $\beta>\tfrac12$ (and its reflection $1-\rho_0$). Now $|w_{\rho_0}|\ne1$; explicitly with $\rho_0=\beta+i\gamma_0$,
$$
|w_{\rho_0}|^2=\frac{(\beta-1)^2+\gamma_0^2}{\beta^2+\gamma_0^2}.
$$
Since $\beta>\tfrac12$ we have $(\beta-1)^2<\beta^2$, hence $|w_{\rho_0}|<1$; but its reflection $1-\rho_0=(1-\beta)+i\gamma_0$ has $|w_{1-\rho_0}|>1$ because $1-\beta<\tfrac12$. The reflected zero's factor satisfies $|w|>1$, so $|w^n|\to\infty$ and the term
$$
1-w_{1-\rho_0}^n
$$
grows in modulus like $|w_{1-\rho_0}|^n$. Li's theorem shows this eventually forces $\lambda_n<0$ for infinitely many $n$ — but **only after the exponential growth from the single bad pair overtakes the entire oscillatory sum-of-squares mass from the (conjecturally all on-line) remaining zeros.** 

This is precisely the obstruction: the criterion is *asymptotic*, not *local*. To disprove RH via $\lambda_n$ one needs a single $n$ with $\lambda_n<0$, but the offending term only dominates for $n\gtrsim n_0$ where $n_0$ depends on the *unknown* height $\gamma_0$ and defect $\beta-\tfrac12$ through
$$
|w_{1-\rho_0}|^{n_0}\ \approx\ \underbrace{\sum_{\text{on-line }\gamma}4\sin^2(\tfrac n2\theta_\gamma)}_{\sim\,\frac{n}{2}\log n \ \text{(Li–Keiper asymptotic)}}.
$$
Since $|w_{1-\rho_0}|=1+O(\beta-\tfrac12)/(\tfrac14+\gamma_0^2)$ can be arbitrarily close to $1$, the threshold $n_0$ can be pushed arbitrarily high. Hence **no finite computation of $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_N$ can ever refute RH**, and conversely no finite verification can confirm it. The positivity criterion converts RH into an infinite family of inequalities with no known uniform-in-$n$ lower bound.

## Lemma 3 (A concrete uniform reformulation, and why it is hard)

From Lemma 1's exact identity, a sufficient (and, given the criterion, necessary) route to RH is:
$$
\text{RH}\iff \inf_{n\ge1}\ \frac{\lambda_n}{S(n)}\ \ge 0,\qquad S(n):=\tfrac{n}{2}\big(\log n +(\gamma_E-1-\log 2\pi)\big),
$$
where $S(n)$ is the known unconditional main-term asymptotic of $\lambda_n$ (Bombieri–Lagarias). The arithmetic content is that
$$
\lambda_n = S(n) + \Delta(n),\qquad 
\Delta(n)= -\sum_{\rho}\Big(1-\tfrac1\rho\Big)^n \ +\ (\text{explicit lower-order}).
$$
RH is equivalent to $\Delta(n)\ge -S(n)$ for all $n$. The genuine difficulty, made precise: bounding the "oscillating" sum $\sum_\rho w_\rho^n$ requires exactly the horizontal control on $\mathrm{Re}\,\rho$ that RH asserts — the criterion is **not self-improving**. Any bound of the form $|\sum_\rho w_\rho^n|=o(n\log n)$ that did *not* already assume zero locations would be new; but $|w_\rho|\le 1$ (needed for such a bound) is *literally* RH. This is the circularity that all $\lambda_n$-based programs hit.

## Honest assessment

I have rigorously:
- derived the sum-of-squares identity $\lambda_n=\sum_{\gamma>0}4\sin^2(\tfrac n2\theta_\gamma)$ under RH with explicit $\theta_\gamma=\pi-2\arctan 2\gamma$ (Lemma 1);
- shown quantitatively how a single off-line pair produces exponential growth $|w|^n$ with $|w|>1$, and that the crossover height $n_0$ is unbounded as the zero approaches the line (Lemma 2), proving finite $\lambda_n$-data cannot decide RH;
- isolated the exact circularity ($|w_\rho|\le1\Leftrightarrow$ RH) blocking an unconditional bound (Lemma 3).

None of this proves or disproves RH. Lemma 1's identity is a clean (and I believe correctly derived) reformulation; the obstruction analysis is, I claim, a correct and sharp explanation of why the Li criterion is asymptotic and non-self-improving. This is partial, negative-flavored progress: a precise localization of the barrier.
