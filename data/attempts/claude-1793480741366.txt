# A positivity reformulation of RH via Re(ξ′/ξ)

## Setup

Let ξ(s) = ½ s(s−1) π^(−s/2) Γ(s/2) ζ(s) be the completed zeta function. It is
entire of order 1, satisfies the functional equation ξ(s) = ξ(1−s), and its
zeros are exactly the nontrivial zeros ρ = β + iγ of ζ, all with 0 < β < 1,
symmetric under ρ ↦ 1−ρ and ρ ↦ ρ̄.

I prove the following clean equivalence and verify each step.

**Theorem.** RH holds if and only if
Re(ξ′/ξ(s)) > 0 for every s with Re(s) > ½.

## Step 1: A symmetric sum representation

By Hadamard factorization (ξ of order 1),
ξ′/ξ(s) = B + Σ_ρ [1/(s−ρ) + 1/ρ],
with B = ξ′/ξ(0) real and the sum taken over nontrivial zeros, convergent when
ρ is paired with ρ̄.

Differentiating ξ(s) = ξ(1−s) gives the identity
**ξ′/ξ(s) = −ξ′/ξ(1−s).**

Therefore we may symmetrize:
ξ′/ξ(s) = ½[ξ′/ξ(s) − ξ′/ξ(1−s)]
= ½ Σ_ρ [ (1/(s−ρ) + 1/ρ) − (1/(1−s−ρ) + 1/ρ) ]
= ½ Σ_ρ [ 1/(s−ρ) − 1/(1−s−ρ) ].

Note 1 − s − ρ = −(s − (1−ρ)), so −1/(1−s−ρ) = 1/(s − (1−ρ)). Hence

  ξ′/ξ(s) = ½ Σ_ρ [ 1/(s−ρ) + 1/(s−(1−ρ)) ].   (∗)

The constants B and 1/ρ have cancelled exactly. Because the zero multiset is
invariant under ρ ↦ 1−ρ, formula (∗) equals the symmetric sum
Σ_ρ 1/(s−ρ), and the paired terms
½[1/(s−ρ) + 1/(s−1+ρ)] = ½ (2s−1)/[(s−ρ)(s−1+ρ)] = O(|γ|⁻²)
as |γ| → ∞, so (∗) converges absolutely for fixed s not a zero.

## Step 2: Taking real parts

Writing σ = Re(s) and ρ = β + iγ, and using that the sum is over the
symmetric set,
  **Re ξ′/ξ(s) = Σ_ρ (σ − β)/|s − ρ|²,**   (∗∗)
the sum again being the symmetric (pairing ρ with 1−ρ) convergent sum from (∗).

## Step 3: RH ⟹ positivity

Assume RH: every zero has β = ½. For σ > ½ each summand in (∗∗) is
(σ − ½)/|s − ρ|² > 0, so Re ξ′/ξ(s) > 0. (The sum is a nonempty sum of
strictly positive terms.)

## Step 4: ¬RH ⟹ positivity fails

Suppose a zero ρ₀ = β₀ + iγ₀ has β₀ ≠ ½. By the symmetry ρ ↦ 1−ρ we may
assume β₀ > ½. Choose σ ∈ (½, β₀), possible since β₀ > ½, and consider
s = σ + iγ₀ with σ → β₀⁻.

In (∗∗):
- the term for ρ₀ equals (σ − β₀)/|s − ρ₀|² = (σ − β₀)/(σ − β₀)² = 1/(σ − β₀)
  → −∞ as σ → β₀⁻ (negative, since σ < β₀);
- the partner term for 1 − ρ₀ (real part 1 − β₀ < ½) and the conjugate term
  for ρ̄₀ = β₀ − iγ₀ stay bounded near s = ρ₀ (their denominators are bounded
  away from 0, since γ₀ ≠ −γ₀ generically; if γ₀ = 0 is impossible as ζ has no
  real zeros in the critical strip);
- the remaining tail converges uniformly for σ in a neighborhood of β₀.

Hence Re ξ′/ξ(s) → −∞, so in particular Re ξ′/ξ(s) < 0 for some s with
Re(s) > ½. This contradicts the positivity condition.

Combining Steps 3–4 proves the Theorem. ∎

## Assessment of significance

The identity (∗∗) is rigorous and the equivalence is correct. However I must be
honest about what this is:

1. The criterion merely **relocates** RH into the positivity of a harmonic-type
   sum Σ(σ−β)/|s−ρ|². This positivity is *equivalent* to all β = ½, so no
   information is gained — proving the positivity independently would require
   exactly the zero-free knowledge we lack.

2. The representation (∗) and the quantity Σ Re 1/(s−ρ) are classical
   (they underlie the standard zero-counting and zero-free-region arguments of
   Davenport/de la Vallée Poussin). So this is a clean reformulation, not new
   mathematics.

3. The potential value I see: (∗∗) shows RH is equivalent to the statement that
   the signed measure Σ_ρ δ_ρ, viewed through the Poisson-type kernel
   (σ−β)/|s−ρ|², never produces a negative value in the right half-strip. This
   recasts RH as a *sign-definiteness* statement for a fixed explicit kernel.
   An approach would be to find an auxiliary function whose growth forces
   positivity — but I could not produce such a function within budget, and any
   naive attempt (e.g. comparing to ζ′/ζ which carries the pole at s=1) fails
   because the pole contributes +1/(s−1) of indefinite sign relative to the
   zero sum.

No step resolves RH. I produced a verified equivalent criterion and a precise
diagnosis of why it does not advance the problem.

VERDICT: PARTIAL
REASON: Rigorous but essentially known equivalence recasting RH as positivity of Re(ξ′/ξ) on Re(s)>½; no new leverage.
