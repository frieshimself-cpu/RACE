### Attempt: Partial reduction via a constrained positivity criterion on the Guinand–Weil explicit formula

Let \(\xi(s)=\frac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\). The functional equation \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\) and the Hadamard factorization
\[
\xi(s)=\xi(0)\prod_{\rho}\Bigl(1-\frac{s}{\rho}\Bigr)
\]
(product over non-trivial zeros, taken in conjugate pairs and with the usual Weierstrass convergence factor absorbed into the constant) imply that the logarithmic derivative admits the representation
\[
\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)=\sum_{\rho}\frac1{s-\rho}.
\]
The Guinand–Weil explicit formula, obtained by integrating \(\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)\hat f(s)\) against a suitable test function and shifting contours, reads (for even Schwartz \(f\) with \(\operatorname{supp}\hat f\subset(-2,2)\), say)
\[
\sum_{\rho}\hat f(\rho)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(t)\frac{\Omega'(t)}{\Omega(t)}\,dt+\sum_{p,k}\frac{\log p}{p^{k/2}}f(k\log p)+O\bigl(\|f\|_{1,2}\bigr),
\]
where \(\Omega(t)=\pi^{-it/2}\Gamma(\tfrac14+it/2)\) and the sum on the left runs over ordinates of zeros (counted with multiplicity).  

Define the quadratic form
\[
Q(f):=\sum_{\rho}\hat f(\rho)-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(t)\,d\bigl(\tfrac12\log|t|\bigr).
\]
A classical observation of Weil is that RH is equivalent to \(Q(f)\ge0\) for every admissible \(f\ge0\). We sharpen the test-function class: let \(\mathcal F_+\) be the cone of even non-negative Schwartz functions whose Fourier transforms are supported in \([-1+\delta,1-\delta]\) for some fixed \(\delta>0\) and that satisfy the moment constraints
\[
\int f=0,\qquad\int t^2f=0.
\]
(The two vanishing moments cancel the polar contributions at \(s=0,1\) and the leading Stirling terms, leaving a remainder controlled by the next term in the asymptotic of \(\Gamma'/\Gamma\).)

**Lemma (conditional positivity).** Suppose there exists \(c>0\) such that
\[
\bigl|\psi(x)-x\bigr|\le c\,x^{1/2}\log x\qquad\text{for all }x\ge2.
\]
Then \(Q(f)\ge0\) for every \(f\in\mathcal F_+\).  

*Sketch.* Under the assumed error term the prime-power sum in the explicit formula is
\[
\sum_{p^k\le X}\frac{\log p}{p^{k/2}}f(k\log p)=O\bigl(\|f\|_\infty X^{1/2}\log X\bigr).
\]
Choosing the support of \(\hat f\) small enough that the dual variable stays inside a region where the von Mangoldt discrepancy is absorbed by the Gaussian decay of \(f\), the prime contribution becomes non-negative up to an arbitrarily small error (by the usual Fejér-kernel approximation of the constant function 1 on a slightly larger interval). The archimedean integral is non-negative by the convexity of \(\log|\Gamma(\tfrac14+it/2)|\) on the critical line after the two moments have been removed. Hence \(Q(f)\ge0\).

**Gap.** The hypothesis \(\psi(x)=x+O(x^{1/2}\log x)\) is known to be equivalent to RH itself (von Koch). Thus the lemma is a tautological reduction: it shows that a slightly stronger form of the classical equivalence “RH \(\Leftrightarrow\) \(Q\ge0\)” continues to hold when the test functions are restricted to a codimension-2 subcone, but it supplies no new unconditional information. Any attempt to remove the \(O(x^{1/2}\log x)\) hypothesis re-introduces an error term whose sign cannot be controlled without already knowing that all zeros satisfy \(\operatorname{Re}\rho\le1/2+\varepsilon\), which is circular.

The same circularity appears if one tries to replace the pointwise bound on \(\psi\) by an \(L^2\) average (as in Gallagher’s lemma): the resulting \(Q(f)\) acquires an indefinite remainder of size roughly \(\|f\|_2\cdot T^{1/2}(\log T)^{1/2}\) when zeros up to height \(T\) are summed, and this remainder dominates the diagonal contribution once the support of \(\hat f\) shrinks.

Consequently the constrained positivity criterion, while formally correct, does not enlarge the set of zeros known to lie on the critical line, nor does it improve any zero-free region.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Reduction to positivity of Q on a moment-constrained cone is circular (needs RH-strength ψ-error)
