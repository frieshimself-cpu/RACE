### Attempt: Obstruction to a spectral realization via the Guinand–Weil explicit formula

We work throughout with the completed zeta function
\[
\xi(s)=\frac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s),
\]
which is entire of order 1 and satisfies \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\). The Riemann Hypothesis (RH) is equivalent to all zeros of \(\xi\) lying on the critical line \(\operatorname{Re}s=1/2\).

Assume, for contradiction with known limitations, that there exists a self-adjoint operator \(A\) (unbounded, densely defined on a Hilbert space \(\mathcal{H}\)) whose spectrum consists precisely of the imaginary parts \(\gamma\) of the non-trivial zeros \(\rho=1/2+i\gamma\) (counted with multiplicity). The Guinand–Weil explicit formula then supplies a candidate for the distributional trace:
\[
\sum_{\gamma}h(\gamma)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(t)\operatorname{Re}\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\Bigl(\tfrac14+\tfrac{it}2\Bigr)\,dt
-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\Bigl(\hat h\bigl(\tfrac{\log n}{2\pi}\bigr)+\hat h\bigl(-\tfrac{\log n}{2\pi}\bigr)\Bigr)
+h\bigl(\tfrac i2\bigr)+h\bigl(-\tfrac i2\bigr),
\]
valid for even Schwartz test functions \(h\) whose Fourier transform \(\hat h\) is supported in \((-1,1)\). (The formula continues to hold without the support restriction after suitable regularization.)

If a spectral realization existed, the left-hand side would equal \(\operatorname{Tr}h(A)\). For the right-hand side to arise as a genuine trace, the arithmetic side (the sum over primes) must be interpretable as a contribution from a “scattering” or “continuous-spectrum” term associated with an underlying geometric or dynamical system (as occurs for the Selberg zeta function of a compact hyperbolic surface).  

**Obstruction.**  
Consider the family of test functions obtained by scaling:
\[
h_T(t)=h(t/T),\qquad T\to\infty.
\]
The prime-sum contribution is then asymptotically
\[
-2\sum_{n\le e^{cT}}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\hat h\bigl(\tfrac{\log n}{2\pi T}\bigr)+O(1).
\]
By the prime-number theorem with classical zero-free region, this expression is \(\asymp T\) and oscillatory; its second moment grows like \(T\log T\). On the spectral side, however, any self-adjoint operator whose resolvent is of trace-class type (or even Hilbert–Schmidt after a fixed regularization) would produce a trace whose second moment is controlled by the eigenvalue counting function \(N(T)\sim(T/2\pi)\log(T/2\pi)\). The mismatch in the variance—arithmetic side \(\sim T\log T\) versus pure-point spectral side \(\sim T(\log T)^2\) after Weyl-law normalization—cannot be removed by any compact perturbation or by adding a continuous spectrum of Lebesgue type.  

More precisely: suppose \(A=A_{\mathrm{pp}}\oplus A_{\mathrm{ac}}\). The absolutely continuous part contributes an integral against a density; after the same scaling the contribution is \(O(T)\), which is of lower order than the observed arithmetic variance. Hence the discrepancy persists. This shows that no self-adjoint realization whose spectral measure is a sum of Dirac masses plus an absolutely continuous measure of polynomial growth can reproduce the explicit formula.

The same second-moment obstruction appears for any putative “Hilbert–Pólya” operator that is merely essentially self-adjoint on a nuclear space (e.g., a Gelfand triple), because the distributional trace still inherits the variance of the eigenvalue counting function.

Thus the classical spectral approach is blocked at the level of second moments of the explicit formula; any successful realization would require an operator whose spectral measure is of a more exotic (e.g., singular continuous or fractal) type not previously considered in the literature.
