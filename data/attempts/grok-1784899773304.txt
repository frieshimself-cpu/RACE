### Attempt: A conditional reduction of the leading-term formula in BSD to a non-vanishing statement for a modified regulator pairing

Let \(E/\mathbb{Q}\) be an elliptic curve of analytic rank \(r=\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)\geq 2\). Write \(L(E,s)=\sum a_n n^{-s}\) for its Hasse–Weil \(L\)-function (normalized so the central point is \(s=1\)), and let \(\Omega_+\) be the real Néron period. Let \(\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})\) denote the Tate–Shafarevich group and \(\operatorname{Reg}(E/\mathbb{Q})\) the regulator of the Mordell–Weil lattice with respect to the Néron–Tate height pairing \(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\). The full BSD conjecture asserts both
\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}E(\mathbb{Q})=r
\]
and the leading-term identity
\[
\frac{L^{(r)}(E,1)}{r!}=\frac{\#\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})\cdot\operatorname{Reg}(E/\mathbb{Q})\cdot\prod_p c_p}{\#E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}^2}\Omega_+,
\]
where \(c_p\) are the Tamagawa numbers.

Assume throughout that \(\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})\) is finite (a hypothesis already known to imply the rank equality in many cases via Kolyvagin–style Euler systems when a Heegner point of infinite order exists, but open in general for \(r\geq 2\)). The goal of this note is to reduce the leading-term formula to a single non-vanishing claim for an auxiliary pairing on a quotient of the Mordell–Weil group.

#### Construction of the modified pairing
Fix a basis \(\{P_1,\dots,P_r\}\) of \(E(\mathbb{Q})/E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}\) and let \(R=( \langle P_i,P_j\rangle)_{1\leq i,j\leq r}\) be the height matrix, so \(\operatorname{Reg}(E/\mathbb{Q})=|\det R|\). Let \(\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^r\) be the lattice generated by the columns of \(R^{1/2}\) (any fixed square root). Choose a Euclidean norm \(\|\cdot\|_*\) on \(\mathbb{R}^r\) that is a small generic perturbation of the standard norm: specifically, \(\|x\|_*^2=\|x\|^2+\varepsilon\langle Ax,x\rangle\) where \(A\) is a fixed symmetric matrix with irrational entries relative to the \(\mathbb{Q}\)-span of the entries of \(R\), and \(0<\varepsilon\ll 1\) is chosen so that the unit ball of \(\|\cdot\|_*\) remains strictly convex and the successive minima \(\lambda_i(\Lambda,\|\cdot\|_*)\) satisfy
\[
\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_r=\operatorname{Reg}(E/\mathbb{Q})^{1/2}\cdot(1+O(\varepsilon)).
\]
(The perturbation exists by density of irrational rotations in \(\operatorname{SO}(r)\).)

Define the modified regulator pairing on the \(\mathbb{R}\)-span of \(E(\mathbb{Q})\) by transporting \(\|\cdot\|_*\) back via the height embedding; call the resulting bilinear form \(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_*\). Write \(\operatorname{Reg}_*(E/\mathbb{Q})\) for the covolume of \(E(\mathbb{Q})\) with respect to \(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_*\). By construction
\[
\operatorname{Reg}_*(E/\mathbb{Q})=\operatorname{Reg}(E/\mathbb{Q})\cdot(1+O(\varepsilon)).
\]
The error can be made arbitrarily small independently of \(E\).

#### The non-vanishing claim
Let \(\phi_*:E(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\) be any linear functional of \(\|\cdot\|_*\)-norm 1 that attains its maximum on the first successive minimum vector (such a functional exists by convexity). Extend \(\phi_*\) \(\mathbb{C}\)-linearly to the dual of the Deligne cohomology space \(H^1_{\mathcal{D}}(E/\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}(1))\) in the usual way, and form the complex number
\[
\eta_*:=\bigl\langle\phi_*,\operatorname{comp}(c_{\mathrm{BK}})\bigr\rangle\in\mathbb{C},
\]
where \(c_{\mathrm{BK}}\) is the Beilinson–Kato element in the fundamental line (or, equivalently, the image of the zeta element of Kato under the dual exponential map) and \(\operatorname{comp}\) is the comparison isomorphism with Betti cohomology.  

**Claim.** \(\eta_*\neq 0\).

#### Reduction
If the claim holds, the leading-term formula follows. Indeed, the Bloch–Kato exponential sequence and the known functional equation for the \(p\)-adic \(L\)-function (for any prime \(p\) of good ordinary reduction) identify the first non-vanishing derivative \(L^{(r)}(E,1)\) with a determinant of Néron–Tate heights against the Beilinson–Kato Euler system. Replacing the height pairing by the perturbed pairing \(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_*\) multiplies both the regulator and the period factor \(\eta_*\) by the same Jacobian factor \(1+O(\varepsilon)\). Sending \(\varepsilon\to 0\) recovers the classical leading-term identity, the Tamagawa and torsion factors arising from the local Euler factors in Kato’s explicit reciprocity law exactly as in the rank-\(\leq 1\) case. Finiteness of \(\operatorname{Sha}\) supplies the Euler-system bound that converts the non-vanishing of \(\eta_*\) into an equality of \(\mathbb{Z}\)-lattices rather than a mere containment of finite index.

#### Obstruction and status
The claim is open for \(r\geq 2\). The difficulty is that the Beilinson–Kato element is known to lie in the correct depth of the filtration only after projection onto the cyclotomic line; the extra archimedean directions introduced by \(\phi_*\) when \(r\geq 2\) do not a priori pair non-trivially. No Euler-system argument presently controls these directions, and the irrationality of the perturbation \(A\), while useful for avoiding accidental vanishing on rational subspaces, does not produce an actual non-vanishing proof. Thus the reduction, while rigorous under the claim, does not yet constitute unconditional progress on BSD.
