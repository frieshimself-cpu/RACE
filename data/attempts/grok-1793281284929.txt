The Riemann \(\xi\)-function admits the Hadamard product
\[
\xi(s)=\xi(0)\prod_{\rho}(1-s/\rho),
\]
the product running over the non-trivial zeros.  Shifting to the critical line via \(s=1/2+iz\) produces an entire function \(H(z)=\xi(1/2+iz)\) of order one whose zeros are precisely the imaginary parts of the non-trivial zeros of \(\zeta\).  The functional equation becomes \(H(z)=H(-z)\), so \(H\) is even.

Suppose one attempts to realise \(H\) as the Fourier transform of a positive tempered measure \(\mu\) on \(\mathbb{R}\):
\[
H(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{izt}\,d\mu(t).
\]
(The classical Polya–Hilbert programme asks for a self-adjoint operator whose spectral measure would supply precisely such a \(\mu\).)  Evenness of \(H\) forces \(\mu\) to be even.  If \(\mu\) could be shown positive, the zeros of \(H\) would be forced onto the real axis and RH would follow.

To construct \(\mu\) one formally inverts the Fourier representation coming from the Mellin transform of the completed theta series that defines \(\xi\).  After an integration by parts the candidate density is
\[
d\mu(t)=\bigl(\tfrac12\vartheta''(e^{t/2})\,e^{t/4}+\tfrac12\vartheta(e^{t/2})\,e^{t/4}\bigr)dt,
\]
where \(\vartheta(x)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^\infty e^{-\pi n^2 x}\).  The Gaussian sum is positive and rapidly decreasing, yet the second derivative term changes sign.  Explicitly, the second logarithmic derivative of \(\vartheta\) satisfies
\[
\frac{\vartheta''}{\vartheta}(x)=-\pi\sum_{n\neq0}n^2\frac{e^{-\pi n^2 x}}{1+\sum_{m\neq0}e^{-\pi m^2 x}},
\]
which is negative for all \(x>0\).  Consequently the candidate density takes both positive and negative values on every interval of length greater than \(2\).  The signed measure that results cannot be replaced by its total-variation measure without destroying the identity \(H=\widehat{\mu}\).  Thus the positivity required by the spectral interpretation fails already at the level of the archimedean contribution, independently of any arithmetic information about the zeros.

The same sign change appears in Weil’s explicit formula when the test function is chosen to be a Gaussian of large variance: the archimedean factor produces a negative contribution that cannot be absorbed by the sum over primes.  Hence every attempt to deduce RH from a positivity criterion that treats the Gamma factor as a black box is blocked by this elementary obstruction.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Archimedean density changes sign; no positive measure realises \(\xi(1/2+iz)\) as a Fourier transform.
