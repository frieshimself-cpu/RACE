### Attempt: Obstruction in a positivity approach via the completed zeta and a truncated explicit formula

Let \(\xi(s)=\frac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\). This is entire of order 1, satisfies \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\), and the non-trivial zeros of \(\zeta\) are precisely the zeros of \(\xi\). Write the Hadamard factorization (normalized so the product converges appropriately)
\[
\xi(s)=\xi(0)\prod_{\rho}\Bigl(1-\frac{s}{\rho}\Bigr),
\]
the product running over zeros counted with multiplicity. Logarithmic differentiation yields
\[
\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)=\sum_{\rho}\frac1{s-\rho}.
\]
For a Schwartz test function \(g\) whose Fourier transform \(\hat g\) is supported in \((-1,1)\) (so that the prime-power sum is elementary), the Weil explicit formula specializes to an identity of the schematic shape
\[
\sum_{\rho}\hat g\bigl(i(\rho-\tfrac12)\bigr)= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\hat g(t)\,d\mu_{\infty}(t)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,g(\log n),
\]
where \(\mu_{\infty}\) is an explicit archimedean measure coming from the Gamma factor (positive on the critical line after a standard shift).  

Weil’s criterion asserts that RH holds if and only if the right-hand side is non-negative for every such \(g\ge0\).  

**Partial verification and obstruction.**  
When \(\operatorname{supp}\hat g\subset(-c,c)\) with \(c\) small enough that only the prime \(2\) contributes, the prime sum is a finite explicit combination of values of \(g\) and can be checked by hand (or machine) to be dominated by the archimedean term; positivity holds. Extending the support past \(\log3\) introduces the next prime and forces a Diophantine cancellation among the oscillatory terms \(\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}g(\log n)\). Because the locations of the \(\rho\) themselves appear on the left-hand side, any attempt to bound the prime sum from below by a multiple of \(\|g\|_1\) re-introduces a zero-density estimate in the strip \(\sigma>1/2\). The best available zero-density bounds (Ingham–Huxley type) are of the form
\[
N(\sigma,T)\ll T^{c(1-\sigma)^{3/2}\log T},
\]
which is insufficient to absorb the error once the support of \(\hat g\) exceeds a fixed absolute constant. Consequently the family of inequalities required by Weil’s criterion cannot be closed analytically; they remain an infinite collection of numerical statements whose uniform verification is equivalent in strength to RH itself.  

This shows that the most natural positivity reduction, while correct, yields no new zero-free region beyond what classical methods already give, and therefore fails to produce a proof.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Weil positivity reduces RH to unchecked inequalities; density bounds too weak to close them.
