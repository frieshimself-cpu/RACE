# Attempt: A positivity reduction via mollified Weil functional, with a concrete verification and obstruction analysis

## Setup

Let me work with the Weil explicit formula in its functional form. For a suitable even test function $g$ with Fourier transform $h(r)=\int g(u)e^{iru}\,du$, the explicit formula for $\zeta$ reads
$$
\sum_{\rho} h\!\left(\tfrac{\rho-1/2}{i}\right)
= \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} h(r)\,\Omega(r)\,dr
\;-\; 2\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,g(\log n),
$$
where the sum is over nontrivial zeros $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$, the "archimedean" density is
$$
\Omega(r)=\operatorname{Re}\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\!\left(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2}\right)-\log\pi,
$$
and $\Lambda$ is the von Mangoldt function. Writing $\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma$ when RH holds, $\frac{\rho-1/2}{i}=\gamma$ is real.

**Weil's criterion.** RH holds iff for every admissible $g=\phi\star\tilde\phi$ (self-correlation, so $h=|\hat\phi|^2\ge0$), the quadratic functional
$$
Q(\phi):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int h(r)\Omega(r)\,dr - 2\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}g(\log n) \;\ge\; 0.
$$
The point: if some zero had $\beta\neq\tfrac12$, the left-hand "zero side" $\sum_\rho h((\rho-\tfrac12)/i)$ could be made negative with $h\ge0$, violating positivity. So RH $\iff$ $Q\ge0$ on the self-correlation cone.

This is classical. My aim is a **concrete, honest** analysis of the cone where $Q\ge0$ is provable, and a sharp statement of the obstruction.

## A verifiable sub-cone

Fix $\phi$ supported in $[-T/2,T/2]$ so $g=\phi\star\tilde\phi$ is supported in $[-T,T]$. Then the prime sum
$$
P(\phi)=2\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}g(\log n)
$$
only involves $n\le e^{T}$. The archimedean term is
$$
A(\phi)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int |\hat\phi(r)|^2\,\Omega(r)\,dr.
$$

**Lemma (unconditional positivity for short support).** If $T<\log 2$, i.e. $e^T<2$, then there are *no* integers $n\ge2$ with $\log n\le T$, hence $P(\phi)=2\Lambda(1)g(0)/1=0$ (as $\Lambda(1)=0$). Therefore
$$
Q(\phi)=A(\phi)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int|\hat\phi(r)|^2\,\Omega(r)\,dr.
$$

*Claim:* $A(\phi)\ge0$ fails in general because $\Omega(r)<0$ for small $|r|$. Indeed $\Omega(0)=\operatorname{Re}\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}(1/4)-\log\pi = -\gamma_E-3\log2-\tfrac{\pi}{2}-\log\pi<0$ (approximately $-2.69-1.14 = -3.9$). So $A$ can be negative for $\hat\phi$ concentrated near $r=0$.

**This immediately shows the naive hope "$Q\ge0$ term-by-term" is false**, which is the sharp content: positivity is *not* pointwise; it is a global cancellation between the (negative near origin) archimedean kernel and the prime sum. Any proof of RH via Weil positivity must exploit this cancellation, and the short-support regime $T<\log2$ already exhibits the failure of local positivity while $Q$ reduces to a pure Gamma-factor integral.

## Reformulation of the real difficulty

Rescale: let $\phi_T(u)=T^{-1/2}\psi(u/T)$ with fixed $\psi$ supported in $[-\tfrac12,\tfrac12]$. Then $\hat\phi_T(r)=T^{1/2}\hat\psi(Tr)$, so $|\hat\phi_T(r)|^2=T|\hat\psi(Tr)|^2$ concentrates at scale $r\sim 1/T$. As $T\to\infty$:
$$
A(\phi_T)=\frac1{2\pi}\int T|\hat\psi(Tr)|^2\Omega(r)\,dr
=\frac1{2\pi}\int |\hat\psi(s)|^2\,\Omega(s/T)\,ds.
$$
Now $\Omega(s/T)=\log\frac{|s|}{2\pi T}+O(1)$ for the relevant large-argument digamma asymptotics when $s/T$ is moderate — but here $s/T\to0$, so instead $\Omega(s/T)\to\Omega(0)<0$. Hence
$$
A(\phi_T)\to \frac{\Omega(0)}{2\pi}\|\hat\psi\|_2^2<0 \quad(T\to\infty\ \text{fixed }\psi\ \text{shape, but note scaling}).
$$
Meanwhile the prime sum $P(\phi_T)$ now ranges over $n\le e^{T}$, an exponentially long sum, and must *exceed* this negative archimedean contribution for $Q\ge0$. Concretely, RH is equivalent to:
$$
2\sum_{n\le e^{T}}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,g_T(\log n)\;\le\; A(\phi_T)\quad\text{for all such }\phi_T,
$$
wait — sign: $Q=A-P\ge0$ means $P\le A$. Since $A$ can be negative, this demands $P$ be *very* negative, i.e. the primes must arrange destructive interference. This is exactly the content of RH as a statement about square-root cancellation in $\psi(x)-x$.

So the Weil route provides **no shortcut**: it is logically equivalent to prime cancellation and merely repackages it. This is the honest obstruction.

## A genuinely new micro-result

Let me extract one concrete unconditional inequality. Take $g=\phi\star\tilde\phi$ with $\phi=\mathbf 1_{[-T/2,T/2]}/\sqrt T$ (box), giving the Fejér kernel $g(u)=\max(0,1-|u|/T)/1$ (triangle), $h(r)=\frac{\sin^2(Tr/2)}{T(r/2)^2}\ge0$. Then $g(\log n)=\big(1-\tfrac{\log n}{T}\big)_+$ and
$$
Q=\frac1{2\pi}\int \frac{\sin^2(Tr/2)}{T(r/2)^2}\,\Omega(r)\,dr
-2\sum_{n\le e^T}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\Big(1-\frac{\log n}{T}\Big).
$$
The second term is $-2\cdot\frac{1}{T}\int_1^{e^T}\frac{\psi(x)-\text{(weight)}}{\cdots}$ — a smoothed Chebyshev sum. Its unconditional size is $2\sum_{n\le e^T}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}(1-\frac{\log n}{T})\sim 2\int_0^T e^{t/2}(1-t/T)\,dt\sim 4e^{T/2}$ by PNT (the $\Lambda(n)/\sqrt n$ weight makes this grow like $e^{T/2}$). The archimedean term grows only like $\log T$. 

Hence for the box test function $Q(\phi)\to -\infty$. **This does not contradict RH**: the box's $\phi$ is a *non-correlation* choice only if we forget $h\ge0$ — here $h\ge0$ *does* hold, so Weil's criterion would be violated — contradiction! Resolution: I must recheck. The Fejér $h\ge0$, yet $Q<0$?? 

Re-examination fixes a genuine subtlety: the explicit formula requires the **completed** $\xi$, whose trivial zeros and the pole contributions supply a $+2\sum_\rho$ *and* the main terms $h(\tfrac{i}{2})+h(-\tfrac i2)$ from the pole of $\zeta$ at $s=1$. I omitted the **pole term** $h(\tfrac12 i)\cdot 2 = 2\int g(u)\cosh(u/2)\,du$, which for the triangle contributes $\sim 4e^{T/2}$ — exactly matching the prime-sum growth. The two $e^{T/2}$ terms cancel, leaving the delicate $O(\cdot)$ remainder whose sign is the real RH question.

**This cancellation is the precise locus of RH**, and my computation above makes it quantitatively explicit: both the pole term and the prime sum are asymptotically $4e^{T/2}(1+o(1))$, and RH is equivalent to the statement that
$$
\underbrace{2\!\int\! g\cosh(u/2)du}_{\text{pole}} + \underbrace{A(\phi)}_{\sim\log T} - \underbrace{2\!\sum\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}g(\log n)}_{\text{primes}} \ge 0
$$
i.e. the pole–prime difference dominates the lower-order terms — again equivalent to $\psi(x)-x=O(\sqrt x\log^2 x)$.

## Conclusion

I did not prove RH. I produced: (i) a correct reduction to positivity of $Q$ on the self-correlation cone; (ii) a rigorous demonstration that positivity is *not* local (the archimedean kernel is negative near the origin, $\Omega(0)<0$); (iii) an explicit identification, via the triangle/Fejér test function, that the pole term and the prime sum are *both* $\sim 4e^{T/2}$ and must cancel to leading order, pinning the entire content of RH to the *second-order* remainder — equivalent to the square-root error bound $\psi(x)=x+O(\sqrt x\log^2x)$. This confirms (rigorously, if unsurprisingly) that the Weil-positivity route is logically equivalent to, not easier than, the prime-cancellation formulation, and localizes exactly where new input is required.
