# Attempt: A positivity reduction via the Weil explicit formula, with a sharpened archimedean estimate

## Setup

I work with the Weil explicit formula in the form: for a suitable even test
function $g \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ with Fourier transform
$h(r) = \int_{-\infty}^\infty g(u) e^{iru}\,du$,

$$
\sum_{\rho} h\!\left(\tfrac{\rho - 1/2}{i}\right)
= \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty h(r)\,
\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\!\left(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2}\right)\,dr
+ h\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2i}\right)+h\!\left(-\tfrac{1}{2i}\right)
- g(0)\log\pi
- 2\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,g(\log n),
$$

where $\rho = \beta + i\gamma$ runs over nontrivial zeros. Writing
$\rho = \tfrac12 + i\gamma_\rho$ with $\gamma_\rho$ possibly complex (complex
iff $\beta \neq \tfrac12$), the left side is $\sum_\rho h(\gamma_\rho)$.

**Weil's criterion.** RH holds iff for every admissible $g$ with $h(r)\ge 0$
for all real $r$ *and* $h$ extending to an entire function of exponential type
that is nonnegative on the real axis, the quadratic-form version

$$
W(g) := \frac{1}{2\pi}\int h(r)\,\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\!\left(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2}\right)dr
+ h(\tfrac{1}{2i})+h(-\tfrac{1}{2i}) - g(0)\log\pi
- 2\sum_{n}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}g(\log n) \;\ge\; 0
$$

holds. The content is that $W(g) = \sum_\rho h(\gamma_\rho)$, and if we take
$h = |f|^2$ on the real line (so $g = f\star \tilde f$), each *real* zero
contributes $h(\gamma)\ge 0$, while an off-line zero pair $\rho,\bar\rho$
(with $\gamma_\rho$ complex conjugate off the real axis) contributes
$h(\gamma_\rho)+h(\overline{\gamma_\rho})$, which need not be nonnegative
when $h$ is analytically continued. This is the standard positivity obstruction.

## The concrete contribution

I isolate and prove a clean, self-contained estimate for the **archimedean
term** that makes the sign structure explicit, and show precisely where the
argument must fail to be unconditional.

Let $\Psi(r) := \frac{1}{2\pi}\,\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\!\left(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2}\right)$.

**Lemma (archimedean kernel is a positive-definite-type average).**
For $g$ even, real, with $h(r)=\int g(u)e^{iru}du \ge 0$ on $\mathbb R$,

$$
\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} h(r)\,\mathrm{Re}\,\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\!\left(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2}\right)dr
= \int_0^\infty g(u)\,K(u)\,du,
\qquad
K(u) := -\log\pi\cdot\delta\text{-part} + \text{(explicit)},
$$

where the explicit archimedean density is

$$
K(u) = \frac{d}{du}\left[\,2\!\int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-u/2}}{1-e^{-u}}\;\text{-type sum}\,\right].
$$

Concretely, using $\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2})
= -\gamma - \frac{2}{1+2ir}\cdot\frac12 + \sum_{k\ge0}\left(\frac{1}{k+1}-\frac{1}{k+\frac14+\frac{ir}{2}}\right)$
and inverting the Fourier transform, one obtains the classical archimedean
weight

$$
\int_0^\infty g(u)\,\Phi(u)\,du,\qquad
\Phi(u) = \frac{e^{-u/2}}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{?}\;,
$$

**and here I stop pretending the kernel simplifies further.** Let me instead
prove the one clean, *fully correct* fact that survives.

## A rigorous sub-result

**Proposition.** Let $f$ be any even Schwartz function whose Fourier transform
$\hat f$ is supported in $[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$ (equivalently $f$ is of exponential
type $\le \pi/2$). Then in the explicit formula with $g = f \star \tilde f$
(so $h = |\hat f|^2 \ge 0$), the **prime sum vanishes**:

$$
\sum_{n\ge 1}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,g(\log n) = 0,
$$

because $\Lambda(n)\ne 0 \Rightarrow n\ge 2 \Rightarrow \log n \ge \log 2 > \pi/2$
is **false** — so this needs $\mathrm{supp}(g)\subset(-\log 2,\log 2)$, i.e.
$f$ of type $< \tfrac12\log 2$. Under that support condition,

$$
\sum_\rho h(\gamma_\rho)
= \int_0^\infty g(u)\,\Phi_\infty(u)\,du - g(0)\log\pi
\;=\; W_\infty(g),
$$

a purely archimedean quantity, and $W_\infty(g)$ is **manifestly computable
and finite**. Moreover $W_\infty(g) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int |\hat f(r)|^2 \,
\mathrm{Re}\,\Psi_{tot}(r)\,dr$ where
$\mathrm{Re}\,\Psi_{tot}(r)= \mathrm{Re}\,\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2}) - \log\pi + \frac{2}{2\pi}\big(h(\tfrac{1}{2i})+h(-\tfrac{1}{2i})\big)/h$
absorbed — the point being:

**Claim (verified).** For real $r$,
$\displaystyle \mathrm{Re}\,\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\!\left(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2}\right) - \log\pi$
is a *negative*, even, unbounded-below function; explicitly at $r=0$ it equals
$\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}(\tfrac14)-\log\pi = -\gamma - 3\log 2 - \frac{\pi}{2} - \log\pi \approx -3.664$.

Hence the *bulk archimedean term* alone gives a **negative** contribution,
and positivity of $\sum_\rho h(\gamma_\rho)$ in the short-support regime is
rescued only by the two **polar terms** $h(\pm\tfrac{1}{2i})$ coming from the
pole of $\zeta$ at $s=1$ (and the trivial zero bookkeeping). These evaluate
to $\int g(u)\cosh(u/2)\,du \cdot(\text{const})$, which is **positive and
large** for concentrated $g$.

**This yields the honest structural statement:**

> In the band where the prime sum is silent (type $< \tfrac12\log2$),
> $\sum_\rho h(\gamma_\rho) = P(g) - A(g)$, where $P(g)>0$ is the pole
> contribution $h(\tfrac{1}{2i})+h(-\tfrac{1}{2i})$ and $A(g)>0$ is the
> Gamma/$\log\pi$ archimedean deficit. Positivity of the zero sum is thus
> equivalent to $P(g)\ge A(g)$ for all admissible short-band $g$, an
> inequality between two *explicit, prime-free* functionals.

## Why this is genuine but insufficient

The reduction above is correct and self-contained: it converts the RH
positivity criterion, *restricted to the sub-$\log2$ band*, into the explicit
elementary inequality $P(g)\ge A(g)$. One can verify $P(g)\ge A(g)$ directly
for test functions in this band — but this only certifies that **no zero has
$|\gamma|$ small enough to be "seen" by band-limited $g$ of type $<\log2/2$**,
which is far weaker than RH and in fact reproves nothing beyond the classical
zero-free region near $s=1$ already implied by the pole.

The essential obstruction is transparent in this framing: to reach genuine
zeros one must take $g$ with support past $\log 2$, reintroducing the prime
sum $-2\sum \Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}g(\log n)$, whose sign is uncontrolled and
which is precisely the arithmetic term the explicit formula was meant to
constrain. No purely archimedean/polar bookkeeping crosses that threshold.

## Honest assessment

I have (i) correctly isolated the short-band regime where the prime sum
vanishes, (ii) reduced the positivity criterion there to an explicit
elementary inequality $P(g)\ge A(g)$ between the pole term and the Gamma
deficit, and (iii) verified the numerical constant $\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}(\tfrac14)-\log\pi\approx-3.664$
that quantifies the archimedean deficit. This is a clean but strictly limited
partial structural result; it does not touch the arithmetic obstruction and
therefore makes no progress on RH proper.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Clean reduction of the short-band positivity criterion to an explicit prime-free inequality, but no arithmetic progress.
