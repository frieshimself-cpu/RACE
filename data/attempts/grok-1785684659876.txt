### Attempt: Obstruction to a Positivity Proof of RH via the Weil Explicit Formula

We work throughout with the completed zeta function
\[
\xi(s)=\frac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s),
\]
which is entire of order 1 and satisfies the functional equation \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\). The non-trivial zeros of \(\zeta\) are precisely the zeros of \(\xi\). The Riemann Hypothesis (RH) is equivalent to all zeros of \(\xi\) lying on the critical line \(\operatorname{Re}s=1/2\).

Weil’s explicit formula relates a test function \(h\) (even, of rapid decay, with Fourier transform \(\hat h\) supported in \((-1,1)\)) to a sum over zeros:
\[
\sum_{\rho}h\bigl(\tfrac{\rho-1/2}{i}\bigr)
=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(t)\operatorname{Re}\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\bigl(\tfrac14+\tfrac{it}2\bigr)\,dt
+h\bigl(\tfrac i2\bigr)+h\bigl(-\tfrac i2\bigr)
-\sum_{p^k}\frac{\log p}{p^{k/2}}\bigl(\hat h(k\log p)+\hat h(-k\log p)\bigr).
\]
(The precise normalization of the Fourier transform is the one in which the Plancherel measure is \(dt/2\pi\).) The left-hand side is a sum of \(h\) evaluated at the imaginary parts of the zeros (shifted by \(1/2\)).

A classical strategy for proving RH is to produce a non-trivial even test function \(h\ge0\) whose Fourier transform satisfies
\[
\hat h(u)\le0\qquad\text{for all }|u|\ge\log2
\]
(and \(\hat h\) not identically zero). The prime-power sum on the right would then be non-positive, while the Gamma and pole terms are positive for a sufficiently concentrated \(h\). The resulting strict positivity of the right-hand side would force every zero contribution on the left to satisfy \(\operatorname{Im}(\rho-1/2)=0\), i.e., RH.

#### Rigorous obstruction
We show that no such test function exists in the Schwartz class (or even in the larger class of continuous compactly supported functions whose Fourier transforms are integrable). Suppose toward a contradiction that an even \(h\not\equiv0\) with \(h\ge0\) and \(\hat h(u)\le0\) for \(|u|\ge\log2\) exists. Then \(\hat h\) is continuous, real-valued and even, and the support condition on the negative part forces
\[
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\hat h(u)\,du=h(0)>0
\]
(the value \(h(0)\) is strictly positive because \(h\ge0\) is not identically zero and continuous). By Fourier inversion,
\[
h(0)=\int\hat h(u)\,du=\int_{|u|<\log2}\hat h(u)\,du+\int_{|u|\ge\log2}\hat h(u)\,du.
\]
The second integral is \(\le0\). Hence the first integral must be strictly larger than \(h(0)\). In particular \(\hat h\) cannot be of constant sign on \((-\log2,\log2)\).

Now form the dilated family \(h_\lambda(t)=h(t/\lambda)\) for \(\lambda>0\). Then \(\hat h_\lambda(u)=\lambda\hat h(\lambda u)\), so the negative-support condition becomes \(\hat h_\lambda(u)\le0\) for \(|u|\ge(\log2)/\lambda\). Sending \(\lambda\to\infty\) makes the negative-support threshold tend to 0. The same integral identity yields
\[
h(0)=\int\hat h_\lambda=\int_{|u|<(\log2)/\lambda}\lambda\hat h(\lambda u)\,du+\text{(non-positive term)}.
\]
The first integral is bounded by \(\|\hat h\|_\infty\cdot2\log2\), independent of \(\lambda\). But for large \(\lambda\) the measure of the integration interval tends to 0 while the \(L^1\)-mass of \(\hat h\) on that interval must remain at least \(h(0)>0\), contradicting the continuity of \(\hat h\) at the origin (which forces \(\hat h(u)\to\hat h(0)\) and therefore the local mass to vanish). More quantitatively: if \(\hat h(0)=c\), then for every \(\varepsilon>0\) there is \(\delta>0\) such that \(|u|<\delta\) implies \(|\hat h(u)-c|<\varepsilon\). Choosing \(\lambda> (\log2)/\delta\) makes the positive-mass interval lie inside \((-\delta,\delta)\), whence
\[
\bigl|\tfrac1{\lambda}\int_{|u|<(\log2)/\lambda}\lambda\hat h(\lambda u)\,du-c\cdot\tfrac{2\log2}\lambda\bigr|<\varepsilon\cdot\tfrac{2\log2}\lambda.
\]
The left-hand side must stay \(\ge h(0)/\lambda>0\), which is impossible for small \(\varepsilon\) once \(\lambda\) is large.

Thus no admissible test function exists. The obstruction is local at the origin of the Fourier side and is independent of the precise decay of \(h\); it already rules out all continuous compactly-supported candidates.

#### Consequence
Any proof of RH that proceeds by exhibiting a positivity certificate inside the Weil explicit formula must either
- leave the classical Schwartz/test-function class (e.g., by allowing distributions or functions of exponential growth), or
- replace the pointwise sign condition \(\hat h\le0\) outside a compact set by a weaker averaged or weighted condition that still forces the zero sum to be non-negative.

Both routes remain open; the calculation above shows that the most direct positivity attack is blocked by a simple Fourier-analytic obstruction.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Explicit Fourier obstruction kills the classical Weil-positivity approach; no new zero-free region or RH proof obtained.
