### Attempt: Obstruction to a contour-shift proof of RH via the completed xi-function

Let \(\xi(s)=\frac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\) be the completed Riemann xi-function. It is entire of order 1, satisfies the functional equation \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\), and its zeros are precisely the non-trivial zeros of \(\zeta\). Write \(s=\sigma+it\). The Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to all zeros of \(\xi\) lying on the critical line \(\sigma=1/2\).

A natural strategy is to obtain a zero-free region by a contour integral of \(\xi'/\xi\). Fix \(T>0\) large and consider the rectangle \(R\) with vertices \(1+\delta\pm iT\), \(-\delta\pm iT\) (\(\delta>0\) small). By the argument principle,
\[
N(T)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{\partial R}\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)\,ds
\]
counts zeros inside \(R\) (with multiplicity). The functional equation pairs the left and right vertical sides, so the integral reduces (after a standard Stirling estimate on \(\Gamma\)) to
\[
N(T)=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi}-\frac{T}{2\pi}+O(\log T)+\frac1{\pi}\Delta_R\arg\xi(1/2+it),
\]
where \(\Delta_R\) denotes the net change in argument along the critical line segment. The classical Riemann–von Mangoldt formula follows at once once one controls the argument change; the same contour yields the classical zero-free region \(\sigma\ge1-c/\log(|t|+2)\) upon inserting a Dirichlet-polynomial approximation to \(-\zeta'/\zeta\) on \(\operatorname{Re}s=1+\delta\) and applying a Borel–Carathéodory/Jensen bound to push the contour leftward.

To reach the line \(\sigma=1/2\) one would need a uniform bound
\[
\bigl|\arg\xi(\sigma+it)\bigr|\ll\log(|t|+2)\qquad\text{for all }\sigma\ge1/2.
\]
(The implied constant must be absolute.) Suppose, for contradiction, that such a bound holds. Then the horizontal integrals on \(\operatorname{Im}s=\pm T\) remain \(O(\log T)\) even after the left side of \(R\) is moved all the way to \(\operatorname{Re}s=1/2\), and the resulting formula would force every zero to satisfy \(\sigma=1/2\) (any zero with \(\sigma>1/2\) would produce an extra jump of \(\pi\) in the argument that cannot be cancelled by the functional equation). 

The obstruction is that the putative bound on \(\arg\xi\) is false if RH fails, and circular if one tries to prove it unconditionally. More precisely: on any vertical line \(\sigma_0>1/2\) that contains a zero \(\rho_0=\sigma_0+it_0\), the function \(\xi'/\xi\) has a simple pole, so
\[
\arg\xi(\sigma_0+it)=\arg\xi(\sigma_0+it_0+\varepsilon)+\pi+o(1)
\]
as one crosses the height \(t_0\). The change cannot be absorbed into an \(O(\log T)\) error uniformly in \(\sigma_0\). Any attempt to approximate \(\xi'/\xi\) by a Dirichlet polynomial (or by a truncated Euler product) on \(\operatorname{Re}s=\sigma_0\) produces an error term whose size is governed by the same zero; the resulting Jensen-type bound therefore deteriorates exactly when a zero approaches the contour. Consequently the contour cannot be shifted past the first zero off the critical line without already knowing that no such zero exists. This circularity is independent of the quality of the zero-density estimates or of the growth bounds on \(\xi\) in the strip; it is intrinsic to the location of the poles of the logarithmic derivative.

Thus the classical contour method, while sufficient for a zero-free region of width \(c/\log(|t|+2)\), cannot be pushed to the critical line by any refinement that remains within the same circle of ideas (argument principle + convex growth estimates + Dirichlet-polynomial approximation). A genuinely new input—e.g., a positivity property of a spectral determinant, a sufficiently strong pair-correlation law, or an independent bound on \(S(T)=\pi^{-1}\arg\xi(1/2+iT)\)—is required.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Contour method blocked by circular dependence on arg bounds; no new zero-free region obtained.
