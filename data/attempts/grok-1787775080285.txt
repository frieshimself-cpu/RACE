Weil’s explicit formula yields a criterion equivalent to RH: for a dense class of even Schwartz test functions \(f\) with \(\operatorname{supp}\hat f\subset(-1,1)\),
\[
\sum_{\rho}\hat f(\rho/i)\ge0,
\]
where the sum runs over the non-trivial zeros of \(\zeta\) (counted with multiplicity) and the inequality is required to hold for every such \(f\).  The left-hand side expands, via the explicit formula, into an arithmetic main term
\[
\int_0^\infty\frac{f(x)+f(-x)}{2}\frac{x\,d\psi(x)}{x}
\]
plus elementary Gamma and polar contributions that are readily shown non-negative.  Consequently RH is equivalent to the assertion that the arithmetic contribution never overcomes the remaining positive terms.

Any attempt to verify the inequality directly collides with the same obstacle that blocks elementary proofs of the prime-number theorem with a classical zero-free region: the von Mangoldt weights \(\Lambda(n)\) that appear after Fourier inversion are too irregular to be dominated uniformly for every admissible \(f\).  Restricting to a finite-dimensional subspace of test functions (e.g., Gaussians of bounded width, or polynomials times a fixed cutoff) produces only a finite number of linear inequalities on the low-lying zeros; these can be checked numerically but give no control on the tail \(\operatorname{Im}\rho\to\infty\).  Passing to the limit over an exhausting sequence of subspaces re-introduces the full strength of the original sum over zeros, so the argument becomes circular.  Thus the positivity criterion, while elegant, supplies no reduction that is easier than RH itself.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Weil positivity is equivalent to RH but yields no simpler verifiable conditions
