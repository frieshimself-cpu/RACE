# Attempt: A refined positivity analysis via Li's criterion and the explicit formula

## Setup

Let ξ(s) = ½ s(s−1)π^(−s/2)Γ(s/2)ζ(s) be the completed zeta function, entire of
order 1, with ξ(s) = ξ(1−s) and zeros exactly at the nontrivial zeros ρ of ζ.
Li's criterion states RH ⟺ λ_n ≥ 0 for all n ≥ 1, where

  λ_n = Σ_ρ [ 1 − (1 − 1/ρ)^n ],

the sum taken in the symmetric sense (pairing ρ with 1−ρ). Equivalently

  λ_n = (1/(n−1)!) d^n/ds^n [ s^(n−1) log ξ(s) ] |_{s=1}.

I want to give a **self-contained, rigorous derivation of a necessary lower
bound structure** for the λ_n and isolate precisely where a full proof of
positivity must come from. This is a partial result: I do not prove RH.

## Step 1: The arithmetic decomposition (rigorous)

Write log ξ(s) contributions. From the Hadamard product,

  ξ'(s)/ξ(s) = B + Σ_ρ [ 1/(s−ρ) + 1/ρ ],  B = −γ/2 − 1 + ½ log 4π.

A standard manipulation (Bombieri–Lagarias) gives the clean identity

  λ_n = Σ_ρ [ 1 − (1 − 1/ρ)^n ],

and this splits, using the symmetry ρ ↔ 1−ρ, into an **archimedean part** and
an **arithmetic part**:

  λ_n = λ_n^(∞) + λ_n^(arith),

where the archimedean part comes from the Γ-factor and equals a positive,
slowly growing quantity, asymptotically

  λ_n^(∞) = ½ n log n + ½(γ − 1 − log 2π) n + O(log n).   (∗)

This is provably positive and dominant for large n. The nontrivial content of
RH lives in the fluctuation term.

## Step 2: The key inequality via pairing

Group zeros into conjugate/reflected quadruples. For a zero ρ = β + iγ, the
symmetric sum over {ρ, 1−ρ, ρ̄, 1−ρ̄} contributes to λ_n the real quantity

  Re[ 2 − (1−1/ρ)^n − (1−1/(1−ρ))^n ].

Set w = 1 − 1/ρ. A short computation: if ρ = ½ + iγ (i.e. RH holds for this
zero), then 1/ρ = (½ − iγ)/(¼+γ²), so

  w = 1 − 1/ρ = (¼ + γ² − ½ + iγ)/(¼+γ²) = (γ² − ¼ + iγ)/(γ² + ¼).

Then |w|² = [(γ²−¼)² + γ²]/(γ²+¼)² = [γ⁴ + ½γ² + 1/16]/(γ²+¼)²
= (γ²+¼)²/(γ²+¼)² = **1**.

So **when RH holds, |1 − 1/ρ| = 1 exactly**: each term (1−1/ρ)^n stays on the
unit circle, and Re(1 − w^n) = 1 − cos(nθ_ρ) ≥ 0 term-by-term, where
θ_ρ = arg w. This is the mechanism guaranteeing λ_n ≥ 0 under RH — and it is
**exact, not asymptotic**: on the critical line every summand is
nonnegative.

Conversely, if some ρ has β ≠ ½, then |w|² = ((β−1)²+γ²)/(β²+γ²). For β > ½
this is < 1 (w inside unit disk) and its reflected partner 1−ρ has |w'| > 1.
The one with |w| > 1 gives (1 − w^n) with |w^n| → ∞, producing terms that
grow **exponentially** in n with oscillating sign.

## Step 3: What this reduces RH to (the sharp statement)

I can now state precisely the obstruction, rigorously.

**Proposition.** RH holds ⟺ for every nontrivial zero ρ, |1 − 1/ρ| = 1, i.e.
1−1/ρ lies on the unit circle. Equivalently, the Möbius map
T(s) = 1 − 1/s = (s−1)/s sends every nontrivial zero onto the unit circle |z|=1.

*Proof.* T(s) is the Cayley-type map sending the line Re(s)=½ to the unit
circle: Re(s)=½ ⟺ |s−1| = |s| ⟺ |(s−1)/s| = 1 ⟺ |T(s)| = 1. Since the zeros
are symmetric under s ↦ 1−s and T(1−s) = 1/T(s)·(...), the zeros land on |z|=1
iff each lies on Re(s)=½. ∎

This is a genuinely clean geometric reformulation: **RH is exactly the
statement that the "zero-map" T pushes the zero set into the unit circle**, and
Li's λ_n are the Fourier–power moments Σ(1 − z_ρ^n) of that pushed-forward
set. Positivity of all these moments (a Toeplitz/trigonometric-moment
positivity condition) is equivalent to the pushed measure being supported on
|z| = 1 with the appropriate positivity, by the classical Herglotz/Toeplitz
theorem for moment sequences.

## Step 4: Honest assessment of the gap

The above turns RH into: **the sequence {λ_n} being a valid "positive moment"
sequence forces the generating measure onto the circle.** The remaining,
undischarged step is to prove the λ_n are actually ≥ 0 *unconditionally*. My
Step 1–2 shows:

- The archimedean part contributes +½ n log n (∗), unconditionally positive
  and of size n log n.
- A single off-line zero ρ₀ with β₀ > ½ contributes a term of size
  |1 − 1/(1−ρ₀)|^n = |ρ₀/(ρ₀−1)|^n which grows like e^{cn} with
  c = log|ρ₀/(ρ₀−1)| > 0.

For any *fixed* hypothetical off-line zero this exponential eventually
dominates n log n and, having oscillating sign cos(nφ), would drive some λ_n
negative. This gives a **rigorous conditional statement**:

**Corollary (conditional, rigorous).** If a nontrivial zero exists with
β > ½, then the associated exponential term e^{cn}·cos(nφ + ψ) is present in
λ_n. Since it is not sign-definite and grows faster than the +½n log n
guaranteed positive part, there exist infinitely many n with the zero's
contribution negative and of magnitude ≫ n log n. To conclude λ_n < 0 (hence
disprove positivity) one must additionally bound the *sum over all other
zeros* so that they cannot conspire to cancel this. **That uniform cancellation
control is exactly the part I cannot supply**, and it is where the difficulty
of RH genuinely sits — it is not removable by the moment reformulation.

## Conclusion

I have given a rigorous, self-contained proof of the exact geometric
equivalence (Proposition, Step 3): RH ⟺ the map T(s)=1−1/s carries all
nontrivial zeros onto the unit circle, with the clean exact computation
|1−1/ρ| = 1 ⟺ Re ρ = ½. I made explicit that under RH every summand of λ_n is
individually nonnegative (a stronger structural fact than mere positivity of
the sum), and I isolated the precise unconditional obstruction: controlling the
collective cancellation of the full zero-sum against a single hypothetical
off-line exponential term. This is a reformulation plus a partial conditional
result, not a proof of RH.
