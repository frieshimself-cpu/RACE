# Attempt: Li's Criterion — a rigorous verification of the base case and structural analysis

## Setup

Let $\xi(s)=\tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)$ be Riemann's entire function, with $\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)$. Define the **Li coefficients**
$$
\lambda_n=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\frac{d^n}{ds^n}\Big[s^{n-1}\log\xi(s)\Big]_{s=1},\qquad n\ge 1 .
$$
Li's theorem: RH holds **iff** $\lambda_n\ge 0$ for every $n\ge1$. Equivalently, with the zeros $\rho$ of $\xi$ (= nontrivial zeros of $\zeta$),
$$
\lambda_n=\sum_{\rho}\Big[1-\Big(1-\tfrac{1}{\rho}\Big)^n\Big],
$$
the sum taken in symmetric pairs $\rho,1-\rho$.

The point I want to make rigorously: the map $\rho\mapsto w=1-1/\rho$ sends the critical line $\Re\rho=\tfrac12$ to the **unit circle** $|w|=1$, the right half $\Re\rho>\tfrac12$ to $|w|<1$, and the left half to $|w|>1$. Since zeros come in pairs $\rho,1-\rho$ mapping to $w,1/\bar{?}$... let me make the geometry precise, because this is the crux of why positivity is natural but non-closing.

## Lemma 1 (geometry of the Li transform)

For $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$, set $w=1-1/\rho$. Then
$$
|w|^2=\frac{(\beta-1)^2+\gamma^2}{\beta^2+\gamma^2}.
$$
Hence $|w|<1\iff(\beta-1)^2<\beta^2\iff \beta>\tfrac12$. So $|w|=1\iff\beta=\tfrac12$.

*Proof.* $w=1-1/\rho=(\rho-1)/\rho$, so $|w|^2=|\rho-1|^2/|\rho|^2=((\beta-1)^2+\gamma^2)/(\beta^2+\gamma^2)$. The inequality $(\beta-1)^2+\gamma^2<\beta^2+\gamma^2$ reduces to $-2\beta+1<0$. $\square$

**Consequence.** Write $\lambda_n=\sum_\rho(1-w_\rho^{\,n})$. For a zero on the critical line, $w_\rho=e^{i\theta}$, and the paired zero $1-\rho=\bar\rho$ (on the line) gives $w_{\bar\rho}=\overline{w_\rho}=e^{-i\theta}$, so the pair contributes
$$
(1-e^{in\theta})+(1-e^{-in\theta})=2(1-\cos n\theta)\ge 0 .
$$
Thus **critical-line zeros contribute a manifestly nonnegative amount to every $\lambda_n$.** Off-line zeros occur in quadruples $\rho,1-\rho,\bar\rho,1-\bar\rho$; one pair gives $|w|<1$ (contribution bounded, real part controlled) and the conjugate pair gives $|w|>1$, whose contribution $1-w^n$ has $|w^n|\to\infty$ and can be **negative and large** for suitable $n$. This is exactly the mechanism by which a single off-line zero would eventually force some $\lambda_n<0$: an off-critical zero $\rho$ with $|w|>1$ makes $\Re(1-w^n)\to-\infty$ along a subsequence of $n$ (since $w^n$ winds with growing modulus).

This gives a clean *conceptual* proof direction (off-line $\Rightarrow$ some $\lambda_n<0$), but turning it into RH requires proving $\lambda_n\ge0$ for all $n$ **unconditionally**, which is open. I now verify the base case rigorously and extract the honest obstruction.

## Lemma 2 (exact value of $\lambda_1$, unconditional, positive)

$$
\lambda_1=1+\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac12\log(4\pi)=\sum_\rho\frac{1}{\rho}>0,
$$
where $\gamma=0.5772156649\ldots$ is Euler's constant.

*Proof.* Take the Hadamard product
$$
\xi(s)=\xi(0)\prod_\rho\Big(1-\frac{s}{\rho}\Big),\qquad \frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)=\sum_\rho\Big(\frac{1}{s-\rho}+\frac1\rho\Big)
$$
(convergent in symmetric pairing). For $n=1$,
$$
\lambda_1=\frac{d}{ds}\log\xi(s)\Big|_{s=1}=\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(1).
$$
Using $\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)$ we also have $\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(1)=-\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(0)$, and the classical evaluation (Riemann–von Mangoldt) gives
$$
\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(1)=\sum_\rho\frac1\rho=1+\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac12\log(4\pi).
$$
Let me confirm the closed form directly. From $\xi(s)=\tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)$,
$$
\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)=\frac1s+\frac{1}{s-1}-\frac12\log\pi+\frac12\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\!\Big(\frac s2\Big)+\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s).
$$
This is singular termwise at $s=1$ (the $1/(s-1)$ and the pole of $\zeta'/\zeta$ cancel). Laurent-expand near $s=1$: $\zeta(s)=\frac{1}{s-1}+\gamma+O(s-1)$, so $\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)=-\frac{1}{s-1}+\gamma+O(s-1)$. Hence
$$
\frac{1}{s-1}+\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)\xrightarrow{s\to1}\gamma .
$$
The remaining terms at $s=1$: $\frac1s=1$, $-\tfrac12\log\pi$, and $\tfrac12\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}(\tfrac12)=\tfrac12(-\gamma-2\log2)=-\tfrac{\gamma}{2}-\log2$ (using $\psi(\tfrac12)=-\gamma-2\log2$). Summing:
$$
\lambda_1=1+\gamma-\tfrac12\log\pi-\tfrac{\gamma}{2}-\log2
=1+\frac{\gamma}{2}-\tfrac12\log\pi-\log2.
$$
Since $\log(4\pi)=2\log2+\log\pi$, $\tfrac12\log(4\pi)=\log2+\tfrac12\log\pi$, so indeed
$$
\lambda_1=1+\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac12\log(4\pi).
$$
Numerically $\tfrac\gamma2=0.288608$, $\tfrac12\log(4\pi)=\tfrac12(2.531024)=1.265512$, giving
$$
\lambda_1=1+0.288608-1.265512=0.023096\ldots>0. \qquad\square
$$

So the base case is positive, **consistent with RH**, and the positivity is genuine but by a narrow margin ($\approx 0.023$), which already signals that positivity for all $n$ is delicate.

## Structural obstruction (why this does not close RH)

The arithmetic formula (Bombieri–Lagarias) gives, for $n\ge1$,
$$
\lambda_n=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(-1)^{j+1}\,\eta_{j-1}\quad\text{where}\quad \eta_k=\text{Stieltjes-type constants},
$$
equivalently
$$
\lambda_n=\frac{n}{2}\big(\log\pi-\log 4\pi? \big)+\dots
$$
More usefully, Voros/Coffey proved the asymptotic (under RH)
$$
\lambda_n=\frac n2\log n+\Big(\frac{\gamma-1-\log 2\pi}{2}\Big)n+O(\sqrt n\,\log n),
$$
so the **leading term $\tfrac n2\log n$ is positive and dominant** — RH predicts large positive growth. The difficulty is purely at the level of controlling the *oscillatory error term*: the contribution
$$
S_n=-\Re\!\!\sum_{|w_\rho|\ge1}w_\rho^{\,n}
$$
is what a hypothetical off-line zero would corrupt. Lemma 1 shows off-line zeros give $|w|>1$ terms; but **bounding $S_n$ from below for all $n$ is equivalent in strength to RH itself** — no reduction of genuine difficulty has been achieved. Concretely:

- The main term $\tfrac n2\log n$ comes from the $\Gamma$ / archimedean factor and is unconditional in sign.
- Proving $\lambda_n\ge0$ requires $|S_n|\le \tfrac n2\log n(1+o(1))$ for every $n$, and a single off-line zero at height $\gamma$ with $\beta>\tfrac12$ produces $|w|^{n}$ growing geometrically with ratio $\big(\tfrac{(\beta-1)^2+\gamma^2}{\beta^2+\gamma^2}\big)^{-n/2}$. For $\gamma$ large this ratio is $1+\tfrac{2\beta-1}{\gamma^2}+O(\gamma^{-4})$, i.e. **exponentially growing but at a rate $\to1$ as $\gamma\to\infty$**. Hence a high off-line zero violates positivity only for astronomically large $n$ — explaining why finite verification of $\lambda_n\ge0$ (done up to large $n$) cannot certify RH and why the criterion, though equivalent, offers no leverage.

## Summary of what is established

1. **Lemma 1** (geometry): rigorous, correct — pins the equivalence critical line $\leftrightarrow$ unit circle for the transform $w=1-1/\rho$.
2. **Lemma 2** (base case): rigorous unconditional computation $\lambda_1=1+\tfrac\gamma2-\tfrac12\log(4\pi)=0.0231\ldots>0$, consistent with RH.
3. A precise, honest identification of the obstruction: positivity of the oscillatory sum $S_n$ for all $n$ is equivalent to, not weaker than, RH; high off-line zeros perturb $\lambda_n$ only at super-exponentially large $n$, so no finite or main-term argument closes the gap.

I did not prove RH. The verified content (the geometric lemma and the exact positive $\lambda_1$) is correct and self-contained; the reduction clarifies precisely where difficulty concentrates.
