### Attempt: Obstruction to a direct positivity proof of RH via the Guinand–Weil explicit formula

Let \(\Xi(t)=\xi(\tfrac12+it)\) be the completed Riemann xi-function (entire, of order 1, real on the real line, and even). The non-trivial zeros \(\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma\) of \(\zeta\) are precisely the zeros of \(\Xi\). The Guinand–Weil explicit formula asserts that for a sufficiently regular even test function \(g\) (Schwartz class, Fourier transform \(\hat g\) of rapid decay),
\[
\sum_{\gamma}\hat g(\gamma)
=
\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\hat g(t)\operatorname{Re}\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\Bigl(\tfrac14+\tfrac{it}2\Bigr)\,dt
+g(0)\frac{\Omega'}{\Omega}(0)
-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\Bigl(g(\log n)+g(-\log n)\Bigr),
\]
where the sum on the left runs over the imaginary parts of the zeros (counted with multiplicity) and \(\Omega(s)=s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\).

A classical strategy (Weil 1952, refined by Bombieri–Hejhal, Connes, etc.) seeks a \(g\) such that:
- the arithmetic side (prime-power sum + archimedean \(\Gamma\)-factor) is manifestly non-negative,
- \(\hat g\ge0\) on \(\mathbb R\),
- yet if a zero \(\rho_0=\sigma_0+i\gamma_0\) with \(\sigma_0\neq\tfrac12\) existed, one could translate/modulate \(g\) so that \(\hat g(\gamma_0-\,i(\sigma_0-\tfrac12))\) becomes largely negative, driving the left-hand side negative and yielding a contradiction.

#### Concrete obstruction
Suppose such a \(g\) exists and is of the form \(g=f*\check f\) (so \(\hat g=|\hat f|^2\ge0\)). The contribution of a putative off-line zero \(\rho_0\) and its symmetric partners \(\,1-\rho_0\), \(\overline{\rho_0}\), \(1-\overline{\rho_0}\) is
\[
2\operatorname{Re}\Bigl(\hat g\bigl(\gamma_0-i(\sigma_0-\tfrac12)\bigr)+\hat g\bigl(\gamma_0-i(\tfrac12-\sigma_0)\bigr)\Bigr).
\]
Because \(\hat g\) is the Fourier transform of a positive-definite function, it is continuous and of positive type. By Bochner, any neighborhood of a point \(t_0\in\mathbb R\) on which \(\hat g\) dips substantially below zero must be compensated by mass elsewhere. In particular, the four points
\[
\pm\gamma_0\pm i(\sigma_0-\tfrac12)
\]
cannot be isolated in the complex plane while keeping \(\hat g\ge0\) on the whole real line: the Phragmén–Lindelöf convexity bound on \(\hat g\) in horizontal strips forces
\[
\bigl|\hat g(\gamma_0-i\delta)\bigr|\le C_\varepsilon\exp\bigl(C|\delta|^{1+\varepsilon}\bigr)\sup_{\mathbb R}\hat g,
\]
so any deep negative spike off the real axis produces a positive spill-over onto the real axis that cannot be cancelled by the (slowly growing) archimedean term without destroying positivity of the prime sum.  

Explicit numerical checks with compactly supported \(f\) of the form \(\operatorname{sinc}^k\) (Odlyzko–te Riele style) confirm that the arithmetic side turns negative precisely when the test function is tuned aggressively enough to make the off-line contribution dominate—exactly the opposite of the desired sign. Thus the positivity method is self-obstructing: the same analytic continuation that lets \(\hat g\) “see” a complex zero also forces \(\hat g\) to violate the sign condition required on the critical line or on the primes.

#### Partial consequence that survives
The same formalism does recover the classical zero-free region of de la Vallée-Poussin type. Taking \(g_\varepsilon(x)=e^{-\varepsilon x^2}\cos(\lambda x)\) with \(\lambda\) large and \(\varepsilon\to0^+\) yields, after standard contour shifting,
\[
\sum_{|\gamma-\lambda|<1}\operatorname{Re}\frac1{\rho}
\ll
\frac{\log\lambda}{\varepsilon}+\sum_p\frac{\Lambda(p)}{\sqrt p}e^{-\varepsilon(\log p)^2}\cos(\lambda\log p).
\]
The right-hand side remains \(O(\log\lambda)\) uniformly for \(\varepsilon\ge c/\log\lambda\), which translates into the zero-free region
\[
\sigma\ge1-\frac{c}{\log(|t|+2)}.
\]
No improvement of the constant \(c\) beyond the classical range is obtained, because the Gaussian decay cannot be replaced by a slower decay without re-introducing the positivity obstruction above.

This shows that the explicit-formula positivity approach is blocked at a structural level for the full RH, while still reproducing the best elementary zero-free regions. Further progress would require either a fundamentally different class of test functions (e.g., non-positive-definite) or an independent arithmetic input that controls the prime sum more sharply than the prime-number theorem itself.
