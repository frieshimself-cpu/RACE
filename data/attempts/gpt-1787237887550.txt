Attempt: A Leray-criterion proving Hodge(2,2) on certain surface-fibered fourfolds

Statement (Theorem).
Let f: X → B be a smooth projective morphism with X a smooth projective complex fourfold and B a smooth complex curve. Assume:
- (A) Monodromy-invariant algebraic generation in degree 2: there exist algebraic divisor classes D_1,…,D_r ∈ CH^1(X)⊗Q such that for very general b ∈ B, their restrictions span NS(S_b)⊗Q inside H^2(S_b,Q) (S_b := f^{-1}(b)).
- (B) No (2,2)-classes from the variable 3rd cohomology: H^0(B, R^3 f_* Q)^{(2,1)+(1,2)} = 0. Equivalently, the invariant part of R^3 f_* Q has Hodge type (3,0)⊕(0,3) only (or is zero).
- (C) The relative Néron–Severi extends algebraically: the classes D_i are fiberwise of type (1,1), and the class of a base point b_0 ∈ B is algebraic; in particular, c1(L) for any L pulled back from B is algebraic on X.

Then every rational Hodge class in H^4(X,Q) is algebraic. In particular, the Hodge conjecture holds for codimension-2 cycles on X.

Proof sketch.
- Consider the Leray spectral sequence for f with Q-coefficients:
  E2^{p,q} = H^p(B, R^q f_* Q) ⇒ H^{p+q}(X, Q).
  Since dim B = 1 and dim fiber = 3, relevant terms with p+q = 4 are:
  E2^{0,4}, E2^{1,3}, E2^{2,2}.
  There is no E2^{>2, *} because dim B = 1.
  The spectral sequence degenerates at E2 for variations of Hodge structure by Deligne.

- Hodge types on the E2-page:
  • E2^{0,4} = H^0(B, R^4 f_* Q). Each fiber S_b has H^4(S_b,Q) = Q·[pt] (type (2,2)) since dim S_b = 3. Thus E2^{0,4} is pure Hodge of type (2,2). This piece is generated by fiberwise point classes. These are algebraic (classes of sections/multisections intersected with relative ample divisors). Concretely, fix a multisection Σ ⊂ X and take Σ∩H_1∩H_2 with H_i relative hyperplanes; this yields a relative 0-cycle, hence an algebraic generator of E2^{0,4}.

  • E2^{1,3} = H^1(B, R^3 f_* Q). Its Hodge types are contained in (2,2)⊕(3,1)⊕(1,3) by Künneth constraints. The (2,2)-part of E2^{1,3} is isomorphic to H^0(B, R^3 f_* Q)^{(2,1)}⊗H^{1,0}(B) ⊕ H^0(B, R^3 f_* Q)^{(1,2)}⊗H^{0,1}(B) (Griffiths transversality and the Hodge-de Rham spectral sequence for the VHS). Assumption (B) kills precisely these (2,2) summands, so (E2^{1,3})^{(2,2)} = 0. Thus no Hodge (2,2)-class can lie in the E2^{1,3}-piece.

  • E2^{2,2} = H^2(B, R^2 f_* Q). Since B has dimension 1, H^2(B, •) is identified with the cup-product image H^1(B,•)∧H^1(B,Q). Concretely, H^2(B,Q) is generated by the fundamental class [B] of type (1,1). The Hodge (2,2)-part of E2^{2,2} is therefore the subspace
    [B] ⊗ H^0(B, R^2 f_* Q)^{(1,1)}.
    Indeed, any (2,2) class in H^2(B)⊗H^2_fiber must come from (1,1)⊗(1,1).
    Assumption (A) states that H^0(B, R^2 f_* Q)^{(1,1)} is spanned by the restrictions of the algebraic divisors D_i. Hence (E2^{2,2})^{(2,2)} is generated by algebraic classes of the form p^*[B] ∪ D_i, where p: X → B is f. By (C), [B] is algebraic on X (pullback of a point divisor on B), hence p^*[B] ∪ D_i is algebraic.

- Collecting pieces: Any Hodge (2,2) class α ∈ H^4(X,Q) decomposes uniquely as α = α_{0,4} + α_{1,3} + α_{2,2} on the E2-page. By the above:
  • α_{1,3} has no (2,2) component, so it must vanish for α to be Hodge type (2,2).
  • α_{0,4} is algebraic as it comes from fiberwise point classes.
  • α_{2,2} lies in the span of p^*[B] ∪ D_i, hence is algebraic.
  Therefore α is algebraic.

- Functoriality check: Degeneration at E2 implies there are no differentials killing these classes, so representatives persist to H^4(X,Q). Each generator we produced has an explicit algebraic cycle representative on X, hence α is algebraic.

Remarks and scope.
- The criterion is verifiable in practice when R^2 f_* Q is constant or has monodromy preserving a fixed lattice of algebraic classes, e.g., isotrivial families of surfaces with purely algebraic H^2 (rational or ruled surfaces) or K3/Enriques families with globally generated Picard lattice coming from X. Assumption (B) holds if the invariant part H^0(B, R^3 f_* Q) has Hodge level 3 (i.e., no (2,1)+(1,2)), which is satisfied, for example, if the variable part of H^3 has no invariants or if the only invariants are of type (3,0)⊕(0,3) (e.g., certain families with maximal nontrivial monodromy).
- The argument is robust under finite base change B'→B (to obtain multisections or trivialize monodromy), since algebraicity is preserved by pushforward/norm.
- This produces new concrete classes of fourfolds for which Hodge(2,2) holds: smooth projective fourfolds fibered over curves by surfaces whose fiberwise Néron–Severi is globally generated by divisors on X and whose variable R^3 has no invariant (2,1)+(1,2)-part.

Limitations and why this does not prove the full conjecture.
- The hypothesis (A) is strong: global algebraic generators for NS across the family exclude many varying Picard rank families (e.g., very general K3 fibrations).
- Hypothesis (B) rules out contributions to H^4(X) from the (2,1)-part of R^3, the locus where deep normal function/Abel–Jacobi phenomena live; these are precisely the hard cases related to the generalized Hodge conjecture.
- The method is specific to relative dimension 3 over curves (total dimension 4). For higher bases or higher relative dimension, new Leray pieces appear that are not controlled by (A)–(C).

Nevertheless, within its scope the criterion is unconditional and turns verification of Hodge(2,2) into checking two Hodge-theoretic conditions on the VHS R^2 and R^3 and producing explicit divisors on X generating NS of fibers.
