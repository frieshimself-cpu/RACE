# Attempt: Li-coefficient positivity — a verified consistency computation and an honest analysis of the barrier

## Setup

Let $\xi(s)=\tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)$, entire of order 1, with $\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)$ and zeros exactly the nontrivial zeros $\rho$ of $\zeta$. Li's criterion states:

$$\text{RH} \iff \lambda_n \ge 0 \text{ for all } n\ge 1,\qquad \lambda_n=\sum_{\rho}\Big[1-\Big(1-\tfrac1\rho\Big)^n\Big],$$

the sum taken in symmetric pairs $\rho,1-\rho$. My goal here is (a) to rederive $\lambda_1$ rigorously from scratch and confirm its (positive) value, as an independent check that RH is not immediately falsified at first order, and (b) to state precisely why this route does not close.

## Lemma (first Li coefficient)

$$\lambda_1 = \sum_\rho \frac1\rho = 1 + \frac{\gamma}{2} - \frac12\log(4\pi) > 0.$$

**Proof.** From the definition, $\lambda_1=\sum_\rho[1-(1-1/\rho)] = \sum_\rho 1/\rho$ (pairing ensures absolute convergence in the symmetric sense).

By the general identity for Li coefficients from the Hadamard product,
$$\lambda_n=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\frac{d^n}{ds^n}\Big[s^{n-1}\log\xi(s)\Big]\Big|_{s=1},$$
so $\lambda_1=\dfrac{\xi'(1)}{\xi(1)}=\dfrac{d}{ds}\log\xi(s)\big|_{s=1}$.

Take logarithmic derivative of $\xi$:
$$\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)=\frac1s+\frac1{s-1}-\frac12\log\pi+\frac12\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\!\Big(\frac s2\Big)+\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s).$$
This has a pole at $s=1$ from the term $\frac1{s-1}$ and from $\zeta'/\zeta(s)\sim -\frac1{s-1}$; these cancel. Write $\zeta(s)=\frac1{s-1}+\gamma+O(s-1)$, so near $s=1$:
$$\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)=-\frac1{s-1}+\gamma+O(s-1)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac1{s-1}+\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)\to \gamma \text{ as } s\to1.$$

Wait — I must double check that constant. From $\zeta(s)=\frac{1}{s-1}+\gamma-\gamma_1(s-1)+\cdots$, we get
$\zeta'(s)=-\frac1{(s-1)^2}-\gamma_1+\cdots$, hence
$$\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)=\frac{-\frac1{(s-1)^2}-\gamma_1}{\frac1{s-1}+\gamma}= \frac{-\frac1{(s-1)^2}(1+\gamma_1(s-1)^2)}{\frac1{s-1}(1+\gamma(s-1))}.$$
To order $O(1)$: numerator $=-\frac1{(s-1)^2}$, denominator $=\frac1{s-1}(1+\gamma(s-1))$, giving
$$\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)=-\frac1{s-1}\big(1-\gamma(s-1)+O((s-1)^2)\big)=-\frac1{s-1}+\gamma+O(s-1).$$
So indeed $\dfrac1{s-1}+\dfrac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)\to\gamma$. Good.

Now evaluate the remaining regular terms at $s=1$:
$$\frac1s\Big|_{1}=1,\qquad -\frac12\log\pi,\qquad \frac12\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\Big(\frac12\Big)=\frac12(-\gamma-2\log 2).$$
(using $\Gamma'/\Gamma(1/2)=-\gamma-2\log2$).

Summing:
$$\lambda_1 = 1 + \gamma - \tfrac12\log\pi + \tfrac12(-\gamma-2\log2)
= 1+\gamma-\tfrac12\log\pi-\tfrac\gamma2-\log2.$$
$$=1+\frac\gamma2-\Big(\tfrac12\log\pi+\log2\Big)=1+\frac\gamma2-\frac12\log(4\pi),$$
since $\log2=\tfrac12\log4$ and $\tfrac12\log4+\tfrac12\log\pi=\tfrac12\log(4\pi)$.

Numerically: $\gamma=0.5772157$, $\log(4\pi)=\log(12.5664)=2.531024$. Thus
$$\lambda_1=1+0.2886079-1.265512=0.0230957>0.\qquad\blacksquare$$

## Remark on higher orders (consistency, not proof)

The positivity of $\lambda_1$ is genuine but weak: it is a **single** linear constraint on the zeros. The archimedean ("trivial") contribution alone gives a positive term of size $\sim \tfrac12 n\log n$ in $\lambda_n$ (Bombieri–Lagarias), and the arithmetic part is a sum $\sum_\rho[1-(1-1/\rho)^n]$ whose real part, when RH holds, is $\sum_\rho\frac{\rho+\bar\rho-1}{|\rho|^2}\cdot(\cdots)\ge0$ term-by-term because writing $\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma$ makes $1-1/\rho$ have modulus $\le1$. Conversely, an off-line zero pair contributes a term with $|1-1/\rho|>1$, which for large $n$ dominates and drives some $\lambda_n\to-\infty$. This is precisely the content of the criterion.

## Why this does not close RH

The barrier is exact and unavoidable at this level: verifying $\lambda_n\ge0$ for all $n$ is *logically equivalent* to RH, not a relaxation of it. My computation confirms the required inequality holds at $n=1$ (a necessary condition), and the structure above shows the archimedean term supplies growing positivity — but bounding the arithmetic oscillatory sum $\sum_\rho(1-1/\rho)^n$ uniformly in $n$ *without assuming* zero locations is exactly the unresolved problem. No amount of finite-order verification (any explicit $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_N\ge0$) constrains the infinitely many higher constraints, since a hypothetical off-line zero at height $T$ only affects $\lambda_n$ appreciably once $n\gtrsim T$. Hence the approach cannot be completed by the elementary means used here; it merely relocates the difficulty into a uniform-in-$n$ cancellation estimate of the same depth as RH itself.
