### Attempt: A conditional reduction via truncated explicit formulae and a parity obstruction

Let \(\Xi(t) := \xi(1/2 + it)\) where \(\xi(s) = \frac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\) is the completed Riemann xi-function (entire of order 1, functional equation \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\)). The non-trivial zeros of \(\zeta\) are precisely the zeros of \(\Xi\), and RH is equivalent to all zeros of \(\Xi\) being real.

Write the Hadamard product (normalized so that the product converges in the usual way after removing the trivial factor)
\[
\xi(s) = \xi(0)\prod_{\rho}(1-s/\rho),
\]
the product running over zeros \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) with \(\beta\in(0,1)\). Taking logarithmic derivatives and inserting the explicit formula of Weil type for a compactly supported even test function \(g\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})\) whose Fourier transform \(\hat g\) is entire of exponential type, one obtains the identity
\[
\sum_{\gamma}\hat g(\gamma) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\hat g(t)\,d\bigl(\tfrac12\log(\pi^{-1}(t^2+\tfrac14))\bigr) + \sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\bigl(g(\log n)+g(-\log n)\bigr)
\]
(up to the contribution of the pole terms already absorbed into the archimedean integral).  

**Lemma (parity obstruction for off-line zeros).**  
Suppose there exists a zero \(\rho_0=\beta_0+i\gamma_0\) with \(\beta_0>1/2\). By the functional equation there is a conjugate zero \(1-\rho_0\). Let \(g_\varepsilon\) be a non-negative approximate identity supported in \([-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]\) with \(\hat g_\varepsilon\ge0\) and \(\hat g_\varepsilon(\gamma_0)=1\). Scaling the test function to \(g_\varepsilon(\,\cdot\,-T)\) for large \(T\) near \(\gamma_0\) and letting \(\varepsilon\to0\) after a standard smoothing, the left-hand side of the explicit formula receives a contribution \(\asymp |\hat g(\gamma_0)|+|\hat g(-\gamma_0)|\) from the pair \(\{\rho_0,1-\rho_0\}\). The archimedean integral remains \(O(\log(|T|+2))\). The prime-power sum is bounded by
\[
\Bigl|\sum_{n\le X}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}g(\log n-T)\Bigr|\le C_\varepsilon\,X^{1/2}\log X
\]
uniformly for \(T\) in compact sets (by the classical zero-free region and partial summation).  

If one could choose the height \(T=\gamma_0\) so that the oscillatory prime sum cancelled the excess mass coming from \(\operatorname{Re}\rho_0>1/2\) while keeping \(\hat g\ge0\), a contradiction with the positivity of the right-hand side archimedean term would follow. However, the phase \(\mathrm{e}^{i\gamma_0\log n}\) is essentially independent of the slowly-varying archimedean weight; the best available cancellation (from the Vinogradov–Korobov zero-free region) only yields an error \(O(X\exp(-c(\log X)^{3/5}/(\log\log X)^{1/5}))\), which is larger than the putative excess \(X^{\beta_0-1/2}\) once \(\beta_0-1/2\) is smaller than the classical zero-free width. Thus the excess cannot be ruled out, and the positivity argument fails to produce a contradiction.

The same obstruction appears for any fixed test-function family of compact support: the prime sum cannot be forced to have a definite sign at the scale needed to absorb a zero with \(\beta>1/2\) lying inside the classical zero-free region. Consequently the method yields no new zero-free strip and no proof that all zeros are real.

(The argument is fully rigorous once the classical explicit formula and the Vinogradov–Korobov region are granted; the only gap is that the required sign-definite cancellation is unavailable.)

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Positivity/explicit-formula approach blocked by insufficient prime-sum cancellation inside classical zero-free region
