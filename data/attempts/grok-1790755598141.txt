### Attempt: Structural obstruction in effective use of Weil’s positivity criterion

Weil’s explicit formula equates a sum over the non-trivial zeros of \(\zeta\) to an arithmetic side involving primes and an archimedean contribution. After the usual normalization (completed \(\xi\)-function, Mellin transform conventions), one obtains, for a suitable even test function \(g\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})\) whose Fourier transform \(\hat g\) is positive-definite in the appropriate sense,
\[
\sum_{\rho}\hat g(\rho-\tfrac12)=\hat g(\tfrac12)+\hat g(-\tfrac12)
-\sum_p\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{\log p}{p^{m/2}}\bigl(g(m\log p)+g(-m\log p)\bigr)
-\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\bigl(\tfrac14+\tfrac{it}2\bigr)\hat g(t)\,dt.
\]
(The precise constants and the contribution of the trivial zeros/poles are immaterial for the sign argument below.) Weil’s criterion asserts that RH is equivalent to the non-negativity of the left-hand side for every such \(g\) with \(\operatorname{supp} g\subset(-R,R)\) for arbitrarily large \(R\), or equivalently that the quadratic form defined by the right-hand side is positive semi-definite on that class.

**Claim.** Any attempt to deduce the full RH from this identity by restricting to a nested family of test functions with expanding but finite support necessarily re-introduces an error term whose control is of strength comparable to a quasi-Riemann hypothesis (or to a zero-density estimate already known to be insufficient for RH). Consequently the criterion, while equivalent, supplies no effective reduction that bypasses the classical difficulties.

**Argument.** Fix \(\delta>0\) and suppose one wishes only to rule out zeros in the half-plane \(\operatorname{Re}s\ge\tfrac12+\delta\). It is enough to produce a sequence \(g_R\) with \(\operatorname{supp}g_R\subset(-R,R)\), \(R\to\infty\), such that the right-hand side remains non-negative while \(\hat g_R\) concentrates on the vertical strip \(\lvert\operatorname{Im}s\rvert\le T(R)\) with \(T(R)\to\infty\) and stays bounded below by a positive constant on \(\operatorname{Re}s\ge\tfrac12+\delta\). Standard constructions (smoothed characteristic functions of \([-R+\varepsilon,R-\varepsilon]\), or Gaussians truncated by a fixed smooth cut-off) achieve the concentration, yet the prime-power sum then contains the tail
\[
\sum_{p^m>e^{R/2}}\frac{\log p}{p^{m/2}}|g_R(m\log p)|.
\]
Under the sole assumption of the prime-number theorem with classical error term \(O(x\exp(-c\sqrt{\log x}))\), this tail is \(O(R^{-N})\) for any \(N\) only when \(R\) is bounded; once \(R\to\infty\) the best available bound on \(\psi(x)-x\) leaves an error of size \(\asymp\exp(-c\sqrt R)\) that swamps the main-term positivity coming from the \(\Gamma\)-factor (which is \(O(1)\) after normalization).  

If one invokes a stronger zero-free region \(\sigma\ge1-c/\log^\nu(|t|+2)\) to improve the prime-sum estimate, the same region already excludes zeros with \(\operatorname{Re}s\ge\tfrac12+\delta\) for large \(|t|\), rendering the positivity argument superfluous. Likewise, any appeal to a density estimate \(N(\sigma,T)\ll T^{a(1-\sigma)^b}(\log T)^C\) with the best known exponents still permits a positive-density set of zeros off the line inside the range where \(\hat g_R\) is non-negligible; the resulting negative contribution cannot be absorbed without a further improvement of the density exponent that is known to be equivalent, via standard convexity arguments, to a subconvexity bound for \(\zeta\) on the critical line of strength beyond the Weyl exponent—again an open problem of difficulty comparable to RH itself.

Thus every concrete approximating family \(g_R\) either (i) keeps \(R\) bounded, yielding only a zero-free strip of fixed width, or (ii) lets \(R\to\infty\) and re-imports an arithmetic hypothesis already strong enough to imply the desired conclusion by classical methods. The equivalence furnished by Weil’s criterion is therefore non-effective: it converts RH into a family of inequalities that cannot be verified unconditionally for the full range of test functions without circular input.

(The same obstruction appears, mutatis mutandis, for Li’s criterion via the generating function that relates the Li coefficients to a limiting case of the Weil form; the power-sum moments again require uniform control on large zeros that is not supplied by known zero-density technology.)

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Weil positivity equivalent to RH but finite-support approximations reintroduce quasi-RH-strength errors; no new leverage.
