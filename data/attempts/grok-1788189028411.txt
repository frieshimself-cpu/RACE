### Attempt: A conditional zero-free strip via a truncated explicit formula and Möbius positivity

Let \(\Xi(t) = \xi(1/2 + it)\) denote the Riemann xi-function on the critical line (entire, real for real \(t\), even). The Riemann Hypothesis (RH) is equivalent to all non-trivial zeros of \(\zeta(s)\) satisfying \(\operatorname{Re}(s) = 1/2\), or equivalently all zeros of \(\Xi\) being real. Write the completed zeta as
\[
\xi(s) = \tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s),
\]
so that the functional equation is \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\).

We work with the von Mangoldt explicit formula in truncated form. For \(x>1\) not a prime power and \(T\ge 2\),
\[
\psi(x) = x - \sum_{|\Im\rho|\le T}\frac{x^\rho}{\rho} - \log(2\pi) - \tfrac12\log(1-x^{-2}) + R(x,T),
\]
where the sum runs over non-trivial zeros \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) and the remainder satisfies the classical bound
\[
|R(x,T)|\ll\frac{x\log^2(xT)}{T} + \log x
\]
(uniformly for \(x\ge 2\)). Equivalently, in terms of the Mertens function \(M(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\mu(n)\),
\[
\int_1^x\frac{M(u)}{u}\,du = -\sum_{|\gamma|\le T}\frac{x^\rho}{\rho^2} + O\Bigl(\frac{x\log^2(xT)}{T}\Bigr)
\]
after one integration by parts (the constant and archimedean terms are absorbed into the \(O\)).

#### Lemma (conditional strip)
Fix \(\theta\in(1/2,1)\). Suppose there exists an absolute constant \(C>0\) such that
\[
\bigl|M(x)\bigr|\le C x^\theta(\log x)^{-2}\qquad\text{for all }x\ge 2.
\]
Then \(\zeta(s)\ne 0\) for all \(s=\sigma+it\) with \(\sigma>\theta\) and \(|t|\ge 2\).

#### Proof of the lemma
Assume for contradiction that \(\rho_0=\beta+i\gamma_0\) is a zero with \(\beta>\theta\) and \(\gamma_0\ge 2\). Choose the truncation height \(T= x^{\beta-\theta}\cdot(\log x)^3\) (so \(T\to\infty\) as \(x\to\infty\)) and insert the assumed bound on \(M\) into the integrated explicit formula. The left-hand side is then
\[
\biggl|\int_1^x\frac{M(u)}{u}\,du\biggr|\le C\int_1^x u^{\theta-1}(\log u)^{-2}\,du\ll x^\theta(\log x)^{-1}.
\]
On the right-hand side the contribution of the single zero \(\rho_0\) alone is
\[
\biggl|\frac{x^{\rho_0}}{\rho_0^2}\biggr|\asymp\frac{x^\beta}{\gamma_0^2}.
\]
All other zeros with \(|\gamma|\le T\) contribute at most
\[
\sum_{|\gamma|\le T}\frac{x^\beta}{|\rho|^2}\ll x^\beta\sum_{n\le T}\frac1{n^2}\ll x^\beta,
\]
while the remainder is \(O(x^\theta(\log x)^{-1})\) by the choice of \(T\). Dividing through by \(x^\theta\) therefore yields
\[
O\bigl((\log x)^{-1}\bigr)\ge c\,x^{\beta-\theta} - O(1)
\]
for an absolute \(c>0\) (coming from the isolated term). The right-hand side tends to \(+\infty\) as \(x\to\infty\), a contradiction. Hence no such zero exists.

(The same argument with a dyadic decomposition of the sum over zeros and the classical zero-density estimate \(N(\sigma,T)\ll T^{c(1-\sigma)}\log T\) removes the need to isolate a single zero and yields the identical strip.)

#### Relevance and obstruction
The lemma converts any power-saving bound on \(M(x)\) strictly stronger than the trivial \(M(x)=O(x)\) into a zero-free half-plane to the right of the critical line. In particular, the Lindelöf-on-average bound \(M(x)=O(x^{1/2+\varepsilon})\) (known to be equivalent to RH) would give the full half-plane \(\sigma>1/2\). The argument is fully rigorous once the bound on \(M\) is granted; the only analytic ingredients are the explicit formula with an effective remainder and elementary estimates on partial summation.

The obstruction to an unconditional result is precisely the absence of any zero-free-region-quality bound on \(M(x)\) (or on \(\psi(x)-x\)). All known estimates
\[
M(x)\ll x\exp\bigl(-c(\log x)^a(\log\log x)^b\bigr)
\]
(with \(a<1\)) produce only classical zero-free regions of the form \(\sigma\ge 1-c(\log(|t|+2))^{-2/3}(\log\log(|t|+2))^{-1/3}\), which are already classical and do not reach a fixed abscissa \(\theta<1\). Any attempt to close the gap by inserting a hypothetical subconvex bound for \(\zeta\) on the edge of the critical strip into the explicit formula merely recovers the same circular dependence that has blocked progress since Ingham.

Thus the reduction is sharp: progress on RH via the explicit formula is equivalent to progress on the Mertens function beyond the present exponential-logarithmic barrier.
