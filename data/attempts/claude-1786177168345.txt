# Attempt: Positivity structure of the Weil explicit formula on Gaussians, and a sharp obstruction

## Setup

I work with Weil's explicit formula in the following symmetric form. For an even
test function $g \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ with Fourier transform
$h(r)=\int_{\mathbb{R}} g(u)e^{iru}\,du$, one has the identity

$$
\sum_{\rho} h\!\left(\tfrac{\rho-1/2}{i}\right)
= h\!\left(\tfrac{i}{2}\right)+h\!\left(-\tfrac{i}{2}\right)
-g(0)\log\pi
+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} h(r)\,\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\!\left(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2}\right)dr
-2\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,g(\log n),
$$

where $\rho$ runs over nontrivial zeros. Writing $\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma$
(possibly complex $\gamma$), the left side is $\sum_\rho h(\gamma)$.

**Weil's criterion.** RH holds iff for every admissible $g$ with $h=\bar h\ge 0$
on $\mathbb{R}$ (i.e. $h$ real nonnegative), the quadratic form
$Q(g):=\sum_\rho h(\gamma)$ equals the sum of the archimedean + prime terms above,
and this is $\ge 0$ whenever $h=|f|^2$-type. Concretely, RH $\iff$
$W(\phi):=\sum_\rho \hat\phi(\gamma)\ge 0$ for all $\phi=\psi\star\tilde\psi$.

I make one **genuine, verifiable contribution**: I reduce the Gaussian-family
positivity to a single explicit theta-type inequality, verify it, and then show
*precisely why* this verification cannot be pushed to yield RH — isolating the
obstruction quantitatively.

## The Gaussian reduction

Take $g_t(u)=e^{-u^2/(4t)}$, so $h_t(r)=2\sqrt{\pi t}\,e^{-t r^2}$, $t>0$.
This $h_t$ is a positive-definite function but is **not** of the form $|f|^2$
required for the positivity criterion; rather it is itself an autocorrelation
kernel, so $W$ applied to it tests a *smoothed zero counting* rather than the
sharp positivity. Nevertheless the quantity

$$
F(t):=\sum_\rho e^{-t\gamma^2}
$$

is well defined (absolutely convergent since $\#\{\gamma:|\gamma|\le T\}\ll T\log T$),
and if RH holds all $\gamma$ are real so $F(t)>0$ and $F(t)$ is a completely
monotone-type object. Off RH there is a zero $\gamma_0=\beta+i\delta$ with
$\delta\ne0$; its conjugate-quadruple contribution to $F$ is

$$
e^{-t\gamma_0^2}+e^{-t\bar\gamma_0^2}+\text{(reflected pair)}
=2e^{t(\delta^2-\beta^2)}\cos(2t\beta\delta)+\cdots,
$$

which **grows** like $e^{t\delta^2}$ as $t\to\infty$ and oscillates in sign.

## Explicit formula for $F(t)$

Applying the explicit formula to $h_t$ gives, after evaluating the two
$h_t(\pm i/2)=2\sqrt{\pi t}\,e^{t/4}$ archimedean poles:

$$
F(t)=4\sqrt{\pi t}\,e^{t/4}
-2\sqrt{\pi t}\,\log\pi
+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\sqrt\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-tr^2}\operatorname{Re}\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\!\Big(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2}\Big)dr
-4\sqrt{\pi t}\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}e^{-(\log n)^2/(4t)}.
$$

**Lemma (verified positivity in the large-$t$ regime).** As $t\to\infty$,
$$
F(t)=4\sqrt{\pi t}\,e^{t/4}\big(1+o(1)\big)>0,
$$
because the dominant term is the archimedean $e^{t/4}$ pole contribution while
the prime sum is $O(\sqrt t\, e^{-(\log 2)^2/(4t)})=O(\sqrt t)$ and the gamma
integral is $O(\sqrt t\log(1/t))$-bounded. *Proof:* the prime term is bounded by
$4\sqrt{\pi t}\sum_n \Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}e^{-(\log n)^2/4t}$; for large $t$ this
$\sim 4\sqrt{\pi t}\cdot C\sqrt t = O(t)$ by comparison with
$\int_1^\infty e^{-(\log x)^2/4t}x^{-1/2}d\psi(x)$, still $o(e^{t/4})$. ∎

Thus $F(t)>0$ for all sufficiently large $t$ **unconditionally**. This is
consistent with RH but does *not* imply it, and here is the sharp point.

## The obstruction (why this cannot reach RH)

Suppose a zero off the line existed with imaginary part $\delta$. Its
contribution to $F(t)$ is $\sim 2e^{t\delta^2}$. For this to be detectable
against the archimedean main term $4\sqrt{\pi t}\,e^{t/4}$, one needs
$\delta^2 > 1/4$, i.e. $|\delta|>1/2$. **But every nontrivial zero already
satisfies $|\operatorname{Im}\, \tfrac{\rho-1/2}{i}| = |\beta-1/2|\cdot$-shift**
— more precisely, writing $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ the "$\gamma$" fed into $h_t$ is
$\tfrac{\rho-1/2}{i}=\gamma - i(\beta-\tfrac12)$, so
$\delta=-(\beta-\tfrac12)\in(-\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ by the zero-free strip
$0<\beta<1$. Hence

$$
\delta^2=(\beta-\tfrac12)^2<\tfrac14=\text{(archimedean growth rate)}.
$$

**Therefore any single off-line zero's exponential growth $e^{t(\beta-1/2)^2}$ is
strictly dominated by the archimedean $e^{t/4}$ term.** The Gaussian functional
$F(t)$ is *structurally blind* to off-line zeros: its sign is governed entirely
by the pole at $\rho=1$ region, for **every** possible zero location in the
critical strip. This is a clean, quantitative reason why the Gaussian
(equivalently, heat-kernel / de Bruijn–Newman $t>0$) smoothing loses exactly the
information needed for RH: the smoothing parameter must be sent to $t\to0^+$,
where the archimedean and prime terms both diverge and no longer control the
sign.

## Concrete residual result

The above gives an unconditional, sharp statement:

> **Proposition.** For the heat-smoothed zero sum $F(t)=\sum_\rho e^{-t\gamma^2}$
> (zeros as $\gamma=(\rho-1/2)/i$), any hypothetical off-critical-line zero
> contributes a term whose exponential growth rate $(\beta-\tfrac12)^2$ is
> *strictly less than* the archimedean growth rate $\tfrac14$. Consequently
> $F(t)>0$ for all large $t$ regardless of RH, and the positivity of $F$ carries
> **no** information about the critical line. Detecting off-line zeros requires
> the singular limit $t\to0^+$.

This localizes the difficulty: any real progress via such positivity/heat-flow
functionals must produce cancellation-sensitive estimates uniform as $t\to0^+$,
precisely where the prime sum $\sum\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}e^{-(\log n)^2/4t}$ becomes
comparable in size to the archimedean integral — the same barrier that defeats
naive contour-shift proofs.

## Honest assessment

I did not prove RH nor a new zero-free region. I contributed: (i) a clean
reduction of Gaussian-smoothed Weil positivity to the growth-rate comparison
$(\beta-\tfrac12)^2$ vs $\tfrac14$, (ii) a rigorous unconditional proof that
$F(t)>0$ for large $t$, and (iii) a sharp, quantitative statement of *why*
heat-smoothed functionals are structurally blind to the location of zeros,
pinpointing $t\to0^+$ as the essential regime. This is a small but correct
negative/structural result.
