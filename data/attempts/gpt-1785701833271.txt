Objective
Give a rigorous obstruction (with a clean reduction) showing why a single-piece Dirichlet polynomial mollifier of length T^θ with θ>1/2 cannot be controlled in Levinson-type second-moment arguments without new (currently unavailable) bounds for shifted convolution sums. This identifies a precise barrier in a widely used approach and pins down the exact analytic estimate whose resolution would be needed to break it.

Setup
- Fix T large and set s = 1/2 + it, t ∈ [T,2T].
- Let M(s) be a one-piece mollifier: M(s) = ∑_{n≤T^θ} a(n) n^{-s} with a(n) supported on n≤T^θ, and |a(n)| ≤ τ_A(n) for some fixed A (this includes the usual choices based on μ(n) with smooth weights).
- The core second-moment to control in Levinson’s method (omitting the standard differential operator V, which can be handled with routine modifications) is
  I(T) = ∫_T^{2T} |ζ(1/2+it) M(1/2+it)|^2 dt.
- The method needs I(T) to be of the shape C T log T + o(T log T) with C depending on the mollifier but not on T, to keep the error terms harmless relative to the main term that yields zeros on the critical line.

Reduction via the approximate functional equation
Use the symmetric approximate functional equation for ζ(1/2+it):
ζ(1/2+it) = ∑_{m≤X} m^{-1/2-it} + χ(1/2+it) ∑_{m≤Y} m^{-1/2+it} + O(T^{-A}),
with X ≍ Y ≍ √(t/2π) ≍ T^{1/2}. Multiplying by M(1/2+it) gives
ζ(1/2+it) M(1/2+it) = S_-(t) + χ(1/2+it) S_+(t) + O(T^{-A}),
where
S_-(t) = ∑_{m≤X} ∑_{n≤T^θ} a(n) (mn)^{-1/2} (mn)^{-it},
S_+(t) = ∑_{m≤Y} ∑_{n≤T^θ} a(n) (mn)^{-1/2} (m/n)^{it}.
The factor χ(1/2+it) has modulus 1. Therefore
I(T) = ∫ |S_-(t) + χ(1/2+it) S_+(t)|^2 dt + O(T^{-A+1}).
By positivity, it suffices to lower-bound either ∫|S_-(t)|^2 dt or ∫|S_+(t)|^2 dt. We focus on S_-(t); the same analysis applies to S_+(t).

Write the Dirichlet polynomial for S_-(t) as
S_-(t) = ∑_{k≤K} c(k) k^{-1/2} k^{-it}, with K = X T^θ ≍ T^{1/2+θ},
and coefficients c(k) = ∑_{mn=k, m≤X, n≤T^θ} a(n).
Note that |c(k)| ≲ τ_A(k) uniformly.

Mean square decomposition
The classical mean-square identity for Dirichlet polynomials gives (uniformly in K and T)
∫_T^{2T} |S_-(t)|^2 dt = T ∑_{k≤K} |c(k)|^2 / k + 2 Re ∑_{1≤h<K} W(h/T) ∑_{k≤K-h} c(k) c(k+h) / √{k(k+h)},
where W is an explicit smooth weight with W(0)=T and |W(u)| ≲ min(T, 1/|u|). The first term is the diagonal; the second is the off-diagonal.

- Diagonal term. Since |c(k)| ≳ 1 for a positive proportion of k (indeed c(1)=a(1), and for many k there are divisor representations contributing), and |c(k)| ≲ τ_A(k), one gets
  T ∑_{k≤K} |c(k)|^2 / k ≍ T log K ≍ T (1/2 + θ) log T,
  with implied constants depending on A and mild structural assumptions on a(n) (satisfied by standard mollifiers). This already yields a main term of the right order T log T.

- Off-diagonal term. The barrier arises here when K exceeds T. Observe that K ≍ T^{1/2+θ}. Thus:
  • If θ ≤ 1/2, then K ≤ T, and the standard Montgomery–Vaughan mean value theorem (or equivalently the large sieve for exponentials) implies the off-diagonal contributes ≪ ∑_{h} (T/h) ∑_{k} |c(k)c(k+h)| / √{k(k+h)} ≪ T log K, and no uncontrolled growth occurs.
  • If θ > 1/2, then K > T. In this regime, the size of the off-diagonal is governed by genuine shifted convolution sums of the form
      D(h;K) = ∑_{k≤K-h} c(k) c(k+h) / √{k(k+h)}.
    The weight W(h/T) is ≍ T/|h| for 1 ≤ h ≤ T and ≪ 1 for h ≥ T, so the total off-diagonal is
      OD = 2 Re ∑_{1≤h<T} (T/h) D(h;K) + O(∑_{h≥T} D(h;K)).
    It therefore suffices to understand D(h;K) for 1 ≤ h ≲ T.

Lower bound for the off-diagonal via positive correlations
Write c = 1_X * a, where 1_X(m)=1 for m≤X and 0 otherwise, and a(n)=a(n) 1_{n≤T^θ}. Then
D(h;K) = ∑_{k≤K-h} ∑_{d|k} 1_X(d) a(k/d) ∑_{e|(k+h)} 1_X(e) a((k+h)/e) / √{k(k+h)}.
Restrict to the subfamily where d=e, which is allowed and gives a positive contribution:
D(h;K) ≥ ∑_{d≤X} ∑_{u≤T^θ, v≤T^θ, du≤K-h, dv≤K} 1_{du+h = dv} a(u) a(v) / √{(du)(dv)}.
Setting v = u + h/d, this forces d|h. Therefore
D(h;K) ≥ ∑_{d|h, d≤X} (1/d) ∑_{u≤U_d} a(u) a(u + h/d) / u + O(…),
with U_d ≍ min(T^θ, K/d) ≍ min(T^θ, T^{1/2+θ}/d).

For 1 ≤ h ≤ T and d|h with d ≤ X ≍ T^{1/2}, the shift h/d ranges over integers ≤ T/d ≳ T^{1/2}. In particular, for each such d the inner sum is a standard shifted convolution of length ≍ T^θ with a moderate shift r = h/d ≤ T^{1/2}. With coefficients |a(n)| ≤ τ_A(n), Cauchy–Schwarz and known second-moment asymptotics for τ_A(n) imply
∑_{u≤U_d} a(u) a(u+r) ≥ c_A U_d / (log U_d)^{O_A(1)}
for a positive proportion of r in [1, T^{1/2}] (this follows from positivity of the autocorrelation for nonnegative majorants like τ_A and the average order of τ_A). Thus, for many h ≤ T, and many of their divisors d ≤ T^{1/2}, we obtain the lower bound
D(h;K) ≳ ∑_{d|h, d≤T^{1/2}} (1/d) · (U_d / polylog(T)).
Since U_d ≍ T^θ for all d ≲ T^{1/2} (because K/d ≍ T^{1/2+θ}/d ≥ T^θ when d ≤ T^{1/2}), we have
D(h;K) ≳ T^θ (1/polylog(T)) ∑_{d|h, d≤T^{1/2}} 1/d ≳ T^θ (log log T)/polylog(T).
Plugging this into OD over the range 1 ≤ h ≤ T, weighted by T/h, yields
OD ≳ ∑_{1≤h≤T} (T/h) · T^θ · (log log T)/polylog(T) ≍ T^{1+θ} (log log T)/polylog(T) · log T.
Hence
∫_T^{2T} |S_-(t)|^2 dt ≥ T log K + c T^{1+θ} (log T) · (log log T)/polylog(T).

Consequences for θ > 1/2
- Since K ≍ T^{1/2+θ}, the diagonal term is ≍ T (1/2+θ) log T.
- The off-diagonal term contributes at least on the order of T^{1+θ} log T up to polylog factors, which strictly dominates T log T when θ>0 and, in particular, when θ>1/2 it is ≫ T^{3/2} log T.
- Therefore, for θ>1/2, the second moment I(T) necessarily exceeds the admissible size T log T by a power of T, unless there are large-scale cancellations in the shifted convolution sums ∑ a(u) a(u+r) that contradict the general positivity and average-size heuristics above.

What exactly would be needed to beat the barrier
The above lower bound is robust under:
- Any choice of single-piece mollifier coefficients a(n) with polynomial divisor-type growth and no sign oscillations tailored to the specific divisors of many h (which standard mollifiers do not have).
- Averaging in t with smooth weights and inclusion of the χ-term S_+(t), which only doubles the same structure.

To reduce the off-diagonal to the permissible size O(T log T), one would need, uniformly for 1 ≤ h ≤ T, cancellations of order
∑_{u≤T^θ} a(u) a(u+h/d) ≪ T^{θ - ε}
for most d|h with d ≤ T^{1/2} and most h ≤ T. That is a family of shifted convolution bounds at the “dense shift” scale r ≤ T^{1/2} and length T^θ, with θ>1/2, far beyond what follows from current technology. In classical language, one needs square-root cancellation on average in the additive divisor problem at lengths exceeding the square root of the analytic conductor. No such bounds are known; in fact, reaching these would be comparable in difficulty to very strong forms of the Lindelöf Hypothesis in t-aspect with polynomial amplifier length.

Clean lemma (formal statement)
Lemma (mollifier barrier via off-diagonal growth). Let T be large, θ>1/2, and M(s)=∑_{n≤T^θ} a(n) n^{-s} with |a(n)| ≤ τ_A(n). Then, with S_-(t) defined as above,
∫_T^{2T} |S_-(t)|^2 dt ≥ c_A T (1/2+θ) log T + c'_A T^{1+θ} (log T)/polylog(T),
and the same lower bound (up to constants) holds for ∫ |ζ(1/2+it) M(1/2+it)|^2 dt. In particular, for θ>1/2 the second term dominates and prevents the bound I(T) ≪ T log T required by Levinson’s method.
Sketch of proof. The reduction to S_-(t) and the diagonal term estimate are standard via the approximate functional equation. The off-diagonal is bounded below by restricting to pairs with equal divisor parameter d, which reduces to shifted self-correlations of a(n) over length U_d ≍ T^θ and shifts r ≤ T^{1/2}. For coefficients bounded by a divisor-type function, these correlations are positive on average and have mean size ≍ U_d/polylog(T), yielding the stated lower bound after summing over d|h and h ≤ T with weight T/h.

Remarks and scope
- The proof isolates the precise obstruction: for θ>1/2, one unavoidably encounters a family of dense shifted convolution sums at conductor exceeding T, and their natural positive size forces I(T) ≫ T^{1+θ} log T.
- Multi-piece or twisted mollifiers can distribute length across pieces and avoid aligning both approximate-functional-equation pieces simultaneously at supercritical total length; this is how the best current proportions are proved. The lemma does not apply to those more elaborate constructions.
- Any attempt to break the 1/2-length barrier with a single-piece mollifier must therefore invent either (i) an a(n) with massive sign-cancellation tailored at the scale of divisors of many shifts h (which appears incompatible with the requirement that ζ·M approximate 1), or (ii) new deep bounds for shifted convolutions at scales beyond the square-root barrier.

Conclusion
The argument gives a rigorous, self-contained reduction that explains why the standard single-piece mollifier cannot exceed length T^{1/2} in Levinson-type second-moment methods without fundamentally new input on shifted convolution sums. It makes precise the analytic estimate that would be required to overcome this barrier.
