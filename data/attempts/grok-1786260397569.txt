### Attempt: A conditional reduction via truncated explicit formulae and a spectral gap obstruction

Let \(\Xi(t) := \xi(1/2 + it)\) denote the completed, real-entire Riemann \(\xi\)-function (normalized so that the functional equation is \(\Xi(t) = \Xi(-t)\)). Write the non-trivial zeros as \(\rho = 1/2 + i\gamma\) (without assuming \(\operatorname{Re}\rho = 1/2\)). The classical explicit formula of Weil, truncated at height \(T\) and smoothed by a positive even test function \(g\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})\) whose Fourier transform \(\hat g\) is supported in \([-1,1]\), reads
\[
\sum_{|\gamma|<T} g(\gamma) = \frac{g(0)}{2}\log\pi + \int_0^\infty\frac{g'(x)}{e^{x/2}-e^{-x/2}}\,dx
- \sum_p\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\log p}{p^{k/2}}\hat g(k\log p) + O\bigl(e^{c|\operatorname{supp}\hat g|}\bigr)T^{-1}\|g\|_{H^1}.
\]
(The error arises from the horizontal integrals and the trivial zeros; the constant \(c\) is absolute.)

Assume, for contradiction, that a zero \(\rho_0 = \sigma_0 + i\gamma_0\) exists with \(\sigma_0 > 1/2\) and \(|\gamma_0|\) minimal among all such off-line zeros. Choose the test function to be a Gaussian mollifier
\[
g_\varepsilon(t) = e^{-\varepsilon t^2}\cdot\operatorname{sinc}^2\bigl(\tfrac{t-\gamma_0}{2}\bigr),\qquad\varepsilon>0,
\]
scaled so that \(\hat g_\varepsilon\) remains supported inside a slightly enlarged interval of length \(1+O(\varepsilon)\). Substituting and isolating the contribution of \(\rho_0\) (and its conjugate and reflection \(1-\rho_0\)) produces the lower bound
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower}
\operatorname{Re}\sum_{\rho\sim\rho_0} g_\varepsilon(\gamma) \ge c(\sigma_0-1/2)e^{-\varepsilon\gamma_0^2} - O(\varepsilon^{1/2}),
\end{equation}
where the sum runs over the four points \(\{\rho_0,\overline{\rho_0},1-\rho_0,1-\overline{\rho_0}\}\) and \(c>0\) is absolute. The prime-power side of the explicit formula remains \(O(1)\) uniformly in \(\varepsilon\) small, because \(\hat g_\varepsilon\) is still essentially supported on \([-1,1]\) and the von Mangoldt weights are absolutely convergent there.

On the other hand, the left-hand side of the explicit formula is controlled by the spectral interpretation of the zeros as eigenvalues of a putative Hilbert–Pólya operator \(H\) (formally self-adjoint on a Hilbert space \(\mathcal{H}\) with simple spectrum). If such an \(H\) exists and possesses a spectral gap of size \(\delta>0\) about the critical line in the sense that
\[
\operatorname{dist}(\operatorname{spec}(H),\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})\ge\delta,
\]
then the smoothed counting function on the left of \eqref{eq:lower} cannot exceed \(O(e^{-c\delta/\varepsilon})\) for any test function of width \(\varepsilon\). Choosing \(\varepsilon\ll\delta\) therefore yields an upper bound \(o(1)\) that contradicts the strictly positive lower bound \eqref{eq:lower} as soon as \(\sigma_0-1/2>\delta\).

Consequently: either (i) no Hilbert–Pólya operator with a uniform spectral gap exists, or (ii) every off-line zero (if any) must satisfy \(\sigma_0-1/2\le\delta\). In the latter case the same argument may be iterated with a sequence of gaps \(\delta_n\to0\), forcing all zeros onto the critical line provided the operator remains essentially self-adjoint under the successive perturbations. This reduces RH to the single analytic question of whether a densely-defined symmetric operator whose formal eigenvalues are the \(\gamma\)’s admits a self-adjoint extension with no residual spectrum off the real line—an assertion that is still open but is now cleanly separated from the arithmetic side of the explicit formula.

(The reduction is sharp in the following sense: if a zero with \(\sigma_0>1/2\) is ever found, the same calculation supplies an explicit numerical obstruction to the existence of any spectral gap larger than \(\sigma_0-1/2\).)
