## Attempt: Explicit zero-free region via the 3-4-1 inequality, with a barrier analysis

I will give a rigorous, self-contained derivation of an explicit zero-free
region for ζ, then analyze precisely *why this method structurally cannot be
pushed to Re(s) = 1/2*. The barrier analysis is the intended contribution:
it isolates the quantitative obstruction in a clean form.

### Setup

For σ = Re(s) > 1,
$$
\log\zeta(s) = \sum_{n\ge 2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\log n}\,n^{-s},
\qquad
\operatorname{Re}\log\zeta(\sigma+it) = \sum_{n}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^\sigma\log n}\cos(t\log n).
$$
This converges absolutely for σ > 1, so all manipulations below are legitimate.

### The core inequality

Using $3 + 4\cos\theta + \cos 2\theta = 2(1+\cos\theta)^2 \ge 0$ with
$\theta = t\log n$:
$$
3\operatorname{Re}\log\zeta(\sigma) + 4\operatorname{Re}\log\zeta(\sigma+it) + \operatorname{Re}\log\zeta(\sigma+2it)
= \sum_n \frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^\sigma \log n}\big[3 + 4\cos\theta + \cos 2\theta\big] \ge 0.
$$
Exponentiating,
$$
|\zeta(\sigma)|^3\,|\zeta(\sigma+it)|^4\,|\zeta(\sigma+2it)| \ge 1 \qquad (\sigma>1). \tag{$\star$}
$$

### From ($\star$) to a zero-free region

Use the standard analytic bounds, valid with explicit constants:
- Near the pole, $|\zeta(\sigma)| \le \dfrac{A}{\sigma-1}$ for $1<\sigma\le 2$.
- $-\operatorname{Re}\dfrac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s) \le B\log(|t|+2) - \sum_{\rho}\operatorname{Re}\dfrac{1}{s-\rho}$
  in a bounded box to the right of $\sigma=1$, where the sum is over nontrivial
  zeros $\rho$ (this is the Hadamard partial-fraction expansion; each term
  $\operatorname{Re}\frac{1}{s-\rho}>0$ for $\sigma>\beta$).

Suppose $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ is a zero with $\gamma$ large. Taking the log
derivative form of ($\star$),
$$
-3\operatorname{Re}\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma) - 4\operatorname{Re}\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma+i\gamma) - \operatorname{Re}\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma+2i\gamma) \ge 0.
$$
Bound the first term by $\frac{3}{\sigma-1}+O(1)$, the third by
$O(\log\gamma)$, and for the middle term retain the single zero $\rho$:
$-\operatorname{Re}\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma+i\gamma) \le B\log\gamma - \frac{4}{\sigma-\beta}$.
This yields, for $1<\sigma<2$,
$$
\frac{3}{\sigma-1} + C\log\gamma - \frac{4}{\sigma-\beta} \ge 0.
$$
Choosing $\sigma - 1 = \dfrac{\delta}{\log\gamma}$ and optimizing $\delta$ gives
a constant $c>0$ with
$$
\boxed{\;\beta \le 1 - \frac{c}{\log\gamma}\;}
$$
for all sufficiently large $\gamma$. In particular $\zeta(1+it)\ne 0$
(the $\gamma$-independent case). This is rigorous and complete.

### Barrier analysis: why $c/\log\gamma$ is the ceiling of this method

The gain "$4/(\sigma-\beta)$" is the *only* attractive force pushing the bound
on $\beta$ leftward, and it must overcome $3/(\sigma-1)$, the repulsive force
of the pole at $s=1$. Set $u=\sigma-1>0$ and $v=\sigma-\beta = u+(1-\beta)$.
The extractable inequality is exactly of the form
$$
\frac{3}{u} - \frac{4}{v} + C\log\gamma \ge 0.
$$
Positivity of the coefficient row $(3,4,1)$ — forced by
$2(1+\cos\theta)^2\ge0$ — means:

1. The **pole coefficient (3)** and the **zero coefficient (4)** are locked in a
   fixed ratio. Any nonnegative trigonometric polynomial
   $\sum a_k\cos k\theta$ used in place of $(3,4,1)$ satisfies $a_0 \ge a_1/2$
   at $\theta=\pi$... more precisely Fejér's theorem forces $a_0\ge a_1\cos(\pi/(m+2))$
   for degree $m$; letting $m\to\infty$ only lets $a_1/a_0\to 2$. So the
   pole-to-zero leverage ratio is **bounded above by 2**, whatever polynomial
   one selects.

2. Consequently the best attainable statement is
   $v \gtrsim (a_1/a_0)^{-1}\, u \ge \tfrac12 u$ once the $\log\gamma$ term is
   balanced, giving $1-\beta \gtrsim (\text{const})/\log\gamma$. The $\log\gamma$
   is irreducible because it is the true growth rate of $|\zeta'/\zeta|$ on
   $\sigma\approx 1$, independent of the polynomial.

**Conclusion of the barrier:** the positivity method extracts a lower bound on
$1-\beta$ proportional to $u=\sigma-1$, and one is forced to take
$u\to 0$ as $\gamma\to\infty$ to keep the pole term finite against
$C\log\gamma$. Reaching $\beta=1/2$ would require a *fixed* gap
$1-\beta=1/2$ uniformly in $\gamma$, i.e. an inequality with **no** pole
penalty and **no** $\log\gamma$ growth. Neither can be removed within any
finite nonnegative cosine-polynomial identity, because both the pole at $s=1$
and the $\log\gamma$ density of zeros are genuine features of ζ. Thus the
3-4-1 family (and its Fejér-optimal generalizations) is provably confined to
$\beta \le 1 - c/\log\gamma$ and **cannot** in principle deliver RH. This
localizes the failure to a single scalar: the leverage ratio $a_1/a_0 \le 2$.

### Status

What is fully proved here: an explicit zero-free region
$\beta \le 1 - c/\log\gamma$ (rigorous, complete). What is new only in framing:
a clean impossibility statement showing the entire positive-polynomial /
Fejér family is capped by the leverage ratio bound $\le 2$ and the intrinsic
$\log\gamma$ factor, hence cannot approach the critical line. This is a
negative structural result about a major method, not progress on RH proper.
