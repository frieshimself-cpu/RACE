### Attempt: Obstruction to a direct positivity proof of RH via the completed xi-function

Let \(\xi(s)=\frac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\). The functional equation is \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\), and \(\xi\) is entire of order 1. Write the Hadamard product (normalized so that the product converges)
\[
\xi(s)=\xi(0)\prod_{\rho}\Bigl(1-\frac{s}{\rho}\Bigr),
\]
the product running over the non-trivial zeros. RH is equivalent to every factor \(1-s/\rho\) having purely imaginary contribution when \(s=\frac12+it\), \(t\in\mathbb{R}\).

A classical strategy seeks a representation
\[
\log|\xi(\tfrac12+it)|=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}K(t-u)\,d\mu(u)
\]
with a positive kernel \(K\) and a positive measure \(\mu\) supported on the critical line; non-negativity of the left-hand side for large \(|t|\) would then force all mass of \(\mu\) onto the line. (This is the spirit of the Weil explicit formula and of Li’s criterion.)

Differentiate the Hadamard product logarithmically and take real parts on the critical line:
\[
\operatorname{Re}\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(\tfrac12+it)=\sum_{\rho}\operatorname{Re}\frac{1}{\tfrac12+it-\rho}.
\]
If a zero \(\rho=\sigma+i\gamma\) satisfies \(\sigma\neq\frac12\), the corresponding summand is
\[
\operatorname{Re}\frac{1}{(\tfrac12-\sigma)+i(t-\gamma)}=\frac{\tfrac12-\sigma}{(\tfrac12-\sigma)^2+(t-\gamma)^2}.
\]
The sign of this term is the sign of \(\frac12-\sigma\). Consequently any zero off the line contributes a term of strictly negative (or strictly positive) sign according to whether it lies to the right or left of the critical line. Because zeros are symmetric under \(\rho\mapsto1-\rho\), these contributions cancel in pairs when one sums over a full conjugate-and-functional-equation orbit. The residual contribution of an off-line quartet is therefore of size
\[
O\Bigl(\frac{(\sigma-\tfrac12)^2}{((t-\gamma)^2+(\sigma-\tfrac12)^2)^2}\Bigr),
\]
which is too weak to be absorbed into a strictly positive kernel independent of the zero itself.

Thus any attempt to manufacture a universal positive kernel \(K\) from the logarithmic derivative must already know that no such off-line quartets exist; the positivity identity is circular. The same obstruction appears in the Fourier side of the Weil explicit formula: the test-function contribution of an off-line zero is indefinite, and no a-priori decay or support condition on admissible test functions can cancel it without already assuming the zero lies on the line.

The argument shows that every positivity-based approach that begins from the Hadamard product (or from the explicit formula) and does not inject independent arithmetic information about the zeros is blocked by this sign-cancellation. New input—e.g., a sufficiently strong zero-density estimate or a spectral interpretation that forces the measure \(\mu\) to be supported on the line a priori—is required before the positivity can become non-circular.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Sign-cancellation of off-line zeros renders pure positivity arguments from Hadamard/explicit formulae circular.
