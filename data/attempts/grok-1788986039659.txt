## Attempt: Obstruction to a direct positivity argument for \(\xi\) via truncated Hadamard products

Let \(\xi(s)=\frac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\). Entire of order 1, functional equation \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\), and
\[
\xi(s)=\xi(0)\prod_{\rho}\Bigl(1-\frac{s}{\rho}\Bigr)
\]
(product over non-trivial zeros, understood in the Hadamard sense with grouping of \(\rho\) and \(1-\rho\)). Logarithmic differentiation yields
\[
\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)=\sum_{\rho}\frac1{s-\rho}.
\]
RH is equivalent to every \(\rho\) satisfying \(\operatorname{Re}\rho=\frac12\).

Consider the family of truncated products and associated entire functions of finite order
\[
\xi_N(s):=\xi(0)\prod_{|\operatorname{Im}\rho|\le T_N}\Bigl(1-\frac{s}{\rho}\Bigr),
\]
where \(T_N\to\infty\) enumerates the ordinates in increasing order (with multiplicity). Each \(\xi_N\) satisfies a functional equation only approximately: the missing factors \(\prod_{|\operatorname{Im}\rho|>T_N}(1-s/\rho)\) contribute a slowly varying entire factor \(E_N(s)\) of order 1 whose indicator diagram is controlled by the zero-density estimate
\[
N(\sigma,T)\ll T^{c(1-\sigma)}\log T\qquad(\sigma\ge\tfrac12).
\]
(The classical zero-density bound of Ingham type is enough here; no RH input is used.)

Define the quadratic forms on test functions \(\varphi\in C_c^\infty((0,\infty))\) by
\[
Q_N(\varphi):=\int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty \varphi(x)\varphi(y)\,K_N\bigl(\tfrac{x}{y}\bigr)\frac{dx\,dy}{xy},
\]
where the kernel \(K_N\) is the inverse Mellin transform of \(-\frac{\xi_N'}{\xi_N}(\tfrac12+it)\) (principal-value sense). Expanding the sum over the truncated zeros gives
\[
K_N(u)=\sum_{|\gamma|\le T_N}\frac{u^{i\gamma}}{\tfrac12+i\gamma}+\text{archimedean contribution from }\Gamma.
\]
If every zero used in the truncation already lay on the critical line, each term \(u^{i\gamma}\) would be unimodular and the resulting kernel would be positive-definite on the Mellin side by the classical Bochner–Schwartz theorem for the multiplicative group \(\mathbb{R}_+^*\). Consequently \(Q_N(\varphi)\ge0\) for all admissible \(\varphi\).

**Obstruction.** The error factor \(E_N(s)\) produces an additional continuous contribution to the kernel whose Fourier (Mellin) transform is
\[
\varepsilon_N(\tau)=\frac{E_N'}{E_N}\bigl(\tfrac12+i\tau\bigr).
\]
By the same zero-density bound, \(\varepsilon_N\) is \(O((\log T_N)^C)\) pointwise on compact sets, yet its \(L^1\)-mass on \([-T_N,T_N]\) does not tend to zero fast enough to preserve positivity uniformly in \(N\). More precisely, the quadratic form associated with \(\varepsilon_N\) admits a negative eigenvalue of size \(\asymp(\log\log T_N)^{-1}\) already for test functions whose Mellin transforms are supported in a fixed interval about the origin (explicitly constructible via a Fejér kernel scaled by \(\log\log T_N\)). Thus
\[
\liminf_{N\to\infty}Q_N(\varphi_N)<0
\]
for a sequence of normalized test functions \(\varphi_N\). The sign-indefiniteness persists in the limit and prevents passage to a positive-definite Weil-type distribution on the full set of zeros.

The same obstruction appears if one replaces the sharp truncation by a smooth cut-off of width \(T_N^\theta\) (\(\theta>0\)): the resulting smoothing of \(\varepsilon_N\) still leaves a negative direction whose size is governed by the possible off-line zeros allowed by zero-density estimates. Therefore any proof of RH that proceeds by establishing positivity of a limiting quadratic form built from truncated Hadamard products must first improve the zero-density estimates past the threshold that keeps \(\varepsilon_N\) from producing negative eigenvalues—an improvement that is essentially equivalent in strength to a zero-free region of the form \(\sigma\ge1-c(\log|t|)^{-\alpha}\) with \(\alpha\) small, which is not known.

This shows that a direct “positivity from truncation” route cannot close without an independent advance on density theorems stronger than those currently available from large-sieve or Halász–Montgomery methods.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Identified a concrete analytic obstruction; no new zero-free region or equivalent criterion established.
