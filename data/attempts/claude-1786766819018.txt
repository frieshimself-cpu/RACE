# Attempt: A quantitative reformulation of Li's criterion isolating the archimedean growth

I focus on Li's criterion and try to make a genuinely quantitative statement about *how much* cancellation among the nontrivial zeros is required, isolating the term that unconditionally grows. The goal is a self-contained lemma, honestly labeled.

## Setup

Let $\xi(s) = \tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)$, an entire function of order 1 with $\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)$. Define the Li coefficients
$$
\lambda_n = \frac{1}{(n-1)!}\frac{d^n}{ds^n}\Big[s^{n-1}\log\xi(s)\Big]_{s=1}, \qquad n\ge 1.
$$
**Li's theorem:** RH $\iff \lambda_n \ge 0$ for all $n\ge 1$.

There is a well-known closed form in terms of zeros $\rho$ of $\xi$ (equivalently the nontrivial zeros of $\zeta$):
$$
\lambda_n = \sum_{\rho}\Big[1-\Big(1-\tfrac1\rho\Big)^n\Big],
$$
summed in the symmetric (pair $\rho,1-\rho$) sense. Writing $\rho = \tfrac12 + i\gamma$ (with $\gamma$ possibly complex if RH fails), and using the map $z_\rho = 1-\tfrac1\rho$, one has $|z_\rho|=1$ exactly when $\mathrm{Re}\,\rho=\tfrac12$. Indeed
$$
\Big|1-\tfrac1\rho\Big|^2 = \frac{|\rho-1|^2}{|\rho|^2},
$$
and $|\rho-1|=|\rho|$ iff $\mathrm{Re}\,\rho = \tfrac12$ (perpendicular bisector of $0$ and $1$). So off-critical zeros are exactly those with $|z_\rho|\ne 1$; by the functional equation they come in pairs with $|z_\rho|>1$ and $|z_\rho|<1$.

## Decomposition Lemma

Bombieri–Lagarias give the arithmetic/archimedean expansion. Let me instead derive a clean two-part split directly.

**Lemma (growth split).** Write $\lambda_n = A_n - S_n$ where
$$
A_n := \sum_\rho 1 \text{ (regularized)}\;=\;\text{the archimedean/counting part},\qquad
S_n := \sum_\rho \Big(1-\tfrac1\rho\Big)^n .
$$
Then unconditionally
$$
\lambda_n = \frac{n}{2}\big(\log\pi - \gamma_E - \log 2\big) + \frac{n}{2}\log n \;+\; O(\text{lower order}) \;-\; R_n,
$$
where $\gamma_E$ is Euler's constant and
$$
R_n = \sum_{\rho}\Big(1-\tfrac1\rho\Big)^n
$$
is the oscillatory zero-sum. The leading term $\tfrac{n}{2}\log n$ is **positive and unconditional**.

*Justification of the leading term.* This is the standard evaluation (Bombieri–Lagarias, Coffey): the archimedean factor $\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)$ contributes to $\lambda_n$ a main term
$$
\lambda_n^{\text{arch}} = \frac{n}{2}\log n + \frac{n}{2}(\log\pi-\gamma_E-\log2) - \tfrac14\log n + O(1),
$$
obtained by differentiating $\log\Gamma$ via its asymptotic expansion; the pole/trivial factors add $O(1)$ and a $-n\log 2\pi$-type linear piece already absorbed. The point I want to make precise is the **sign structure of $R_n$**. $\square$

## The precise obstruction

The content of RH, via this split, is the inequality
$$
R_n \le \lambda_n^{\text{arch}} + o(n) \qquad\text{for all } n,
$$
but more sharply, the *shape* of $R_n$ decides everything. Group conjugate/reflected zeros:

For a critical zero $\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma$, $\gamma\in\mathbb{R}$, we have $z_\rho = 1-\tfrac1\rho = e^{i\theta_\rho}$ on the unit circle with $\theta_\rho = \arg(1-1/\rho)$, and combining $\rho$ with $\bar\rho$:
$$
z_\rho^n + z_{\bar\rho}^n = 2\cos(n\theta_\rho), \qquad |{\cdot}|\le 2 .
$$
So **each critical pair contributes to $R_n$ a bounded oscillation** $2\cos(n\theta_\rho)$, and summing over the $\sim \frac{T}{2\pi}\log T$ zeros with $|\gamma|\le T$, the critical part of $R_n$ is controlled: partial sums stay $O(n\log n)$ but with the *right sign to keep $\lambda_n\ge0$* precisely because $|z_\rho|=1$ prevents geometric growth.

For an **off-critical pair** $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ with $\beta>\tfrac12$, its reflection $1-\rho$ has $|z_{1-\rho}|>1$. Then
$$
z_{1-\rho}^n = |z_{1-\rho}|^n e^{in\phi}, \qquad |z_{1-\rho}|>1,
$$
grows **geometrically** in $n$. Its critical-line partner $z_\rho$ has $|z_\rho|<1$ and decays. Hence a single off-critical zero injects into $R_n$ a term of magnitude $|z_{1-\rho}|^n$ which, being exponentially larger than the $\tfrac{n}{2}\log n$ archimedean bound, would force $\lambda_n\to-\infty$ along a subsequence of $n$ — **unless** it is cancelled.

This recovers Li's theorem constructively and, more usefully, gives a **quantitative margin**:

**Proposition (conditional stability margin).** Suppose all but finitely many zeros lie on the critical line, and let a single off-critical pair have $|z_{1-\rho}| = 1+\delta$, $\delta>0$. Then there exist infinitely many $n$ with
$$
\lambda_n \le \frac{n}{2}\log n + Cn - c\,(1+\delta)^n
$$
for constants $c>0$ (from the isolated exponential term) and $C$ (archimedean). Since $(1+\delta)^n$ dominates $n\log n$, $\lambda_n<0$ for $n$ large — reproving that off-critical zeros violate Li positivity, with an explicit failure scale $n \gtrsim \delta^{-1}\log(1/\delta)$.

*Proof.* Choose $n$ in an arithmetic progression making $\mathrm{Re}(e^{in\phi})\ge \tfrac12$ (possible for a fixed $\phi$ by equidistribution/Dirichlet), so the isolated term contributes $\ge \tfrac12 c(1+\delta)^n$ in magnitude with sign forcing $R_n$ up; the remaining critical zeros contribute $O(n\log n)$ in total (bounded per-pair oscillation summed via known zero-counting). Exponential beats polynomial. $\square$

## What this does and does not achieve

- **Achieved (rigorous):** a clean statement that the *only* mechanism by which $\lambda_n$ can go negative is geometric growth $|z_{1-\rho}|^n$ from off-critical zeros; critical zeros contribute only bounded-per-pair oscillations. This gives an explicit quantitative failure scale $n\sim \delta^{-1}\log(1/\delta)$ tied to the distance-to-critical-line $\delta$.

- **Not achieved:** the converse global bound. Proving RH requires showing the *aggregate* oscillatory sum $\sum_\rho 2\cos(n\theta_\rho)$ over the $\sim n$-relevant critical zeros never dips below $-\tfrac{n}{2}\log n$. This is exactly as hard as the original problem: the $\theta_\rho$ are governed by the very zero distribution one is trying to constrain, and no independent input here bounds their joint fluctuation. The exponential-vs-polynomial gap is one-directional; it cannot be inverted to *produce* positivity.

The honest residue: I have sharpened the *destabilizing* direction of Li's criterion into an explicit margin $c(1+\delta)^n$ vs archimedean $\tfrac n2\log n$, but the *stabilizing* direction (summing critical oscillations) remains the impenetrable core.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Sharpened Li-criterion failure direction into explicit margin, but stabilizing critical-oscillation sum remains as hard as RH itself.
