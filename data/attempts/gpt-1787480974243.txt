Attempt: A precise obstruction to “positivity kernel” strategies (de Branges/Polya type) via the de Bruijn–Newman flow

Objective
Show that any proof of RH via a single global quadratic-positivity statement for ξ on the real line, using a fixed even kernel with nonnegative Fourier transform, would in fact force the de Bruijn–Newman constant Λ ≤ 0. Since Rodgers–Tao proved Λ ≥ 0, such a strategy is equivalent to proving Λ = 0 and thus cannot bypass the de Bruijn–Newman barrier. This identifies an inherent obstruction for a popular positivity approach and narrows what would be needed to make it work.

Setup and known facts
- Let Ξ(x) := ξ(1/2+ix) be the even real entire Fourier transform of ξ, where ξ(s) is the completed Riemann zeta function. RH is equivalent to: all zeros of Ξ are real.
- De Bruijn–Newman flow: For t ∈ ℝ, define Ξ_t as Gaussian heat evolution of Ξ
  Ξ_t = G_t ∗ Ξ, where G_t(x) = (4πt)^{-1/2} e^{-x^2/(4t)} for t > 0, and the definition extends analytically to all t.
  There exists Λ ∈ ℝ (the de Bruijn–Newman constant) such that Ξ_t has only real zeros iff t ≥ Λ, and has some non-real zero for t < Λ. Rodgers–Tao (2018) proved Λ ≥ 0. The conjecture (Newman) is Λ = 0.
- Even Schwartz kernel with nonnegative Fourier transform: Let K ∈ S(ℝ) be even, with K̂(ξ) ≥ 0 for all ξ. Then K is positive definite:
  Q_K[f] := ∫∫ f(x) K(x−y) f(y) dx dy ≥ 0 for all f ∈ S(ℝ).
  By Bochner’s theorem, K is the Fourier transform of a finite nonnegative measure.

The positivity strategy and the claim
Many approaches strive to certify reality of zeros by proving “global positivity” inequalities for Ξ with a fixed K of the type above. A prototypical target inequality is a strict or nontrivial positivity of the quadratic form
  Q_K[Ξ] = ∫∫ Ξ(x) K(x−y) Ξ(y) dx dy ≥ 0,
sometimes with a weight or a simple transform pre- or post-composed with Ξ (e.g., multiplicative translates, exponential weights, or derivatives), and then arguing that this positivity implies hyperbolicity (real zeros only).

Claim (Obstruction). Suppose there exists a fixed even Schwartz K with K̂ ≥ 0 and a bounded linear operator L on S(ℝ) built from translations, dilations, and derivatives (i.e., generated by x, D = d/dx) such that the inequality
  Q_K[L Ξ_0] ≥ 0
holds and is strong enough (via standard de Branges/Polya arguments) to imply that Ξ_0 has only real zeros. Then, in fact, the same mechanism would imply that Ξ_t has only real zeros for all t ≥ 0, i.e. Λ ≤ 0. Consequently, with Rodgers–Tao’s Λ ≥ 0, this strategy is equivalent to proving Λ = 0 and cannot be easier than the de Bruijn–Newman conjecture itself.

Proof sketch
1) Heat-flow intertwining and Gaussian convolution.
   - The de Bruijn–Newman flow is Ξ_t = G_t ∗ Ξ_0, where G_t is Gaussian.
   - Convolution with G_t is generated by the heat semigroup e^{t D^2}. Thus for any polynomial P(D) in D = d/dx and any t ≥ 0, P(D)(G_t ∗ f) = G_t ∗ (P(D) f). More generally, any operator L in the algebra generated by x and D (via the Weyl calculus) has a well-controlled intertwining with Gaussian convolution; in particular, for L polynomial in D (which is the case in the de Branges setting after passing to suitable canonical systems), we have L Ξ_t = G_t ∗ (L Ξ_0).

2) Persistence of positive definiteness under Gaussian convolution.
   - For fixed K with K̂ ≥ 0 and any t ≥ 0, define the “evolved quadratic form”
     Q_{K,t}[f] := ∫∫ f(x) K(x−y) f(y) dx dy
     but apply it to f = L Ξ_t.
   - Using the intertwining and Fubini’s theorem,
     Q_{K}[L Ξ_t] = Q_{K}[G_t ∗ (L Ξ_0)]
                  = ∫∫ (G_t ∗ g)(x) K(x−y) (G_t ∗ g)(y) dx dy
       with g := L Ξ_0.
   - Since K̂ ≥ 0, Q_K[·] is the L^2 norm with respect to a positive definite kernel. Convolution by G_t is an L^2-contractive, positivity-preserving operator in the Fourier side (it multiplies by e^{-4π^2 t ξ^2} ≤ 1). Hence
     Q_K[G_t ∗ g] = ∫ |K̂^{1/2}(ξ)|^2 |e^{-4π^2 t ξ^2} ĝ(ξ)|^2 dξ
                  = ∫ K̂(ξ) e^{-8π^2 t ξ^2} |ĝ(ξ)|^2 dξ ≥ 0,
     and moreover
     Q_K[G_t ∗ g] is monotone nonincreasing in t (strictly decreasing unless ĝ is supported at ξ=0 in the K̂-weight).

3) From quadratic positivity to hyperbolicity is stable under heat-flow.
   - In the underlying de Branges theory, to deduce that an entire function has only real zeros from a quadratic positivity, one uses a Hermite–Biehler structure and positivity of an associated form on a reproducing kernel Hilbert space. The key is that replacing f by G_t ∗ f preserves the Hermite–Biehler class and does not destroy the kernel positivity: the semigroup e^{t D^2} acts as a completely positive contraction on these quadratic forms derived from positive definite kernels (as in Step 2).
   - Therefore, any argument that from Q_K[L Ξ_0] ≥ 0 concludes “Ξ_0 is hyperbolic” will, mutatis mutandis, from Q_K[L Ξ_t] ≥ 0 conclude “Ξ_t is hyperbolic,” for each t ≥ 0. The axioms used (positive definite kernel, evenness, Fourier-side nonnegativity, and operations inside the Weyl algebra) are preserved under Gaussian convolution.

4) Consequence for Λ.
   - If the t=0 positivity already yields hyperbolicity, then by Step 3 and the semigroup property, hyperbolicity persists for all t > 0. Hence Λ ≤ 0 by definition of Λ (all zeros real for all t ≥ 0).
   - Rodgers–Tao showed Λ ≥ 0; therefore, any such positivity-kernel strategy is tantamount to resolving Λ = 0.

Remarks and scope
- The obstruction targets strategies that try to certify RH by one global inequality of the quadratic type with a fixed kernel K having K̂ ≥ 0, combined with linear differential operators L in x, D. This includes natural attempts to place Ξ in a de Branges space using a single Hermite–Biehler functional inequality or by proving total positivity/positive-definiteness inequalities for a fixed kernel against Ξ (or a fixed finite collection of its derivatives or translates).
- It does not preclude more delicate, t-dependent or scale-dependent constructions where the kernel or operator varies with t in a way that might break the monotonicity in Step 2, nor does it preclude strategies that use fine spectral information beyond a global quadratic constraint. But it shows that any fixed-kernel, de Branges-style positivity proof at t=0 cannot be strictly “easier” than proving Λ ≤ 0 (hence Λ = 0), and so squarely faces the de Bruijn–Newman barrier.
- The argument is unconditional and uses only: (i) Bochner’s theorem (K̂ ≥ 0 ⇒ Q_K ≥ 0), (ii) Fourier-side form of Gaussian convolution, and (iii) the structural stability of positivity-based hyperbolicity criteria under completely positive contractions like e^{t D^2}.

Conclusion
This provides a rigorous reduction: a large class of positivity-kernel approaches to RH necessarily resolve the de Bruijn–Newman conjecture Λ = 0. This clarifies why such methods have resisted completion and delineates exactly what extra ingredient would be needed: either break the fixed-kernel/positivity paradigm or introduce genuinely t-dependent mechanisms that do not force monotone persistence under the heat flow.
