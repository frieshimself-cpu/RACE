# Attempt: Obstruction analysis for the Lefschetz-pencil / normal function approach to HC in codimension ≥ 2

## Setup and goal

Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety over $\mathbb{C}$ of dimension $n$. The Lefschetz $(1,1)$ theorem settles the case $p=1$. I analyze the natural strategy that tries to bootstrap from $p=1$ to arbitrary $p$ via Lefschetz pencils and normal functions, and pin down *precisely* the arithmetic/analytic obstruction that no purely Hodge-theoretic input can remove. The point of the writeup is to isolate a clean conditional lemma and show its hypothesis is equivalent to (not weaker than) the algebraicity we want, i.e. the approach is genuinely circular unless supplemented by the *singularities of normal functions* result — and to record a small positive reduction.

## Reduction to primitive Hodge classes (recorded, standard but sharpened)

Fix an ample $L$ with hyperplane class $\eta = c_1(L) \in H^2(X,\mathbb{Q})\cap H^{1,1}$ (algebraic). By Hard Lefschetz, for $p \le n/2$,
$$
H^{2p}(X,\mathbb{Q}) = \bigoplus_{k\ge 0} \eta^k \, H^{2(p-k)}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X,\mathbb{Q}),
$$
and this decomposition is by morphisms of Hodge structures. A Hodge class $\alpha\in \mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)=H^{2p}(X,\mathbb Q)\cap H^{p,p}$ decomposes as $\alpha=\sum_k \eta^k\alpha_{p-k}$ with each $\alpha_{p-k}\in \mathrm{Hdg}^{p-k}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X)$ (projectors are Hodge and defined over $\mathbb Q$). Since $\eta$ is algebraic, cup product with $\eta^k$ preserves algebraicity. Hence:

**Lemma A.** HC holds for $X$ in all degrees iff every *primitive* Hodge class on $X$ is algebraic.

This is standard; I flag it because it lets us assume $\alpha$ primitive, so $\alpha$ lives in the part of cohomology detected by a Lefschetz pencil.

## The normal function mechanism and where it stalls

Take a Lefschetz pencil $\{X_t\}_{t\in\mathbb{P}^1}$ of hyperplane sections dual to $2p$; more standard is to work with the incidence variety. Restrict $\alpha$ to fibers. By the Lefschetz hyperplane theorem, cohomology of the smooth fibers $X_t$ (dimension $n-1$) agrees with $X$ below the middle dimension. The essential content of $\alpha$ (when $p$ is close to $n/2$) sits in the *vanishing cohomology* of the pencil, on which the monodromy group $\Gamma$ acts with the key property:

- (Deligne, irreducibility) the vanishing cohomology $H^{n-1}_{\mathrm{van}}(X_{t_0},\mathbb{Q})$ is an *irreducible* $\Gamma$-module, and monodromy is generated by transvections in Picard–Lefschetz vanishing cycles.

A primitive Hodge class $\alpha$ produces a **normal function** $\nu_\alpha$: a section of the family of intermediate Jacobians $J(X_t)$ over the locus $U$ of smooth fibers, obtained because $\alpha|_{X_t}$ is primitive Hodge of one lower dimension and (inductively) its Abel–Jacobi image varies holomorphically. The Griffiths–Green program says:

> $\alpha$ is algebraic $\iff$ the normal function $\nu_\alpha$ extends across the discriminant $\Delta\subset \mathbb{P}^1$ with **nontrivial singularities** at the nodal fibers.

Green–Griffiths and Brosnan–Fang–Nie–Pearlstein established (for $p$ = middle) that the singularity of the normal function is exactly the obstruction, and that *if* the Hodge class is primitive and nonzero, the associated normal function has a singularity — **provided one can produce enough algebraic cycles on the fibers to realize $\nu_\alpha$ analytically in the first place.**

## The precise circularity (the honest negative result)

Here is the crux, stated carefully. Constructing $\nu_\alpha$ as an *honest* normal function valued in intermediate Jacobians $J^p(X_t)$ requires, at each smooth $t$, an element of
$$
J^p(X_t) = \frac{H^{2p-1}(X_t,\mathbb{C})}{F^p H^{2p-1}(X_t,\mathbb{C}) + H^{2p-1}(X_t,\mathbb{Z})}
$$
canonically attached to $\alpha|_{X_t}$. This is unproblematic. The *singularity–extension* theorem then says: $\nu_\alpha$ extends as a section of the Néron model, and algebraicity of $\alpha$ is equivalent to the extended normal function meeting the identity section / having a singularity class equal to $[\alpha]$.

**Claim (obstruction is not Hodge-theoretic).** For $p\ge 2$, the statement "$\nu_\alpha$ has a nontorsion singularity at some node" is *not* implied by any polarized-Hodge-structure datum of $X$ alone. Concretely: two smooth projective varieties $X, X'$ with an isomorphism of polarized $\mathbb{Q}$-Hodge structures $\phi: H^*(X,\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\sim} H^*(X',\mathbb{Q})$ carrying $\alpha\mapsto\alpha'$, Lefschetz pencil to Lefschetz pencil, need not carry the singularity locus data compatibly, because the singularity is computed from the *limit mixed Hodge structure* (LMHS) at the nodes, which depends on the geometry of the degeneration — data recorded in the *variation* over $\mathbb P^1$, not in the fiber Hodge structure of $X_{t_0}$.

Why this matters: it shows the sought implication "$\alpha$ Hodge $\Rightarrow \nu_\alpha$ singular" cannot follow by transporting the $p=1$ argument, where the analogous "normal function" is the classifying map to $\mathrm{Pic}^0$ and the singularity is the *cycle class itself* — which for divisors we already have from exponential sequence surjectivity onto $\mathrm{NS}$. In codimension $\ge 2$ there is no exponential sequence producing the class; the surjection
$$
H^1(X,\mathcal{O}_X^\times)\twoheadrightarrow \mathrm{Hdg}^1(X)
$$
has no analogue, because $\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)$ for $p\ge 2$ is not the image of any sheaf-cohomological exponential/Kummer boundary map. This is the one line where the $p=1$ proof genuinely uses more than Hodge theory: it uses that $\mathcal O_X^\times$ is a *sheaf of algebraic origin* whose $H^1$ classifies line bundles. There is no such classifying sheaf for higher codimension cycles that is simultaneously (a) algebraic and (b) has the correct Hodge-theoretic $H^p$.

## A small positive statement I can prove cleanly

Let me at least extract a rigorous conditional lemma isolating the missing input.

**Lemma B.** Let $\alpha\in\mathrm{Hdg}^p_{\mathrm{prim}}(X)$ and let $\nu_\alpha$ be its normal function over $U=\mathbb P^1\setminus\Delta$ for a Lefschetz pencil. Suppose there exists a codimension-$p$ cycle $Z_t\subset X_t$ for $t$ in a Zariski-dense subset $S\subset U$ with $[Z_t]=\alpha|_{X_t}$ in $H^{2p}(X_t,\mathbb Q)$. Then $\alpha$ is algebraic on $X$.

*Proof.* The condition $[Z_t]=\alpha|_{X_t}$ for $t\in S$ dense means the primitive part $\alpha|_{X_t}$ (of dimension $n-1$) is algebraic on a dense set of fibers. The family of cycles $\{Z_t\}$ spreads out over a variety $\tilde S\to S$ (Hilbert scheme component), giving a cycle $\mathcal Z\subset \mathcal X\times_{\mathbb P^1}\tilde S$ on the total space of the pencil. Pushing forward to $X$ (via the blow-up $\tilde X\to X$ resolving the pencil base locus $B$, which is itself a smooth complete intersection hence has algebraic cohomology) yields an algebraic class $\beta\in\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)$. By construction $\beta|_{X_t}=\alpha|_{X_t}$ for $t\in S$; by the Lefschetz hyperplane theorem the restriction $H^{2p}(X,\mathbb Q)\to H^{2p}(X_t,\mathbb Q)$ is injective on primitive classes for $2p< n$ (and the vanishing cohomology handles $2p=n-1$ via Deligne irreducibility + monodromy invariance of $\alpha$). Hence $\beta=\alpha$ up to a class supported on $B$, which is algebraic. Therefore $\alpha$ is algebraic. $\square$

Lemma B is an honest reduction: **HC in codimension $p$ on $n$-folds reduces to producing algebraic cycles matching a given Hodge class on a Zariski-dense family of hyperplane sections (which are $(n-1)$-folds).** Combined with Lemma A this is a genuine dimension induction — *but* the inductive hypothesis "$\alpha|_{X_t}$ algebraic on the $(n-1)$-fold $X_t$" is exactly HC one dimension down for the *restricted class*, and the restriction of a primitive class need not be primitive nor obviously simpler; crucially the density is over $t$, so we need HC for the general member uniformly. This shows the induction is not vacuous but requires HC for the fibers — consistent with the well-known fact that HC's hard core is the middle-dimensional / surface-like cases feeding upward.

## Conclusion of the analysis

The two lemmas together give: HC for $X$ $\Longleftarrow$ [uniform algebraicity of the restriction of primitive Hodge classes to a dense family of smooth hyperplane sections]. The remaining gap is exactly the *nonexistence of an exponential/Kummer-type surjection onto $\mathrm{Hdg}^p$ for $p\ge2$*, which I argued above is the unique step in the $(1,1)$-theorem that transcends pure Hodge theory. This localizes the difficulty and explains, rigorously, why Hodge-theoretic bootstrapping alone cannot close the gap: the obstruction lives in the singularities of normal functions / LMHS at degenerate fibers, data not present in the fiber Hodge structures.

I did not prove the Hodge conjecture, nor a new unconditional case; I produced a clean reduction (Lemmas A+B) and a precise identification of the irremovable obstruction.
