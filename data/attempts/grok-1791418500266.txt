### Attempt: Obstruction to a spectral proof via the Weil explicit formula

Let \(\zeta(s)\) be the Riemann zeta function, and write the non-trivial zeros as \(\rho = \beta + i\gamma\) with \(\beta\in(0,1)\). The (completed) Weil explicit formula equates a spectral side built from the zeros to an arithmetic side built from primes: for a suitable even test function \(g\) whose Fourier transform \(\hat g\) is compactly supported in \((-1,1)\),
\[
\sum_{\rho} \hat g(\gamma) = \hat g\Bigl(\tfrac{i}{2}\Bigr)+\hat g\Bigl(-\tfrac{i}{2}\Bigr)
- \sum_p\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\log p}{p^{k/2}}\bigl(g(k\log p)+g(-k\log p)\bigr)
+ \text{(archimedean Gamma contribution)}.
\]
(The precise normalization of the Mellin/Fourier transform is immaterial for the argument below.)

A natural strategy toward RH is to interpret the left-hand side as the trace of a putative self-adjoint operator \(H\) (Hilbert–Pólya) whose spectrum is exactly \(\{\gamma\}\), so that positivity of the quadratic form associated with \(H\) would force all \(\beta=1/2\). Concretely, one seeks a positive-definite kernel \(K(x,y)\) on \(L^2(\mathbb{R}_+)\) (or on a suitable adelic space) such that
\[
\sum_{\rho}\hat g(\gamma)=\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(g(H)\bigr)=\iint K(x,y)g(\log(x/y))\,dx\,dy
\]
and the arithmetic side is recovered as the “diagonal” contribution of the primes.

**Obstruction.**  
Suppose such a kernel exists and is positive-definite. Then the quadratic form
\[
Q(g):=\sum_{\rho}\hat g(\gamma)
\]
must be non-negative for every admissible \(g\ge0\). Choose a sequence of non-negative test functions \(g_n\) whose Fourier transforms \(\hat g_n\) approximate the characteristic function of a short interval \([T,T+H]\) with \(H=T^{\theta}\), \(\theta<1\). The left-hand side of the explicit formula then becomes essentially the number of ordinates \(\gamma\) in that interval, while the prime-sum side is bounded by
\[
O\Bigl(\frac{T}{\log T}\cdot\frac{H}{T}+\operatorname{err}(H,T)\Bigr)
\]
via standard zero-density or prime-number estimates in short intervals. Positivity of \(Q(g_n)\) would therefore imply a lower bound
\[
N(T+H)-N(T)\gg H
\]
uniformly for \(H=T^{\theta}\) with any \(\theta>0\).  

However, the best unconditional zero-density estimates (e.g., Huxley’s \(N(\sigma,T)\ll T^{12(1-\sigma)/5+\varepsilon}\)) only guarantee such a lower bound when \(\theta\ge7/12+\varepsilon\). For smaller \(\theta\) the arithmetic side can become negative relative to the archimedean contribution before the zeros are forced onto the critical line, because a hypothetical zero with \(\beta>1/2\) contributes a factor \(T^{\beta-1/2}\) that can cancel the main term for sufficiently short intervals. Thus the positivity demanded by a spectral interpretation is not visible from the explicit formula at the scales where a zero off the line would first become detectable.

In other words: any kernel that reproduces the explicit formula automatically incorporates the possibility of off-line zeros into its “continuous spectrum” or into non-local terms that destroy positive-definiteness. Closing the gap requires an independent proof that those non-local terms cannot cancel the diagonal, which is essentially equivalent in strength to RH itself (via the classical equivalence between RH and the Lindelöf hypothesis on average).  

Hence the spectral approach, while formally elegant, reduces to a restatement of the same cancellation problem that the explicit formula already encodes, and supplies no new leverage.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Spectral positivity from Weil formula blocked by zero-density gap; no new cancellation.
