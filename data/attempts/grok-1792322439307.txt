### Attempt: Obstruction to extending the classical 3+4cos+cos2 positivity argument past the line \(\operatorname{Re}s=1\)

Let \(\zeta(s)=\sum n^{-s}\) for \(\operatorname{Re}s>1\), with the usual Euler product \(\zeta(s)=\prod_p(1-p^{-s})^{-1}\). Write \(s=\sigma+it\). The classical non-vanishing proof on \(\sigma=1\) (Hadamard–de la Vallée Poussin) rests on the elementary trigonometric inequality
\[
3+4\cos\theta+\cos 2\theta=2(1+\cos\theta)^2\ge0
\]
for all real \(\theta\). Expanding the real part of a suitable linear combination of logarithms of the Euler factors yields, for \(\sigma>1\),
\[
\operatorname{Re}\bigl(3\log\zeta(\sigma)+4\log\zeta(\sigma+it)+\log\zeta(\sigma+2it)\bigr)
=\sum_p\sum_{k\ge1}p^{-k\sigma}k^{-1}\bigl(3+4\cos(kt\log p)+\cos(2kt\log p)\bigr)\ge0.
\]
The left-hand side is therefore non-negative. If \(\zeta(1+it_0)=0\) for some \(t_0\neq0\), the middle term \(\log\zeta(\sigma+it_0)\) tends to \(-\infty\) as \(\sigma\downarrow1\), while the other two factors remain bounded (or grow at most like \(\log(1/(\sigma-1))\)), producing a contradiction. Hence \(\zeta(1+it)\neq0\).

#### Proposed extension and precise failure
Suppose one attempts the same device for a putative zero \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) with \(\beta>1/2\). Fix a real parameter \(\alpha\in(0,1)\) and consider the three vertical lines
\[
\sigma,\qquad\sigma+i\gamma,\qquad\sigma+2i\gamma
\]
with \(\sigma>\beta\). The identical trigonometric weight still gives
\[
\sum_p\sum_k p^{-k\sigma}k^{-1}\bigl(3+4\cos(k\gamma\log p)+\cos(2k\gamma\log p)\bigr)\ge0,
\]
so
\[
\operatorname{Re}\bigl(3\log\zeta(\sigma)+4\log\zeta(\sigma+i\gamma)+\log\zeta(\sigma+2i\gamma)\bigr)\ge0.
\]
If \(\zeta(\beta+i\gamma)=0\), then as \(\sigma\downarrow\beta\) the middle logarithm again tends to \(-\infty\). For a contradiction it is therefore necessary that the sum of the remaining two real parts stay bounded from above by something strictly smaller than the divergence rate of \(-\log|\zeta(\sigma+i\gamma)|\).

This fails for two independent, rigorously checkable reasons.

1. **Growth on the neighbouring lines.**  
   For \(\sigma\le1\) the function \(\zeta(\sigma+2i\gamma)\) is no longer given by an absolutely convergent Euler product. The functional equation
   \[
   \pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)=\pi^{-(1-s)/2}\Gamma((1-s)/2)\zeta(1-s)
   \]
   implies, via Stirling’s formula, that
   \[
   |\zeta(\sigma+2i\gamma)|\asymp|\gamma|^{(1/2-\sigma)/2+o(1)}\,|\zeta(1-\sigma-2i\gamma)|
   \]
   when \(|\gamma|\to\infty\). On the reflected line \(\operatorname{Re}s=1-\sigma<\frac12\) one has only the convexivity bound (or the Weyl-type subconvexity bound)
   \[
   |\zeta(1-\sigma-2i\gamma)|\ll|\gamma|^{c(1-\sigma)^{3/2}+\varepsilon}
   \]
   for an absolute \(c>0\). Consequently
   \[
   \operatorname{Re}\log\zeta(\sigma+2i\gamma)=\log|\zeta(\sigma+2i\gamma)|
   \]
   may tend to \(+\infty\) at a polynomial rate in \(|\gamma|\). This positive divergence can cancel the \(-\infty\) coming from the central factor, destroying the sign contradiction. (The same occurs, a fortiori, for the term at \(\sigma\).)

2. **Absence of a uniform zero-free width.**  
   Even if one inserts a smooth cut-off that keeps \(\sigma\) a fixed distance \(\delta>0\) above \(\beta\), the resulting lower bound on the weighted Dirichlet series is only
   \[
   \gg\sum_{p\le X}p^{-\sigma}\bigl(1+\cos(\gamma\log p)\bigr)^2+O(X^{1-\sigma}/\log X).
   \]
   Optimising \(X\) produces a zero-free region of the classical shape
   \[
   \beta\le1-\frac{c}{\log(|\gamma|+2)},
   \]
   but never forces \(\beta\le1/2\). The obstruction is quantitative: the trigonometric weight \(1+\cos\theta\) vanishes on a positive-density set of angles, so the primes that could detect a zero near height \(\gamma\) are too sparse to overcome the possible polynomial growth on the companion lines once \(\sigma\) drops below 1.

Thus the positivity method, while decisive on the line \(\operatorname{Re}s=1\), cannot cross into the critical strip without an independent subconvex bound strong enough to keep \(\log|\zeta(\sigma+2i\gamma)|\) from diverging positively—an input essentially equivalent in strength to a zero-density estimate already known to be insufficient for the full Riemann Hypothesis. No additional elementary trigonometric identity (higher-order Fejér kernels, etc.) repairs the gap, because any fixed finite combination of horizontal translates still leaves a reflected factor whose free growth is controlled only by the functional equation.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Classical positivity argument cannot be pushed below Re s=1; growth from functional equation blocks contradiction.
