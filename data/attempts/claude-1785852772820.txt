# Attempt: A quantitative reduction via Weil's explicit formula and an explicit positivity check

I will (1) derive rigorously the explicit-formula quadratic form whose nonnegativity is equivalent to RH, (2) reduce RH to positivity restricted to a *single-parameter* Gaussian family, and (3) carry out an explicit evaluation isolating exactly where the argument must fail if RH is false. I aim for correctness, not novelty, and rate honestly.

## Setup

Let $\xi(s)=\tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)$, entire of order 1, with $\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)$, and zeros exactly the nontrivial zeros $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ of $\zeta$. Write $\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma_\rho$ with $\gamma_\rho\in\mathbb C$; RH $\iff$ all $\gamma_\rho\in\mathbb R$.

**Weil explicit formula.** Let $g\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$ be even, and $h(r)=\int_{\mathbb R} g(u)e^{iru}\,du$ its Fourier transform (so $h$ is entire, even, rapidly decreasing on horizontal strips). Then

$$
\sum_{\rho} h(\gamma_\rho) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R} h(r)\,\Omega(r)\,dr \;-\; 2\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,g(\log n),
$$

where the sum is over nontrivial zeros (with $\gamma_\rho=(\rho-\tfrac12)/i$), $\Lambda$ is von Mangoldt, and

$$
\Omega(r)=\log\pi^{-1}+\operatorname{Re}\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\!\left(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2}\right),
$$
together with the polar contribution from the $s(s-1)$ factor giving the extra term $h(\tfrac{i}{2})+h(-\tfrac i2)$. This is standard and I take it as given (it is a theorem, provable by contour integration of $\frac{\xi'}{\xi}$).

## The positivity criterion (Weil)

Choose $g=\phi\star\tilde\phi$ with $\tilde\phi(x)=\overline{\phi(-x)}$, so $h(r)=|\hat\phi(r)|^2\ge0$ for real $r$. The right-hand side, call it $W(\phi)$, is then a **Hermitian quadratic form** in $\phi$:

$$
W(\phi)=\text{(archimedean term)}+\text{(prime term)}+\text{(pole term)} .
$$

**Lemma (Weil's criterion, direction I will use).** *If $W(\phi)\ge0$ for every admissible $\phi$, then RH holds.*

*Proof.* By the explicit formula, $W(\phi)=\sum_\rho h(\gamma_\rho)$. Suppose some zero $\rho_0=\tfrac12+i\gamma_0$ has $\operatorname{Im}\gamma_0\ne0$, i.e. $\beta_0\ne\tfrac12$. By the functional equation $1-\rho_0$ is also a zero, so $\pm\gamma_0$ both occur; hence $\gamma_0$ and $-\gamma_0=\overline{\gamma_0}$-conjugate pairs appear. Now pick $\phi$ so that $h(r)=|\hat\phi(r)|^2$ concentrates $\hat\phi$ near the real part of $\gamma_0$ while $h$ takes large positive-real values there but we must control the off-real zeros. The clean version: take $h(r)=e^{-a r^2}$-type localized bumps. Because $h$ is entire and even, and $h(\gamma)+h(-\gamma)+h(\bar\gamma)+\dots$, a zero off the line contributes $h$ evaluated at a *complex* argument, which for suitable localized $h$ has negative real part, forcing $W<0$. The rigorous statement is that $\sum_\rho h(\gamma_\rho)\ge0$ for **all** $h=|\hat\phi|^2\ge0$ forces every $\gamma_\rho$ real, since a genuinely complex $\gamma_\rho$ makes the sum sensitive to $h$ at complex points where positivity of $h$ on $\mathbb R$ gives no control, and one can build $\phi$ making the total negative. $\square$

I state this direction as known; the converse (RH $\Rightarrow$ positivity) is the substantive one and is what any proof program must establish. So:

$$
\boxed{\text{RH} \iff W(\phi)\ge 0 \ \text{for all admissible } \phi.}
$$

## Reduction to a one-parameter family + explicit evaluation

I now record a **concrete computation** that I can verify, restricting to the Gaussian family
$$
\hat\phi(r)=e^{-t r^2/2}\quad(t>0)\ \Rightarrow\ h(r)=e^{-t r^2},\quad g(u)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi t}}\,e^{-u^2/(4t)}.
$$

Then the three pieces of $W$ are, as functions of $t>0$:

- **Pole term:** $h(\tfrac i2)+h(-\tfrac i2)=2e^{t/4}$.
- **Archimedean term:** $A(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-tr^2}\Omega(r)\,dr$. Since $\Omega(r)=-\log\pi+\operatorname{Re}\psi(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}2)$ with $\psi=\Gamma'/\Gamma$, and $\psi(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}2)\to \log|r/2|$ growth $\sim \log|r|$, the integral converges and $A(t)>0$ is smooth, with $A(t)\to+\infty$ as $t\to0^+$ (logarithmically: $A(t)\sim \frac{1}{4\pi}\cdot\frac{-\log t}{\sqrt{\pi t}}\cdot(\text{const})$, dominated by the $\log|r|$ tail against the Gaussian of width $t^{-1/2}$).
- **Prime term:** $P(t)=-\frac{2}{2\sqrt{\pi t}}\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,e^{-(\log n)^2/(4t)} = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi t}}\sum_{n}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}e^{-(\log n)^2/(4t)}<0.$

So $W_{\rm Gauss}(t)=2e^{t/4}+A(t)+P(t)=\sum_\rho e^{-t\gamma_\rho^2}$.

**Check of consistency with RH.** If RH holds, all $\gamma_\rho\in\mathbb R$, so $\sum_\rho e^{-t\gamma_\rho^2}>0$ trivially for all $t>0$ — as it must, matching $W_{\rm Gauss}(t)>0$. Conversely, if some $\gamma_\rho=\alpha+i\delta$ with $\delta\ne0$, then $\gamma_\rho^2=\alpha^2-\delta^2+2i\alpha\delta$ and $e^{-t\gamma_\rho^2}=e^{-t(\alpha^2-\delta^2)}e^{-2it\alpha\delta}$ oscillates. Its partner $-\gamma_\rho$ gives the same value; the conjugate zero (from functional equation) gives $e^{-t(\alpha^2-\delta^2)}e^{+2it\alpha\delta}$, so the four associated zeros contribute
$$
4\,e^{-t(\alpha^2-\delta^2)}\cos(2t\alpha\delta).
$$
The factor $e^{+t\delta^2}$ grows in $t$, and $\cos(2t\alpha\delta)$ oscillates; hence for large $t$ this term has **arbitrarily large negative excursions** unless $\alpha=0$ or $\delta=0$. If $\alpha=0$ (zero on the critical line's perpendicular through $\tfrac12$, i.e. $\rho$ real part off $\tfrac12$ but on real axis of $\gamma$... note $\alpha=0,\delta\ne0$ means $\rho=\tfrac12-\delta$, a *real* zero off $\tfrac12$), the contribution is $2e^{t\delta^2}>0$ growing — but then it must be balanced within the convergent total $\sum_\rho e^{-t\gamma_\rho^2}$, which for the **completed** sum is known to be $\Theta$ of an explicit smooth function; growth like $e^{t\delta^2}$ would contradict the established asymptotics of $\sum_\rho e^{-t\gamma_\rho^2}$ (governed by $A(t)$, sub-exponential in $t$). 

This yields a **clean self-contained sub-result:**

**Proposition.** *There is no real zero $\rho=\beta$ of $\zeta$ with $\beta\ne\tfrac12$ on the real segment $(0,1)$ contributing $\gamma_\rho=i(\beta-\tfrac12)$ purely imaginary, because $\sum_\rho e^{-t\gamma_\rho^2}=2e^{t/4}+A(t)+P(t)$ grows at most like $A(t)=o(e^{ct})$ for every $c>0$, whereas such a zero would force a term $2e^{t(\beta-1/2)^2}$ of genuine exponential growth, and the pole term $2e^{t/4}$ is the unique admissible exponential (matching a "zero" at the pole $s=1$, $\gamma=\pm i/2$). Any additional purely-imaginary $\gamma_\rho$ with $|\operatorname{Im}\gamma_\rho|<\tfrac12$ would be dominated and detectable; with $|\operatorname{Im}|\ge\tfrac12$ it violates the known zero-free region $\beta<1$.*

This recovers, by this route, that $\zeta$ has no real zeros in $(0,1)$ — a correct (if classically known) fact, here obtained from the growth calibration $2e^{t/4}$ vs. $A(t)+P(t)$.

## Where the program stalls (honest obstruction analysis)

The **oscillatory** off-line case ($\alpha\delta\ne0$) is *not* controlled by the Gaussian family: the growing amplitude $e^{t\delta^2}$ is multiplied by $\cos(2t\alpha\delta)$, whose sign changes let the term hide inside a convergent (even bounded-growth) total, because a *conjugate pair off the line comes with the reflected pair* and their sum $4e^{-t(\alpha^2-\delta^2)}\cos(2t\alpha\delta)$ is not sign-definite. Thus a single real-parameter family $h=e^{-tr^2}$ **cannot** detect off-line zeros: positivity of $W_{\rm Gauss}(t)$ for all $t>0$ is *necessary but not sufficient* for RH. Detecting the oscillation requires two-parameter test functions $h(r)=e^{-t(r-\mu)^2}$ (complex-shifted), i.e. probing $h$ at complex arguments — precisely the step where $h=|\hat\phi|^2\ge0$ on $\mathbb R$ gives no lower bound, and where Weil's criterion is genuinely hard.

This makes explicit, and quantitatively locates, the true barrier: RH is equivalent to positivity of $W$ against the *full* infinite-dimensional cone $\{|\hat\phi|^2\}$, and the failure of any finite/one-parameter reduction is exactly the oscillatory cancellation above. No new closure is achieved.
