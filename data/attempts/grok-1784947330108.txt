The Riemann Hypothesis (RH) is equivalent to the assertion that the Weil explicit formula induces a positive-definite quadratic form on a suitable space of test functions. We isolate a concrete, checkable lemma that would imply a zero-free half-plane slightly larger than the classical zero-free region, and then show precisely where the argument collapses.

Let \(\Xi(t)=\xi(1/2+it)\) be the completed, self-dual xi-function (so RH \(\Leftrightarrow\) all zeros of \(\Xi\) are real). For a Schwartz test function \(g\) whose Fourier transform \(\hat g\) is supported in \((-2,2)\), the explicit formula reads
\[
\sum_{\rho}g\bigl(\tfrac{\rho-1/2}{i}\bigr)
=\hat g(0)\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}(1/4)-\int_0^\infty\frac{\hat g(t)+\hat g(-t)}{2}\frac{\Theta'(t)}{\Theta(t)}\,dt
+\sum_p\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\log p}{p^{k/2}}\bigl(\hat g(k\log p)+\hat g(-k\log p)\bigr),
\]
where the sum runs over the non-trivial zeros \(\rho\) of \(\zeta\) and \(\Theta(t)=\pi^{-it/2}\Gamma(1/4+it/2)\). Define the quadratic form
\[
Q(g)=\sum_{\rho}g\bigl(\tfrac{\rho-1/2}{i}\bigr)
\]
on the real vector space \(V\) of even Schwartz functions with \(\operatorname{supp}\hat g\subset(-2,2)\). Positivity \(Q(g)\ge0\) for all \(g\in V\) is known to be equivalent to RH.

Now restrict to the one-parameter family
\[
g_\lambda(t)=\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt\pi}\,e^{-\lambda^2 t^2},\qquad\lambda>1.
\]
A direct computation with the functional equation yields the asymptotic
\[
Q(g_\lambda)=\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt\pi}\Bigl(2\sum_{\gamma}\operatorname{Re}e^{-\gamma^2/\lambda^2}-C\log\lambda+O(1)\Bigr),
\]
where \(\gamma\) runs over the imaginary parts of the zeros and \(C>0\) is an absolute constant coming from the Gamma factor. Suppose there exists a zero \(\rho_0=\beta_0+i\gamma_0\) with \(\beta_0>1/2\). Then the contribution of the pair \(\rho_0,\overline{\rho_0}\) alone is
\[
2\operatorname{Re}g_\lambda\bigl(\tfrac{\rho_0-1/2}{i}\bigr)=2\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt\pi}\,e^{(\beta_0-1/2)^2/\lambda^2}\cos\bigl(2\gamma_0(\beta_0-1/2)/\lambda^2\bigr)e^{-\gamma_0^2/\lambda^2}.
\]
For \(\lambda\) large enough that \(\lambda^2>2|\gamma_0|/(\beta_0-1/2)\), the cosine is positive and the exponential growth \(e^{(\beta_0-1/2)^2/\lambda^2}\) dominates every other term (the remaining zeros contribute at most \(O(\lambda\log\lambda)\) by the classical density estimate \(N(T)=O(T\log T)\)). Hence \(Q(g_\lambda)<0\) for all sufficiently large \(\lambda\), contradicting positivity.

The obstruction is that the support restriction \(\operatorname{supp}\hat g\subset(-2,2)\) cannot be removed: the prime-power sum diverges as soon as the support reaches length \(2+\varepsilon\). Consequently the family \(\{g_\lambda\}\) does not lie in \(V\) for any \(\lambda\), and the sign of \(Q(g_\lambda)\) is uncontrolled. Extending the admissible support beyond length 2 while preserving positivity of the prime-power contribution remains open; the classical zero-free region \(\sigma\ge1-c/\log(|t|+2)\) is recovered precisely by cutting the support at length \(1-\delta\), which is the largest interval on which the von Mangoldt sum stays positive by the prime-number theorem with classical error term.

Thus the lemma “\(Q(g_\lambda)\ge0\) for all large \(\lambda\)” would give a zero-free strip \(\operatorname{Re}s>1/2+\delta\), but the lemma lies outside the range of the explicit formula.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Support obstruction blocks the Gaussian family from the admissible test-function space; no new zero-free region.
