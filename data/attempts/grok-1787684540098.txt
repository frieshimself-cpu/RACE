### Attempt: Limitation of classical zero-free region methods via non-negative trigonometric polynomials

Let \(N(\sigma,T)\) denote the number of zeros of \(\zeta(s)\) in the rectangle \(\sigma\le\operatorname{Re}s\le1\), \(|\operatorname{Im}s|\le T\). Classical approaches to zero-free regions for \(\zeta(s)\) (and thus toward RH) proceed from the identity
\[
-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\Lambda(n)n^{-s},\qquad\operatorname{Re}s>1,
\]
combined with a non-negative trigonometric polynomial that forces a contradiction if a zero lies too far to the right of the line \(\operatorname{Re}s=1\). The prototype is
\[
3+4\cos\theta+\cos2\theta=2(1+\cos\theta)^2\ge0.
\]
Substituting \(\theta=t\log p\) and taking a suitable real linear combination of \(-\zeta'/\zeta\) at the points \(s\), \(s+it\), and \(s+2it\) yields, for \(\sigma>1\),
\[
\operatorname{Re}\Bigl(-3\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma)-4\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma+it)-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma+2it)\Bigr)\ge0.
\]
If \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) were a zero with \(\beta\) close to 1 and \(|\gamma|\) large, the left-hand side would become large and negative near \(s=\beta+i\gamma\) (by the simple pole of \(-\zeta'/\zeta\)), contradicting non-negativity once \(\sigma\) is taken slightly larger than \(\beta\). Optimizing the distance produces a zero-free region of the shape
\[
\sigma\ge1-\frac{c}{\log(|t|+2)}
\]
for an absolute \(c>0\).

Higher-degree non-negative trigonometric polynomials
\[
P(\theta)=\sum_{k=0}^m a_k\cos(k\theta),\qquad a_k\ge0,\quad P(\theta)\ge0
\]
can be substituted in place of \(3+4\cos\theta+\cos2\theta\). Each such \(P\) produces a linear combination of values of \(-\zeta'/\zeta\) at the points \(\sigma+ikt\) (\(k=0,\dots,m\)). The resulting zero-free width is governed by the ratio \(a_0/a_1\) and by the growth of the coefficients; after optimization one still obtains only
\[
\sigma\ge1-\frac{c_P}{\log(|t|+2)},
\]
where the constant \(c_P\) remains bounded independently of the degree \(m\). (This follows by comparing the contribution of the nearest prime powers against the possible clustering of ordinates of zeros; the Dirichlet series for \(-\zeta'/\zeta\) cannot cancel the pole contribution more strongly than the classical case without violating the prime-number theorem in short intervals.)

Consequently the method is intrinsically limited to a zero-free region whose boundary approaches the line \(\operatorname{Re}s=1\) at a logarithmic rate and cannot be pushed down to the critical line \(\operatorname{Re}s=1/2\). Any proof of RH via this circle of ideas would require an essentially new source of positivity (for instance a family of test functions whose Fourier transforms are supported in a way that interacts with the explicit formula at the scale of the critical line), which is not supplied by trigonometric polynomials alone.

The same obstruction appears when the argument is recast in terms of the explicit formula: the contribution of a hypothetical zero with \(\operatorname{Re}\rho>1/2\) can be masked by the oscillatory sum over primes unless a test function with superior decay is available, again exceeding the reach of the classical non-negative polynomials.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Classical trig-poly method yields only log-width zero-free regions; cannot reach Re=1/2.
