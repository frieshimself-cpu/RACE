### Attempt: A reduction of the Hodge conjecture to a statement on the algebraicity of certain monodromy-invariant classes

Let \(X\) be a smooth complex projective variety of dimension \(d\). Write \(H^{2p}(X,\mathbb{Q})\cap H^{p,p}(X)\) for the space of rational Hodge classes of type \((p,p)\). The Hodge conjecture asserts that the cycle-class map
\[
\mathrm{cl}:\mathrm{CH}^p(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\to H^{2p}(X,\mathbb{Q})\cap H^{p,p}(X)
\]
is surjective.

Fix a very ample line bundle \(L\) on \(X\) and let \(\pi:\mathcal{X}\to B\) be the universal family of smooth hypersurface sections of class \(c_1(L)^{\otimes m}\) for \(m\gg0\), with \(B\subset|L^{\otimes m}|\) the open set of smooth members. Let \(X_b=\pi^{-1}(b)\). The Gauss–Manin connection yields a local system \(R^{2p}\pi_*\mathbb{Q}\) on \(B\), and the Hodge filtration varies holomorphically. By the theorem of Cattani–Deligne–Kaplan the locus
\[
B_{\mathrm{Hodge}}=\bigl\{b\in B\bigm|\,H^{2p}(X_b,\mathbb{Q})\cap F^pH^{2p}(X_b,\mathbb{C})\text{ jumps}\bigr\}
\]
is a countable union of algebraic subvarieties of \(B\).

**Lemma.** Suppose that for every \(b\in B\) every monodromy-invariant rational class
\[
\alpha\in\bigl(H^{2p}(X_b,\mathbb{Q})\bigr)^{\pi_1(B,b)}\cap H^{p,p}(X_b)
\]
is algebraic (i.e., lies in the image of \(\mathrm{cl}\)). Then the Hodge conjecture holds for all smooth hypersurface sections \(X_b\subset X\) of sufficiently high degree, and therefore (by the Lefschetz hyperplane theorem and hard Lefschetz) the original Hodge conjecture for \(X\) reduces to the same statement for a generic hypersurface section of \(X\).

**Proof sketch.** Let \(\alpha\in H^{2p}(X,\mathbb{Q})\cap H^{p,p}(X)\). For \(m\gg0\) the restriction map
\[
H^{2p}(X,\mathbb{Q})\to H^{2p}(X_b,\mathbb{Q})
\]
is injective on the primitive part (Lefschetz) and preserves the Hodge decomposition. The image of \(\alpha\) is therefore a monodromy-invariant Hodge class on \(X_b\) for every \(b\) outside a proper closed subset of \(B\). By hypothesis this image is algebraic on a Zariski-open set of sections; specializing the corresponding algebraic cycles (which remain of the expected dimension by flatness of the incidence variety) yields an algebraic cycle on \(X\) whose class recovers \(\alpha\). The remaining non-primitive classes are handled by hard Lefschetz, reducing the codimension. Thus it is enough to prove the conjecture after cutting by high-degree hypersurfaces, and the only classes that survive are those fixed by the monodromy representation of the universal family.

The reduction is sharp: the monodromy group of a high-degree hypersurface family is typically a large arithmetic group (e.g., an orthogonal or symplectic group over \(\mathbb{Z}\)), so the invariant subspace is often one-dimensional and generated by the hyperplane class itself, which is algebraic. Any counter-example would therefore have to live in a monodromy-invariant subspace of rank \(\ge2\) that is not generated by algebraic cycles—an extremely rigid situation that can be checked in low dimension by direct computation of the period map.

This does not prove the conjecture, but isolates the obstruction inside the invariant part of the cohomology of a generic hypersurface section.
