### Attempt: Obstruction to closing the zero-free gap via the classical explicit formula and Turán power-sum method

Let \(\xi(s)=\frac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\). The completed function is entire of order 1, satisfies the functional equation \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\), and the non-trivial zeros of \(\zeta\) are precisely the zeros of \(\xi\). Write zeros as \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) (with \(\beta\in(0,1)\)). The Riemann–von Mangoldt formula gives
\[
N(T)=\#\{\rho:0<\gamma\le T\}=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi}-\frac{T}{2\pi}+O(\log T).
\]
The classical explicit formula (in Weil’s form, for a Schwartz test function \(g\) with Fourier transform \(\hat g\) of compact support in \((-1,1)\)) reads
\[
\sum_{\rho}\hat g(\rho)=\hat g(0)+\hat g(1)-\sum_p\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\log p}{p^{k/2}}\bigl(g(k\log p)+g(-k\log p)\bigr)+O\bigl(\|g\|_{1,w}\bigr),
\]
where the weight accounts for the archimedean contribution from \(\Gamma'/\Gamma\).

Weil’s positivity criterion asserts that RH holds if and only if
\[
\sum_{\rho}\hat g(\rho)\ge0
\]
for every admissible \(g\ge0\) (with equality only for the zero function). A natural attempt to force all \(\beta=1/2\) is therefore to produce a family of test functions for which the prime-power side is strictly dominated by the archimedean and trivial terms whenever a zero with \(\beta>1/2\) is assumed to exist.

Turán’s power-sum method supplies one concrete realization: if a zero \(\rho_0=\beta_0+i\gamma_0\) with \(\beta_0>1/2\) exists, then for large \(N\) the moments
\[
S_k=\sum_{|\gamma|\le T}(\rho-\tfrac12)^{-k},\qquad k=1,\dots,N,
\]
can be made to violate the Turán inequalities that hold for purely imaginary spectra. Inserting the explicit formula converts the power sums into short Dirichlet polynomials
\[
\sum_{n\le x}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{1/2}}\,n^{it}P(\log n),
\]
where \(P\) is a polynomial of degree \(O(N)\) coming from the generating function of the power sums. The resulting \(L^2\) mean is estimated by the large-sieve inequality
\[
\int_T^{2T}\Bigl|\sum_{n\le x}a_n n^{it}\Bigr|^2\,dt\ll(T+x)\sum|a_n|^2.
\]
Optimizing \(x=T^\theta\) and \(N=\lfloor c\log T\rfloor\) yields a zero-free region of the shape
\[
\beta\le1-c(\log|\gamma|)^{-2/3}(\log\log|\gamma|)^{-1/3}
\]
(the Korobov–Vinogradov width). The same optimization, however, shows that the method saturates precisely when the Dirichlet polynomial begins to feel the possible Siegel zero or the resonance produced by a putative zero with \(\beta-1/2\asymp(\log|\gamma|)^{-2/3}\). In that regime the large-sieve constant ceases to improve, and the archimedean contribution from \(\Gamma'/\Gamma\) (Stirling) cannot cancel the extra positive term \(\hat g(\rho_0)\) without forcing the support of \(\hat g\) outside the admissible interval \((-1,1)\), which re-introduces the full prime sum and destroys positivity.

Consequently every test-function family generated by power sums or by band-limited approximations to the indicator of a vertical strip fails to produce a contradiction once \(\beta-1/2=o(1/\log\log|\gamma|)\). Closing the remaining gap therefore requires either (i) a new large-value estimate for zeta that beats the convexivity bound on the critical line by a factor larger than any power of \(\log\log\), or (ii) an arithmetic input stronger than the prime-number theorem with classical zero-free region (e.g., a quasi-RH on a positive-density set of zeros). No such input is supplied by the explicit formula alone. The obstruction is thus structural: the method is limited by the same spectral density that it is trying to control.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Explicit-formula/Turán approach saturates at Korobov width; no new zero-free or positivity lemma.
