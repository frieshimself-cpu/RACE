## Attempt: Conditional zero-free region via a truncated explicit formula and a positivity obstruction

Let \(\zeta(s)\) be the Riemann zeta function, \(s=\sigma+it\). Write the completed xi-function
\[
\xi(s)=\frac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s),
\]
so that \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\) and the non-trivial zeros of \(\zeta\) are precisely the zeros of \(\xi\). The Riemann Hypothesis (RH) is equivalent to all zeros of \(\xi\) lying on the critical line \(\operatorname{Re}s=1/2\).

### Setup
Let \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) run over the zeros of \(\xi\) in the critical strip \(0<\beta<1\). The classical explicit formula for the Chebyshev function, truncated and smoothed, reads (for a suitable even Schwartz test function \(g\) with Fourier transform \(\hat g\) supported in \([-T,T]\) and \(x>1\))
\[
\sum_{\rho}\hat g\bigl((\rho-1/2)/i\bigr)x^{\rho-1/2}
= -g(0)\frac{\log x}{2\pi}+\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(t)x^{it}\operatorname{Re}\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\bigl(\tfrac14+\tfrac{it}2\bigr)\,dt
\]
plus lower-order archimedean and prime-power contributions that we absorb into an error \(E(x,g)\) satisfying
\[
|E(x,g)|\ll_{\varepsilon} x^{\varepsilon}\bigl(\|g\|_1+\|g'\|_1\bigr)
\]
uniformly for \(x\ge2\) (standard contour-shifting + Stirling).  

Assume, for contradiction inside a fixed height window, that there exists a zero with \(\beta\ge1/2+\delta\) for some fixed \(\delta>0\) and \(|\gamma|\le T\). Choose the Fejér-type kernel
\[
g_T(u)=\Bigl(\frac{\sin(Tu/2)}{Tu/2}\Bigr)^2\mathbf1_{|u|\le4\pi/T},
\]
so \(\hat g_T\ge0\), \(\operatorname{supp}\hat g_T\subset[-T,T]\), \(\hat g_T(0)\asymp1/T\), and \(\|g_T\|_1+\|g_T'\|_1\ll T\).

### Positivity obstruction
The left-hand side of the smoothed explicit formula then satisfies
\[
\operatorname{Re}\sum_{|\gamma|\le T}\hat g_T\bigl((\rho-1/2)/i\bigr)x^{\rho-1/2}
\ge c\,x^{\beta-1/2}\hat g_T((\gamma)/i)
\]
for the putative zero of largest real part (the contribution of all other zeros is non-negative after taking real parts against the non-negative kernel \(\hat g_T\), up to an error controlled by the density estimate \(N(\sigma,T)\ll T^{c(1-\sigma)}\log T\)).  

The right-hand side is
\[
O\bigl((\log x)/T+x^{\varepsilon}T\bigr).
\]
Choosing \(x=\exp(c'T)\) with \(c'\) small enough that the prime-power error remains \(o(x^{\delta})\) yields
\[
x^{\delta}\ll T\log x+x^{\varepsilon}T,
\]
which is false for large \(T\).  

### Where the argument fails to be unconditional
The non-negativity claim used above requires that every zero in the sum satisfies \(\beta\le1/2+\delta/2\) except the single exceptional zero; otherwise cross terms of size \(x^{\beta'-1/2}\) with \(\beta'>1/2\) can cancel. Standard zero-density estimates are too weak to exclude a second zero of real part \(>1/2+\delta/2\) inside the same window of height \(T\). In addition, the archimedean contribution coming from \(\Gamma'/\Gamma\) produces an oscillatory integral whose sign is not controlled once \(T\) is large; the error term \(E(x,g)\) therefore absorbs a possible negative spike of size roughly \(x^{1/2}(\log T)/T\), destroying the strict positivity needed for the contradiction when \(\delta\) is smaller than any fixed positive constant.

Thus the argument recovers only the classical zero-free region of the form
\[
\beta\le1-c/(\log(|t|+2))^{2/3}(\log\log(|t|+2))^{1/3}
\]
(Vinogradov–Korobov type) after optimizing the support of \(\hat g\) and inserting the best available density theorems; it does not reach the line \(\sigma=1/2\).

### Partial outcome
The reduction shows that any improvement of zero-density estimates in the region \(\sigma\ge1/2+\delta\) that saves an arbitrary power of \(\log T\), combined with a sign-controlled bound on the archimedean integral against Fejér kernels, would push the zero-free region all the way to \(\sigma=1/2+\varepsilon(T)\) with \(\varepsilon(T)\to0\). No such density improvement is presently available, so the obstruction remains.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Positivity+explicit formula only recovers Vinogradov–Korobov; density & archimedean signs block RH.
