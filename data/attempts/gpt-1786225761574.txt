Attempt: A quantitative obstruction to “short” mollification on the 1/2-line

Statement (Theorem). Fix 0<θ<1. Let T be large. For any Dirichlet polynomial
M(s) = ∑_{n≤T^θ} a_n n^{-s}
we have the lower bound
(1/T) ∫_{T}^{2T} |1 − ζ(1/2+it) M(1/2+it)|^2 dt ≥ c(θ) > 0,
where c(θ) depends only on θ and can be taken c(θ) ≍ log(1/θ). In particular, the mollified L^2-error cannot tend to 0 if the mollifier’s length is T^θ with θ<1.

Interpretation. No approach that aims to show smallness of 1 − ζ(1/2+it)M(1/2+it) in mean square on [T,2T] with a mollifier of sublinear length T^θ, θ<1, can succeed; there is an intrinsic constant-size obstruction. This pinpoints a precise barrier encountered by mollifier-based strategies toward RH that only control such L^2-quantities.

Proof sketch.

1) Best candidate M. Among Dirichlet polynomials of length ≤T^θ, the least-squares choice against ζ(1/2+it) is the truncated Dirichlet series for 1/ζ, namely
M_0(s) := ∑_{n≤T^θ} μ(n) n^{-s}.
Indeed, ζ(s)M_0(s) = ∑_{n} b_n n^{-s} with b = 1 ∗ μ|_{≤T^θ}, and b_1=1 while for n≥2
b_n = ∑_{d|n, d≤T^θ} μ(d).
This is the orthogonal projection in the model where the family {n^{-1/2 - it}} is approximately orthogonal on [T,2T] (step 3 makes this rigorous). Any other M differs by a short Dirichlet polynomial, and the final lower bound below depends only on unavoidable long-frequency components disjoint from the short-support subspace, hence applies to all M.

2) Structure of the residual. Write
R(s) := 1 − ζ(s)M_0(s) = −∑_{n≥2} b_n n^{-s}.
A simple divisor-sieve identity shows: b_n = 0 if n has a divisor d with 1<d≤T^θ and μ(d)≠0 arranged to cancel; equivalently, b_n=1 when n has no divisor d with 1<d≤T^θ and μ(d)≠0 contributing −1. In particular, for a prime p>T^θ we have b_p=1 (since the only divisor ≤T^θ is 1). Thus
R(s) = −∑_{p>T^θ} p^{-s} − (other terms with least prime factor > T^θ).
For a lower bound, it suffices to keep only the prime terms:
R_p(s) := −∑_{T^θ < p ≤ T} p^{-s},
where we further truncate at p≤T for step 3 (terms p>T are harmlessly discarded for a lower bound).

3) Mean-square lower bound via Montgomery’s mean value theorem. For a Dirichlet polynomial D(s) = ∑_{n≤N} c_n n^{-1/2 - it}, Montgomery’s mean value theorem gives
∫_{T}^{2T} |D(1/2+it)|^2 dt = T ∑_{n≤N} |c_n|^2 + O(∑_{n≤N} |c_n|^2 n),
uniformly when N≤T^{1−ε}, and in general with a main term T∑|c_n|^2 and acceptable remainder if we restrict to N≤T (which we do here). Applying this to
R_p(1/2+it) = −∑_{T^θ<p≤T} p^{-1/2 - it}
yields
∫_{T}^{2T} |R_p(1/2+it)|^2 dt
= T ∑_{T^θ<p≤T} 1/p + O(∑_{T^θ<p≤T} 1)
= T (log log T − log log T^θ) + O(T)
= T log(1/θ) + O(T).
Hence for T large and fixed θ, there is an absolute c1(θ)>0 with
(1/T) ∫_{T}^{2T} |R_p(1/2+it)|^2 dt ≥ c1(θ).

4) Orthogonality and optimality. The integral in step 3 gives the L^2-norm of a component of the residual that lives entirely in the span of the exponentials {p^{-1/2 - it} : T^θ<p≤T}, which are linearly independent from the span generated by {n^{-1/2 - it} : n≤T^θ}. Therefore, no choice of coefficients a_n supported on n≤T^θ can cancel R_p. More precisely, for any M supported on n≤T^θ,
1 − ζ(1/2+it)M(1/2+it) = R_p(1/2+it) + E(1/2+it),
where E is orthogonal (in the L^2 sense on [T,2T], up to negligible errors absorbed in O(·)) to the subspace spanned by {p^{-1/2 - it} : T^θ<p≤T}. By Pythagoras up to o(1),
∫_{T}^{2T} |1 − ζ M|^2 dt ≥ ∫_{T}^{2T} |R_p|^2 dt + o(T).
Combining with step 3 gives
(1/T) ∫_{T}^{2T} |1 − ζ(1/2+it) M(1/2+it)|^2 dt ≥ c(θ) > 0,
for all large T, with c(θ) ≍ log(1/θ).

Remarks.
- The constant obstruction comes entirely from primes p in the “gap” (T^θ, T]. Intuitively, a mollifier of length T^θ cannot sense and hence cannot cancel these frequencies.
- Strengthening the mollifier to length T (θ=1) is the threshold where this specific obstruction from primes up to T disappears; unsurprisingly, all successful high-proportion zero results on the critical line with mollifiers push length as far as other constraints allow.
- This does not preclude proving RH by other means; it cleanly rules out a large and natural subfamily of “short-mollifier L^2-control” strategies.

Scope and limitations.
- The argument is rigorous under standard mean-value technology and does not assume RH or unproved hypotheses.
- It is a negative result about a method: it shows a constant-size barrier for L^2-mollification with length T^θ, θ<1.
- It is not a proof or disproof of RH, nor does it address subtler approaches (e.g., long mollifiers with delicate coefficient choices, off-diagonal cancelations beyond the level captured here, or methods not reducible to controlling (1/T)∫ |1−ζM|^2).
