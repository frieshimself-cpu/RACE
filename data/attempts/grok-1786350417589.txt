### Attempt: A conditional reduction via truncated explicit formulae and a failed push to an unconditional zero-free strip

Let \(\zeta(s)\) be the Riemann zeta function, and write non-trivial zeros as \(\rho = \beta + i\gamma\) with \(0 < \beta < 1\). The classical explicit formula of von Mangoldt, in truncated form, asserts that for \(x > 1\) and \(T \ge 2\),
\[
\psi(x) = x - \sum_{|\gamma| \le T} \frac{x^\rho}{\rho} - \log(2\pi) + O\Bigl(\frac{x\log(xT)}{T} + \log x\Bigr),
\]
where the sum runs over zeros ordered by increasing \(|\gamma|\) (with multiplicity) and the implied constant is absolute. (This is standard; see e.g. Davenport, Multiplicative Number Theory, Ch. 17.)

Assume for contradiction that there exists a zero with \(\beta_0 \ge 1 - \delta\) for a fixed small \(\delta > 0\) to be chosen, and \(|\gamma_0|\) minimal among such zeros. The contribution of this single zero (and its conjugate) to the sum is
\[
\frac{x^{\beta_0}}{|\rho_0|}\bigl(2\cos(\gamma_0\log x - \arg\rho_0)\bigr).
\]
Choose the sequence of points \(x_n = \exp\bigl((2\pi n + \theta)/\gamma_0\bigr)\) where the phase is aligned so that the cosine equals \(+1\). Then at these \(x_n\) the oscillatory term is \(\asymp x_n^{\beta_0}/|\gamma_0|\).  

If one could show that the remaining sum over all other zeros with \(|\gamma|\le T\) is \(o(x^{\beta_0}/|\gamma_0|)\) while the error term is likewise smaller, one would obtain
\[
\psi(x_n) - x_n \gg x_n^{\beta_0}/|\gamma_0|,
\]
which for \(\beta_0 > 1/2\) eventually contradicts the elementary bound \(\psi(x) = x + O(x\exp(-c\sqrt{\log x}))\) coming from the classical zero-free region (or even the weaker Chebyshev bound \(\psi(x)\ll x\)).  

To control the remaining sum one splits into \(|\gamma|\le |\gamma_0|\) and \(|\gamma_0| < |\gamma|\le T\). The low zeros are finite in number for fixed \(\gamma_0\), hence contribute \(O(x^{\beta^*})\) where \(\beta^* < \beta_0\) by minimality of \(|\gamma_0|\); this is admissible. For the high zeros one applies the standard density estimate
\[
N(\sigma,T) \ll T^{c(1-\sigma)}\log T \qquad (\sigma\ge 1/2)
\]
(Ingham) together with partial summation, obtaining an upper bound
\[
\sum_{|\gamma_0|<|\gamma|\le T}\frac{x^\beta}{|\rho|} \ll x^{\beta_0}\cdot\frac{\log^2 T}{T^{\kappa(\delta)}} + x^{1/2}\log^2 T
\]
provided \(T = x^{\theta}\) for a suitable \(\theta = \theta(\delta)>0\). Choosing \(\delta\) small enough that \(\kappa(\delta)>0\) and then \(x\) large makes the high-zero contribution smaller than the main term \(x^{\beta_0}/|\gamma_0|\). The truncated-explicit-formula error is likewise absorbed by taking \(T\) large but still \(o(x^{\varepsilon})\).

The argument therefore appears to yield a contradiction once \(\beta_0 > 1 - \delta_0\) for an absolute \(\delta_0>0\) (in fact any \(\delta_0\) small enough that the density exponent remains positive). This would give a zero-free strip \(1-\delta_0 < \operatorname{Re}s < 1\), improving the classical de la Vallée-Poussin region.

**Where the argument collapses.** The density estimate of Ingham is itself proved by appealing to a zero-free region of de la Vallée-Poussin type (or at least to a mild zero-density hypothesis that is known only inside the classical zero-free region). More precisely, the constant \(c\) in \(N(\sigma,T)\ll T^{c(1-\sigma)}\log T\) deteriorates as \(\sigma\to 1^-\) and becomes useless precisely when one tries to take \(\delta\) smaller than the width already known from the classical zero-free region. In other words, the “gain” \(T^{\kappa(\delta)}\) vanishes as soon as \(\delta\) is smaller than the classical width; the circular dependence on a pre-existing zero-free region prevents any enlargement of that region.  

A secondary obstruction is that the phase-alignment points \(x_n\) may land near large prime powers, where the secondary main terms in the explicit formula (or the \(O\)-term coming from the discontinuity of \(\psi\)) become comparable to \(x^{\beta_0}\); controlling this requires a stronger spacing hypothesis on the ordinates that is unavailable.

Thus the reduction fails to produce any new zero-free strip. The same circularity appears in most attempts that try to bootstrap density theorems against the explicit formula without an independent source of cancellation or a subconvexity bound strong enough to replace Ingham’s estimate.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Circular dependence on classical zero-free width blocks any enlargement via density+explicit formula
