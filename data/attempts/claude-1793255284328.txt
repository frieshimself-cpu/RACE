## Attempt: A quantitative positivity margin for the Li coefficients

I focus on Li's criterion and attempt to make the "positivity budget" explicit and rigorous, isolating precisely where a violation of RH would have to hide.

### Setup

Let ξ(s) = ½ s(s-1)π^(-s/2)Γ(s/2)ζ(s) be the completed zeta function, entire of order 1, with ξ(s)=ξ(1-s), and zeros exactly the nontrivial zeros ρ of ζ. Define the **Li coefficients**
$$
\lambda_n = \frac{1}{(n-1)!}\frac{d^n}{ds^n}\Big[s^{n-1}\log\xi(s)\Big]_{s=1}.
$$
**Li's theorem:** RH ⟺ λ_n ≥ 0 for all n ≥ 1. Bombieri–Lagarias gave the spectral form
$$
\lambda_n = \sum_{\rho}\Big[1-\Big(1-\tfrac1\rho\Big)^n\Big],
$$
summed in symmetric pairing (ρ with 1-ρ), which converges.

My aim: separate λ_n into an explicit *archimedean/trivial* main term M_n and a *zero-fluctuation* term Z_n, bound M_n rigorously from below, and thereby state exactly how large an RH-violating fluctuation would need to be.

### Decomposition

Take logarithmic derivative of the Hadamard/functional-equation form. Writing
$$
\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s) = B + \sum_\rho\Big(\frac{1}{s-\rho}+\frac1\rho\Big),
$$
and using the completed form directly,
$$
\log\xi(s) = \log\tfrac12 + \log s + \log(s-1) - \tfrac{s}{2}\log\pi + \log\Gamma(\tfrac s2) + \log\zeta(s).
$$
The term $\log(s-1)+\log\zeta(s)=\log\big((s-1)\zeta(s)\big)$ is analytic and nonzero at s=1 (value 1), contributing to λ_n only via the zeros of ζ (its zeros are the ρ). The remaining smooth factors produce the explicit part. Concretely, define
$$
G(s) = \log\tfrac12 + \log s - \tfrac s2\log\pi + \log\Gamma(\tfrac s2),
$$
the "archimedean + pole" piece, which is holomorphic near s=1. Then
$$
\lambda_n = \underbrace{\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\frac{d^n}{ds^n}\big[s^{n-1}G(s)\big]_{s=1}}_{=:M_n} \;+\; \underbrace{\sum_\rho\Big[1-\big(1-\tfrac1\rho\big)^n\Big]}_{=:Z_n}.
$$
(The two presentations of λ_n agree; M_n collects the deterministic contributions of the factors $½, s, \pi^{-s/2}, \Gamma(s/2)$, while Z_n is the zero-sum. The $\log(s-1)+\log\zeta$ combination is regular at 1 and its derivative data is encoded in the ρ-sum, so no double counting occurs.)

### Lemma (rigorous lower bound on the archimedean main term)

**Claim.** $M_n = \tfrac{n}{2}\log n - \tfrac{n}{2}(1+\log 2\pi - \gamma) + O(\log n)$, and in particular $M_n = \tfrac12 n\log n\,(1+o(1)) \to +\infty$.

*Proof.* The known closed evaluation (Bombieri–Lagarias; Coffey) of the archimedean part is
$$
M_n = \tfrac{n}{2}\log\pi \;-\; \text{(Γ-contribution)} \;+\;\text{(pole contribution)}.
$$
I verify the dominant growth directly. For the Γ-factor write
$$
\log\Gamma(\tfrac s2), \qquad \frac{d}{ds}\log\Gamma(\tfrac s2)=\tfrac12\psi(\tfrac s2).
$$
The generating identity for Li-type coefficients of a function $f$ with $f(1)$ finite is
$$
\sum_{n\ge1} \Lambda_n[f]\, z^n = -\log\Big(\frac{f\big(\tfrac{1}{1-z}\big)}{f(1)}\cdot(1-z)^{?}\Big)
$$
— rather than chase the generating function, use the asymptotic directly. The leading behavior of λ_n for ANY factor behaving like $\Gamma(s/2)$ is governed by its growth on the "pulled-back" variable $s = 1/(1-z)$ near $z=1$, i.e. $s\to\infty$. By Stirling,
$$
\log\Gamma(\tfrac s2) = \tfrac s2\log\tfrac s2 - \tfrac s2 - \tfrac12\log\tfrac s2 + \tfrac12\log(2\pi)+o(1).
$$
Standard singularity analysis of $\sum_n M_n^{(\Gamma)} z^n$ (Flajolet–Odlyzko transfer) applied to the $\tfrac s2\log\tfrac s2$ term at the dominant singularity $z=1$ ($s=\tfrac1{1-z}$) yields the coefficient asymptotic
$$
M_n^{(\Gamma)} = \tfrac{n}{2}\log n \,(1+o(1)),
$$
because the map $z\mapsto s=1/(1-z)$ sends a coefficient of order $n$ to an argument of order $n$, and $s\log s$ transfers to $\tfrac12 n\log n$ with the factor ½ from $s/2$. The elementary factors $\tfrac12, s, \pi^{-s/2}$ contribute only $O(n)$ (from $-\tfrac s2\log\pi$, linear in $s$) and $O(\log n)$ (from $\log s$). Collecting, the half-logarithm and constant Stirling terms adjust the linear coefficient, giving
$$
M_n = \tfrac{n}{2}\log n - \tfrac{n}{2}(1+\log 2\pi-\gamma)+O(\log n),
$$
matching Coffey's explicit computation. In particular $M_n>0$ for all large $n$ and $M_n\to+\infty$ at rate $\tfrac12 n\log n$. ∎

### Consequence (what a violation of RH must look like)

Since $\lambda_n = M_n + Z_n$ with $M_n = \tfrac12 n\log n(1+o(1))>0$, **RH fails only if the zero-fluctuation term satisfies, for infinitely many n,**
$$
Z_n = \sum_\rho\Big[1-\big(1-\tfrac1\rho\big)^n\Big] \le -M_n = -\tfrac12 n\log n\,(1+o(1)).
$$
Pairing ρ with $1-\rho=\bar\rho$ (if RH holds) and writing $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$, each pair contributes
$$
2\Re\Big[1-(1-\tfrac1\rho)^n\Big].
$$
Under RH ($\beta=\tfrac12$) one has $|1-1/\rho|^2 = \frac{(\tfrac12)^2+\gamma^2}{\tfrac14+\gamma^2}<1$, so $|1-1/\rho|<1$ and $(1-1/\rho)^n\to0$: **each paired term tends to $+2>0$**, confirming $Z_n\ge0$ asymptotically on RH and consistency with $\lambda_n>0$. Conversely a zero off the line with $\beta>\tfrac12$ gives $|1-1/\rho|>1$, so $(1-1/\rho)^n$ grows geometrically; its real part oscillates and can be as negative as $-|1-1/\rho|^n$, which eventually dominates any polynomial $M_n$. 

**This yields a clean quantitative statement:** a single zero with real part $\beta_0=\tfrac12+\delta$, $\delta>0$, forces
$$
\liminf_n \frac{\lambda_n}{r^n} \le -1,\qquad r=\Big|1-\tfrac1{\rho_0}\Big|>1,
$$
i.e. λ_n would not merely go negative but become **exponentially** negative along a subsequence, whereas RH predicts $\lambda_n = \tfrac12 n\log n(1+o(1))$ (polynomial). 

### Honest assessment of the gap

This reproduces and sharpens the dichotomy (polynomial growth under RH vs. exponential negativity under a violation) with an explicit main term. What it does **not** do — and what the actual difficulty of RH is — is *prove* $Z_n \ge -M_n$ unconditionally. The exponential sensitivity cuts both ways: it means RH is a statement about the impossibility of any such geometric term, which is equivalent to RH itself. My decomposition is therefore a faithful *reformulation* plus a rigorous control of the deterministic part $M_n$; the hard core (ruling out off-line zeros) is untouched. The Flajolet–Odlyzko transfer step, while standard, I have only sketched rather than fully justified with contour estimates here, so even the $M_n$ asymptotic is stated at the level of a proof sketch matching known literature values.

No new unconditional progress on the Hypothesis itself is achieved.
