### Attempt: Analysis of the failure of Sturm-type oscillation methods for the Hardy \(Z\)-function

Let \(\theta(t)\) denote the Riemann–Siegel theta function
\[
\theta(t)=\arg\bigl(\pi^{-it/4}\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac14+it/2\bigr)\bigr)
\]
(continuous, odd, with \(\theta(0)=0\)). The Hardy function
\[
Z(t)=e^{i\theta(t)}\zeta\bigl(\tfrac12+it\bigr)
\]
is real-analytic and real-valued for real \(t\). The non-trivial zeros of \(\zeta(s)\) on the critical line are precisely the real zeros of \(Z\). The Riemann Hypothesis is therefore equivalent to the assertion that \(Z\) has no non-real zeros in the complex plane (more precisely, that every zero of \(\zeta\) in the critical strip arises from a real zero of \(Z\)).

A natural strategy, inspired by classical Sturm oscillation theory, is to realize \(Z\) (or a suitable multiple) as the solution of a second-order linear ODE
\[
y''+Q(t)y=0
\]
on the real line, for which the Prüfer phase or the Sturm comparison theorem would force all zeros to be real and simple once a positivity or monotonicity condition on \(Q\) is verified. Concretely, one seeks a potential \(Q\) such that
\[
Z''+QZ=0,\qquad Q>0\quad\text{and}\quad Q'\text{ of controlled sign}.
\]
If such a \(Q\) existed with, e.g., \(Q(t)\ge(\pi/\ell)^2\) on successive intervals of length \(\ell\), the classical Sturm separation theorem would imply that zeros are simple and that the zero-counting function \(N_Z(T)\) grows at least linearly, while the known asymptotic
\[
N_Z(T)=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi}-\frac{T}{2\pi}+O(\log T)
\]
would then force every zero of \(\zeta\) in the strip to lie on the critical line (by comparing with the total zero-counting function \(N(T)\)).

This approach fails for a precise analytic reason. From the functional equation and the definition of \(\theta\), one has the representation
\[
Z(t)=2\sum_{n\le\sqrt{t/2\pi}}n^{-1/2}\cos\bigl(\theta(t)-t\log n\bigr)+R(t),
\]
where the remainder \(R\) is of lower order. Differentiating twice under the sum produces
\[
Z''(t)=-\bigl(\theta'(t)\bigr)^2Z(t)+E(t),
\]
with an error \(E\) that still contains the rapidly oscillating sum
\[
\sum n^{-1/2}(\log n)^2\cos\bigl(\theta(t)-t\log n\bigr)
\]
plus derivatives of the phase. Consequently the formal potential would have to be
\[
Q(t)=\bigl(\theta'(t)\bigr)^2-\frac{E(t)}{Z(t)}.
\]
The main term \(\theta'(t)=\frac12\log(t/2\pi)+O(1/t)\) is positive and slowly varying, but the error term \(E/Z\) is a ratio of two almost-periodic sums of comparable magnitude. On the real line this ratio remains bounded on average, yet it changes sign on every interval of length \(O(1)\) (by the density of the frequencies \(\log n\)). In particular \(Q\) is not eventually positive, nor does it satisfy any uniform Sturm comparison hypothesis on successive intervals. Extending the same formal ODE off the real axis only worsens the situation: the resulting \(Q(s)\) acquires essential singularities from the Gamma factor and fails to be real on any horizontal line \(\operatorname{Im}s=c\neq0\).

Thus no Sturmian comparison is available, and the oscillation theorems cannot be applied. The obstruction is structural: the arithmetic almost-periodicity of the Dirichlet series cannot be absorbed into a potential of controlled sign, which is why every attempt to force reality of the zeros of \(Z\) by ODE methods has remained inconclusive.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Sturm-ODE approach to Z(t) blocked by sign-changing arithmetic error in the formal potential Q
