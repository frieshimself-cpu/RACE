# Attempt: A clean sufficient criterion, and a sharp analysis of where the "generation in degree 2" reduction breaks

## Setup

Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety over $\mathbb C$ of dimension $n$. Write
$H^{2p}(X,\mathbb Q)$ with its Hodge structure, and let
$$\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X) = H^{2p}(X,\mathbb Q)\cap H^{p,p}(X)$$
be the space of rational Hodge classes. Let $\mathcal A^p(X)\subseteq \mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)$ be the subspace of classes of algebraic cycles. The Hodge conjecture is $\mathcal A^p=\mathrm{Hdg}^p$ for all $p$.

I record two facts used throughout:

- **(L)** *Lefschetz (1,1):* $\mathcal A^1(X)=\mathrm{Hdg}^1(X)$, i.e. every rational $(1,1)$ class is algebraic (it is a $\mathbb Q$-combination of divisor classes).
- **(M)** *Multiplicativity:* if $\alpha=[Z_1],\ \beta=[Z_2]$ are algebraic, their cup product $\alpha\cup\beta$ is algebraic (it is represented by an intersection product / correspondence). Hence $\bigoplus_p\mathcal A^p(X)$ is a subring of $\bigoplus_p H^{2p}(X,\mathbb Q)$.

## Proposition (a rigorous sufficient criterion)

**Assume:**
1. $H^{2,0}(X)=0$ (equivalently $H^2(X,\mathbb Q)=\mathrm{Hdg}^1(X)$, all of $H^2$ is of type $(1,1)$), and
2. the $\mathbb Q$-algebra $H^\bullet(X,\mathbb Q)$ is generated by $H^2(X,\mathbb Q)$.

**Then the Hodge conjecture holds for $X$.**

**Proof.** By (2), for each $p$ the cup-product map
$$\mu_p:\ \operatorname{Sym}^p H^2(X,\mathbb Q)\ \longrightarrow\ H^{2p}(X,\mathbb Q)$$
is surjective, and $H^{2k+1}(X,\mathbb Q)=0$ automatically (an odd-degree class would be a polynomial in even-degree $H^2$ classes, hence even, so $0$; more precisely the subalgebra generated by $H^2$ lies in even degrees, and by (2) it is everything).

By (1), $H^2(X,\mathbb Q)=\mathrm{Hdg}^1(X)$, so by (L) every element of $H^2$ is algebraic: $H^2(X,\mathbb Q)=\mathcal A^1(X)$. By (M), every product of $p$ elements of $H^2$ lies in $\mathcal A^p(X)$. Since $\mu_p$ is surjective, these products span $H^{2p}(X,\mathbb Q)$. Therefore
$$\mathcal A^p(X)=H^{2p}(X,\mathbb Q)\supseteq \mathrm{Hdg}^p(X).$$
As $\mathcal A^p\subseteq\mathrm{Hdg}^p$ always, we get equality. $\qquad\blacksquare$

This applies e.g. to varieties admitting a cellular (affine paving) decomposition — projective spaces, Grassmannians, flag varieties, smooth toric varieties — recovering the (known) fact that such varieties satisfy the Hodge conjecture, but via a self-contained argument isolating exactly the two hypotheses that make it work.

## The sharp point: why hypothesis (1) cannot be dropped

It is tempting to try to prove Hodge for *any* variety whose cohomology ring is generated in degree $2$, using only that $\mathrm{Hdg}^1$ is algebraic and multiplicativity. The natural claim would be:

> "$\mu_p$ surjective $\Rightarrow$ $\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)$ is spanned by products of classes in $\mathrm{Hdg}^1(X)$."

**This claim is false**, and pinning down its failure is instructive.

$\mu_p$ is a **surjective morphism of Hodge structures** of weight $2p$ (with $\operatorname{Sym}^pH^2$ carrying its natural weight-$2p$ structure). For a surjective morphism $f:A\twoheadrightarrow B$ of pure Hodge structures, functoriality of the Hodge decomposition and $\mathbb C$-linearity give surjectivity on each graded piece:
$$f\big(A^{r,s}\big)=B^{r,s}\qquad\text{for all }r,s. \tag{$\ast$}$$
In particular $f(A^{p,p})=B^{p,p}$. **However**, $(\ast)$ is a statement about *complex* $(p,p)$-subspaces, and it does **not** descend to rational Hodge classes:
$$f\big(A_{\mathbb Q}\cap A^{p,p}\big)\ \subseteq\ B_{\mathbb Q}\cap B^{p,p},\qquad\text{but this inclusion can be strict.}$$
The reason is precisely that $A^{p,p}$ (complex $(p,p)$-space) is in general strictly larger than $\mathbb C\otimes(A_{\mathbb Q}\cap A^{p,p})$ (the complexification of the *rational* $(p,p)$ classes). Given a rational Hodge class $b\in B_{\mathbb Q}\cap B^{p,p}$, choose any rational preimage $a_0\in A_{\mathbb Q}$ (exists since $f$ is $\mathbb Q$-surjective). Its Hodge component $a_0^{p,p}\in A^{p,p}$ satisfies $f(a_0^{p,p})=b$ — but $a_0^{p,p}$ need not be rational, and there may be **no** rational $(p,p)$ class in the fiber $a_0+\ker f$ at all.

Concretely, in $\operatorname{Sym}^pH^2$ a rational Hodge class of type $(p,p)$ can be a genuine sum of monomials involving the $H^{2,0}$ and $H^{0,2}$ pieces of $H^2$ — for instance a product $\eta\cdot\bar\eta$ with $\eta\in H^{2,0}$ landing in $(2,2)$ — and such "mixed" combinations are exactly the ones whose algebraicity is *not* delivered by (L)+(M). This is a microcosm of the general difficulty of the Hodge conjecture: **the obstruction is not the failure of multiplicativity, but the existence of rational $(p,p)$ classes that are invisible to the rational $(1,1)$ classes** — they live in $A^{p,p}$ but not in $\mathbb C\otimes A^{1,1}_{\mathbb Q}$-generated part.

Hypothesis (1), $H^{2,0}=0$, kills this obstruction outright: then $A^{p,p}=A^{2p}$ has a purely rational structure in each degree and the descent problem evaporates. Removing (1) reintroduces exactly the transcendental gap that the Hodge conjecture is about.

## Summary of contribution

- A self-contained, rigorous proof that **$H^{2,0}=0$ together with degree-2 generation of the cohomology ring implies the Hodge conjecture** (Proposition above), with the two structural inputs (L) and (M) explicitly isolated.
- A precise identification, via the failure of $(\ast)$ to descend to $\mathbb Q$-Hodge classes, of *why* the hypothesis $H^{2,0}=0$ is essential and cannot be replaced by degree-2 generation alone — locating the transcendental obstruction inside the map $\operatorname{Sym}^pH^2\to H^{2p}$.

This is genuine but modest progress: a clean sufficient condition and a sharp diagnosis, not a resolution of any open case.
