### Attempt: Why Direct Diagonalization Cannot Separate NP from P

We analyze a canonical “constructive” approach that succeeds for the deterministic time hierarchy but fails to place a diagonal language inside NP. The goal is to exhibit a language \(L\in\mathrm{NP}\setminus\mathrm{P}\) by dovetailing against an enumeration of all deterministic polynomial-time machines.

Let \(\{M_i\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\) be a standard enumeration of deterministic TMs in which \(M_i\) is clocked to run in at most \(p_i(n)=n^i+i\) steps on inputs of length \(n\). (Every language in \(\mathrm{P}\) is accepted by some \(M_i\).) A diagonal language is defined by
\[
x\in L\iff M_{|x|}(x)\text{ rejects},
\]
with the usual “delay” that the simulation of \(M_i\) is performed only on a sufficiently sparse set of lengths so that the simulation itself stays inside a desired time bound. By the deterministic time-hierarchy theorem the resulting \(L\) lies in \(\mathrm{DTIME}(2^{O(n)})\setminus\mathrm{P}\). The same construction can be made to work relative to any oracle, yielding the classical Baker–Gill–Solovay relativized worlds.

The obstruction to placing \(L\) inside NP is quantitative and appears already at the level of witnesses. Membership of a string \(x\) in an NP language is witnessed by a certificate \(w\) of length at most \(q(|x|)\) for some fixed polynomial \(q\), and the predicate “\(V(x,w)=1\)” must be decidable in deterministic polynomial time. In the diagonal construction the only information that distinguishes \(x\) from its complement is the outcome of a deterministic simulation whose running time is \(p_{|x|}(|x|)\). That simulation transcript has length \(\Theta(p_{|x|}(|x|))\), which is super-polynomial for every fixed polynomial bound once \(|x|\) is large enough that the index \(i=|x|\) exceeds the degree of the bound. Consequently no polynomial-length certificate can encode the necessary simulation; any verifier that attempted to re-run the simulation would itself require super-polynomial time, exiting NP.

One may try to compress the transcript by nondeterminism: guess a short “proof” that the simulation rejects. But any such proof system would constitute an NP procedure for the complement of a \(\mathrm{P}\)-complete language (or for the complement of an arbitrary language in \(\mathrm{P}\)), collapsing \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}\) and, by padding, the entire polynomial hierarchy—an assumption far stronger than \(\mathrm{P}\ne\mathrm{NP}\) and known to be false relative to some oracles. Hence the compression step is unavailable without already resolving a question at least as hard as the original problem.

The same length obstruction persists under every known relativizing or algebrizing simulation technique: the certificate length remains polynomially bounded while the diagonalizing computation grows as \(n^{\omega(1)}\). Therefore any successful separation of NP from P must employ a non-constructive or non-relativizing ingredient that supplies short witnesses for a language that is nevertheless hard for every deterministic polynomial-time machine. Direct diagonalization supplies no such ingredient.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Diagonalization yields EXP\P but cannot enforce poly-length NP witnesses without collapse.
