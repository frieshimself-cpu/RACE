### Attempt: Obstruction to “axiomatic” proofs of RH via functional equation + Euler product alone

Let \(\xi(s)=\frac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\). The functional equation \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\) together with the fact that \(\xi\) is entire of order 1 and the Euler product for \(\zeta\) on \(\operatorname{Re}s>1\) are the most elementary global properties of \(\zeta\). A natural strategy is to deduce that every zero of \(\xi\) is simple and lies on the critical line \(\operatorname{Re}s=\frac12\) from these properties alone (or from a mild strengthening such as a zero-free half-plane \(\operatorname{Re}s\ge1\) and a growth bound \(|\xi(\sigma+it)|\ll_\varepsilon |t|^{C(1-\sigma)+\varepsilon}\)).

We show that no such deduction is possible. Consider the broader class of completed \(L\)-functions attached to ideal-class characters of imaginary quadratic fields, or more simply the Epstein zeta functions
\[
Z_Q(s)=\sum_{(m,n)\ne(0,0)}Q(m,n)^{-s},\qquad Q(x,y)=ax^2+bxy+cy^2
\]
associated with positive-definite binary quadratic forms of discriminant \(d=b^2-4ac<0\). After multiplication by the usual Gamma factor \(\pi^{-s}\Gamma(s)\) one obtains an entire function \(\xi_Q\) of order 1 satisfying the identical functional equation \(\xi_Q(s)=\xi_Q(1-s)\). For many choices of \(Q\) (e.g., \(Q(x,y)=x^2+5y^2\)) it is classical that \(\xi_Q\) possesses zeros with real part strictly larger than \(\frac12\). These counter-examples obey every axiom that is customarily extracted from \(\xi\) (order-1 entirety, functional equation, Euler-product-type representation in a half-plane, polynomial growth in vertical strips). Consequently any argument that uses only those axioms would prove the analogue of RH for \(\xi_Q\), which is false.

The obstruction is sharp: the missing ingredient that distinguishes \(\zeta\) from the Epstein examples is the complete multiplicativity (or at least the Ramanujan bound on coefficients) coming from the unique factorization in \(\mathbb{Z}\), together with the precise location of the pole at \(s=1\). Any successful proof must therefore exploit arithmetic information beyond the functional equation and the existence of an Euler product.

(The same obstruction rules out naïve Hilbert–Pólya strategies that would realize the zeros as eigenvalues of a hypothetical self-adjoint operator constructed solely from the functional equation; such an operator, if it existed at that level of generality, would force the Epstein zeros onto the critical line as well.)
