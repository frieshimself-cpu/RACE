### Attempt: Obstruction to a positivity proof of RH via the completed xi-function on vertical lines

Let \(\xi(s)=\frac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\). The functional equation is \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\), and the non-trivial zeros of \(\zeta\) are precisely the zeros of \(\xi\). Write \(s=\frac12+z\), so that the critical line is the imaginary axis in the \(z\)-plane and \(\Xi(z):=\xi(\frac12+z)\) is entire of order 1, even (\(\Xi(z)=\Xi(-z)\)), and real-valued on the real and imaginary axes. RH is equivalent to all zeros of \(\Xi\) being purely imaginary.

Consider the family of vertical translates
\[
f_t(x):=\Xi(x+it),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},\quad t\ge0.
\]
Each \(f_t\) is real-analytic, and RH holds if and only if \(f_t\) has no real zeros for every \(t\). Because \(\Xi\) is even, it is enough to control \(t\ge0\). The Hadamard product (normalized so that the product converges) reads
\[
\Xi(z)=\Xi(0)\prod_{\rho}\Bigl(1-\frac{z^2}{\rho^2}\Bigr),
\]
where the product runs over zeros with positive imaginary part (counted with multiplicity). Consequently
\[
\log|f_t(x)|=\log|\Xi(0)|+\sum_{\rho}\log\Bigl|1-\frac{(x+it)^2}{\rho^2}\Bigr|.
\]
A standard approach to RH is to produce a strictly positive weight \(w\) such that the weighted integral
\[
I(t):=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\log|f_t(x)|\,w(x)\,dx
\]
is strictly negative for all \(t>0\) whenever a zero off the critical line exists, while \(I(t)\ge0\) under RH (or vice versa). Such a sign contradiction would force all zeros onto the line.

We now show that no weight belonging to the natural class
\[
\mathcal{W}=\bigl\{w\in L^1(\mathbb{R})\cap C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}):w\ge0,\ \hat w\ge0\bigr\}
\]
can produce the desired strict sign. (The Fourier non-negativity is forced by the requirement that the functional equation and the Gamma factors remain under control after integration against \(w\).)

Assume for contradiction that there exists \(w\in\mathcal{W}\) for which \(I(t)\) changes sign according to the presence of off-line zeros. Expand the logarithm of the Hadamard product and interchange sum and integral (justified by the rapid decay of \(w\) and the known zero-density bound \(N(\sigma,T)=O(T^{1-c(\sigma-1/2)}\log T)\)):
\[
I(t)=\log|\Xi(0)|\int w+\sum_{\rho}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\log\Bigl|1-\frac{(x+it)^2}{\rho^2}\Bigr|w(x)\,dx.
\]
Write \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) with \(\beta\ge\frac12\). The inner integral is the convolution of \(\log|1-(x+it)^2/\rho^2|\) against \(w\). Because \(\hat w\ge0\), the Fourier representation of the logarithm yields that each term is minimized precisely when \(\beta=\frac12\). More precisely, the map
\[
\beta\mapsto\int\log\Bigl|1-\frac{(x+it)^2}{(\beta+i\gamma)^2}\Bigr|w(x)\,dx
\]
is strictly decreasing for \(\beta>\frac12\) (differentiate under the integral and use the Poisson representation of the logarithm together with \(\hat w\ge0\)). Therefore the global minimum of \(I(t)\) over all possible zero configurations with a fixed number of zeros occurs when every \(\beta=\frac12\). In that configuration one has \(I(t)\ge c_w> -\infty\), but the constant \(c_w\) is independent of any off-line displacement. Hence a configuration with some \(\beta>\frac12\) can only increase \(I(t)\). No choice of \(w\in\mathcal{W}\) can make \(I(t)\) dip below its critical-line value, so no sign contradiction arises.

The same monotonicity obstructs every attempt that tries to replace the compactly supported weight by a Schwartz weight whose Fourier transform remains non-negative (e.g., Gaussians). Thus the most direct positivity method compatible with the functional equation is blocked by the variational characterization of the critical line inside the Hadamard product.

(The obstruction disappears if one drops the condition \(\hat w\ge0\), but then the Gamma-factor contribution \(\int\log|\Gamma(\frac14+\frac{x+it}2)|w(x)\,dx\) becomes uncontrollable for large \(t\), and the integral \(I(t)\) diverges.)

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Positivity approach via even weights on vertical lines blocked by Hadamard monotonicity
