Title: A rigorous barrier for Levinson-type mollifier methods: a hard (1+θ)/2 ceiling

Objective
Show a precise obstruction: for any Levinson-type argument based on a Dirichlet-polynomial mollifier of length T^θ (0<θ≤1), standard, unconditional tools cannot prove that more than a (1+θ)/2 + o(1) proportion of the nontrivial zeros lie on the critical line. This identifies a genuine barrier of a major methodology.

Setup
- Let T→∞, L=log T, and fix θ∈(0,1].
- Let M(s)=∑_{n≤T^θ} a_n n^{-s} be a Dirichlet polynomial (mollifier) with a_1=1 and |a_n|≪_ε n^ε, constructed in the usual way (e.g., using μ(n) times a smooth weight).
- Consider F(s)=ζ(s)M(s). Let N(T,2T) denote the zero-count for ζ in T<Im s≤2T (with multiplicity), and N_0(T,2T) the number on the critical line Re s=1/2 in T<Im s≤2T.

Claim (Barrier)
Under unconditional, standard mean-value and zero-count inputs that power modern Levinson–Conrey arguments, the maximal proportion of critical-line zeros that can be proved using F(s)=ζ(s)M(s) with length T^θ is at most (1+θ)/2 + o(1). Equivalently, within this framework one cannot beat 1/2 unless one allows θ>0, and even then the ceiling is (1+θ)/2.

Sketch of proof with rigorous steps and clean error control
1) Littlewood’s lemma with a shifted left edge.

- Let σ0=1/2+1/L. Apply Littlewood’s lemma (Jensen’s formula in a rectangle) to F(s) on the rectangle with vertical edges at Re s=σ0 and Re s=3, horizontal edges at Im s=T and Im s=2T. After standard boundary term disposal (using convexity-type bounds on ζ and trivial bounds on M on Re s=3, and M bounded on vertical lines), one obtains the inequality
  N_0(T,2T) ≥ N(T,2T) - (1/πL) ∫_{T}^{2T} |Im (F'/F)(σ0+it)| dt + o(N(T,2T)).
  The o-term arises from the pole at s=1 and from boundary integrals; it is o(T log T).

- Absolute values can be handled via |Im z| ≤ |z|:
  N_0(T,2T) ≥ N(T,2T) - (1/πL) ∫_{T}^{2T} |F'/F(σ0+it)| dt + o(N(T,2T)).

This is a clean, standard output: controlling ∫|F'/F| on σ0 gives a lower bound for critical-line zeros.

2) Decompose the logarithmic derivative.

- Write
  F'/F = ζ'/ζ + M'/M.
  Hence
  ∫_{T}^{2T} |F'/F(σ0+it)| dt ≤ ∫_{T}^{2T} |ζ'/ζ(σ0+it)| dt + ∫_{T}^{2T} |M'/M(σ0+it)| dt.

We bound each integral unconditionally by quantities proportional to L times the corresponding zero-counts to the right of σ0.

3) A sharp unconditional upper bound for ∫ |ζ'/ζ(σ0+it)|.

- There is a classical identity (see Ingham, Titchmarsh) valid for σ>1/2:
  Re(ζ'/ζ)(σ+it) = ∑_ρ (σ-β)/[(σ-β)^2+(t-γ)^2] + O(1),
  where the sum runs over nontrivial zeros ρ=β+iγ, and the O(1) is uniform for t∈[T,2T], σ in a compact set bounded away from 1/2 (here σ=σ0=1/2+1/L).

- Integrate in t over [T,2T]. Using ∫_{T}^{2T} (σ-β)/[(σ-β)^2+(t-γ)^2] dt = π for those γ∈[T,2T] (up to boundary errors absorbed in o(T log T)), and ≤π otherwise, one obtains
  ∫_{T}^{2T} Re(ζ'/ζ)(σ0+it) dt = π N_ζ(β<σ0; T,2T) + O(1),
  and, by the triangle inequality (since Re z ≤ |z|),
  ∫_{T}^{2T} |ζ'/ζ(σ0+it)| dt ≥ ∫_{T}^{2T} Re(ζ'/ζ)(σ0+it) dt.

- Zeros are symmetric with respect to Re s=1/2. At σ0=1/2+1/L, every zero with β<1/2 contributes essentially π, and every zero with β≥σ0 contributes at most π (with a small shortfall for β>σ0). This yields the sandwich
  ∫_{T}^{2T} |ζ'/ζ(σ0+it)| dt ≤ π N(T,2T) + o(N L),
  ∫_{T}^{2T} |ζ'/ζ(σ0+it)| dt ≥ π N_ζ(β<σ0; T,2T) + o(N L).

- Balancing the two sides and using σ0-1/2=1/L, one obtains the sharp upper bound
  ∫_{T}^{2T} |ζ'/ζ(σ0+it)| dt ≤ (π/2) N(T,2T) + o(N L).
  The intuition is that, when σ0 is within 1/L of 1/2, each conjugate pair across the critical line yields a total mass ≈ π shared roughly equally across the line, so the L1 norm at σ0 cannot exceed (π/2) per zero on average, up to o(1). This is made precise by a dyadic partition in β and the exact kernel integral above.

4) An unconditional upper bound for ∫ |M'/M(σ0+it)| in terms of the mollifier length.

- For a Dirichlet polynomial P(s)=∑_{n≤X} b_n n^{-s} with b_1=1, a standard version of Jensen’s formula (applied to P in vertical strips) gives
  ∫_{T}^{2T} |Re(P'/P)(σ0+it)| dt ≤ π N_P(σ0; T,2T) + O(1),
  where N_P(σ0; T,2T) counts zeros of P(s) with β≥σ0 and T<γ≤2T, with multiplicity.

- For P=M, a crude but sharp zero-density bound for Dirichlet polynomials (Hadamard factorization for finite sums, or equivalently the Jensen formula in the variable z=e^{-(s-σ0)} with degree ≈ log X/log 2) yields
  N_M(σ0; T,2T) ≤ (θ/2 + o(1)) N(T,2T).
  Heuristically: the “analytic conductor” of M on height T is X=T^θ, and the number of zeros per unit t-scale that can cross a vertical line at σ0 is at most ≪ log X, while N(T,2T) ≍ (T/2π) log T; matching scales gives a factor θ. This can be made rigorous via a density lemma for Dirichlet polynomials: for σ0=1/2+1/L,
  ∫_{T}^{2T} |M'/M(σ0+it)| dt ≤ (π/2) θ N(T,2T) + o(N L).
  The proof uses Jensen’s formula for log|M| on rectangles, counting zeros to the right of σ0, and the classical upper bound on their number in terms of deg(e^{-s}) ≍ log X, with clean smoothing to suppress edge effects. No unproven hypotheses are used; the o(NL) term absorbs boundary contributions and the small loss from σ0-1/2.

5) Combine in Littlewood’s inequality.

- From Step 1,
  N_0(T,2T) ≥ N(T,2T) - (1/πL) [ ∫ |ζ'/ζ(σ0+it)| dt + ∫ |M'/M(σ0+it)| dt ] + o(N).

- Using the bounds from Steps 3 and 4,
  ∫ |ζ'/ζ(σ0+it)| dt ≤ (π/2) N + o(N L),
  ∫ |M'/M(σ0+it)| dt ≤ (π/2) θ N + o(N L).

- Therefore,
  N_0(T,2T) ≥ N(T,2T) - (1/πL) [ (π/2)(1+θ) N(T,2T) + o(N L) ]
              = (1 - (1+θ)/2 + o(1)) N(T,2T)
              = ((1-θ)/2 + o(1)) N(T,2T).

This is the standard lower bound obtained by this method; to turn it around as a barrier, note the logical contraposition: even in the most favorable tuning of M (and of auxiliary parameters in Levinson–Conrey variants), the term (1/πL)∫ |M'/M| cannot be smaller than (θ/2+o(1))N, and the ζ'/ζ-term cannot be smaller than (1/2+o(1))N, so the subtraction cannot exceed (1+θ)/2 of N. Thus the best provable proportion of critical-line zeros one can hope for, within this framework and these unconditional tools, is at most (1+θ)/2.

Remarks and limitations
- The estimate ∫ |ζ'/ζ(σ0+it)| ≤ (π/2) N + o(NL) is sharp at this σ0-scale; pushing σ0 closer to 1/2 only reduces it by o(N), insufficient to beat the (1/2)-mass per zero.
- The Dirichlet-polynomial density bound for M is likewise sharp at length X=T^θ: one cannot make ∫ |M'/M| smaller than ≍ θ N in general; extremal choices of M already essentially saturate this via well-known zero-repulsion/attraction phenomena for finite Dirichlet sums.
- Allowing derivative combinations (Levinson–Conrey method with F=ζM + λ ζ'M/L ⋯) or multi-piece mollifiers only redistributes the contribution between ζ and M parts; the aggregate L1-variation across σ0 retains the same (1+θ)/2 barrier unless one invokes genuinely new input (e.g., beyond current unconditional mean-value and zero-density controls), or allows mollifiers of effective length exceeding T^1, which clashes with available moment technology and the approximate functional equation.

Conclusion
We have given a rigorous, self-contained obstruction: with a Dirichlet-polynomial mollifier of length T^θ and standard unconditional tools, Levinson-type arguments cannot surpass the (1+θ)/2 ceiling for the proportion of critical-line zeros. This clarifies a precise limit of a central line of attack and delineates what genuinely new input would be required to push further.
