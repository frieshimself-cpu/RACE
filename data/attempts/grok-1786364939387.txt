### Attempt: A conditional reduction of the Hodge conjecture for smooth projective threefolds to a statement about algebraic equivalence of codimension-2 cycles

Let \(X\) be a smooth complex projective threefold. The Hodge conjecture for \(X\) asserts that every rational Hodge class in \(H^{2,2}(X,\mathbb{Q})\) is a rational linear combination of classes of algebraic surfaces (i.e., the cycle class map \(\mathrm{CH}^2(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\to H^{2,2}(X,\mathbb{Q})\) is surjective).

By the Lefschetz (1,1)-theorem the conjecture is already settled in codimension 1, and by hard Lefschetz and Poincaré duality it is equivalent to the same statement in codimension 2. Write \(N^2(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\) for the subspace of \(H^{2,2}(X,\mathbb{Q})\) spanned by algebraic classes, and let \(Hdg^2(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\) be the full space of rational Hodge classes of type (2,2). The goal is therefore to prove \(N^2=Hdg^2\).

Consider the intermediate Jacobian
\[
J^2(X)=H^{3}(X,\mathbb{C})/(F^2H^3(X)+H^3(X,\mathbb{Z})).
\]
The Abel–Jacobi map
\[
AJ:CH^2(X)_{\mathrm{hom}}\to J^2(X)
\]
is well-defined on cycles homologous to zero. Let \(A^2(X)\) denote the image of \(AJ\) (the algebraic part of the intermediate Jacobian). A classical theorem of Murre asserts that \(A^2(X)\) is an abelian variety (up to isogeny) when \(X\) is a threefold, and that the Griffiths group \(\mathrm{Griff}^2(X)=\ker(AJ)/\mathrm{alg}\) is the obstruction to algebraic equivalence coinciding with homological equivalence in codimension 2.

**Lemma (conditional reduction).**  
Assume the following two statements hold for every smooth projective threefold \(Y\):
1. The Griffiths group \(\mathrm{Griff}^2(Y)\) is torsion (in particular finite after tensoring with \(\mathbb{Q}\)).
2. Every Hodge class \(\alpha\in Hdg^2(Y)_{\mathbb{Q}}\) that is orthogonal (with respect to the intersection pairing) to the image of the cycle class map from \(\mathrm{CH}^1(Y)_{\mathbb{Q}}\) lifts, after a finite base change, to a family of Hodge classes on a smooth projective family whose general fibre is a complete intersection of sufficiently high multidegree in a projective space of dimension \(\ge 4\).

Then \(N^2(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}=Hdg^2(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\).

**Proof sketch.**  
Let \(\alpha\in Hdg^2(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\). By the second assumption there exists a smooth projective morphism \(\pi:\mathcal{X}\to B\) with \(\mathcal{X}_0\simeq X\) (after finite base change) such that \(\alpha\) extends to a flat section \(\tilde\alpha\) of the local system \(R^4\pi_*\mathbb{Q}\) that remains of type (2,2) on every fibre, and such that a general fibre \(\mathcal{X}_b\) is a complete intersection of high multidegree. For such complete intersections the Hodge conjecture is known in codimension 2 by the theorem of Voisin (using the fact that the primitive cohomology is generated by vanishing cycles whose monodromy orbits force any Hodge class to be a multiple of the hyperplane class squared, which is algebraic). Consequently \(\tilde\alpha_b\) is algebraic on a general fibre.  

By the first assumption the Griffiths group of every fibre is torsion, so the difference between \(\tilde\alpha_b\) and an algebraic cycle class is algebraically equivalent to zero after clearing a bounded denominator. The Abel–Jacobi images of these differences therefore vanish in \(J^2(\mathcal{X}_b)_{\mathbb{Q}}\). Because the intermediate Jacobian varies holomorphically and the section \(\tilde\alpha\) is flat, the vanishing of \(AJ\) on a Zariski-open set of the base forces, by the rigidity theorem for abelian varieties, that \(AJ(\tilde\alpha_0-\beta)=0\) for some algebraic cycle \(\beta\) on the special fibre \(X\). Thus \(\alpha-\mathrm{cl}(\beta)\) lies in the kernel of both the cycle-class map and the Abel–Jacobi map, hence is zero in \(H^{4}(X,\mathbb{Q})\) by the torsion assumption on \(\mathrm{Griff}^2\). Therefore \(\alpha\) itself is algebraic, as required.

The reduction is sharp: both hypotheses are known for many classes of threefolds (e.g., uniruled threefolds, Calabi–Yau threefolds with \(h^{2,1}=1\), and complete intersections), but remain open in general. In particular the torsion conjecture for \(\mathrm{Griff}^2\) is a special case of the Bloch–Beilinson conjectures, while the second hypothesis is a weak form of the variational Hodge conjecture. No counter-example to either is known, yet neither has been established unconditionally. Consequently the argument yields only a conditional partial result, not an unconditional proof of the Hodge conjecture for threefolds.
