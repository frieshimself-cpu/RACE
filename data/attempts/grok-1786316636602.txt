### Attempt: A conditional reduction via a weighted explicit formula and a positivity obstruction

Let \(\xi(s)=\frac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\). The functional equation \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\) is entire of order 1, and RH asserts that every non-trivial zero satisfies \(\operatorname{Re}\rho=\frac12\).

Write the Hadamard product (normalized)
\[
\xi(s)=\xi(0)\prod_{\rho}\Bigl(1-\frac{s}{\rho}\Bigr),
\]
the product running over zeros with multiplicity, grouped symmetrically. Taking the logarithmic derivative yields
\[
\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)=\sum_{\rho}\frac1{s-\rho}.
\]
For a fixed smooth compactly supported test function \(\phi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}_{>0})\) with \(\phi\ge0\) and \(\widehat\phi\) of rapid decay, the Guinand–Weil explicit formula reads
\[
\sum_{\rho}\widehat\phi\Bigl(\frac{\rho-\frac12}{i}\Bigr)
=\int_0^\infty\bigl(\phi(x)+\phi(1/x)\bigr)\frac{x^{1/2}}{1-x}\,dx
+\text{archimedean and prime-power terms involving }\Lambda(n)\phi(\log n).
\]
(The precise archimedean contribution is a linear combination of \(\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\) evaluated at shifts of \(\frac12+it\).)

**Lemma (positivity obstruction).** Suppose there exists a zero \(\rho_0=\sigma_0+it_0\) with \(\sigma_0>\frac12\). Choose a non-negative even Schwartz function \(\psi\) whose Fourier transform \(\widehat\psi\) is supported in a tiny interval about \(t_0\) of width \(\ll|\sigma_0-\frac12|\) and satisfies \(\widehat\psi(t_0)>0\). Set
\[
\phi(x)=x^{-1/2}\psi(\log x).
\]
Then the left-hand side of the explicit formula receives a contribution
\[
\widehat\phi\Bigl(\frac{\rho_0-\frac12}{i}\Bigr)+\widehat\phi\Bigl(\frac{1-\rho_0-\frac12}{i}\Bigr)
=2\operatorname{Re}\bigl(e^{i\theta}c\,|\widehat\psi(t_0)|\bigr)
\]
whose real part can be made strictly larger than any fixed multiple of the archimedean and prime-power remainder (by scaling the height of \(\psi\) while keeping support width fixed). The prime-power terms remain \(O(\|\phi\|_1+\|\phi'\|_1)\) by the prime-number theorem with classical zero-free region, while the archimedean terms are controlled by the Stirling expansion of \(\Gamma'/\Gamma\). Consequently the right-hand side cannot match the left-hand side, a contradiction.

The argument therefore shows that any zero off the critical line must lie outside every region in which a sufficiently concentrated non-negative test function can be constructed whose Fourier transform overwhelms the explicit-formula error. In particular it recovers the classical zero-free region \(\sigma\ge1-c/\log(|t|+2)\) (by taking wider and wider Gaussians) but yields no improvement: the obstruction saturates precisely at the edge of the known zero-free region because the prime-power contribution cannot be made smaller without enlarging the support of \(\widehat\psi\), which then collides with possible zeros closer to the line.

Thus the method reduces RH to the existence of a family of test functions for which the prime-power side remains dominated even when the Fourier support shrinks slower than any classical zero-free width—an open analytic-number-theoretic question equivalent in strength to a quasi-Riemann-hypothesis of the form \(\sigma\le\frac12+O(1/\log\log|t|)\). No such family is presently known, and the reduction therefore stops short of a proof.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Positivity via explicit formula only recovers classical zero-free regions; no new test functions found.
