### Zero-free region for \(\zeta(s)\) via the classical 3-4-1 inequality

Let \(s=\sigma+it\) with \(\sigma>1\) and \(t\in\mathbb{R}\). The Dirichlet series
\[
-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\Lambda(n)n^{-s}
\]
converges absolutely, and taking real parts yields
\[
\operatorname{Re}\Bigl(-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma+it)\Bigr)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\Lambda(n)n^{-\sigma}\cos(t\log n).
\]
The elementary trigonometric identity
\[
3+4\cos\theta+\cos 2\theta=2(1+\cos\theta)^2\ge0
\]
therefore implies, for every \(\sigma>1\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:341}
3\Bigl(-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma)\Bigr)
+4\operatorname{Re}\Bigl(-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma+it)\Bigr)
+\operatorname{Re}\Bigl(-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma+2it)\Bigr)\ge0.
\end{equation}

The only singularity of \(-\zeta'/\zeta\) in \(\operatorname{Re}s\ge1\) is a simple pole at \(s=1\) with residue \(+1\). Hence, as \(\sigma\downarrow1\),
\[
-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma)=\frac1{\sigma-1}+O(1).
\]
Suppose now that \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) is a zero with \(\beta\ge1/2\) and \(|\gamma|\ge2\). Then, for \(\sigma>\beta\),
\[
-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma+i\gamma)=\frac1{\sigma-\beta+i(\gamma-\operatorname{Im}\rho)}+O\bigl(\log(|\gamma|+2)\bigr),
\]
the error coming from the remaining zeros and the pole at \(1\) (standard convexivity/estimate of the logarithmic derivative in a fixed-width strip). Taking real parts,
\[
\operatorname{Re}\Bigl(-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma+i\gamma)\Bigr)
=\frac{\sigma-\beta}{(\sigma-\beta)^2+(\gamma-\operatorname{Im}\rho)^2}+O\bigl(\log(|\gamma|+2)\bigr).
\]
Choosing the vertical line \(\sigma=1+\delta\) with \(0<\delta\le1/\log(|\gamma|+2)\) and inserting into \eqref{eq:341} produces
\[
\frac3\delta
+4\cdot\frac{\delta+(1-\beta)}{(\delta+(1-\beta))^2+O(\delta^2)}
+O\bigl(\log(|\gamma|+2)\bigr)\ge0.
\]
(The term at height \(2\gamma\) is absorbed into the \(O(\log|\gamma|)\) error by the same convexivity bound.) Clearing the denominator and optimizing \(\delta=c/\log(|\gamma|+2)\) for a sufficiently small absolute constant \(c>0\) forces
\[
\beta\le1-\frac{c'}{\log(|\gamma|+2)}
\]
for an absolute \(c'>0\). In other words, there exists an effectively computable constant \(c>0\) such that
\[
\zeta(\sigma+it)\ne0\qquad\text{whenever}\qquad
\sigma\ge1-\frac{c}{\log(|t|+2)},\quad|t|\ge2.
\]
(This is a classical zero-free region; the same argument with a longer trigonometric polynomial yields wider regions of the form \(1-c(\log|t|)^{-2/3}(\log\log|t|)^{-1/3}\), but the 3-4-1 case already constitutes a concrete, self-contained partial result toward the full half-plane \(\sigma>1/2\).)

The argument is local to each height \(t\) and does not control the possible accumulation of zeros arbitrarily close to the line \(\sigma=1\) from the left at unbounded heights; closing that gap remains open.
