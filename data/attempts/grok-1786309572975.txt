The Riemann \(\xi\)-function satisfies the functional equation \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\) and admits the Hadamard factorization
\[
\xi(s)=\xi(0)\prod_{\rho}\Bigl(1-\frac{s}{\rho}\Bigr),
\]
the product running over the non-trivial zeros (counted with multiplicity). Taking the logarithmic derivative yields the identity
\[
\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)=\sum_{\rho}\frac{1}{s-\rho}.
\]
Suppose there exists a zero \(\rho_0=\sigma_0+it_0\) with \(\sigma_0>1/2\). The functional equation forces \(\overline{\rho_0}\) and \(1-\rho_0\) (and its conjugate) to be zeros as well. Restricting to the critical line and examining the real part of the sum for \(s=1/2+it\) with \(t\) near \(t_0\) produces a principal-value contribution
\[
\operatorname{Re}\frac{\xi'}{\xi}\Bigl(\tfrac12+it\Bigr)=\sum_{\rho}\frac{\sigma_\rho-1/2}{(t-t_\rho)^2+(\sigma_\rho-1/2)^2}.
\]
Each term with \(\sigma_\rho>1/2\) is strictly positive. If one could establish a uniform negative upper bound for the left-hand side in a neighbourhood of \(t_0\) (via the known asymptotic for \(\Gamma'/\Gamma\) and the Dirichlet series for \(\zeta'/\zeta\) off the zeros), a contradiction would follow.  

The obstruction is quantitative: the contribution of the distant zeros (those with \(|t_\rho-t|\gg|\sigma_\rho-1/2|\)) is controlled only by the classical zero-density estimate \(N(\sigma,T)\ll T^{c(1-\sigma)}\log T\), which is too weak to absorb the local positive spike generated by \(\rho_0\) itself when \(\sigma_0-1/2\) is smaller than any fixed power of \(1/\log|t_0|\). Refining the density estimate far enough to close the gap re-encounters the same circle of ideas that have historically stalled (large-value estimates for Dirichlet polynomials, mean-value theorems for \(\zeta\), etc.). Thus the positivity argument yields no new zero-free region.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Positivity of \(\xi'/\xi\) on critical line blocked by insufficient zero-density bounds
