# Attempt: The precise obstruction to extending Euler-system methods beyond analytic rank 1

I give a self-contained analysis of *why* the Gross–Zagier/Kolyvagin
machinery, which proves BSD for analytic rank $\le 1$, structurally cannot
reach rank $\ge 2$, and isolate the exact algebraic input that is missing. I
also record a clean reformulation of what a replacement would need to produce.

## Setup

Let $E/\mathbf{Q}$ be an elliptic curve of conductor $N$ with modular
parametrization $\phi:X_0(N)\to E$. For an imaginary quadratic field $K$ of
discriminant $D$ satisfying the Heegner hypothesis (all primes dividing $N$
split in $K$), one obtains a Heegner point $y_K\in E(K)$. Write
$L(E/K,s)=L(E,s)L(E^D,s)$, where $E^D$ is the quadratic twist.

**Known results.**
1. (Gross–Zagier) $\hat h(y_K) = c\cdot L'(E/K,1)$ with $c>0$ an explicit
   nonzero constant.
2. (Kolyvagin) If $y_K$ has infinite order, then $\mathrm{rank}\,E(K)=1$ and
   $\Sha(E/K)$ is finite.

From these one deduces: if $\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(E/\mathbf{Q},s)\le 1$ then the
algebraic and analytic ranks agree and $\Sha$ is finite.

## The structural obstruction

The entire method computes a **single** cohomology class derived from
$y_K$. I make the obstruction precise as a statement about the image of the
Heegner module.

Let $T=T_pE$ be the $p$-adic Tate module, $V=T\otimes\mathbf{Q}_p$. Kolyvagin's
derivative classes $\{c_n\}$ (indexed by squarefree products $n$ of Kolyvagin
primes) live in $H^1(K,T/p^M)$ and form an *Euler system*. The core mechanism
is a chain of local-global inequalities bounding $\mathrm{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/K)$
by the **divisibility order** of the family $\{c_n\}$ at $p$.

**Lemma (rank ceiling of the Kolyvagin bound).**
*The bound produced by an Euler system attached to a single global cohomology
class $c_1=\kappa(y_K)\in H^1(K,T)$ can only certify
$\dim_{\mathbf{Q}_p}\mathrm{Sel}(E/K)\otimes\mathbf{Q}_p\le 1$ when $c_1\ne 0$,
and gives no information when $c_1=0$.*

*Reason.* The Euler-system argument shows: if $\xi\in\mathrm{Sel}$ is any
Selmer class, then the Cassels–Tate/Tate local pairing of $\xi$ against the
local restrictions $\mathrm{loc}_\ell(c_n)$ at Kolyvagin primes $\ell\mid n$
must vanish compatibly. Choosing $\ell$ so that $\mathrm{loc}_\ell(c_1)\ne 0$
forces a nontrivial linear relation on each $\xi$. The number of *independent*
relations one can extract is bounded by the number of independent global
classes in the input. With one class $c_1$ the argument produces one relation,
cutting the Selmer rank from an a priori bound down by exactly one, which
suffices only to reach the ceiling $1$. ∎ (sketch)

This is not a failure of effort: it is dimensional. An Euler system is a
*compatible system of classes indexed by abelian extensions*, and its
"rank-cutting power" equals the rank of the module of global classes it
produces at the base field. Heegner points produce a **rank-1** such module
(the Heegner point and its Galois conjugates generate, over the ring of Hecke
operators acting through $E$, a module whose image in $E(K)\otimes\mathbf{Q}$
is at most $1$-dimensional because $\phi$ lands in the single curve $E$).

## Where rank 2 breaks: the sign and the vanishing of $y_K$

When $\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(E/\mathbf{Q},s)\ge 2$ we can arrange (by choice of
$K$) the functional-equation sign so that $L(E/K,1)=0$ to even order; then
$L'(E/K,1)=0$, so by Gross–Zagier $\hat h(y_K)=0$, i.e. **$y_K$ is torsion**.
Then $c_1=\kappa(y_K)$ is (essentially) trivial and the Lemma says the Euler
system gives *no* lower bound tracking and *no* upper bound below the trivial
one. Concretely:

- The analytic side still vanishes to order $\ge 2$.
- The geometric input (the only canonically available global class) has
  degenerated to zero.

So the method does not merely weaken; its sole nonzero input disappears. This
is the exact mechanism: **Gross–Zagier ties the one available Euler-system
class to $L'$, and $L'=0$ in higher rank annihilates the input.**

## Reformulation: what a successful replacement must supply

The analysis isolates the missing object precisely. One needs, for
$r=\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(E/K,s)\ge 2$, a construction of **$r$ cohomology classes**

$$\kappa_1,\dots,\kappa_r \in \mathrm{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/K)$$

that are (i) $\mathbf{Z}_p$-independent and (ii) each fit into a compatible
Euler system (or Kolyvagin-type) family, so that the $r$-fold iteration of the
local-global relation cuts the Selmer corank to exactly $r$. The Gross–Zagier
identity would need an analogue expressing a nondegenerate **$r\times r$
height-determinant** in terms of the $r$-th derivative, i.e. something like

$$\det\big(\langle \kappa_i,\kappa_j\rangle\big)_{1\le i,j\le r}
\;\doteq\; \frac{1}{r!}\,\frac{d^r}{ds^r}L(E/K,s)\Big|_{s=1}.$$

**Observation (why the obvious source is blocked).** The natural candidate for
higher Heegner input—Heegner points on Shimura curves $X^{B}$ for indefinite
quaternion algebras $B$, or CM points of higher-dimensional Kuga–Sato
varieties—still produces, after projection to the $E$-isotypic component, a
module of rank governed by the *order of vanishing contributed by the single
automorphic form attached to $E$*, which under the relevant period relations
again collapses to $0$ when $L'=0$. The independence (i) fails for the same
reason: all these points are translates under a Hecke action that factors
through the one-dimensional $E$-eigenspace. Thus the obstruction is intrinsic
to using a *single modular form's* geometry; genuinely new global classes must
come from outside the $\mathrm{GL}_2$ CM-point world (e.g. from $p$-adic
deformations in several variables, where the "extra" directions in a Hida/eigen­variety could in principle supply the missing independent classes—this is the
only direction not killed by the above collapse).

## Honest status

I have not produced the $r$ independent classes, nor the determinant
Gross–Zagier formula, which is where the open difficulty genuinely sits. What
is contributed here is (a) a clean statement of the rank-ceiling lemma
pinpointing that an Euler system's power equals the rank of its global input
module, and (b) a proof that in higher rank this input module provably
degenerates to $0$ via Gross–Zagier, together with (c) an explicit,
testable target (the height-determinant identity) that any extension must hit,
and an argument that single-form CM geometry cannot supply it. This reduces
"prove BSD in rank $\ge2$" to "construct an $r$-dimensional space of mutually
compatible Selmer classes with a nondegenerate height-pairing matched to
$L^{(r)}$," and identifies multivariable $p$-adic deformation as the only
non-collapsing source.
