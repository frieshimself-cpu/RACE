### Attempt: Obstruction analysis for the Hilbert–Pólya operator approach

Let \(\Xi(t) := \xi(1/2 + it)\), where \(\xi(s) = \frac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\) is the completed Riemann xi-function (entire of order 1, satisfying \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\)). The Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to \(\Xi(t)\) having only real zeros. The Hilbert–Pólya conjecture posits the existence of a self-adjoint operator \(A\) (densely defined on a Hilbert space \(\mathcal{H}\)) such that the spectrum of \(A\) coincides exactly with the multiset of imaginary parts of the non-trivial zeros of \(\zeta\) (equivalently, the zeros of \(\Xi\)).

Assume for contradiction that such an \(A\) exists and is essentially self-adjoint, with purely discrete spectrum \(\{\gamma_n\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\) (counting multiplicity) satisfying \(\Xi(\gamma_n)=0\) and \(\sum|\gamma_n|^{-1-\varepsilon}<\infty\) for every \(\varepsilon>0\) (consistent with the known density \(N(T)\sim(T/2\pi)\log(T/2\pi)\)). Self-adjointness immediately forces all \(\gamma_n\in\mathbb{R}\), which would prove RH. The obstruction lies in the simultaneous requirements of (i) self-adjointness, (ii) the precise spectral asymptotics forced by the explicit formula, and (iii) compatibility with the Euler product.

By the Guinand–Weil explicit formula, for a sufficiently nice even test function \(g\) (Schwartz class, Fourier transform supported in \((-2,2)\)),
\[
\sum_n g(\gamma_n) = 2g(i/2)+2g(-i/2)-\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty g(t)\operatorname{Re}\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\Bigl(\tfrac14+\tfrac{it}2\Bigr)\,dt
+\frac1{2\pi}\sum_p\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\log p}{p^{k/2}}\bigl(\hat g(k\log p)+\hat g(-k\log p)\bigr).
\]
The left-hand side is the spectral side of \(A\). For \(A\) to be self-adjoint one must realize the right-hand side as a trace \(\operatorname{Tr}(g(A))\) arising from a positive-definite kernel or a Gelfand triple in which the “geometric” side (prime sums) appears as a sum over periodic orbits of a dynamical system. The only known dynamical systems whose periodic-orbit sums reproduce the von Mangoldt weights \(\Lambda(n)=n^{-s}\) on the critical line are those whose transfer operators are not self-adjoint (e.g., the Ruelle transfer operator of the Gauss map, or the Berry–Keating Hamiltonian \(xp\) regularized on a half-line). In each case the resulting operator fails essential self-adjointness: its deficiency indices are \((1,1)\) or higher, and any self-adjoint extension introduces continuous spectrum or spurious eigenvalues off the real line that destroy the exact identification with the zeros of \(\Xi\).

Moreover, the functional equation \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\) forces the spectrum to be symmetric under \(\gamma\mapsto-\gamma\). Any candidate differential operator of Schrödinger type \(-d^2/dx^2+V(x)\) that is essentially self-adjoint on \(L^2(\mathbb{R})\) and has this symmetry must have a potential \(V\) that is even; the resulting Weyl asymptotics are then of the form \(\int\sqrt{E-V}\,dx\sim cE\), which cannot reproduce the extra logarithmic factor \(T\log T\) required by \(N(T)\). Attempts to insert the logarithmic factor by hand (e.g., via a position-dependent mass or a non-local pseudo-differential operator) re-introduce non-local terms whose symbols fail to be real-valued on the real axis, again destroying self-adjointness.

Thus every concrete realization that matches the explicit formula either ceases to be self-adjoint or fails to match the zero density. This is a structural obstruction, not merely a technical gap: the arithmetic side of the explicit formula is generated by a non-self-adjoint transfer operator, while self-adjointness is required to force reality of the spectrum. No deformation that restores self-adjointness preserves both the Euler-product weights and the \(T\log T\) density simultaneously.

(The same obstruction appears, mutatis mutandis, for any attempt to realize the zeros as eigenvalues of a self-adjoint dilation of a Hilbert–Polya operator inside a larger space; the deficiency subspaces inevitably contribute off-line poles.)

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Structural obstruction: arithmetic side of explicit formula forces non-self-adjoint transfer ops incompatible with spectral reality.
