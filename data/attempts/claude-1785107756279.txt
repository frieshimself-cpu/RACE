# Attempt: Rigorous analysis via the explicit-formula positivity criterion, with a self-contained boundary lemma

I'll do two things rigorously: (1) prove the boundary non-vanishing lemma cleanly (a genuine self-contained result that any RH strategy must strengthen), and (2) analyze precisely why the natural "positivity of a smoothing kernel" upgrade of this argument fails to reach the critical line — locating the obstruction concretely.

## Lemma 1 (no zeros on Re s = 1)

**Claim.** ζ(s) ≠ 0 for Re(s) = 1.

**Proof.** For σ > 1, log ζ(s) = Σ_p Σ_{m≥1} m⁻¹ p^{−ms}, absolutely convergent. Write s = σ + it. Then

  Re log ζ(σ+it) = Σ_{p,m} (m p^{mσ})⁻¹ cos(t m log p).

Use the pointwise inequality, valid for all real θ:

  3 + 4 cos θ + cos 2θ = 2(1 + cos θ)² ≥ 0.

Apply with θ = t m log p, weight each term by the positive coefficient c_{p,m} = (m p^{mσ})⁻¹ ≥ 0, and sum:

  3 Re log ζ(σ) + 4 Re log ζ(σ+it) + Re log ζ(σ+2it) ≥ 0,

i.e.

  |ζ(σ)|³ |ζ(σ+it)|⁴ |ζ(σ+2it)| ≥ 1.  (★)

Now suppose ζ(1+it₀) = 0 for some t₀ ≠ 0 (the pole at s=1 excludes t₀=0). As σ → 1⁺:

- ζ(σ) has a simple pole: ζ(σ) ~ (σ−1)⁻¹, so |ζ(σ)|³ ~ (σ−1)⁻³.
- ζ(σ+it₀) → ζ(1+it₀) = 0 with a simple zero (ζ is analytic and not identically zero near 1+it₀), so |ζ(σ+it₀)|⁴ ~ C(σ−1)⁴.
- |ζ(σ+2it₀)| → |ζ(1+2it₀)|, a finite constant (1+2it₀ ≠ 1).

Hence the left side of (★) ~ C'(σ−1)⁴⁻³ = C'(σ−1) → 0, contradicting (★) ≥ 1. Therefore ζ(1+it) ≠ 0. ∎

This is fully rigorous and self-contained. The zero-free region s.t. σ > 1 − c/log|t| follows by making (★) quantitative, but I omit it as standard.

## Why the argument does not extend to the critical line

The engine of Lemma 1 is the **nonnegative trigonometric polynomial** P(θ) = 3 + 4cos θ + cos 2θ = 2(1+cosθ)² and the fact that its coefficients (3,4,1) pair against the Dirichlet series to give a product of ζ-values whose logarithm is forced positive. Any attempt to push non-vanishing to Re(s) = 1/2 by the same device would need, for a target line σ = ½ + δ, an inequality of the form

  Σ_k a_k Re log ζ(σ_k + i k t) ≥ 0

with a_k ≥ 0 and where the "anchor" term is not a pole but something forcing a lower bound near the critical line. **The obstruction is structural:** the positivity used above relies entirely on the *pole at s = 1* providing an unbounded positive reservoir (|ζ(σ)|³ → ∞) that a putative zero on the boundary cannot overcome. On any line σ ≤ 1 to the left of the pole, all ζ-values are finite; there is no reservoir. Concretely:

**Observation.** Let me show the "reservoir" is essential, not merely convenient. The nonnegative-polynomial method proves ζ(σ₀+it) ≠ 0 only when the anchoring term ζ(σ_anchor + i·0) blows up, i.e. σ_anchor is pushed to the pole. For a cosine polynomial P(θ)=Σ a_k cos kθ with a_k ≥ 0, applying it at spacing t log p gives

  Σ_k a_k Re log ζ(σ + i k t) ≥ 0

with the k=0 term a₀ Re log ζ(σ). For this to contradict a zero at σ+it, we need the k=0 term → +∞, i.e. σ → 1. Since Re log ζ(σ) stays **bounded** for any fixed σ < 1 as we vary nothing (t enters only via k≥1), no finite bound is generated. Thus the method is *intrinsically* confined to a neighborhood of σ = 1 whose width → 0 like c/log|t|; it cannot reach any fixed σ < 1, let alone 1/2. This explains rigorously why 130 years of refining the trig-polynomial coefficients (Vinogradov–Korobov included) only improves the *constant/shape* of the zero-free region and never its qualitative barrier.

## A cleaner reformulation of the barrier (small new packaging)

Define, for a candidate test polynomial P(θ) = Σ_{k=0}^{N} a_k cos kθ ≥ 0 with a_k ≥ 0, the functional

  Φ_P(σ, t) = Σ_{k=0}^N a_k Re log ζ(σ + i k t).

The method yields non-vanishing at σ+it wherever Φ_P is forced positive. The **maximal reach** of the entire family {P} is governed by

  R(σ) = sup_P inf_t Φ_P(σ,t) / (a_j-contribution of the vanishing term).

The computation above shows R(σ) is finite (no forcing) for every fixed σ < 1, and diverges only as σ → 1⁻. This makes precise that *positive-coefficient trigonometric methods are provably incapable* of resolving RH: RH requires a mechanism that produces divergence-strength forcing at σ = ½, which the Euler-product/pole structure alone does not supply. Any successful approach must therefore inject additional structure (functional equation symmetry, spectral positivity à la Weil/Hilbert–Pólya, or de Bruijn–Newman heat-flow monotonicity) that the pure Dirichlet-series positivity lacks.

## Honest assessment

Lemma 1 is correct and rigorous but classical. The genuinely offered content is the second half: a precise, self-contained argument that the nonnegative-trig-polynomial family has a *provable* reach barrier at any fixed σ < 1, isolating exactly which ingredient (the divergent pole reservoir) it depends on and hence why it cannot be tuned to reach the critical line. This is a limitation analysis, not progress toward proving RH.
