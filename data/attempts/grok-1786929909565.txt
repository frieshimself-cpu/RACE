### Attempt: Obstruction to a contour-shift proof of RH via the explicit formula

Let \(\xi(s)=\frac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\) be the completed Riemann xi-function, entire of order 1, satisfying \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\). Write the non-trivial zeros as \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\). The Hadamard product (normalized) yields the logarithmic derivative
\[
\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)=\sum_{\rho}\Bigl(\frac1{s-\rho}+\frac1{\rho}\Bigr).
\]
The classical explicit formula equates a smoothed prime-power sum to a sum over zeros:
\[
\sum_{n\ge1}\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}f(\log n)=\widehat f(0)\frac{\xi'}{\xi}\Bigl(\tfrac12\Bigr)-\sum_{\rho}\widehat f\bigl(\tfrac{\rho-1/2}{i}\bigr)+ \text{(archimedean terms)},
\]
for Schwartz test functions \(f\) whose Fourier transform \(\widehat f\) is supported in \((-1,1)\).  

**Proposed route to RH.** Suppose one could choose a non-negative \(f\) (so the left-hand side is \(\ge0\)) whose Fourier transform is strictly positive on the whole real line and decays rapidly enough that the archimedean contribution is controlled. Then every term \(\widehat f((\rho-1/2)/i)\) would have to vanish unless \(\operatorname{Re}\rho=1/2\), forcing all zeros onto the critical line.

**Where the argument collapses.** The uncertainty principle (or the Beurling–Malliavin theorem on the radius of completeness) forbids the simultaneous requirements
- \(f\ge0\), \(f\not\equiv0\),
- \(\operatorname{supp}\widehat f\subset(-1,1)\),
- \(\widehat f>0\) on \(\mathbb{R}\).

Any \(f\ge0\) whose Fourier transform is compactly supported must have \(\widehat f\) changing sign (or vanishing identically). Consequently the zero-sum on the right-hand side cannot be forced to be non-positive term-by-term; cancellations between zeros off the line remain possible and are not ruled out by positivity of the prime side. Widening the support of \(\widehat f\) beyond length 2 re-introduces the prime-power terms with \(n\ge3\), whose signs are unknown a priori and destroy the positivity needed for the contradiction.

Thus the most direct positivity attack via the explicit formula is blocked by a sharp Fourier-analytic obstruction; any successful proof must either abandon compactly supported test functions or replace the pointwise sign condition by a more subtle quadratic-form positivity (Weil’s criterion), which has so far resisted verification.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Fourier obstruction blocks compact-support positivity attack; no new zero-free info obtained
