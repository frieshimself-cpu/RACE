### Attempt: Obstruction analysis for a spectral (Hilbert–Pólya) route

Assume there exists a densely defined self-adjoint operator \(H\) on a separable Hilbert space \(\mathcal{H}\) such that the non-trivial zeros \(\rho\) of \(\zeta(s)\) satisfy
\[
\rho=\frac12+i\lambda,\qquad\lambda\in\operatorname{Spec}(H).
\]
(The continuous spectrum is empty or accounted for by the trivial zeros/pole via a separate factor.) Self-adjointness immediately forces \(\operatorname{Spec}(H)\subset\mathbb{R}\), hence all non-trivial zeros lie on the critical line, which is RH.

The arithmetic input that must be encoded is the Euler product (or equivalently the von Mangoldt explicit formula). Any candidate \(H\) is therefore required to satisfy an identity of the schematic form
\[
\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(f(H)\bigr)=\sum_p\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\log p}{p^{k/2}}\,\hat f(k\log p)+ \text{(archimedean/Gamma terms)}
\]
for a sufficiently rich class of test functions \(f\), where the left-hand side is a spectral side and the right-hand side is the prime side of the explicit formula. (Precisely, this is the content of Weil’s explicit formula once the zeros are replaced by eigenvalues.)

**Obstruction.**  
No locally compact group, nilpotent Lie algebra, or natural geometric quantization of the primes is known that produces an operator whose spectral measure reproduces the above distribution exactly. The formal Berry–Keating operator \(H=xp\) on \(L^2(\mathbb{R}_+)\) yields, after a suitable regularization, a semiclassical density of states identical to the Riemann–von Mangoldt formula, yet:
- it is not essentially self-adjoint on any natural domain that also incorporates the functional equation;
- its rigorously defined self-adjoint extensions possess continuous spectrum or eigenvalues unrelated to the zeros of \(\zeta\);
- the Euler product does not arise from any trace formula associated with the geodesic flow of \(xp\).

Pair-correlation results (Montgomery) and the GUE statistics of consecutive spacings are compatible with a chaotic quantum Hamiltonian, but compatibility is only asymptotic and statistical; it supplies no matrix elements or commutation relations that would force the eigenvalues to be exactly the imaginary parts of the zeros. Consequently the existence of \(H\) is equivalent in logical strength to RH itself: the spectral reformulation replaces one unproved assertion about an analytic function by an equally unproved assertion about the existence of an operator with prescribed arithmetic trace formula. No reduction in the difficulty of the problem is achieved, and no new zero-free region or density estimate follows from the formal analogy alone.

(The same obstruction appears, mutatis mutandis, for the Connes operator-theoretic approach via the adèle class space: the required trace formula can be written formally, yet the absence of a spectral gap or a rigorous cutoff that isolates the critical line prevents a deduction of RH.)

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Hilbert–Pólya spectral reformulation yields no constructive operator and no logical simplification of RH.
