### Attempt: Obstruction analysis for positivity-based zero-free methods

Let \(\xi(s)=\frac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\). This is entire of order 1, real on the real line, and satisfies the functional equation \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\). Non-trivial zeros of \(\zeta\) are precisely the zeros of \(\xi\), and RH asserts that every such zero satisfies \(\operatorname{Re}\rho=\frac12\).

A classical family of approaches to zero-free regions begins from the Dirichlet series
\[
-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\Lambda(n)n^{-s},\qquad\operatorname{Re}s>1,
\]
whose coefficients are non-negative. For real \(\sigma>1\) the sum is therefore positive. More generally, if \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) were a zero with \(\beta\) close to 1, the harmonic sum of the three terms at \(s=\sigma\), \(s=\sigma+i\gamma\) and \(s=\sigma+2i\gamma\) would produce a linear combination
\[
3\Bigl(-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma)\Bigr)+4\operatorname{Re}\Bigl(-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma+i\gamma)\Bigr)+\operatorname{Re}\Bigl(-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma+2i\gamma)\Bigr)
\]
that can be bounded from below by a positive multiple of \(\sum\Lambda(n)n^{-\sigma}(1-\cos(\gamma\log n))^2\ge0\). The trigonometric weight \(3+4\cos\theta+\cos2\theta\ge0\) forces a contradiction once \(\beta>1-c/\log(|\gamma|+2)\) for an absolute \(c>0\). (The same argument with a longer trigonometric polynomial yields wider but still slowly shrinking regions.)

The method extends, via the functional equation, to a zero-free region symmetric about the critical line, but the width remains of size \(O(1/\log|t|)\). The obstruction is structural:

- Positivity of \(\Lambda(n)\) is available only inside the half-plane of absolute convergence \(\operatorname{Re}s>1\). Analytic continuation of \(-\zeta'/\zeta\) into the critical strip replaces the Dirichlet series by an explicit formula whose archimedean and oscillatory prime-power contributions no longer share a common sign.
- Any attempt to push the contour leftward past \(\operatorname{Re}s=1\) introduces horizontal integrals and residues at the zeros themselves; the resulting error terms grow like \(\lvert t\rvert^{c(1-\sigma)}\) and overwhelm the main-term positivity precisely when \(\sigma\le1-c/\log|t|\).
- Substituting a smoothed weight (as in Weil’s explicit formula) restores a quadratic form in the zeros, but the prime-power side is then an integral transform of a test function against \(d\psi(x)\). Without an a-priori bound on the zeros, this transform cannot be shown positive for every admissible test function; the required positivity is essentially equivalent to RH (Weil’s criterion). Thus the circle closes: the only known source of positivity cannot cross the barrier that RH itself would remove.

Consequently every positivity argument that begins from the Euler product or from \(\Lambda(n)\ge0\) is confined to a zero-free region of width \(o(1)\) and cannot reach the line \(\operatorname{Re}s=\frac12\). New input—either a different positivity certificate valid throughout the strip, or a fundamentally non-positivity technique—is required.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Only obstruction analysis of classical positivity methods; no new zero-free region or reduction obtained.
