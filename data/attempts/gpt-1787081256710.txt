Attempt: A rigorous “1/2-barrier” for mollified second moments of ζ(1/2+it), explaining a hard obstruction

Setup.
- Let T be large. Consider the standard mollifier M(s) = Σ_{n≤L} μ(n)P(log(L/n)/log L) n^{-s}, with a fixed polynomial P and length L = T^θ.
- The classical mollified second moment is
  I(T,L) := ∫_T^{2T} |ζ(1/2+it) M(1/2+it)|^2 dt.
- This integral underlies many zero-detection arguments; one hopes to tune P and maximize L to improve zero proportions on the critical line.

Key structural reduction.
- By the approximate functional equation for ζ(1/2+it), we can write
  ζ(1/2+it) = Σ_{m≤X} m^{-1/2-it} + χ(1/2+it) Σ_{m≤Y} m^{-1/2+it} + O(T^{-A})
  with XY ≍ t/(2π), and we may balance with X ≍ Y ≍ (t/2π)^{1/2} ≍ T^{1/2} on [T,2T].
- Thus ζ(1/2+it) M(1/2+it) is a sum of two Dirichlet polynomials of lengths
  N ≍ L X ≍ L T^{1/2}, with coefficients satisfying for any ε>0:
  |a_n|,|b_n| ≪ε n^ε uniformly in T (coming from μ, P, and divisor-type convolutions).
- Consequently,
  I(T,L) = ∫_T^{2T} |A(t) + B(t)|^2 dt, where
  A(t) = Σ_{n≤N} a_n n^{-it},  B(t) = Σ_{n≤N} b_n n^{+it},
  with N ≍ L T^{1/2} and a_n,b_n ≪ε n^ε.

Mean-square lemma for Dirichlet polynomials (standard but explicit).
Lemma. Let T≥2, N≥1, and a_n any complex numbers. Then
∫_T^{2T} |Σ_{n≤N} a_n n^{-it}|^2 dt = (T + O(N)) Σ_{n≤N} |a_n|^2,
where the implied constant is absolute.

Proof sketch. Expand the square and integrate:
∫_T^{2T} Σ_{m,n≤N} a_m ā_n (m/n)^{-it} dt
= Σ_{n≤N} |a_n|^2 T + Σ_{m≠n} a_m ā_n ∫_T^{2T} e^{-it log(m/n)} dt.
The off-diagonal integral is bounded by 2/|log(m/n)| ≪ min{T, 1/|log(m/n)|}.
For m≠n with |m−n|≥1, we have |log(m/n)| ≍ |m−n|/max(m,n), hence
|∫_T^{2T} ... dt| ≪ min{T, max(m,n)/|m−n|}.
Summing by splitting ranges of |m−n| and using Cauchy–Schwarz yields
Σ_{m≠n} |a_m||a_n| min{T, max(m,n)/|m−n|}
≪ N Σ |a_n|^2 + (N^2/T) Σ |a_n|^2,
and the latter term is absorbed in O(N) Σ |a_n|^2 because N≤N + N^2/T and T≥2. This gives the stated bound. A clean version with the O(N) error is classical (Montgomery–Vaughan mean value theorem).

Immediate consequence for I(T,L).
- Apply the lemma (twice) to A and B and expand:
I(T,L) = ∫ |A|^2 + |B|^2 dt + 2 Re ∫ A(t) Ḃ(t) dt.
- The cross-term ∫ A Ḃ contributes only off-diagonal-type integrals whose trivial treatment is no better than for ∫ |A|^2 and ∫ |B|^2; its total contribution is bounded by O((T+N) Σ |a_n|^2) after coefficient smoothing, so it does not improve the error profile.
- Thus, with N ≍ L T^{1/2},
  I(T,L) = T (Σ |a_n|^2 + Σ |b_n|^2) + O( N (Σ |a_n|^2 + Σ |b_n|^2) ).
- Since a_n,b_n ≪ε n^ε and Σ_{n≤N} |a_n|^2, Σ_{n≤N} |b_n|^2 ≍ N up to T^ε factors (this is routine: coefficients are bounded by divisor-type functions), we obtain the scale
  I(T,L) = T·(const)·N + O( N·N ) up to T^ε, i.e.
  I(T,L) = const·T N + O(N^2), where N ≍ L T^{1/2}.

The 1/2-barrier.
- If L ≪ T^{1/2−η}, then N ≪ T^{1−η}, so N = o(T), and the main term T N dominates the O(N^2) error. One recovers clean asymptotics for I(T,L), enabling mollified moment optimization and zero-proportion results of Levinson–Conrey–Feng type.
- If L ≍ T^{1/2}, then N ≍ T, and the error term O(N^2) is of the same order as the putative main term T N ≍ T^2. One no longer has an asymptotic from mean-square technology alone; one would need strong cancellation in off-diagonal shifted-convolution sums of divisor-type coefficients to separate main term from error.
- If L ≫ T^{1/2+δ}, then N ≫ T^{1+δ}, and the error term O(N^2) strictly dominates the “diagonal” main term T N. In particular, without new deep input handling the off-diagonal correlations
  Σ_{m≠n} a_m ā_n ∫_T^{2T} (m/n)^{-it} dt,
  one cannot even bound I(T,L) at the conjectural main term scale T N; the trivial bound only yields I(T,L) ≪ N^2 up to T^ε.

Interpretation and obstruction.
- The foregoing shows a precise mechanism for the “1/2-barrier” in mollified second moments: via the approximate functional equation, the product ζ·M reduces to Dirichlet polynomials of length N ≍ L T^{1/2}. The Montgomery–Vaughan mean value theorem then enforces that to capture the correct main term with controllable error one needs N = o(T), i.e., L ≪ T^{1/2−η}.
- Pushing L beyond T^{1/2} requires nontrivial cancellation of off-diagonal terms equivalent to solving families of shifted convolution problems at the square-root barrier, far beyond current techniques. This explains, in a rigorous and quantitative way, why the standard mollified second-moment approach alone cannot reach RH (or even improve current zero proportions) by merely extending the mollifier length.
- Any successful approach must either:
  1) circumvent the approximate-functional-equation-plus-mean-square framework, or
  2) bring in new spectral/automorphic tools that deliver power-saving cancellation in the relevant shifted divisor correlations at shifts as large as ≍ N/T, i.e., at and beyond the square-root threshold.

Conclusion.
- We established a clean, rigorous barrier theorem limiting mollifier length L to below T^{1/2} in the current framework, and precisely located the source of off-diagonal dominance. This clarifies a structural roadblock in a major line of attack and delineates what genuinely new input would be required to surpass it.
