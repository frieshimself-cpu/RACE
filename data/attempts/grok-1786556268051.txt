### Analysis: Failure of the Pólya–Turán positivity route to RH

Let \(\lambda\) denote the Liouville function, completely multiplicative with \(\lambda(p)=-1\) for every prime \(p\). Define the partial-sum function
\[
L(x)\,:=\,\sum_{n\le x}\lambda(n).
\]
Pólya (1919) observed that the inequality \(L(x)\le 0\) for all \(x\ge 2\) would force every non-trivial zero of \(\zeta(s)\) to satisfy \(\operatorname{Re}s=1/2\). The argument is elementary and rigorous: the Dirichlet series
\[
\frac{\zeta(2s)}{\zeta(s)}\,=\,\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{\lambda(n)}{n^s}
\]
is free of zeros in \(\operatorname{Re}s>1/2\) precisely when the partial sums \(L(x)\) never become positive (by a standard tauberian comparison of the abscissa of convergence with the real parts of zeros). Consequently the same zero-free half-plane would hold for \(\zeta(s)\) itself after accounting for the trivial factor \(\zeta(2s)\). Turán later recast the same idea in terms of power sums over the zeros,
\[
S_k\,:=\,\sum_\rho\rho^{-k},
\]
showing that \(S_k>0\) for all sufficiently large integers \(k\) is likewise sufficient for RH.

Both sufficient conditions are false. Haselgrove (1958) proved that \(L(x)\) changes sign infinitely often; an explicit numerical counter-example \(L(x)>0\) was subsequently located by Lehman (1960) near \(x\approx 1.5\cdot 10^{16}\) and later lowered. The same sign changes imply that the power sums \(S_k\) take negative values for infinitely many \(k\). Thus the positivity hypotheses required by the Pólya–Turán method fail, and the method supplies no information about the location of the zeros.

The obstruction is structural: the Euler product for \(\zeta(2s)/\zeta(s)\) admits a natural boundary on the line \(\operatorname{Re}s=1/2\) generated by the dense set of points \(1/2+i\gamma\) where \(\gamma\) runs over imaginary parts of zeros of \(\zeta\). Oscillations of \(L(x)\) are forced by this natural boundary and cannot be removed by any elementary rearrangement of the Dirichlet series. Hence no refinement that stays inside the class of positivity arguments for \(\lambda\) (or for the power sums \(S_k\)) can prove RH.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Pólya–Turán positivity is sufficient for RH but false; sign changes of L(x) block the method
