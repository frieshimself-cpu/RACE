# Attempt: Li's Criterion and the Positivity Obstruction

## Setup

By the Bombieri–Lagarias/Li criterion, RH is equivalent to the nonnegativity of the **Li coefficients**
$$
\lambda_n \;=\; \sum_{\rho}\Big[1-\big(1-\tfrac{1}{\rho}\big)^n\Big]\qquad(n\ge 1),
$$
the sum over nontrivial zeros taken with the symmetric pairing $\rho\leftrightarrow 1-\rho$. Equivalently,
$$
\lambda_n=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\left.\frac{d^n}{ds^n}\Big[s^{\,n-1}\log\xi(s)\Big]\right|_{s=1},
\qquad \xi(s)=\tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s).
$$

I give a fully self-contained, verifiable computation of $\lambda_1$ and then isolate precisely where the argument for general $n$ breaks down.

## Rigorous computation of $\lambda_1$

For $n=1$, $\lambda_1=\dfrac{d}{ds}\log\xi(s)\Big|_{s=1}$. Taking logs of the completed zeta:
$$
\log\xi(s)=\log\tfrac12+\log s+\log(s-1)-\tfrac{s}{2}\log\pi+\log\Gamma(s/2)+\log\zeta(s),
$$
$$
\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)=\frac1s+\frac{1}{s-1}-\tfrac12\log\pi+\tfrac12\psi(s/2)+\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s).
$$

The terms $\frac{1}{s-1}$ and $\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)$ each blow up at $s=1$; I check the cancellation. From $\zeta(s)=\frac{1}{s-1}+\gamma+O(s-1)$ one gets, dividing series,
$$
\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)=-\frac{1}{s-1}+\gamma+O(s-1),
$$
so
$$
\lim_{s\to1}\Big(\frac{1}{s-1}+\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)\Big)=\gamma .
$$

Using $\psi(1/2)=-\gamma-2\log 2$, the remaining terms at $s=1$ give
$$
\lambda_1 = 1+\gamma-\tfrac12\log\pi+\tfrac12\psi(1/2)
        = 1+\tfrac{\gamma}{2}-\tfrac12\log\pi-\log 2 .
$$

**Numerical check.** $\gamma/2=0.2886078$, $\tfrac12\log\pi=0.5723649$, $\log2=0.6931472$:
$$
\lambda_1 = 1+0.2886078-0.5723649-0.6931472 = 0.0230957 > 0,
$$
in exact agreement with the tabulated value $\lambda_1\approx0.023095709$. So the first nontrivial instance of the RH-equivalent positivity holds, and my closed form is independently confirmed.

## Structural decomposition and the obstruction

Writing each zero as $\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma_\rho$ (assuming nothing about $\gamma_\rho$ being real), pair $\rho$ with $1-\rho=\bar\rho$ under RH. In general, put $\rho=\beta+i t$. Then
$$
1-\Big(1-\tfrac1\rho\Big)^n=\sum_{k=1}^n\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k+1}\rho^{-k}.
$$
So $\lambda_n=\sum_{k=1}^n\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k+1}\sigma_k$ with $\sigma_k=\sum_\rho\rho^{-k}$ (symmetrized). The $\sigma_k$ are the classical secondary zeta values, expressible via $\xi'/\xi$ derivatives.

The key positivity mechanism is the identity (valid unconditionally)
$$
\big|1-(1-1/\rho)^n\big|^2\ \text{controls}\ \lambda_n\quad\text{only after pairing},
$$
and specifically **under RH** ($\rho=\tfrac12+it$) one has $\Big|1-\tfrac1\rho\Big|=1$, so each term $1-(1-1/\rho)^n$ has real part
$$
\Re\Big[1-e^{-in\theta_\rho}\Big]=1-\cos(n\theta_\rho)\ge 0,\qquad \tfrac1\rho=1-e^{i\theta_\rho}.
$$
This is exactly why RH $\Rightarrow \lambda_n\ge0$: on the critical line $|1-1/\rho|=1$ forces termwise nonnegative real parts.

**Where an unconditional proof stalls.** The converse (positivity $\Rightarrow$ RH) — and any attempt to prove $\lambda_n\ge0$ directly — requires controlling the *sign* of the arithmetic contribution. Splitting via the explicit formula,
$$
\lambda_n=\underbrace{\lambda_n^{\mathrm{arch}}}_{\sim \frac n2\log n\;(>0)}+\;\lambda_n^{\mathrm{prime}},
$$
the archimedean part grows like $\tfrac{n}{2}(\log n)(1+o(1))$ and is positive, while $\lambda_n^{\mathrm{prime}}$ is an oscillatory sum over prime powers whose size is $O(\sqrt n\,\log n)$ conditionally but is **not** unconditionally bounded below by $-\lambda_n^{\mathrm{arch}}$. The failure is precisely that a single hypothetical off-line zero pair $\beta\pm it$ with $\beta>\tfrac12$ contributes, for large $n$, a term growing like $\big(|1-1/\rho|\big)^{n}$ with $|1-1/\rho|>1$, i.e. **geometrically**, eventually overwhelming the polynomial archimedean growth and forcing some $\lambda_n<0$. This is the real content of the criterion — and it is not something one can establish without already excluding such zeros.

So the computation confirms the mechanism and the exact break point, but converts RH into the still-open problem of an unconditional lower bound for the oscillatory prime sum, which I cannot supply.

## Honest assessment

I rigorously (re)derived the closed form $\lambda_1=1+\gamma/2-\tfrac12\log\pi-\log2>0$ and pinned down the exact analytic obstruction to a general positivity proof (geometric vs. polynomial growth in $n$). This is a verified consistency result and a sharp diagnosis, not new progress toward the full theorem.
