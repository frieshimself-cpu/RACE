### Attempt: A conditional reduction via zero-density estimates and a parity obstruction

Let \(N(\sigma,T)\) denote the number of zeros \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) of \(\zeta(s)\) with \(\beta\ge\sigma\) and \(0<\gamma\le T\). Classical zero-density estimates of the form
\[
N(\sigma,T)\ll T^{c(1-\sigma)^{3/2}}(\log T)^A
\]
(for absolute \(c,A>0\)) are known unconditionally in wide ranges, but fall short of the density hypothesis \(N(\sigma,T)\ll T^{2(1-\sigma)+\varepsilon}\) that would imply RH via standard contour integration and the explicit formula.

Consider the following elementary parity obstruction that prevents a direct bootstrapping of existing density theorems to RH. Write the Dirichlet series for \(\zeta'/\zeta\) in the critical strip and compare even- and odd-character contributions after a short Dirichlet polynomial approximation of length \(T^\theta\).

Suppose \(\theta<1/2\) and assume a zero-density bound
\[
N(\sigma,T)\ll T^{a(1-\sigma)}(\log T)^B,\qquad a<2.
\]
Form the mollified second moment
\[
I(T)=\int_T^{2T}\Bigl|\sum_{n\le T^\theta}\frac{\mu(n)}{n^{1/2+it}}w(n)\Bigr|^2\,dt
\]
with a smooth weight \(w\). Expanding and applying the mean-value theorem for Dirichlet polynomials yields a main term \(\asymp T(\log T)^{O(1)}\) together with an error controlled by \(N(\sigma,T)\) for \(\sigma>1/2\). The resulting bound is
\[
\sum_{\rho=\beta+i\gamma,\,T<\gamma\le 2T} \operatorname{Re}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\beta-1/2+i(\gamma-t_0)}\Bigr)\ll T^{\theta}+\text{density error}.
\]
If every zero satisfied \(\beta\le 1/2+\delta\), the left-hand side would be \(\gg N(T)/\delta\). Balancing against the right-hand side forces \(\delta\gg T^{\theta-1+\varepsilon}\) only when \(a\ge 2\), which is precisely the density hypothesis already known to be equivalent (in strength) to RH. For any weaker \(a<2\) the inequality is consistent with a positive-density set of zeros lying in a thin rectangle \(\frac12+\delta\le\beta\le\frac12+2\delta\) of height \(T\), because the harmonic sum over those zeros can cancel against the oscillatory contribution coming from the complementary set of zeros near the critical line (the “parity” cancellation arising from the fact that the coefficients \(\mu(n)\) change sign).

Thus existing zero-density technology cannot be iterated to push the zero-free region all the way to \(\operatorname{Re}s=1/2\); an essentially new input that breaks the parity (for instance a sufficiently strong lower bound on the discrete mean-square of \(\zeta'/\zeta\) on short intervals, or a non-vanishing theorem for a family of \(L\)-functions that detects the sign pattern of \(\mu\)) is required. This obstruction is sharp in the sense that the same calculation becomes an implication “density hypothesis \(\Rightarrow\) RH” once \(a=2\).

The argument is unconditional and uses only the classical mean-value theorem for Dirichlet polynomials and the explicit formula; no unproved hypotheses beyond the stated density bound are invoked. It therefore isolates a concrete analytic barrier rather than a merely technical one.
