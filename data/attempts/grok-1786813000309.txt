### Attempt: Failure analysis of a contour-shift argument for a zero-density estimate that would imply RH

Let \(\zeta(s)\) denote the Riemann zeta function, \(s=\sigma+it\), and write \(N(\sigma,T)\) for the number of zeros \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) (counted with multiplicity) satisfying \(\beta\ge\sigma\) and \(0<\gamma\le T\). A classical sufficient condition for the Riemann Hypothesis is the zero-density bound
\[
N(\sigma,T)\ll T^{c(1-\sigma)}\log^{O(1)}T
\]
for some absolute \(c<2\) and all \(\sigma\ge1/2\), uniformly in \(T\ge2\). (Indeed, the classical zero-free region already gives \(N(\sigma,T)=0\) for \(\sigma\ge1-c/\log T\); an exponent strictly less than 2 forces all but \(o(T\log T)\) zeros onto the line \(\sigma=1/2\) by the Riemann–von Mangoldt formula, and a standard argument with the explicit formula then upgrades this to RH.)

We attempt to prove such a bound by a contour integral of \(\zeta'/\zeta\). Fix \(\sigma_0\in(1/2,1)\) and large \(T\). Let \(\mathcal{R}\) be the rectangle with vertices \(\sigma_0\pm i\), \(2\pm iT\), indented by small semicircles about any zeros or poles on the boundary. Then
\[
2\pi i\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal{R}}\frac1{\rho-s_0}=\int_{\partial\mathcal{R}}\frac{\zeta'(s)/\zeta(s)}{s-s_0}\,ds
\]
for a test point \(s_0=\sigma_0+iT/2\). The horizontal integrals are \(O((\log T)/T)\) by the standard convex bound \(\zeta'/\zeta(s)\ll\log(|t|+2)\) for \(\sigma\ge\sigma_0\). The right-hand vertical integral (at \(\operatorname{Re}s=2\)) is \(O(1)\) by the absolutely convergent Dirichlet series for \(-\zeta'/\zeta\). Thus any non-trivial contribution must come from the left-hand vertical side \(\operatorname{Re}s=\sigma_0\).

On that side we write the logarithmic derivative via the Dirichlet series over primes and prime powers:
\[
\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)=-\sum_{n\le x}\Lambda(n)n^{-s}+O\bigl(x^{1-\sigma_0}\log(xT)+T x^{-\sigma_0}\log x\bigr),
\]
valid for \(1\le x\le T\), by a truncated Perron formula with error controlled by the convex bound. Substituting and integrating termwise against \(1/(s-s_0)\) produces a main term that is essentially a smoothed sum of \(\Lambda(n)n^{-\sigma_0}\) over an interval of length \(\asymp T\). If one could show that this smoothed sum is \(\ll T^{\theta}\) for some \(\theta<1\), the desired density estimate would follow after a standard dyadic decomposition and application of Halász-type large-value estimates.

The obstruction appears precisely here. After partial summation the smoothed sum is comparable to
\[
\int_{T/2}^{2T}\Bigl|\sum_{n\le x}\Lambda(n)n^{-\sigma_0-it}\Bigr|\frac{dt}{t}.
\]
Mean-value estimates for Dirichlet polynomials give
\[
\int_0^T\Bigl|\sum_{n\le x}\Lambda(n)n^{-\sigma_0-it}\Bigr|^2\,dt\ll(T+x)x^{1-2\sigma_0}(\log x)^O(1).
\]
Cauchy–Schwarz then yields only the upper bound \(T^{1/2}x^{1/2-\sigma_0}(\log x)^{O(1)}\) for the \(L^1\) mean. Optimizing \(x=T\) produces an exponent \(1-\sigma_0\), which is strictly larger than \(1-\sigma_0\) only by logarithmic factors; the resulting density exponent is \(c=2\), exactly the threshold that is known to be true (Ingham) but insufficient to force RH. Improving the \(L^1\) mean requires either a better than square-root cancellation in short Dirichlet polynomials over primes (which is equivalent to a quasi-RH in the zero-density range) or a large-value estimate that beats the Halász–Montgomery bound by a factor \(T^{\varepsilon(\sigma_0)}\). Both improvements are circular: they are known to be equivalent in strength to the very density hypothesis one is trying to prove.

Thus the contour-shift + Dirichlet-polynomial approach closes only at the classical density exponent 2 and cannot be made to yield the stricter exponent needed for RH without importing an assumption already comparable to RH itself. The same circularity appears, with only notational changes, when the argument is recast in terms of the explicit formula or in terms of the mollified second moment of \(\zeta\).

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Contour+Dirichlet-poly method yields only classical density exp. 2; stricter bound circularly requires RH-level cancellation.
