## Attempt: the geometry of the Li coefficients and a sharp reformulation

I work with Li's criterion and isolate the exact geometric content of RH inside it, then
analyze precisely where an unconditional positivity proof breaks.

### Setup

Let $\xi(s)=\tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)$, entire of order $1$, with
$\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)$ and zeros exactly the nontrivial zeros $\rho$ of $\zeta$. The Li
coefficients are
$$
\lambda_n=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\frac{d^n}{ds^n}\Big[s^{n-1}\log\xi(s)\Big]_{s=1},
\qquad n\ge 1 .
$$
Using the Hadamard product $\xi(s)=\xi(0)\prod_\rho(1-s/\rho)$ (zeros paired
$\rho\leftrightarrow 1-\rho$ to ensure convergence) one gets the standard identity
$$
\boxed{\;\lambda_n=\sum_{\rho}\Big[1-\Big(1-\tfrac1\rho\Big)^n\Big]\;}
\tag{1}
$$
the sum taken symmetrically over paired zeros. Li's theorem: **RH $\iff$ $\lambda_n\ge 0$
for all $n\ge1$.**

### The exact geometric observation

Set, for a zero $\rho$,
$$
w_\rho := 1-\frac1\rho=\frac{\rho-1}{\rho}.
$$

**Lemma 1.** $|w_\rho|=1 \iff \operatorname{Re}\rho=\tfrac12$. Moreover $|w_\rho|<1\iff \operatorname{Re}\rho>\tfrac12$ and $|w_\rho|>1\iff\operatorname{Re}\rho<\tfrac12$.

*Proof.* $|w_\rho|=\dfrac{|\rho-1|}{|\rho|}$, and $|\rho-1|=|\rho|$ exactly when $\rho$ is
equidistant from $0$ and $1$, i.e. lies on the perpendicular bisector $\operatorname{Re}\rho=\tfrac12$.
If $\operatorname{Re}\rho>\tfrac12$ then $\rho$ is closer to $1$, so $|\rho-1|<|\rho|$, giving
$|w_\rho|<1$; symmetrically for the other side. $\qquad\blacksquare$

So RH is *exactly* the statement that the map $\rho\mapsto w_\rho$ (a Möbius map sending
the critical line to the unit circle, $0,1$ to $\infty,0$) carries **every** zero onto the
unit circle. Writing a critical zero $\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma$,
$$
w_\rho=\frac{-\tfrac12+i\gamma}{\tfrac12+i\gamma}=e^{i\theta_\gamma},\qquad
\theta_\gamma=\pi-2\arctan(2\gamma).
$$

**Corollary (RH $\Rightarrow$ positivity, clean form).** Under RH, pairing $\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma$
with $\bar\rho=\tfrac12-i\gamma$ (so $w_{\bar\rho}=\overline{w_\rho}=e^{-i\theta_\gamma}$),
$$
\big[1-w_\rho^n\big]+\big[1-w_{\bar\rho}^n\big]
=2\big(1-\cos n\theta_\gamma\big)\ge 0 .
$$
Summing (1) over such pairs gives $\lambda_n\ge0$. The nonnegativity is *termwise* and comes
purely from $|w_\rho|=1$. $\blacksquare$

This makes transparent **why** RH forces positivity: each zero contributes a point on the
unit circle, and $1-\operatorname{Re}(e^{in\theta})\ge 0$ always.

### Where an unconditional proof must do work

For the converse / an unconditional attack one must show $\lambda_n\ge0$ *without* knowing
$|w_\rho|=1$. Group a hypothetical off-line zero quadruple
$\{\rho,\bar\rho,1-\rho,1-\bar\rho\}$ with $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$, $\beta>\tfrac12$. Note the
functional equation forces $1-\rho$ to also be a zero, and
$$
w_{1-\rho}=1-\frac1{1-\rho}=\frac{-\rho}{1-\rho}=\frac{1}{\,w_\rho-1\,}\cdot(\dots)
$$
more usefully: $w_{1-\rho}=\dfrac{-\rho}{1-\rho}$, while $w_\rho=\dfrac{\rho-1}{\rho}$, so
$$
w_\rho\cdot w_{1-\rho}=\frac{(\rho-1)(-\rho)}{\rho(1-\rho)}=1 .
$$

**Lemma 2.** For a functional-equation pair, $w_\rho\,w_{1-\rho}=1$; thus the two moduli are
reciprocal, $|w_{1-\rho}|=1/|w_\rho|$, and the two arguments are equal:
$\arg w_{1-\rho}=\arg w_\rho \pmod{2\pi}$ (since their product is the positive real $1$ only
if arguments cancel — more precisely $\arg w_\rho+\arg w_{1-\rho}\equiv0$).

Let me recompute the argument relation carefully. $w_\rho w_{1-\rho}=1$ forces
$\arg w_\rho+\arg w_{1-\rho}\equiv 0\pmod{2\pi}$ and $|w_\rho|\,|w_{1-\rho}|=1$. Write
$w_\rho=re^{i\phi}$, $r>1$ (since $\beta>\tfrac12$). Then $w_{1-\rho}=r^{-1}e^{-i\phi}$.
Including complex conjugates, the four zeros give the contribution to (1):
$$
S_n:=4-\big(r^ne^{in\phi}+r^ne^{-in\phi}+r^{-n}e^{in\phi}+r^{-n}e^{-in\phi}\big)
=4-2\big(r^n+r^{-n}\big)\cos n\phi .
$$

**This is the crux.** $r^n+r^{-n}\ge 2$ with equality iff $r=1$ (iff $\beta=\tfrac12$). So an
off-line quadruple contributes
$$
S_n=4-2(r^n+r^{-n})\cos n\phi,
$$
which is **negative for those $n$ with $\cos n\phi$ close to $1$** (it can be as small as
$4-2(r^n+r^{-n})<0$ once $r^n>$ roughly $3$). Thus a single off-line quadruple produces
infinitely many $n$ with large *negative* contributions $\sim -2r^n$, growing geometrically.

**Consequence (a genuine, if known, quantitative statement).** If $\zeta$ had a zero with
$\beta>\tfrac12$, i.e. $r=|w_\rho|>1$, then along the subsequence $n_k$ for which
$\{n_k\phi/2\pi\}$ is small (which has positive density by Weyl/Dirichlet, since such $n$
exist with $\cos n\phi\ge\tfrac12$ for a positive proportion of $n$), that quadruple alone
contributes $\le 4-(r^{n_k}+r^{-n_k})\to-\infty$. For $\lambda_{n_k}\ge0$ to survive, the
*on-line* zeros (each contributing the bounded quantity $2(1-\cos n\theta)\in[0,4]$) must
compensate a term growing like $r^{n_k}$.

Here is the precise obstruction, stated as a clean sub-problem equivalent to RH:

> **Reformulation.** RH holds $\iff$ for every $n$,
> $$\sum_{\beta_\rho>1/2}\big(|w_\rho|^n+|w_\rho|^{-n}\big)\cos(n\arg w_\rho)\;\le\;
> \tfrac12\,\#\{\text{on-line pairs}\}\cdot(\text{bounded})\; +\;\dots$$
> i.e. the (geometrically growing) off-line contribution never outweighs the (bounded) on-line
> contribution.

Because the on-line contributions are uniformly bounded per zero while an off-line
contribution grows like $r^n$, **any** off-line zero makes $\lambda_n<0$ for suitable large
$n$ — *provided* its negative excursions are not cancelled by other off-line zeros. That
proviso is the whole difficulty: zeros come in symmetric families and their arguments
$\phi$ could in principle conspire so that for the bad $n$ of one quadruple, another quadruple
with larger modulus contributes a positive $-2(r')^n\cos n\phi'>0$ (when $\cos n\phi'<0$).

So the real analytic content RH hides inside Li positivity is:

**Open sub-lemma (unproven here).** One cannot have a configuration of off-critical zeros
whose geometric-weight phases $\{\phi_\rho\}$ simultaneously cancel the negative excursions
for *all* $n$. Equivalently: the trigonometric sum
$\sum_\rho (r_\rho^n+r_\rho^{-n})\cos n\phi_\rho$ with at least one $r_\rho>1$ cannot stay
bounded above by the on-line total for all $n$.

### Assessment

What I have rigorously established that is genuinely clarifying:
- Lemma 1: RH $\iff$ *every* $w_\rho$ lies on the unit circle (exact Möbius geometry).
- Lemma 2: the functional-equation pairing forces reciprocal moduli $r,\,1/r$ and opposite
  arguments, giving the exact quadruple contribution $S_n=4-2(r^n+r^{-n})\cos n\phi$.
- A clean proof that off-line zeros inject **geometrically growing** oscillatory terms into
  $\lambda_n$, so positivity can only fail-safe through *inter-quadruple phase cancellation*.

What is **not** proved: that such cancellation is impossible. This is exactly the hard core,
equivalent to RH, and I have no unconditional handle on the joint distribution of the phases
$\phi_\rho=\arg w_\rho$ of hypothetical off-line zeros. The growth asymmetry ($r^n$ vs.
bounded) is suggestive but a single pair of off-line quadruples with $r'>r$ and
anti-correlated phases defeats any naive sign argument, so no contradiction follows.

I did not close the gap. The contribution is a sharpened, self-contained geometric
dissection of where Li positivity encodes RH, reducing it to a non-cancellation statement
for an explicit geometric-exponential trigonometric sum.
