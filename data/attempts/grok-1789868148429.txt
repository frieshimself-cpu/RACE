### Attempt: Obstruction to an elementary Möbius-function proof of RH via Dirichlet series cancellation

Let \(M(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\mu(n)\). It is classical that RH is equivalent to
\[
M(x)=O_\varepsilon(x^{1/2+\varepsilon})\qquad(\forall\varepsilon>0).
\]
We examine whether a purely elementary cancellation argument on the Dirichlet series for \(1/\zeta(s)\) can force this bound (or even the weaker \(M(x)=o(x)\)), and isolate a precise analytic obstruction.

#### Setup
Write
\[
\frac1{\zeta(s)}=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{\mu(n)}{n^s},\qquad\operatorname{Re}s>1.
\]
Partial summation gives, for \(\sigma>1\),
\[
\int_1^\infty M(x)x^{-s-1}\,dx=\frac1{s\zeta(s)}.
\]
Suppose one attempts to obtain cancellation in \(M(x)\) by contour integration of the right-hand side against a smooth weight, shifting leftward past \(\operatorname{Re}s=1\). The only poles that can appear after the shift are the zeros of \(\zeta\). If every non-trivial zero satisfies \(\operatorname{Re}\rho=1/2\), the resulting contour integral is \(O(x^{1/2+\varepsilon})\) after standard convexivity estimates on \(\zeta\) and a truncated Perron formula. The converse direction (RH \(\Rightarrow\) the \(O\)-bound) is equally standard.

#### Elementary obstruction
An “elementary” proof would have to produce the same cancellation without ever invoking the analytic continuation of \(\zeta\) or the location of its zeros. Concretely, one would need a combinatorial identity or sieve inequality that forces
\[
\Bigl|\sum_{n\le x}\mu(n)f(n)\Bigr|\ll x^{1/2+\varepsilon}\|f\|
\]
for a sufficiently rich class of test functions \(f\) (e.g., characteristic functions of intervals, or smooth approximations thereto).  

Consider the simplest candidate: the pure Möbius inversion identity
\[
\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)=\mathbf{1}_{n=1}.
\]
Summing up to \(x\) and applying the hyperbola method yields only
\[
M(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\mu(n)=O\bigl(x\exp(-c\sqrt{\log x})\bigr)
\]
(the classical elementary zero-free region bound), which is far weaker than \(x^{1/2+\varepsilon}\). Any attempt to improve the exponent by inserting a smooth weight \(\phi\) supported on \([1,x]\) and expanding
\[
\sum_n\mu(n)\phi(n)=\sum_{k}\Bigl(\sum_{d\mid k}\mu(d)\Bigr)\widehat\phi(k)
\]
merely recovers the same identity; the diagonal term is 1 and the off-diagonal terms are controlled solely by the divisor bound, again giving only a power-saving of size \(x^{1-\delta}\) for an absolute \(\delta>0\) that cannot be pushed to \(1/2\).

#### Precise gap
The obstruction is spectral. The Dirichlet series \(1/\zeta(s)\) possesses an Euler product, but the partial sums of its coefficients are governed by the zeros via the explicit formula
\[
M(x)=-\sum_{|\operatorname{Im}\rho|\le T}\frac{x^\rho}{\rho\zeta'(\rho)}+O\bigl(x(\log x)^2/T\bigr)+O(1).
\]
Any elementary majorant that ignores the locations of the \(\rho\) can at best reproduce the convexivity bound \(\zeta(1/2+it)\ll |t|^{1/6+\varepsilon}\) (or the weaker elementary zero-free region), both of which translate into an error term larger than \(x^{1/2+\varepsilon}\). Closing the gap to the critical line therefore requires an input that is at least as strong as a zero-density estimate or a mean-value theorem for \(\zeta\) on vertical lines—precisely the analytic information one hoped to avoid.

Thus no purely combinatorial cancellation on the Möbius function, short of an identity that secretly encodes the functional equation, can prove RH.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Elementary Möbius cancellation only recovers weak zero-free regions; spectral input from zeros is indispensable.
