### Attempt: A reduction of RH to a spectral gap for a truncated Weil explicit formula operator

Let \(\Xi(t) := \xi(1/2 + it)\) denote the completed Riemann xi-function restricted to the critical line (entire, real for real \(t\), and of order 1). The Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to all zeros of \(\Xi\) being real. Write the Hadamard product (normalized)
\[
\Xi(t) = \Xi(0)\prod_{\rho}(1 - t^2/\rho^2),
\]
where the product runs over the non-trivial zeros with \(\operatorname{Re}\rho>0\), counted with multiplicity, and \(\rho = 1/2 + i\gamma\).

Weil’s explicit formula, after a standard Fourier transform and smooth test-function cutoff, yields an identity of the schematic form
\[
\sum_{\gamma} \hat f(\gamma) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,d\bigl(\tfrac12 x + \psi(e^{|x|})\bigr) + \text{archimedean Gamma terms},
\]
where \(\psi(u)=\sum_{n\le u}\Lambda(n)\). Define a family of compactly supported even test functions \(f_T\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)\) with \(\operatorname{supp}\hat f_T\subset[-T,T]\) and \(\hat f_T\ge0\), normalized so that \(\hat f_T(0)=1\). The associated quadratic form on the zero side is
\[
Q_T(f) := \sum_{\gamma}\hat f_T(\gamma).
\]
If one could exhibit a self-adjoint operator \(A_T\) (on a suitable Hilbert space of functions on the primes, or on \(L^2(\mathbb R_+,dx/x)\)) whose spectrum is bounded below by a positive constant independent of \(T\), and such that
\[
Q_T(f) = \langle f,A_T f\rangle + o(1)\qquad(T\to\infty),
\]
then all \(\gamma\) would be forced to be real (the contribution of any complex conjugate pair \(\beta+i\gamma\), \(\beta-i\gamma\) with \(\beta\neq1/2\) produces an exponentially growing term that would eventually dominate and drive \(Q_T\) negative for a suitably chosen positive-definite \(\hat f_T\)).

**Concrete construction attempted.** Let \(H_T\) be the finite-dimensional space of step functions constant on the intervals \([p_k,p_{k+1})\) for the first \(N(T)\approx e^T\) primes, equipped with the inner product
\[
\langle u,v\rangle = \sum_{p\le e^T}\frac{\Lambda(p)}{p}u(p)\overline{v(p)}.
\]
Define the truncated “Weil operator” by its matrix elements
\[
(A_T)_{p,q} = \sqrt{\Lambda(p)\Lambda(q)}\,K_T(\log(pq)) + \delta_{pq}\,c_T(p),
\]
where \(K_T\) is the Fourier transform of a fixed non-negative mollifier supported in \([-T,T]\) and \(c_T(p)\) absorbs the archimedean and diagonal prime-power corrections (explicitly bounded by \(O(\log\log T)\)). The quadratic form identity
\[
\langle f,A_T f\rangle = Q_T(f) + E_T(f)
\]
holds with an error \(E_T\) that is \(O(T^{-1}\|f\|_{H^1}^2)\) by the classical prime-number theorem with classical zero-free region.

**Gap.** The lowest eigenvalue \(\lambda_{\min}(A_T)\) can be shown (by a Gershgorin argument plus the known zero-density estimate \(N(\sigma,T)\ll T^{c(1-\sigma)^{3/2}}\log T\)) to satisfy
\[
\lambda_{\min}(A_T)\ge -C\exp(-c\sqrt{\log T})
\]
for an absolute \(C,c>0\). This lower bound tends to zero (albeit slowly) and is therefore insufficient to absorb the possible contribution of a hypothetical zero with \(|\operatorname{Re}\rho-1/2|\ge\delta>0\). Improving the bound to a uniform spectral gap \(\lambda_{\min}(A_T)\ge\delta>0\) appears to require a zero-free region of width \(\gg 1/\log\log T\) or better, which is circular: such a region is already known to be essentially equivalent to a quasi-RH statement. Thus the reduction does not close.

The same obstruction appears if one replaces the prime-step space by a space of smooth functions on the adele class group (Connes’ approach): the “absorption” of the continuous spectrum still leaves an error term whose size is governed by the same zero-density estimates.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Spectral gap for truncated Weil operator decays; cannot rule out off-line zeros without circular zero-free data
