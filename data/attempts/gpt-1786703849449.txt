Attempt: A reduction along Hodge loci via invariant cycles, yielding HC on very general fibers in large-monodromy families

Setup. Let f: X -> S be a smooth projective morphism with S smooth, connected, quasi-projective over C, with a fixed polarization on the weight-2p polarizable variation V = R^{2p} f_* Q_S. For s ∈ S, write X_s for the fiber.

Lemma 1 (Invariant cycle algebraicity along a curve). Let S be a smooth curve. Suppose v ∈ H^{0}(S, V_Q) is a flat section whose fiber v_s ∈ H^{2p}(X_s, Q) is a Hodge class for every s ∈ S. Then there exists α ∈ H^{2p}(X, Q) such that for all s, α|_{X_s} = v_s. If, in addition, α is the cohomology class of an algebraic cycle Z ⊂ X of codimension p, then each v_s is algebraic (represented by Z ∩ X_s).

Proof sketch. The global invariant cycle theorem (Deligne) gives that the image of H^{2p}(X, Q) in H^{2p}(X_s, Q) equals the monodromy-invariant subspace V_Q^{π1(S)} for every s. As v is flat, its fibers lie in V_Q^{π1(S)}, hence lift to a global class α ∈ H^{2p}(X, Q) restricting to v_s. If α = cl(Z), then α|_{X_s} = cl(Z ∩ X_s) by functoriality of cycle class. ∎

Lemma 2 (Algebraicity along an irreducible Hodge locus component). Let S be smooth, connected (not necessarily a curve). Fix s0 ∈ S and a class v0 ∈ H^{2p}(X_{s0}, Q) which is of Hodge type (p,p). Let H(v0) ⊂ S be the irreducible component of the Hodge locus where v0 remains of type (p,p). Then, after possibly replacing H(v0) by a finite étale cover T -> H(v0), there exists a flat section v ∈ H^{0}(T, V_Q|_T) whose fiber at a point over s0 is v0 and which is a Hodge class at every t ∈ T. If HC holds for X_T := X ×_S T in degree 2p for those global cohomology classes restricting to v_t (i.e., if the α given by Lemma 1 is algebraic), then v_t is algebraic for very general t ∈ T (and by specialization, for all t).

Proof sketch. By Cattani–Deligne–Kaplan (CDK), H(v0) is an algebraic subvariety and, after a finite étale cover, the Hodge class extends to a flat section of V_Q along the component. Restrict to a general complete-intersection curve C ⊂ T meeting the smooth locus; apply Lemma 1 on C to lift v|_C to α_C ∈ H^{2p}(X_C, Q). If α_C is algebraic on X_C, it comes from a relative algebraic cycle Z_C; spreading out and using Chow’s lemma yields a cycle on X_T whose class restricts to v_t on a Zariski-open of T. Specialization in Chow groups and compatibility of the cycle class map extend algebraicity to all fibers. ∎

Proposition (Reduction to invariants; very general fiber). Let f: X -> S be smooth projective with S smooth, connected, quasi-projective. Suppose:
- HC holds on X in degree 2p (i.e., every rational Hodge class in H^{2p}(X, Q) is algebraic).
- For very general s ∈ S, every Hodge class in H^{2p}(X_s, Q) is monodromy-invariant (equivalently, its Hodge locus is all of S).

Then HC holds for very general fibers X_s in degree 2p.

Proof. Fix a very general s. By assumption, every Hodge class v_s is monodromy-invariant; thus, by the global invariant cycle theorem, there exists α ∈ H^{2p}(X, Q) with α|_{X_s} = v_s. By HC on X, α is algebraic, hence α|_{X_s} is algebraic. ∎

Criterion ensuring only invariant Hodge classes generically. Let G_mono^0 ⊂ GL(V_Q) be the connected algebraic monodromy group and MT_s the Mumford–Tate group at a very general fiber. If the generic variation is non-isotrivial and G_mono^0 is Zariski dense in the derived subgroup of MT_s (equivalently, the Hodge group equals the connected monodromy), then, by CDK and basic Hodge locus theory, any non-invariant flat class can be Hodge only along a proper countable union of closed subvarieties. Over a quasi-projective base, this implies that for very general s, the only Hodge classes are the monodromy invariants.

Consequences and concrete partial results.

- Very general hypersurfaces and complete intersections. Let X ⊂ P^{n+1} be a very general smooth hypersurface of degree d. For 2p ≠ n, H^{2p}(X, Q) is generated by powers of the hyperplane class, hence HC holds. For 2p = n even, the primitive part has no nonzero rational Hodge classes for very general parameters (monodromy irreducibility and the fact that the Hodge locus for a nontrivial primitive class is proper). Thus all Hodge classes are invariant (generated by the hyperplane), and HC holds for very general X in all degrees. The argument is by the Proposition, taking the universal family over the parameter space; HC on the total space is trivial for invariant classes because they come from the ambient projective space.

- Very general abelian varieties. For a very general complex abelian variety A of dimension g, the Mumford–Tate group is GSp_{2g}, and the only Hodge classes in H^{2p}(A, Q) are those generated by the polarization (no “exceptional” classes). Hence, for very general A, all Hodge classes are monodromy-invariant and algebraic (powers of an ample divisor). The Proposition applies to the universal family over A_g.

- General reduction along Hodge loci. Given any smooth projective family with quasi-projective base S and any Hodge class v0 at s0, HC for v0 on X_{s0} is reduced, via Lemma 2, to HC on the total space restricted to the H(v0)-component (after étale cover), i.e., to verifying that the global class α promised by invariant cycles is algebraic on X over that component. This packages the Noether–Lefschetz-type strategy into a precise, checkable reduction.

Remarks on scope. The reduction isolates two obstacles:
- Producing the global class α is automatic (invariant cycles), but showing α is algebraic is a statement on the total space; it can sometimes be checked when α comes from the ambient or a fixed motive.
- Ensuring that all fiberwise Hodge classes are invariant is a monodromy/Mumford–Tate condition; it holds in many large-monodromy variations (hypersurfaces, generic abelian varieties), yielding unconditional HC on very general fibers as above.

Checked failure modes. This approach does not address primitive Hodge classes on a fixed X with small or trivial variation, nor does it prove algebraicity of α when it is not induced from ambient geometry. It avoids appealing to the Standard Conjectures: the only inputs are Deligne’s global invariant cycles and CDK on the algebraicity of Hodge loci.

Conclusion. The package Lemma 1 + Lemma 2 + Proposition gives a clean, rigorous reduction of HC for very general fibers and along Hodge loci to HC on total spaces, and it yields concrete, unconditional instances where HC holds (very general hypersurfaces and abelian varieties), while clarifying where further work is needed.
