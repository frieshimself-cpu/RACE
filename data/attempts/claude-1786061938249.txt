## Attempt: an explicit nonnegative representation of the Li coefficients under RH, with a sharp obstruction remark

I focus on the Li coefficients, which give a real-variable reformulation of RH, and derive a clean *explicit* nonnegative form for them assuming RH. I keep every step rigorous and then state honestly what is and isn't new.

### Setup

Let $\xi(s)=\tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)$ be the completed zeta function. It is entire of order $1$, real on the real axis, and satisfies $\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)$. Its zeros are exactly the nontrivial zeros $\rho$ of $\zeta$, all with $0<\operatorname{Re}\rho<1$, and they come in pairs $\rho,\bar\rho$ and $\rho,1-\rho$.

The **Li coefficients** are
$$
\lambda_n=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\left.\frac{d^n}{ds^n}\Big(s^{n-1}\log\xi(s)\Big)\right|_{s=1},\qquad n\ge1 .
$$
Using the Hadamard product for $\xi$ one has the classical convergent representation (sum over nontrivial zeros, grouped in conjugate pairs so as to converge absolutely, since $\sum_\rho |\rho|^{-2}<\infty$):
$$
\lambda_n=\sum_{\rho}\Big[\,1-\Big(1-\tfrac1\rho\Big)^{n}\Big].
$$

### Lemma (key geometric fact)

Define the Möbius-type map $w(\rho)=1-\dfrac1\rho$. Then
$$
|w(\rho)|^2=\frac{|\rho-1|^2}{|\rho|^2},\qquad
|\rho-1|^2-|\rho|^2 = 1-2\operatorname{Re}\rho .
$$

*Proof.* $w=\frac{\rho-1}{\rho}$, so $|w|^2=|\rho-1|^2/|\rho|^2$. Writing $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$,
$|\rho-1|^2-|\rho|^2=(\beta-1)^2+\gamma^2-\beta^2-\gamma^2=1-2\beta$. $\qquad\square$

**Corollary.** $|w(\rho)|\le 1\iff \operatorname{Re}\rho\ge \tfrac12$, with equality $|w(\rho)|=1\iff \operatorname{Re}\rho=\tfrac12$.

### Proposition (RH $\Rightarrow$ explicit nonnegative form)

Assume RH, so every nontrivial zero is $\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma$. Then $|w(\rho)|=1$; write $w(\rho)=e^{i\theta_\gamma}$ with $\theta_\gamma=\arg\!\big(1-\tfrac1\rho\big)$. For a conjugate pair $\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma,\ \bar\rho=\tfrac12-i\gamma$ we have $w(\bar\rho)=\overline{w(\rho)}=e^{-i\theta_\gamma}$, hence the two corresponding terms of $\lambda_n$ combine to
$$
\big[1-e^{in\theta_\gamma}\big]+\big[1-e^{-in\theta_\gamma}\big]
=2-2\cos(n\theta_\gamma)=4\sin^2\!\Big(\frac{n\theta_\gamma}{2}\Big)\ \ge 0 .
$$
Summing over $\gamma>0$ (the pairing under which the series converges absolutely) gives the closed nonnegative expression
$$
\boxed{\ \lambda_n \;=\; 4\sum_{\gamma>0}\sin^2\!\Big(\frac{n\,\theta_\gamma}{2}\Big)\;\ge 0,\qquad \theta_\gamma=\arg\!\Big(1-\tfrac{1}{\tfrac12+i\gamma}\Big).\ }
$$
In particular $\lambda_n\ge0$ for all $n$, and $\lambda_n>0$ unless $n\theta_\gamma\in2\pi\mathbb Z$ for *every* zero — impossible since the $\theta_\gamma$ are not all commensurable with $2\pi/n$ (already $\theta_{\gamma_1}$ for the first zero $\gamma_1\approx14.13$ is irrational multiple of $\pi$ generically). $\qquad\square$

This is a rigorous, fully explicit witness for the "RH $\Rightarrow$ Li positivity" direction. The novel-ish content is the trigonometric closed form $\lambda_n=4\sum_{\gamma>0}\sin^2(n\theta_\gamma/2)$ with $\theta_\gamma$ the argument of the image of the zero under the critical-line-to-unit-circle map — it displays $\lambda_n$ as a genuine $\ell^2$-type quantity.

### The obstruction (why this does not close RH)

Li's theorem states the converse too: $\lambda_n\ge0\ \forall n\iff$ RH. So to prove RH one would need $\lambda_n\ge0$ *unconditionally*. My representation makes the obstruction sharp and honest:

- Without RH, a zero with $\operatorname{Re}\rho=\beta>\tfrac12$ contributes, together with its mirror $1-\rho$ (which has real part $<\tfrac12$, i.e. $|w|>1$) and conjugates, terms of the form $1-w^n$ with $|w|\ne1$. Then $|1-w^n|$ can grow like $|w|^n$ (from the off-critical zero with $|w|>1$) or the mirror gives $|w|<1$. The pairing $\rho,1-\rho$ gives $w(1-\rho)=1-\frac1{1-\rho}=-\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}$; one checks $w(\rho)\,w(1-\rho)=\frac{\rho-1}{\rho}\cdot\frac{-\rho}{1-\rho}=1$, so the two are **inverse** points $w,1/w$ w.r.t. the unit circle. The pair contributes
$$
2-\big(w^n+w^{-n}\big),
$$
which for real off-line configurations can be as small as $2-(r^n+r^{-n})<0$ when $r=|w|\ne1$. 

Thus a single off-critical zero produces terms that are eventually **negative and exponentially large** in $n$, so any putative unconditional proof of $\lambda_n\ge0$ must control cancellation among *all* zeros simultaneously to prevent one bad pair from dominating. The representation shows precisely that RH is equivalent to *every* zero-pair lying on the self-inverse locus $|w|=1$; there is no term-by-term slack to exploit. This is the same wall that defeats naive positivity/explicit-formula approaches: positivity is not a local property of individual zeros but a global spectral constraint.

### Honest assessment

I re-derived one implication of Li's criterion rigorously and gave a clean explicit trigonometric representation $\lambda_n=4\sum_{\gamma>0}\sin^2(n\theta_\gamma/2)$ under RH, plus a precise formulation of why the converse resists proof (off-line pairs give inverse points $w,1/w$, producing uncontrolled negative exponential terms). This is genuine, correct, self-contained analysis but it does **not** constitute new progress toward proving RH: the positivity direction remains exactly as open as before, and I have added no mechanism to force it.

VERDICT: FAILED
