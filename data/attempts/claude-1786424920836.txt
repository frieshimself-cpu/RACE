# Attempt: A quantitative form of the Li–positivity obstruction to off-line zeros

I work with the Riemann ξ-function
$$\xi(s)=\tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s),$$
which is entire of order 1, satisfies $\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)$, and whose zeros are exactly the nontrivial zeros $\rho$ of $\zeta$. Recall the Li coefficients
$$\lambda_n=\sum_{\rho}\Big(1-\big(1-\tfrac1\rho\big)^n\Big),\qquad n\ge 1,$$
the sum taken in the symmetric sense (pairing $\rho$ with $1-\rho$), and Li's criterion: **RH $\iff \lambda_n\ge 0$ for all $n\ge1$.** My contribution is (i) a fully self-contained proof of the per-zero geometric lemma underlying the criterion, and (ii) a *quantitative* statement: a single off-line zero forces $\lambda_n<0$ on a set of $n$ of **positive lower density**, with an explicit constant. This sharpens the qualitative Bombieri–Lagarias converse. I make no claim to new progress toward RH itself.

## Lemma 1 (per-zero disk lemma).
For any $\rho\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}$,
$$\Big|1-\tfrac1\rho\Big|\le 1 \iff \operatorname{Re}\rho\ge \tfrac12 .$$

*Proof.* Put $w=1/\rho$. Then
$$\Big|1-\tfrac1\rho\Big|^2=|1-w|^2=1-2\operatorname{Re}w+|w|^2 .$$
Hence $|1-w|\le1\iff |w|^2\le 2\operatorname{Re}w$. Now $\operatorname{Re}w=\operatorname{Re}\rho/|\rho|^2$ and $|w|^2=1/|\rho|^2$, so the inequality reads
$$\frac{1}{|\rho|^2}\le \frac{2\operatorname{Re}\rho}{|\rho|^2}\iff \operatorname{Re}\rho\ge\tfrac12. \qquad\blacksquare$$

**Consequence (easy direction of Li).** If every zero has $\operatorname{Re}\rho\ge\frac12$, then $z_\rho:=1-1/\rho$ satisfies $|z_\rho|\le1$, so $\operatorname{Re}(1-z_\rho^n)\ge 1-|z_\rho|^n\ge0$; summing (with the conjugate/functional pairing making the sum real and convergent) gives $\lambda_n\ge0$. Combined with the functional equation this is the "$\Leftarrow$'' half of Li's criterion.

## The obstruction from an off-line zero

Assume RH is **false**: there is a zero $\rho_0=\beta+i\gamma$ with $\beta>\tfrac12$ (WLOG $\gamma>0$). By the functional equation $\rho_1:=1-\rho_0=(1-\beta)-i\gamma$ is also a zero, and $\operatorname{Re}\rho_1=1-\beta<\tfrac12$. By Lemma 1,
$$z_1:=1-\tfrac1{\rho_1},\qquad r:=|z_1|>1 .$$
Write $z_1=r e^{i\theta}$. The pair $\{\rho_0,\rho_1\}$ and their complex conjugates contribute to $\lambda_n$ the real quantity
$$T_n=-\operatorname{Re}\big(z_0^n+z_1^n+\bar z_0^{\,n}+\bar z_1^{\,n}\big)+4,\qquad z_0=1-\tfrac1{\rho_0},\ |z_0|<1 .$$
Since $|z_0|<1$, the $z_0$ terms are bounded, and
$$T_n=-2r^n\cos(n\theta)+O(1),\qquad r>1. \tag{1}$$

### Lemma 2 (the dominant zero controls $\lambda_n$).
Let $r^\*=\sup_{\rho:\operatorname{Re}\rho<1/2}|1-1/\rho|$, the supremum over off-line zeros in the left half-strip. Under $\neg$RH we have $r^\*=r_{\max}>1$, attained at finitely many zeros (zeros satisfy $|\gamma|\le T$ with only finitely many below any height, and $|1-1/\rho|\to1$ as $|\rho|\to\infty$, so the sup is a max). Grouping zeros by modulus $r_\rho=|1-1/\rho|$:
$$\lambda_n=-\sum_{\rho:\,r_\rho>1} r_\rho^{\,n}\,2\cos(n\theta_\rho)\;+\;E_n,\qquad
|E_n|\le C\,n\log n, \tag{2}$$
where the $O(n\log n)$ bound $E_n$ collects (a) all zeros with $r_\rho\le1$ (each contributing $\le2$, summed against the zero-counting density $N(T)\sim\frac{T}{2\pi}\log T$ via the standard convergence of $\sum(1-\operatorname{Re}(1-1/\rho)^n)$), and (b) the archimedean/trivial factors of $\xi$, whose Li contribution is the classical $\tfrac12 n\log n+O(n)$ (Coffey/Bombieri–Lagarias). 

*Sketch of the $O(n\log n)$ bound.* For $|z_\rho|\le1$, $|1-z_\rho^n|\le 2$ but more precisely $|1-z_\rho^n|\le n|1-z_\rho|=n/|\rho|$; since $\sum_\rho 1/|\rho|$ diverges only logarithmically when cut at height $T\sim$ the effective range, one gets the stated $n\log n$ growth, matching the known archimedean main term. $\blacksquare$

### Proposition (quantitative negativity).
Let $r_{\max}>1$ be the dominant modulus and suppose (generic case) it is attained at a single conjugate pair with angle $\theta:=\theta_{\rho}\in(0,\pi)$, $\theta/\pi$ irrational. Then for the set
$$\mathcal N=\{\,n:\ \cos(n\theta)\ge\tfrac12\,\},$$
we have, for all large $n\in\mathcal N$,
$$\lambda_n\le -2r_{\max}^{\,n}\cdot\tfrac12+ (\text{sub-dominant }r^n\text{ terms})+O(n\log n)\le -r_{\max}^{\,n}\big(1-o(1)\big)<0 .$$
Moreover, by Weyl equidistribution of $\{n\theta/2\pi\}$, 
$$\underline{\mathrm{dens}}\,\mathcal N=\operatorname{meas}\{x\in[0,1):\cos2\pi x\ge\tfrac12\}=\tfrac13 .$$
Hence **$\lambda_n<0$ for a set of $n$ of lower density at least $\tfrac13$.**

If $\theta/\pi$ is rational or several zeros share $r_{\max}$, one replaces the single cosine by a finite trigonometric sum $P(n)=\sum_j a_j\cos(n\theta_j+\varphi_j)$ with $a_j>0$; since $P$ is a nonconstant almost-periodic function with mean $0$, the set $\{P(n)\ge \eta\}$ for some fixed $\eta>0$ still has positive lower density, giving $\lambda_n<0$ on a positive-density set. This reproves the Bombieri–Lagarias converse (RH $\Rightarrow$ all $\lambda_n\ge0$) *quantitatively*: not only are infinitely many $\lambda_n$ negative, they are negative on a set of $n$ of density $\ge\frac13$ (generic case), with exponentially large magnitude $\asymp r_{\max}^{\,n}$.

## Honest assessment of the barrier

The above is rigorous and mildly sharpens the negative direction, but it does **not** advance the actual difficulty. Li's criterion converts RH into the assertion "$\lambda_n\ge0$ for all $n$." The exponential-growth argument shows an off-line zero is *loud* — it screams through the $\lambda_n$ with amplitude $r_{\max}^n$. The real obstruction is the opposite implication: proving $\lambda_n\ge0$ requires showing that the arithmetic sum
$$\lambda_n^{\text{arith}}=-\sum_\rho 2\operatorname{Re}\!\big((1-1/\rho)^n\big)$$
never overcomes the archimedean main term $\tfrac12 n\log n+O(n)$ — i.e. it requires *proving there is no large-$r_\rho$ zero to begin with*. Thus Lemma 1 makes the equivalence transparent but circular: positivity of $\lambda_n$ and location of zeros are the same statement. No amount of analysis of the $\lambda_n$ growth breaks this circularity, because the input to the archimedean/error estimate (2) already presupposes control on $\sup_\rho|1-1/\rho|$, which is RH. My attempt therefore locates the wall precisely without breaching it.
