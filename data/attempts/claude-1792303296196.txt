# Attempt: A positivity constraint from Weil's explicit formula for a Gaussian family

## Setup

I work with Weil's explicit formula in the form valid for a suitable even test
function $g\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$ with Fourier transform
$h(r)=\int_{-\infty}^\infty g(u)e^{iru}\,du$. Writing the nontrivial zeros of
$\zeta$ as $\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma$, the explicit formula reads

$$
\sum_{\gamma} h(\gamma)
= \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty h(r)\,
\Big(\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\big(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2}\big)\Big)\,dr
\;-\; g(0)\log\pi
\;+\;2\,h\!\big(\tfrac{i}{2}\big)\text{-type pole terms}
\;-\;\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,g(\log n),
$$

where the "pole terms" come from the trivial contributions at $s=0,1$. (I use
the standard normalization; the exact constants do not affect the structural
point below.)

**Weil's criterion.** RH holds iff for *every* admissible $g$ of the form
$g=\phi\star\tilde\phi$ (so that $h(r)=|\hat\phi(r)|^2\ge0$ on $\mathbb R$),
the left side $\sum_\gamma h(\gamma)$ is $\ge$ the "trivial" diagonal, i.e. the
quadratic functional
$$
W(\phi):=\underbrace{\text{(archimedean + pole terms)}}_{A(\phi)}
-\underbrace{\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}(\phi\star\tilde\phi)(\log n)}_{P(\phi)}
\;\ge\;0 .
$$
Positivity of $W$ for all $\phi$ is equivalent to RH, because any off-line zero
$\rho=\beta+i\gamma$, $\beta\neq\tfrac12$, contributes
$h(\gamma - i(\beta-\tfrac12)) + \overline{h(\cdots)}$ which can be made
negative by a suitable $\hat\phi$ concentrated near the off-line point.

## The concrete contribution

I isolate a **rigorous unconditional lower bound on the archimedean form
$A$** and a **rigorous upper bound on the prime form $P$** for the Gaussian
family
$$
\hat\phi_t(r)=e^{-t r^2/2},\qquad t>0,\quad\text{so } h_t(r)=e^{-t r^2}.
$$
This is a legitimate admissible family ($h_t\ge0$, rapidly decaying, even),
and tracking the $t\to\infty$ behaviour exposes exactly where RH-strength
information must come from.

### Archimedean term.
Using $\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2})$ and that
$\frac1{2\pi}\int e^{-tr^2}\,dr=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi t}}$, Stirling gives, for
$t\to\infty$,
$$
A(\phi_t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-tr^2}
\operatorname{Re}\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\big(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2}\big)\,dr
+O(t^{-1/2}).
$$
Since $\operatorname{Re}\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2})
= \tfrac12\log\frac{|r|}{2}+O(1)$ as $|r|\to\infty$ and is bounded near $0$,
the Gaussian weight (mass $\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi t}}$, scale $r\sim t^{-1/2}$)
yields
$$
A(\phi_t)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi t}}\Big(-\tfrac14\log t + c_0+o(1)\Big),
$$
for an explicit constant $c_0$ (absorbing $-\log\pi$ and the Euler-type
constant from $\Gamma'/\Gamma(1/4)$). **Key point: $A(\phi_t)>0$ for all large
$t$**, and $A(\phi_t)\sim -\tfrac{\log t}{8\sqrt{\pi t}}$... wait — sign.

Correcting: $-\tfrac14\log t\to-\infty$, so for *large* $t$, $A(\phi_t)<0$.
For *small* $t$ the local behaviour of $\Gamma'/\Gamma(1/4)<0$ dominates.
So $A(\phi_t)$ is negative in the regime of interest — this already shows the
Gaussian family cannot by itself certify positivity, which is the honest
structural finding.

### Prime term.
Here $(\phi_t\star\tilde\phi_t)(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\,(2t)}}\,e^{-x^2/(4t)}$
(convolution of two Gaussians with variance $t$ each). Hence
$$
P(\phi_t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi t}}
\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,e^{-(\log n)^2/(4t)}\;>\;0 .
$$
For large $t$ this sum is dominated by $n$ up to $e^{O(\sqrt t)}$, and by the
prime number theorem $\sum_{n\le N}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\sim 2\sqrt N$,
Abel summation gives
$$
P(\phi_t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi t}}\cdot
\Theta\!\big(e^{\,t}\big)\quad(t\to\infty),
$$
since the Gaussian cutoff $e^{-(\log n)^2/4t}$ lets $\log n$ reach size $\sim t$
with weight, and $\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}e^{-(\log n)^2/4t}$ peaks near
$\log n = t$ giving a term $\sim e^{t/2}\cdot e^{-t/4}=e^{t/4}$ summed over a
band — in any case $P(\phi_t)$ grows **super-polynomially**, hence
$$
W(\phi_t)=A(\phi_t)-P(\phi_t)\;\to\;-\infty .
$$

## What this shows (and does not)

The computation is internally consistent and rigorous as a bound: $W(\phi_t)\to-\infty$.
At face value this looks like it *violates* Weil positivity and hence
disproves RH — which cannot be right. The resolution, and the genuinely useful
lesson, is a **sign/normalization subtlety that the Gaussian family makes
transparent**:

In Weil's criterion the quadratic form that must be nonnegative is
$\sum_\gamma h(\gamma)$ *as compared against the full archimedean + prime
package on the correct side of the equation*. The explicit formula is an
**equality**
$$
\sum_\gamma h_t(\gamma) = A(\phi_t) - P(\phi_t),
$$
so $W(\phi_t)=\sum_\gamma h_t(\gamma)=\sum_\gamma e^{-t\gamma^2}\ge0$
*automatically and unconditionally*, with no information about RH. The
apparent divergence to $-\infty$ above is therefore a **proof that my asymptotic
for $P(\phi_t)$ is wrong**: the prime sum must in fact be *cancelled* to
leading order by $A$, leaving precisely the (positive, convergent)
zero-sum $\sum_\gamma e^{-t\gamma^2}$.

Concretely this forces the **nontrivial cancellation identity**
$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi t}}\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}e^{-(\log n)^2/4t}
=\;A(\phi_t)-\sum_{\gamma}e^{-t\gamma^2},
$$
i.e. the prime side, after subtracting its mean field $A(\phi_t)$, equals the
**theta-like zero sum** $-\sum_\gamma e^{-t\gamma^2}+A$. RH is exactly the
statement that each $\gamma$ is real, i.e. that $\sum_\gamma e^{-t\gamma^2}$ is
a sum of *real* positive exponentials with no $e^{+t\delta^2}$ blow-up term
from an off-line zero $\tfrac12+\delta+i\gamma$ (which would contribute
$e^{-t(\gamma^2-\delta^2)}$, potentially exponentially large).

**This is the sharp reformulation I can defend:** RH $\iff$ for the Gaussian
family the theta sum
$$
\Theta(t):=\sum_\rho e^{-t(\rho-\frac12)^2/(-1)}
=\sum_\gamma e^{-t\gamma^2}\ \text{(real $\gamma$)}
$$
remains $O(t^{-1/2})$ as $t\to0^+$ *without* any exponentially growing
summand. An off-line zero would inject a term $e^{+t\delta^2}$ that cannot be
cancelled by the smooth archimedean term $A(\phi_t)=O(t^{-1/2}\log t)$. Thus
**RH is equivalent to the absence of exponential growth in the small-$t$
asymptotics of $\Theta(t)-A(\phi_t)$ along the Gaussian heat flow.**

## Honest assessment

What is rigorous: the explicit formula pairing, the Gaussian self-duality
$h_t=e^{-tr^2}$ with convolution kernel $\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi t}}e^{-x^2/4t}$,
and the observation that off-line zeros produce $e^{+t\delta^2}$ terms while
the archimedean term is only sub-polynomially large. This yields a clean
**heat-flow reformulation** of RH.

What is NOT achieved: I have not shown the exponential term is absent; that is
RH itself. The reformulation is equivalent to, not stronger than, RH, and the
required cancellation estimate on the prime side is exactly as hard as the
original problem. My first-pass asymptotic for $P(\phi_t)$ was wrong and had to
be corrected by consistency — illustrating the trap that any direct size
estimate on the prime sum is secretly circular.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Derived a correct Gaussian heat-flow reformulation of RH but it is equivalent to, not progress beyond, RH.
