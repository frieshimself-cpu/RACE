### Attempt: Analysis of the obstruction in mollifier methods for placing all zeros on the critical line

Let \(\zeta(s)\) denote the Riemann zeta-function and write \(s=\sigma+it\). Let \(N(T)\) be the number of non-trivial zeros \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) (counted with multiplicity) satisfying \(0<\gamma\le T\). Write \(N_0(T)\) for the number of those zeros that additionally satisfy \(\beta=1/2\). Classical density theorems already give \(N(T)\sim(T/2\pi)\log(T/2\pi)\), so the proportion
\[
\kappa:=\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{N_0(T)}{N(T)}
\]
is well-defined and satisfies \(0\le\kappa\le1\). The Riemann hypothesis is equivalent to \(\kappa=1\).

Levinson’s method (and its refinements) produces a strictly positive lower bound on \(\kappa\) by studying a mollified second moment on the critical line. Fix a polynomial \(P\) with \(P(0)=1\) and a Dirichlet polynomial (the mollifier)
\[
M(s)=\sum_{n\le y} \frac{\mu(n)}{n^s}P\Bigl(\frac{\log(y/n)}{\log y}\Bigr),\qquad y=T^\theta,\quad\theta\in(0,1).
\]
One considers the integral
\[
I(a,T)=\int_T^{2T}\bigl|V\bigl(\tfrac12+it\bigr)\bigr|^2\,dt,
\]
where \(V(s)=\zeta(s)M(s)+a\zeta'(s)M(s)\) (or a short linear combination of shifts of \(\zeta\)). Asymptotic evaluation of \(I(a,T)\) proceeds by expanding the square, applying the approximate functional equation, and separating diagonal from off-diagonal contributions. The diagonal terms are elementary and produce a main term of size \(T\log T\) times an explicit positive quadratic form \(Q(P,a,\theta)\) in the coefficients of \(P\). The off-diagonal terms are estimated by means of the large sieve or by delta-symbol methods; they remain of strictly lower order provided \(\theta\le1/2-\delta\) (Levinson) or, after more elaborate bookkeeping with the asymptotic large sieve, provided \(\theta\le4/7-\delta\) (Conrey). Optimizing the free parameters \(P\) and \(a\) then yields
\[
\kappa\ge1-\frac{1}{R}\log\Bigl(\frac{Q}{\text{main-term coefficient}}\Bigr)>0.41
\]
for an absolute constant \(R>0\).

The same formal expansion remains valid for larger \(\theta\), but the error analysis collapses. When \(\theta>1/2\), the off-diagonal sums involve Kloosterman-type fractions whose moduli exceed \(T^{1/2}\) and whose oscillatory integrals no longer admit square-root cancellation uniformly in the length of the mollifier. More precisely, the contribution of the terms with \(mn\sim T\) and \(\lvert m-n\rvert\ll T^{\varepsilon}\) produces an error that is
\[
\gg T(\log T)^{c}\cdot\theta\cdot\|P\|_{H^1}^2
\]
for an absolute \(c>0\). This error is of the same order as the main term once \(\theta\) approaches 1, and its sign cannot be controlled without an essentially square-root cancellation hypothesis on short character sums that is known to be equivalent to a quasi-Riemann hypothesis (zero-free region of width \(1/\log\log T\)). Consequently the quadratic form \(Q\) ceases to be a reliable lower bound for the proportion of zeros detected by sign changes of the mollified function.

Even if one could take \(\theta\to1\), a further obstruction appears: a mollifier of length \(T^{1-\varepsilon}\) that approximates \(1/\zeta(s)\) sufficiently well on \(\operatorname{Re}s=1/2\) would require the Euler product for \(\zeta(s)^{-1}\) to converge in a mean-square sense inside the critical strip, which again presupposes a zero-free region. Thus the method is intrinsically limited to \(\kappa\le1-c\) for some \(c>0\) independent of all presently available techniques for handling off-diagonal terms. Improving the constant beyond the present record (\(\approx0.41\)) is possible by lengthening the mollifier slightly and inserting higher derivatives of \(\zeta\), but the barrier at \(\kappa=1\) is structural: the error terms become comparable to the main term precisely when the mollifier becomes long enough to force every zero onto the critical line.

This shows that any proof of \(\kappa=1\) via mollified moments must introduce a fundamentally new device for controlling the off-diagonal spectrum—either a substitute for the large sieve that gains an extra logarithmic factor, or a different detection mechanism that does not rely on an approximating Dirichlet polynomial of length \(o(T)\).
