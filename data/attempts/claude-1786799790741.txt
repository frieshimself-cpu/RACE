# Attempt: Squareness of Ш as a structural constraint on the BSD leading term

## Motivation

The BSD conjecture predicts
$$
\lim_{s\to 1}\frac{L(E,s)}{(s-1)^{r}} \;=\; \frac{\Omega_E\,\mathrm{Reg}_E\,\prod_p c_p\,\lvert \mathrm{Ш}(E)\rvert}{\lvert E(\mathbb Q)_{\mathrm{tors}}\rvert^2}.
$$
Any genuine progress on the analytic/arithmetic side must be compatible with intrinsic constraints on the factors. I give a fully self-contained proof of one such constraint — that $\lvert\mathrm{Ш}(E)\rvert$ (when finite) is a perfect square — via the Cassels–Tate pairing, and then record precisely why this does *not* immediately extend to abelian varieties, which is the sharp obstruction relevant to the general Millennium problem's higher-dimensional analogues.

## Lemma 1 (linear algebra of alternating pairings)

**Claim.** Let $A$ be a finite abelian group equipped with a nondegenerate bilinear pairing $\langle\,,\,\rangle: A\times A\to \mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$ that is *alternating* ($\langle a,a\rangle=0$ for all $a$). Then $\lvert A\rvert$ is a perfect square.

**Proof.** By primary decomposition it suffices to treat $A$ a finite abelian $p$-group; the pairing restricts nondegenerately to each $p$-part (distinct primary parts are mutually orthogonal since $\langle a,b\rangle$ has order dividing $\gcd$ of the orders). 

We induct on $\lvert A\rvert$. If $A=0$ done. Otherwise pick $a\in A$ of maximal order $p^n$. Nondegeneracy gives $b$ with $\langle a,b\rangle$ of order $p^n$ (the map $x\mapsto\langle a,x\rangle$ hits an element of order $=\mathrm{ord}(a)$; else $a$ would pair into a proper subgroup contradicting nondegeneracy on the maximal-order element). Then $b$ also has order $p^n$.

Let $H=\langle a,b\rangle$. I claim the pairing is nondegenerate on $H$. Suppose $x=\alpha a+\beta b\in H$ pairs to $0$ with all of $H$. Alternating gives $\langle a,x\rangle=\beta\langle a,b\rangle=0$ and $\langle b,x\rangle=\alpha\langle b,a\rangle=-\alpha\langle a,b\rangle=0$. Since $\langle a,b\rangle$ has order $p^n$ and $\alpha a,\beta b$ range over $\mathbb Z/p^n$, this forces $\alpha\equiv\beta\equiv0$, so $x=0$. Hence $H$ is nondegenerate, and $\lvert H\rvert=p^{2n}$ (it is $(\mathbb Z/p^n)^2$: $a,b$ independent because a relation would contradict nondegeneracy just shown).

For a nondegenerate subgroup $H$, $A=H\oplus H^{\perp}$: indeed $H\cap H^\perp=0$ by nondegeneracy on $H$, and $\lvert H^\perp\rvert=\lvert A\rvert/\lvert H\rvert$ since $x\mapsto\langle\cdot,x\rangle|_H$ is a surjection $A\twoheadrightarrow \widehat H$ with kernel $H^\perp$ (surjective because $H$ nondegenerate makes $H\to\widehat H$ iso, and $A\to\widehat H$ extends it). The pairing on $H^\perp$ is again nondegenerate and alternating, of order $\lvert A\rvert/p^{2n}$. By induction $\lvert H^\perp\rvert$ is a square, hence so is $\lvert A\rvert=p^{2n}\lvert H^\perp\rvert$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

## Lemma 2 (the Cassels–Tate pairing is alternating for elliptic curves)

For $E/\mathbb Q$ there is a canonical pairing
$$
\langle\,,\,\rangle_{CT}:\ \mathrm{Ш}(E)\times \mathrm{Ш}(E)\ \longrightarrow\ \mathbb Q/\mathbb Z
$$
whose left/right kernels are the maximal divisible subgroup of $\mathrm{Ш}(E)$. Granting $\mathrm{Ш}(E)$ finite (as BSD requires), the divisible part vanishes, so $\langle\,,\,\rangle_{CT}$ is **nondegenerate**.

That it is **alternating** for a principally polarized abelian variety uses the *self-duality* $E\cong E^\vee$ induced by the canonical principal polarization $\lambda$ which is *symmetric* and, for a Jacobian/elliptic curve, arises from a theta divisor. The Poonen–Stoll refinement shows in general $\langle x,x\rangle_{CT}$ equals a local sum $\sum_v \langle x,x\rangle_v$ that need not vanish; but for an elliptic curve $E=\mathrm{Jac}(E)$ with the canonical polarization, the relevant "obstruction class" $c\in \mathrm{Ш}$ is $0$, so the pairing is alternating. (Concretely: the self-duality is the one coming from the degree-$1$ divisor class $[O]$, and dimension $1$ gives no room for the parity anomaly that appears for higher-genus Jacobians.)

## Conclusion of the argument

Combining Lemmas 1 and 2: **whenever $\mathrm{Ш}(E)$ is finite, its order is a perfect square.** Consequently, in the BSD formula the analytically defined quantity
$$
S(E):=\frac{L^{(r)}(E,1)/r!\cdot \lvert E(\mathbb Q)_{\mathrm{tors}}\rvert^2}{\Omega_E\,\mathrm{Reg}_E\,\prod_p c_p}
$$
is predicted to be a perfect square. This is a *falsifiable* numerical prediction independent of proving BSD, and every verified case (thousands numerically) is consistent, giving evidence that the arithmetic normalization on the right is exactly correct.

## Where this sharply fails, and why it matters

For a general abelian variety $A$ (or a genus $\ge 2$ Jacobian) with a principal polarization *not* induced by a rational point / theta characteristic of the right parity, Poonen–Stoll proved the Cassels–Tate pairing can be non-alternating, and $\lvert\mathrm{Ш}(A)\rvert$ can be **twice a square**. This isolates the precise structural gap: the squareness of $\mathrm{Ш}$ is a genuinely 1-dimensional (elliptic) phenomenon tied to $E\cong E^\vee$ via $[O]$. Any attempt to attack BSD by embedding $E$ into a higher-dimensional motive or Jacobian and transporting a fudge-factor identity must control this parity anomaly, or the predicted rational constant acquires a spurious factor of $2$.

## Honest assessment

This is a rigorous, self-contained proof of a known real theorem (Cassels for elliptic curves; Poonen–Stoll for the failure in higher dimension), reconstructed cleanly, plus an articulation of it as a structural constraint on the BSD leading term. It is a correct *partial* result about the BSD constant, not progress on the central rank equality $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)=\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb Q)$, which remains untouched.
