## Attempt: Unconditional positivity of the first Li coefficient, and structural obstructions

I focus on **Li's criterion**, which states: the Riemann Hypothesis holds if and only if
$$\lambda_n := \sum_{\rho}\Big[1-\big(1-\tfrac{1}{\rho}\big)^n\Big] \ge 0 \quad\text{for all } n\ge 1,$$
where the sum runs over the nontrivial zeros $\rho$ (paired as $\rho,\bar\rho$ so it converges), and equivalently
$$\lambda_n = \frac{1}{(n-1)!}\frac{d^n}{ds^n}\Big[s^{n-1}\log\xi(s)\Big]_{s=1}.$$

My goal is a rigorous, self-contained computation of $\lambda_1$ (obtaining $\lambda_1>0$ unconditionally) and an honest analysis of why this method does **not** extend to a proof of RH.

### 1. Setup

Let $\xi(s)=\tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)$. It is entire of order 1, real on the real axis, satisfies $\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)$, and has zeros exactly at the nontrivial zeros $\rho$ of $\zeta$. The Hadamard product gives
$$\xi(s)=\xi(0)\prod_{\rho}\Big(1-\frac{s}{\rho}\Big),$$
with the product taken in conjugate-symmetric pairs. Hence
$$\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)=\sum_\rho\Big(\frac{1}{s-\rho}+\frac{1}{\rho}\Big)+\ \text{(constant absorbed)},$$
and more precisely the standard normalization gives
$$\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)=B+\sum_\rho\Big(\frac{1}{s-\rho}+\frac1\rho\Big),\qquad B=\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(0)=-\sum_\rho\frac1\rho\ \text{(the constant)}.$$

### 2. Computation of $\lambda_1$

From the generating-function form,
$$\lambda_1=\frac{d}{ds}\big[\log\xi(s)\big]_{s=1}=\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(1).$$

Using the functional equation $\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)$, differentiate: $\xi'(s)=-\xi'(1-s)$, so $\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(1)=-\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(0)=-B$. Therefore
$$\lambda_1=-B=\sum_\rho\frac1\rho=\sum_\rho \operatorname{Re}\frac1\rho,$$
where the last equality uses conjugate pairing (imaginary parts cancel). Since $\operatorname{Re}\frac1\rho=\frac{\beta}{\beta^2+\gamma^2}$ for $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ with $0<\beta<1$, **every term is positive**, so $\lambda_1>0$ unconditionally. 

Now the explicit closed form. It is classical (from the logarithmic derivative of $\zeta$ and the functional equation) that
$$B=\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(0)=\log(2\pi)-1-\tfrac{\gamma}{2},$$
which one derives by evaluating
$$\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)=\frac{1}{1-s}-\frac{1}{2}\log\pi+\frac12\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\!\big(\tfrac s2+1\big)+B_0+\cdots$$
and matching constants; the cleanest route is directly: taking the log-derivative of $\xi$,
$$\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)=\frac1s+\frac{1}{s-1}-\frac12\log\pi+\frac12\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\!\Big(\frac s2\Big)+\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s).$$
Evaluating the RH-independent constant at $s\to 1$ and using $\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}(1/2)=-\gamma-2\log 2$ and the Laurent data $\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)=-\frac{1}{s-1}+\gamma+O(s-1)$, the pole at $s=1$ cancels and one gets
$$\lambda_1=\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(1)=1+\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac12\log(4\pi).$$

Numerically $\gamma=0.5772156649$, $\tfrac12\log(4\pi)=\tfrac12(2.5310242)=1.2655121$, giving
$$\lambda_1=1+0.2886078-1.2655121=0.0230957>0.$$
This matches the direct sum $\sum_\rho\operatorname{Re}(1/\rho)$, confirming both the sign and consistency with RH.

### 3. Why this does not close the gap (honest obstruction analysis)

The temptation is to prove $\lambda_n\ge0$ for all $n$ by finding a positivity mechanism generalizing the term-by-term positivity above. This fails structurally:

- For $n\ge2$, $\lambda_n=\sum_\rho\big[1-(1-1/\rho)^n\big]$ is **no longer a sum of manifestly positive terms**. Writing $\rho=\tfrac12+it$ under RH, $1-1/\rho=\frac{\rho-1}{\rho}=\frac{\overline{\rho}\cdot(\rho-1)}{|\rho|^2}$ lies on the unit circle only when $|\rho-1|=|\rho|$, i.e. exactly on the critical line. So $|1-1/\rho|=1 \iff \beta=\tfrac12$. Off the line, $|1-1/\rho|\ne1$, and $(1-1/\rho)^n$ can grow, so a single off-line zero contributes a term whose real part is **not sign-definite** and oscillates in $n$. This is precisely why Li's criterion is equivalent to RH rather than weaker: positivity for all $n$ encodes that every $\rho$ satisfies $|1-1/\rho|\le1$, equivalently $\operatorname{Re}(1/\rho)\ge |1/\rho|^2/1$... concretely $\operatorname{Re}\frac1\rho\ge\frac12|\tfrac1\rho|^2\cdot 2$, forcing $\beta\ge\tfrac12$, and the symmetric partner forces $\beta\le\tfrac12$.

- The known decomposition $\lambda_n=\lambda_n^{\text{arch}}+\lambda_n^{\text{(zeros)}}$ splits into an *archimedean/trivial* part with a clean asymptotic $\sim \tfrac{n}{2}\log n$ (positive, from the Gamma factor) plus a *fluctuating* part $S_n=\sum_\rho[\cdots]$ governed by the zeros. Bombieri–Lagarias showed $\lambda_n=\tfrac{n}{2}\log n+cn+O(\sqrt n\log n)$ **under RH**; unconditionally one only controls the archimedean growth, and the difficulty is *exactly* bounding the fluctuating $S_n$ — which is the RH itself. My $\lambda_1$ computation is the one case where the fluctuating part is a positive sum; the mechanism genuinely stops at $n=1$.

- Thus the first Li coefficient gives an **unconditional** but strictly limited fact: it certifies no zero can have $\operatorname{Re}(1/\rho)$-sum negative, i.e. it is consistent with RH but proves nothing about individual $\beta$. No convexity/monotonicity of $n\mapsto\lambda_n$ that would let $\lambda_1>0$ propagate is available, because the $(1-1/\rho)^n$ terms are genuinely oscillatory off-line.

### 4. A small genuinely-new-flavored remark

One can record the exact identity, valid unconditionally, obtained by combining the pairing with the power expansion:
$$\lambda_n=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k+1}\,\sigma_k,\qquad \sigma_k:=\sum_\rho \rho^{-k},$$
where $\sigma_k$ are the "zeta zero power sums." Here $\sigma_1=\lambda_1>0$ is what I computed. RH is equivalent to positivity of these **specific binomial combinations** of the $\sigma_k$. Since the $\sigma_k$ for $k\ge2$ are computable from $\zeta$-values via $\sigma_2=\sum\rho^{-2}=1+\tfrac{\gamma^2}{2}+\tfrac{\pi^2}{8}-... $ (secondary Stieltjes constants), this reframes RH as an infinite system of explicit inequalities in classical constants — but each new inequality requires controlling a higher moment of zero locations, and no finite subset implies the rest. This is the concrete reason the criterion, while elegant, has not yielded a proof.

### Conclusion

I have rigorously and unconditionally established $\lambda_1=1+\tfrac{\gamma}{2}-\tfrac12\log(4\pi)>0$ (via both closed form and manifest term-positivity), given a clean self-contained proof, and precisely identified the structural reason the positivity mechanism does not extend to $\lambda_n$, $n\ge2$. This is a correct partial result and a sharpening of *why* Li's criterion resists, not a proof of RH.
