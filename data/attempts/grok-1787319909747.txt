### Attempt: Obstruction in the Weil positivity criterion for RH

Let \(\xi(s)=s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\) be the completed Riemann xi-function (entire of order 1, functional equation \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\)). The non-trivial zeros of \(\zeta\) are precisely the zeros of \(\xi\). Write zeros as \(\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma\) (allowing \(\operatorname{Re}\rho\neq\tfrac12\)).

Weil’s explicit formula associates to a suitable even test function \(g\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})\) the identity
\[
\sum_{\gamma}\hat g(\gamma)=g(0)\Bigl(\tfrac12\log\pi-\tfrac12\tfrac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}(\tfrac14)+\cdots\Bigr)
+\int_0^\infty\frac{g(\tfrac12\log t)}{t-1}\,dt
-\sum_p\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{\log p}{p^{m/2}}g(m\log p),
\]
where the left-hand side runs over all ordinates \(\gamma\) (with multiplicity) and \(\hat g\) is the Fourier transform. The classical positivity criterion of Weil states that RH is equivalent to
\[
\sum_{\gamma}\hat g(\gamma)\ge0
\]
for every \(g\) whose Fourier transform is non-negative (more precisely, for the Weil distribution \(W(g)\ge0\) on a suitable cone of test functions).

#### Attempted progress
Suppose one tries to prove the inequality directly by constructing a family of approximants \(g_\varepsilon\) that concentrate near the origin while keeping \(\hat g_\varepsilon\ge0\). A natural candidate is a Gaussian mollifier
\[
g_\varepsilon(x)=e^{-\pi\varepsilon x^2}\ast\phi(x),\qquad\varepsilon\downarrow0,
\]
where \(\phi\ge0\) is a fixed compactly supported even bump with \(\hat\phi\ge0\). The prime-power sum on the right-hand side of the explicit formula is then absolutely convergent and can be bounded by the prime-number theorem with classical error term:
\[
\Bigl|\sum_{p,m}\frac{\log p}{p^{m/2}}g_\varepsilon(m\log p)\Bigr|\le C(\phi)\varepsilon^{-1/2}.
\]
The archimedean contribution (Gamma factor + integral) admits an asymptotic expansion
\[
\tfrac12\log(1/\varepsilon)+O(1)
\]
that is positive for small \(\varepsilon\). Consequently the whole right-hand side is eventually positive, which would force
\[
\sum_{\gamma}\hat g_\varepsilon(\gamma)\ge0.
\]
If one could pass to the limit \(\varepsilon\to0\) inside the sum over zeros, one would conclude that every \(\gamma\) is real, i.e., RH.

#### Rigorous obstruction
The passage to the limit is illegitimate. The partial sums
\[
S(T;\varepsilon)=\sum_{|\gamma|\le T}\hat g_\varepsilon(\gamma)
\]
satisfy only the crude bound
\[
|S(T;\varepsilon)|\ll_\varepsilon T\log T
\]
coming from the Riemann–von Mangoldt formula \(N(T)=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi}-\frac{T}{2\pi}+O(\log T)\). The error term \(O(\log T)\) is too large to dominate the possible contribution of a hypothetical zero with \(\operatorname{Re}\rho=\sigma_0>\tfrac12\): such a zero produces a factor \(e^{2\pi(\sigma_0-1/2)|t|}\) in the inverse Fourier transform, which grows exponentially and cannot be absorbed by any fixed power of \(\log T\). In other words, the family \(\{g_\varepsilon\}\) does not lie in the Weil cone uniformly in \(\varepsilon\); the constants implicit in the positivity of the archimedean terms deteriorate precisely when one tries to control zeros far from the critical line.

Thus the argument yields only the weaker statement
\[
\sum_{|\gamma|\le T}\operatorname{Re}\hat g(\gamma)\ge-C_\phi T\log T,
\]
which is already known from the classical zero-density estimates and does not force \(\sigma_0=\tfrac12\).

(The same obstruction appears for any approximate identity whose Fourier transform has Gaussian decay: the necessary uniformity in the zero sum fails exactly on the scale where a zero off the line would become visible.)

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Weil-positivity limit obstructed by insufficient zero-sum uniformity; recovers only classical density bounds
