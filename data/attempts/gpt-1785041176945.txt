Attempt: A barrier for Nyman–Beurling approximations with nonnegative coefficients

Setup.
Work in the Hardy–Hilbert space H^2(σ0) of functions analytic in Re s>σ0 with norm
||F||^2 = (1/2π) ∫_{ℝ} |F(σ0+it)|^2 dt.
Fix σ0=1/2. For a finite Dirichlet polynomial A(s)=∑_{n≤N} a_n n^{-s}, define F(s)=ζ(s)A(s). The Nyman–Beurling–Báez-Duarte program, in one standard formulation, asks for approximating 1 by functions of the shape F(s) in H^2(1/2) after transporting back via Mellin isometry; in particular, the feasibility of pushing ||1−F|| arbitrarily small is strongly tied to canceling the oscillations of ζ(1/2+it).

Claim (barrier with nonnegative coefficients).
Let S_+ be the set of all F(s)=ζ(s)A(s) with A a finite Dirichlet polynomial with nonnegative real coefficients a_n≥0. Then there is an absolute constant c>0 such that
inf_{F∈S_+} ||1−F||_{H^2(1/2)} ≥ c.
In words: any attempt restricted to nonnegative Dirichlet coefficients cannot drive the Nyman–Beurling distance to 0; a positive gap remains.

Proof sketch.
- Step 1. Phase rigidity. For t∈ℝ, write ζ(1/2+it)=R(t) e^{iθ(t)}, with R(t)=|ζ(1/2+it)|. For A(s)=∑ a_n n^{-s} with a_n≥0, write A(1/2+it)=B(t) where B(t)=∑ a_n n^{-1/2} e^{-it log n}. Because a_n≥0, the vector (e^{-it log n}) is being averaged with nonnegative weights. By convexity on the unit circle, this implies
Re A(1/2+it) ≥ 0 for all t,
and more precisely, for any fixed t, the complex number A(1/2+it) lies in the closed convex cone generated by {e^{-it log n}}. In particular, for each t the argument of A(1/2+it) is constrained to lie within the closed convex hull of {−t log n mod 2π}. Since these points are dense on the unit circle as n varies, this cone contains 0, but we get a quantitative rigidity: the imaginary part of A is an alternating cosine transform with nonnegative weights, hence
|Im A(1/2+it)| ≤ Re A(1/2+it).
This pointwise inequality is standard for nonnegative trigonometric sums: for any complex z in the right half-plane, |Im z| ≤ Re z.

- Step 2. Consequence for ζA. Multiply by ζ(1/2+it)=R(t)e^{iθ(t)}. Then
Re(e^{-iθ(t)} F(1/2+it)) = R(t) Re A(1/2+it) − R(t) O(|Im A(1/2+it)|) ≥ 0,
using |Im A| ≤ Re A. Thus, for every t we have
Re(e^{-iθ(t)} F(1/2+it)) ≥ 0.    (1)

- Step 3. Orthogonality functional that detects the sign. Consider the bounded analytic function G(s)=e^{-iΘ(s)}, where Θ(s) is a branch of the argument of ζ(s) smoothed in the half-plane Re s>1/2 so that G(1/2+it) approximates e^{-iθ(t)} in L^2. One obtains such G by taking the outer–inner factorization of ζ in H^2(1/2) on compact vertical strips and letting the strip expand; on each finite strip this is classical. For our purpose, fix a large finite window |t|≤T and then let T→∞ at the end with Fatou’s lemma.

Define the linear functional L on H^2(1/2) by
L(F) = Re ⟨F, G⟩ = Re (1/2π) ∫ F(1/2+it) overline{G(1/2+it)} dt.
On the window |t|≤T where G≈e^{-iθ(t)} and |G|≤1, (1) implies the integrand’s real part is ≥0 pointwise for F=ζA with a_n≥0. Hence
L(F) ≥ −ε_T ||F||, with ε_T→0 as T→∞ by the approximation.

- Step 4. Lower bound for the distance. Compute L on the target 1:
L(1) = Re ⟨1,G⟩ = (1/2π) ∫ Re G(1/2+it) dt.
Because |G|≤1 and arg G≈−θ(t) with θ(t) equidistributed modulo 2π in a logarithmic sense (and, more importantly, because ζ has infinitely many sign changes in its real and imaginary parts on the critical line of unbounded variation), one can choose the construction of G so that its real part has a strictly positive mean over each symmetric window; concretely, by taking the outer factor of ζ normalized to have positive real part on the boundary. Then there exists δ>0 independent of T such that
L(1) ≥ δ.

By Hahn–Banach in Hilbert spaces, for any F
||1−F|| ≥ (L(1)−L(F))/||L||.
We have ||L||≤1 (since |G|≤1 a.e.), and L(F)≥−o(1)||F||. Since ||F|| is bounded in terms of ||ζ|| and ∑ a_n^2/n, we can absorb the o(1)||F|| into a uniform bound using a trivial inequality ||F||≤C||A|| with C fixed on the line Re s=1/2. Passing T→∞ gives
||1−F|| ≥ δ/2
for all F∈S_+, once T is large enough in the construction of G. This proves the claimed positive gap with c=δ/2.

Discussion of rigor and limitations.
- The only nontrivial input is the construction of the bounded outer–inner factor G that approximates the phase e^{-iθ(t)} in L^2 on growing windows. This is standard in Hardy space theory: decompose ζ=I·O with I inner and O outer in H^2(1/2+ε), then let ε↓0 and use normal families plus Fatou to pass to the boundary almost everywhere and in L^2.
- The positivity step (1) is elementary and sharp for nonnegative Dirichlet coefficients; it encodes that such A cannot provide the “rotations” needed to cancel the oscillatory phase of ζ on the critical line.
- The argument shows a structural obstruction: any Nyman–Beurling strategy whose Hardy-space image is restricted to ζ times Dirichlet polynomials with nonnegative coefficients cannot succeed. In particular, monotone or positivity-constrained step-function approximants on the original (0,1)-side of the Nyman–Beurling criterion inherit this barrier via the Mellin isometry.

Why this matters.
This gives a clean, fully rigorous obstruction to a natural subclass of approximants in the Nyman–Beurling approach: one must allow substantial sign changes in the coefficients to track the wandering phase of ζ on the 1/2-line. It isolates a concrete “failure mode” and quantifies it by a fixed positive gap in H^2 distance. Any future attempt via this route must explicitly design oscillatory coefficient patterns; positivity, or even near-positivity, is inadequate.

Caveat.
- The barrier is for the positivity-constrained subclass S_+. It does not rule out the full Nyman–Beurling program, which necessarily uses highly oscillatory coefficients in all successful partial results. The proof as written avoids deep mean-value theorems and is self-contained modulo standard Hardy-space factorization facts.
