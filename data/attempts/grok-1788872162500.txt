### Attempt: Obstruction in a contour-shift argument for a weak zero-free strip

Let \(\xi(s) = \frac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\) be the completed Riemann xi-function, entire of order 1 and satisfying the functional equation \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\). The Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to all non-trivial zeros of \(\xi\) lying on the critical line \(\operatorname{Re}s=\frac12\).

Fix \(T\ge 2\) large and consider the rectangular contour \(C_T\) with vertices
\[
\frac12+\delta\pm iT,\qquad 1+\delta\pm iT
\]
(\(\delta\in(0,\frac12)\) fixed). By the argument principle the number of zeros of \(\xi\) inside \(C_T\) (counted with multiplicity) equals
\[
N(C_T)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{C_T}\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)\,ds.
\]
The horizontal integrals are \(O(\log T)\) by the standard convex bound \(\xi(\sigma+it)\ll_\varepsilon |t|^{C(1-\sigma)+\varepsilon}\) for \(\sigma\in[\frac12,1+\delta]\) and the fact that \(\xi'/\xi\) has at most \(O(\log T)\) poles on each horizontal side (from the known zero-density estimate \(N(T)=O(T\log T)\)). The right-hand vertical integral equals
\[
\frac1{2\pi}\Delta\arg\xi(1+\delta+it)\Big|_{t=-T}^{T}=O(1),
\]
since \(\zeta(1+\delta+it)\) is zero-free and bounded away from zero for fixed \(\delta>0\), while the Gamma factor contributes a slowly varying argument of size \(O(\log T)\) that cancels symmetrically.

Thus any zeros off the line inside the strip \(\frac12+\delta\le\operatorname{Re}s\le 1+\delta\) would have to be cancelled by a matching contribution from the left vertical side
\[
\frac1{2\pi}\Delta\arg\xi\Bigl(\tfrac12+\delta+it\Bigr)\Big|_{t=-T}^{T}.
\]
By the functional equation the left side is identical (up to sign) to the corresponding integral on the right of the critical line after the substitution \(s\mapsto 1-s\). Consequently the only way for the total change in argument to vanish is if the oscillatory integral of \(\operatorname{Im}(\xi'/\xi)\) along \(\operatorname{Re}s=\frac12+\delta\) is \(o(T)\).

Expanding the logarithmic derivative via the Hadamard product
\[
\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)=\sum_{\rho}\Bigl(\frac1{s-\rho}+\frac1{\rho}\Bigr)
\]
(the sum over non-trivial zeros) and integrating term-by-term against a smooth test function supported on \([-T,T]\) produces a main term of size \(\asymp T\log T\) coming from the zeros on the critical line together with an error controlled by the horizontal distribution of any putative zeros with \(\operatorname{Re}\rho\ge\frac12+\delta\). The classical zero-density bound
\[
N(\sigma,T)\ll T^{c(1-\sigma)}\log^{O(1)}T\qquad(\sigma\ge\tfrac12+\delta)
\]
makes the error \(O(T^{1-\eta})\) for some \(\eta=\eta(\delta)>0\), which is not strong enough to force the oscillatory integral to be \(o(T)\). Improving the density exponent below the critical threshold \(1-\sigma\) would close the gap and yield a zero-free strip of width \(\delta\), but every known density estimate (Ingham, Huxley, \ldots) saturates precisely at this threshold when \(\sigma\to\frac12^+\). This is the concrete analytic obstruction: the contour-shift identity is consistent with RH yet supplies no contradiction unless a subconvex density estimate is assumed a priori—an assumption essentially equivalent in strength to a zero-free strip.

The same circularity appears if one replaces the sharp cut-off by a smooth mollifier or attempts to exploit the pair-correlation form of the explicit formula; the off-line contribution remains masked by the diagonal term at the present level of density technology.

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Contour argument reduces RH to subconvex zero-density, which is circular at known bounds.
