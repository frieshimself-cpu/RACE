### Attempt: Obstruction in positivity methods for zero-free regions

Let \(\sigma>1\) and \(t\in\mathbb{R}\). Write
\[
-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\Lambda(n)n^{-s},\qquad s=\sigma+it.
\]
The classical non-negative trigonometric polynomial
\[
P(\theta)=3+4\cos\theta+\cos 2\theta=2(1+\cos\theta)^2\ge0
\]
yields the linear combination
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pos}
3\Bigl(-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma)\Bigr)
+4\operatorname{Re}\Bigl(-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma+it)\Bigr)
+\operatorname{Re}\Bigl(-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma+2it)\Bigr)
=\sum_n\Lambda(n)n^{-\sigma}P(t\log n)\ge0.
\end{equation}
Near a putative zero \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) with \(\beta\) close to \(1\) one has
\[
\operatorname{Re}\Bigl(-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma+i\gamma)\Bigr)
\le-\frac1{\sigma-\beta}+O\bigl(\log(|\gamma|+2)\bigr)
\]
(with an analogous bound at \(2\gamma\)), while the polar term contributes
\[
-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma)=\frac1{\sigma-1}+O(1).
\]
Substituting these estimates into \eqref{eq:pos} and optimizing \(\sigma=1+\delta\) produces the classical zero-free region
\[
\beta\le1-\frac{c}{\log(|\gamma|+2)}
\]
for an absolute \(c>0\).

#### Why the method cannot reach the critical line
The same formal combination remains valid for \(1/2<\sigma\le1\) after analytic continuation, but two independent obstructions appear.

1. **Loss of Euler-product positivity.**  
   For \(\sigma\le1\) the Dirichlet series no longer converges absolutely. The identity
   \[
   \sum_n\Lambda(n)n^{-\sigma}P(t\log n)
   \]
   must be replaced by a truncated explicit formula involving a sum over zeros. Each zero \(\rho'\) contributes a term of size \(\asymp|\sigma-\beta'|^{-1}\) whose sign is uncontrolled; the non-negativity of \(P\) is therefore lost and cannot be used to force a contradiction when \(\beta\) approaches \(1/2\).

2. **Growth versus pole strength.**  
   Even if one restores an approximate positivity via a smooth weight of length \(X\), the error incurred by truncation is
   \[
   O\bigl(X^{1-\sigma}\log(tX)+t^{C(1-\sigma)}\bigr)
   \]
   (standard convexivity bound). Balancing this error against the main term \(1/(\sigma-\beta)\) forces
   \[
   \sigma-\beta\gg\frac{\log\log t}{\log t}
   \]
   at best; any attempt to push \(\sigma\) down to \(1/2+\varepsilon\) makes the error dominate the polar contribution uniformly in the height \(t\). Refinements that replace \(P\) by higher-degree non-negative polynomials (or by Vinogradov-type zero-density estimates) improve only the logarithmic power, never the linear distance to the line \(\operatorname{Re}s=1/2\).

Thus every positivity argument that begins from an Euler product at \(\sigma>1\) and continues inward is limited by an intrinsic gap of size \(\asymp(\log|t|)^{-\theta}\) for some \(\theta<\infty\). Closing the remaining gap to the critical line requires an essentially different source of positivity (e.g., a spectral interpretation or a sharp form of the pair correlation conjecture) that is not supplied by the classical method.
