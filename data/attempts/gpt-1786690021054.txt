Claim (indistinguishability of planted vs uniform k-CNF by bounded‑local statistics).
Fix k ≥ 3. Let n → ∞ and m = m(n) satisfy n ≪ m ≪ n^2 (e.g., m = n^{1+ε} with 0<ε<1). Consider two distributions over k-CNF formulas Φ on n variables with m uniformly random clauses:

- Uniform: U = Unif over all m independent uniformly random k-clauses on n variables;
- Planted: P = Unif over σ ∈ {±1}^n chosen uniformly at random and then m i.i.d. k-clauses chosen uniformly at random among those satisfied by σ.

For a fixed integer L = L(n) with L = o(m/n) (e.g., any L = n^{ε’} for 0<ε’<ε), let F_L be the σ‑algebra generated by the multiset counts of all local templates of radius at most L, defined as follows: a template of radius r≤L specifies a choice of r distinct variables x_{i_1},…,x_{i_r}, a choice of polarities ℓ_j ∈ {x_{i_j}, ¬x_{i_j}}, and a multiset of t clauses among the m that contain a fixed pattern on these r literals with all remaining k−r literals arbitrary; the statistic counts how many clauses realize that literal pattern on those r variables. Equivalently, F_L is generated by all counts of r‑literal restrictions inside clauses for all r≤L and choices of r variables and their polarities.

Then the total variation distance between the projections of U and P onto F_L tends to 0:
d_TV( U|_{F_L}, P|_{F_L} ) → 0 as n→∞.

Consequences. Any (possibly randomized) algorithm whose decision depends only on F_L—i.e., that views the input formula through any function of these bounded‑local counts—cannot, with probability tending to 1, distinguish planted from uniform instances significantly better than random guessing. In particular, any bounded‑width resolution refutation heuristic, any DPLL/branching algorithm with lookahead bounded by L, and any statistic based on subformula frequencies up to L literals cannot refute random k‑SAT (or certify satisfiability under planting) at densities m ∈ [n^{1+ε}, n^{2−o(1)}] for the above regime of L.

Proof sketch.

1) Model and statistics.
- Let V = {1,…,n}. For each clause c, define its r‑trace on an ordered r‑tuple (i_1,…,i_r) and polarity vector s ∈ {±1}^r as the indicator that c contains exactly those r literals (x_{i_j}=s_j) among its k literals; the other k−r literals are unconstrained.
- For fixed r, (i_1,…,i_r), and s, define the count
  X_{r,i,s} = number of clauses whose r‑trace on (i,s) occurs.
Let S_L = {X_{r,i,s} : 1≤r≤L, all (i,s)}. The σ‑algebra F_L is generated by S_L. Note that |S_L| ≤ ∑_{r=1}^L n^r 2^r ≤ (2n)^{L+1} which is subexponential for L = o(log n), and still subpolynomial for L = n^{ε’} as long as we only require approximation statements below to hold uniformly.

2) Distribution of a single count under U and P.
Fix r ≤ L, indices i=(i_1,…,i_r) with all distinct, and s ∈ {±1}^r. Consider one random clause C.

- Under U: C is a uniformly random k‑subset of variables with uniform random polarities. The probability that C’s r‑trace matches (i,s) equals
  p_r = [choose the remaining k−r variables from the n−r variables] / [choose any k variables from n] × (1/2)^{k−r}
      = [ (n−r choose k−r) / (n choose k) ] × 2^{−(k−r)}.
This depends only on (n,k,r), not on i or s.

- Under P: First σ ∈ {±1}^n is uniform; then a clause is chosen uniformly from all k‑clauses satisfied by σ. A uniformly random σ implies that, marginally over σ, every r‑trace (i,s) appears with exactly the same probability as under U. To see this, fix (i,s) and expose σ on coordinates i_1,…,i_r. Conditioned on σ(i)=s, the set of clauses satisfied by σ is invariant under permutations of variables outside i and flips of literals aligned with σ; the probability the random satisfied clause C contains the prescribed r literals is again p_r. Since Pr[σ(i)=s]=2^{−r}, and p_r conditioned on σ(i)=s is independent of s and equals the U value multiplied by 2^r (to account for the conditioning), the unconditional marginal equals p_r.

Therefore, for any fixed (r,i,s), a single clause contributes a Bernoulli(p_r) indicator to X_{r,i,s} under both U and P.

3) Joint distribution across clauses.
For each fixed (r,i,s), over the m independent clauses, X_{r,i,s} ∼ Binomial(m, p_r) under U. Under P, conditioned on σ, clauses are i.i.d. uniformly among satisfied clauses. By the marginal argument above, for any fixed (r,i,s), the indicator per clause has the same success probability p_r averaged over σ. Moreover, for different clauses, the indicators are conditionally i.i.d. given σ, hence unconditionally exchangeable with the same mean and variance m p_r (1−p_r) as under U. In particular, for each fixed (r,i,s), the unconditional distribution of X_{r,i,s} under P matches that under U up to an error that arises only from potential correlations across different (r,i,s) when considering the full vector S_L. We bound these correlations next.

4) Covariances among different counts and a multivariate CLT.
Let X_a and X_b be two counts in S_L (each summing m indicators over clauses, where indicator I^t_a depends only on clause t). For t≠t’, clause independence gives Cov(I^t_a, I^{t’}_b)=0 in both U and P (the latter due to conditional independence given σ). For t=t’, Cov(I^t_a, I^t_b) concerns a single random clause. Under U, this covariance is determined combinatorially and is O(p_{r_a∪r_b}) in magnitude; under P, the same symmetry/marginal argument shows the single‑clause joint distribution of r‑traces matches that of U when averaged over σ (because a uniformly random σ makes each literal equally likely to align/misalign, and the conditioning “clause is σ‑satisfied” preserves symmetry among patterns that are σ‑compatible). Concretely, for any fixed finite index set T of r‑trace predicates applied to one clause, the joint probability that a single clause satisfies all predicates in T is the same under U and under P after averaging over σ. Hence the entire covariance matrix of the per‑clause indicator vector over all templates coincides under U and P.

Therefore, the vector S_L (a sum over m i.i.d. per‑clause indicator vectors) has the same mean vector and covariance matrix under U and under P. By the multivariate Berry–Esseen theorem for sums of i.i.d. bounded vectors, the distribution of the normalized S_L approaches a common multivariate normal in total variation at rate O( (|S_L|)^{3/2} / √m ), uniformly over both U and P.

5) Parameter regime and total variation bound on S_L.
Each X_{r,i,s} has variance Θ(m p_r) with p_r ≍ Θ(n^{−r}) (up to constant factors depending on k and r). For r ≤ L with L = o(m/n), we have m p_r ≫ 1 and the central limit approximation is accurate. The Berry–Esseen error for dimension d=|S_L| obeys
sup_B |Pr_U[S_L ∈ B] − Pr_P[S_L ∈ B]| ≤ C · ρ · d^{3/2} / √m,
where ρ is a uniform bound on third absolute moments of the per‑clause vector (a constant here), and B ranges over all convex Borel sets (Levy–Prokhorov/TV can be tied via standard smoothing for discrete lattices with negligible additional error since m p_r→∞). With d ≤ (2n)^{L+1} and m = n^{1+ε}, we can choose L = n^{ε’} with any ε’<ε so that
d^{3/2} / √m ≤ exp( O( n^{ε’} log n ) ) / n^{(1+ε)/2} = o(1).
Thus, the distributions of S_L under U and under P converge in total variation.

6) Conclusion.
Because S_L generates F_L and the joint law of S_L under U and P converges in total variation, we have d_TV( U|_{F_L}, P|_{F_L} ) → 0. Therefore, any test whose decision depends only on F_L—i.e., any algorithm restricted to bounded‑local statistics up to radius L—cannot asymptotically distinguish the planted distribution from uniform better than chance.

Discussion and relevance to P vs NP.
- The indistinguishability shows a formal barrier for a broad family of “local” proof and algorithmic techniques—including bounded‑width resolution heuristics, limited‑lookahead branching, bounded‑neighborhood counting, and any algorithm whose features are counts of constant/slowly‑growing size literal patterns. These families are often the first stepping stones to unconditional lower bounds and to proof‑complexity separations tied to NP vs P.
- The parameter window m ∈ [n^{1+ε}, n^{2−o(1)}] covers polynomial densities well below the classical refutation thresholds. In this window, our result explains why these techniques fail even to exploit the extra signal in planted instances; hence they a fortiori fail to refute typical random instances. This helps delineate where new, genuinely global techniques are necessary.

Limitations and open directions.
- The barrier applies to statistics of radius up to L = o(m/n); pushing L as large as Θ(m/n) likely requires finer dependency control across templates.
- Extending the indistinguishability to algorithms that adaptively probe a sublinear number of clauses (beyond fixed‑feature statistics) would connect to statistical query lower bounds; proving such an extension for k‑SAT remains an attractive target.
- Bridging from this local barrier to concrete lower bounds in mainstream proof systems (e.g., Cutting Planes, degree‑d SOS) at these densities would constitute further progress relevant to P vs NP.
