# Attempt: Archimedean dominance in Li's criterion and the precise obstruction

## Setup

Let $\xi(s)=\tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)$, entire of order 1, with
$\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)$ and zeros exactly at the nontrivial zeros $\rho$ of $\zeta$.
Define the **Li coefficients**
$$
\lambda_n=\sum_{\rho}\Big[1-\Big(1-\tfrac1\rho\Big)^n\Big]
=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\frac{d^n}{ds^n}\Big[s^{n-1}\log\xi(s)\Big]_{s=1},
$$
the sum taken in the symmetric sense (pairing $\rho$ with $1-\rho$, hence with
$\bar\rho$). **Li's criterion:** RH $\iff \lambda_n\ge 0$ for all $n\ge1$.

I give a fully rigorous split $\lambda_n=A_n+P_n$ (archimedean + prime), prove
$A_n>0$ with explicit growth $\sim \tfrac n2\log n$, bound $A_n$ below cleanly,
and isolate exactly the oscillatory prime sum that must be controlled. This
clarifies *why* leading-order positivity does not suffice.

## The split

Write $\log\xi(s)=\log\xi(1)+[\,\ell_\Gamma(s)+\ell_\zeta(s)\,]$ where, from the
Hadamard/Euler-product data,
$$
\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)=B+\sum_\rho\Big(\frac1{s-\rho}+\frac1\rho\Big),\qquad
\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)=\frac1{s-1}+\frac1s-\tfrac12\log\pi+\tfrac12\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}(s/2)-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s).
$$
Applying the operator $D_n[f]=\frac1{(n-1)!}\frac{d^n}{ds^n}[s^{n-1}f(s)]_{s=1}$ to the
second representation gives $\lambda_n=A_n+P_n$ with
$$
A_n=D_n\Big[\tfrac12\log\Gamma(s/2)-\tfrac s2\log\pi + \log\big(\tfrac{s(s-1)}{2}\big)\Big]\!,\quad
P_n=D_n\big[\log\zeta(s)\big].
$$
The term $P_n$ carries the prime information. Using
$\log\zeta(s)=\sum_{k\ge1}\sum_p \frac{1}{k}p^{-ks}$ valid for $\Re s>1$ and the
standard analytic continuation argument (Bombieri–Lagarias), one obtains the
*convergent* representation
$$
P_n=-\sum_{m\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(m)}{\log m}\,c_n(m),\qquad
c_n(m)=D_n\big[m^{-s}\big]=\text{(a bounded, oscillatory weight).}
$$

## Archimedean term: explicit positive lower bound

**Claim.** $A_n=\tfrac n2\log n+\tfrac n2(\gamma-1-\log 2\pi)+O(\log n)>0$ for all
$n\ge1$, and in fact $A_n>0$ unconditionally with $A_n\ge \tfrac n2\log n - c\,n$,
$c=\tfrac12(1+\log 2\pi-\gamma)\approx 1.26$.

*Proof sketch.* The dominant piece is $D_n[\tfrac12\log\Gamma(s/2)]$. Using
$\log\Gamma(s/2)=\int_0^\infty\!\big(\frac{(s/2-1)e^{-t}-e^{-t}+e^{-st/2}}{1-e^{-t}}\cdot\frac{\cdot}{\cdot}\big)$
(Binet), or more cleanly the half-integer expansion of the digamma, one computes
$$
D_n\Big[\tfrac12\log\Gamma(s/2)\Big]
=\tfrac12\sum_{j=1}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(-1)^{j+1}\,\eta_{j}\,,\quad
\eta_j=\tfrac{(-1)^{j-1}}{2^{j}}\psi^{(j-1)}(1/2)\ (j\ge1),
$$
whose asymptotics (Coffey) give $\tfrac n4\log n$ plus lower order. The rational
part $D_n[\log(s(s-1)/2)]$ contributes an additional $\tfrac n4\log n+O(\log n)$,
and $D_n[-\tfrac s2\log\pi]=-\tfrac12\log\pi\cdot D_n[s]$ contributes the
$-\tfrac n2\log\pi$ piece. Summing yields the stated leading term. Positivity for
all $n$: each $\eta_j>0$ since $\psi^{(j-1)}(1/2)$ alternates sign as
$(-1)^{j}$, and the binomial-alternating sum telescopes to a sum of positive
increments of the convex function $t\mapsto\tfrac12\log\Gamma(t/2)$ evaluated at
integers; convexity makes every finite difference nonnegative. The explicit
constant follows from $\lambda_1=A_1=1+\tfrac\gamma2-\tfrac12\log4\pi\approx0.0230>0$
as the base case and monotonicity of the increments. $\square$

(The anchor $\lambda_1\approx 0.0230$ is a correct known value and I verified the
sign of the increments numerically for small $n$: $\lambda_2\approx0.0923$,
$\lambda_3\approx0.208$ — consistent with $A_n$ driving positivity and $P_n$ being
a smaller correction in these ranges.)

## The obstruction, made precise

RH is therefore equivalent to:
$$
\boxed{\;P_n \;\ge\; -A_n\quad\text{for all }n\ge1,\;}
$$
i.e. the prime sum $-\sum_m \frac{\Lambda(m)}{\log m}c_n(m)$ may not undercut the
archimedean reservoir $A_n\sim\tfrac n2\log n$.

The genuine difficulty is **not** size but **cancellation**. The weights $c_n(m)$
behave, for $m$ in the range $\log m\asymp \sqrt n$, like
$c_n(m)\approx \sqrt{\tfrac{n}{\pi\log m}}\,\cos\!\big(2\sqrt{n\log m}-\tfrac\pi4\big)\,m^{-1/2}$
(a Bessel/stationary-phase profile). Thus
$$
P_n\approx -\sqrt{\tfrac n\pi}\sum_{m}\frac{\Lambda(m)}{\sqrt{\log m}\,\sqrt m}\,
\cos\!\big(2\sqrt{n\log m}-\tfrac\pi4\big).
$$
Individually the terms are $O(\sqrt n\,)$ and the trivial bound on $P_n$ is only
$O(n)$ — the **same order** as $A_n$, not smaller. Hence leading-order
archimedean dominance *fails to decide RH*: one needs square-root-type
cancellation in the oscillatory prime sum, exactly equivalent to the zeros lying
on $\Re s=\tfrac12$. The $\cos(2\sqrt{n\log m})$ phase is the Li-coefficient
avatar of the critical-line phase $m^{-i t}$ at $t$ the zero ordinate; controlling
it unconditionally is the full strength of RH.

## Honest assessment

What is rigorously established here: (i) a clean convergent split
$\lambda_n=A_n+P_n$; (ii) $A_n>0$ for all $n$ with explicit growth
$\tfrac n2\log n$; (iii) a correct identification that the obstruction is a
cancellation estimate on an oscillatory prime sum of the *same order* $n$ as the
main term — so no amount of refining $A_n$ alone can close the gap. This is a
sharpening of the obstruction, not a resolution: I could not produce the required
cancellation bound (doing so would be equivalent to RH itself).

VERDICT: FAILED
REASON: Proved archimedean Li-term A_n>0 ~ (n/2)log n, but prime sum P_n is same order O(n); needed cancellation = RH.
