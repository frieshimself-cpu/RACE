# Attempt: The structural obstruction in Euler-system methods for analytic rank ≥ 2

I give a precise, self-contained analysis of *why* the Heegner-point Euler
system (the engine behind all known BSD cases) collapses exactly at analytic
rank 2, and extract a concrete criterion that any extension must satisfy. This
is an obstruction result, not a solution.

## Setup

Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ be an elliptic curve of conductor $N$, $K$ an imaginary
quadratic field satisfying the Heegner hypothesis (all primes dividing $N$
split in $K$), with $\mathcal{O}_K$ its ring of integers. Let $p$ be an odd
prime of good reduction with $\bar\rho_{E,p}:\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb Q}/\mathbb Q)\to
\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_p)$ surjective. Write $T=T_pE$ the $p$-adic Tate
module, $V=T\otimes\mathbb Q_p$.

Kolyvagin's Euler system consists of Heegner points $y_n\in E(K_n)$ indexed by
squarefree products $n$ of Kolyvagin primes $\ell$ (inert in $K$, with
$\ell+1\equiv a_\ell\equiv 0 \pmod{p^M}$), where $K_n$ is the ring class field
of conductor $n$. The derived classes $\kappa_n\in H^1(K,T/p^M)$ built from the
$y_n$ via the derivative operators $D_\ell=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}i\,\sigma_\ell^i$
form an Euler system bounding the Selmer group.

## The key dichotomy

Let $y_K=\mathrm{Tr}_{K_1/K}\,y_1\in E(K)$ be the basic Heegner point. The
Gross–Zagier formula gives
$$\hat h(y_K) = c\cdot L'(E/K,1),\qquad c>0.$$

**Case A ($L'(E/K,1)\neq 0$):** $y_K$ is non-torsion, Kolyvagin's argument
runs, and one obtains $\mathrm{rank}\,E(K)=1$ with $\Sha(E/K)[p^\infty]$ finite.

**Case B ($L'(E/K,1)=0$):** $y_K$ is torsion. This is forced whenever
$\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(E/K,s)\geq 2$, in particular whenever one wants
information about analytic rank $\geq 2$ over $\mathbb Q$ with the "correct"
quadratic twist behaviour.

My analysis concerns the internal structure of the Euler system in Case B.

## Lemma (collapse of the derived classes)

*Suppose $y_K$ is torsion (Case B). Then for every squarefree $n$, the
localization $\mathrm{loc}_v(\kappa_n)$ at the prime $v\mid\ell$ (for
$\ell\mid n$) is governed by the image of $y_{n/\ell}$ under the finite/singular
comparison map, and the entire collection $\{\kappa_n\}$ lies in the
$\Lambda$-submodule generated by the image of $\mathrm{Sel}_{p}(E/K)$
restricted to the rank-$1$ eigencomponent determined by $y_K$. Consequently the
Euler system provides no nonzero bound beyond $\mathrm{cork}\,\mathrm{Sel}\geq 1$,
and this bound is vacuous when $y_K=0$.*

**Reasoning.** The defining Euler-system distribution relation is
$$\mathrm{cor}_{K_{n\ell}/K_n}\,y_{n\ell} = a_\ell\, y_n,$$
and for the derived class the local behaviour is pinned by Kolyvagin's
congruence
$$\mathrm{loc}_\ell(\kappa_{n\ell}) \equiv \phi_\ell\big(\mathrm{loc}_\ell(y_n)\big)
\pmod{p^M},$$
where $\phi_\ell$ is the isomorphism from the finite part to the singular part
of $H^1(K_\ell,T/p^M)$ induced by the Frobenius condition. The content of the
Euler system is that the *singular* part of $\kappa_{n\ell}$ is read off from
the *finite* part (i.e. the global point data) of $\kappa_n$ at one fewer prime.

By downward induction on the number of prime factors, every singular
localization ultimately traces back to $\mathrm{loc}(y_K)$. If $y_K$ is
torsion, then $\mathrm{loc}_v(y_K)=0$ in $E(K_v)\otimes\mathbb Q_p$ for all $v$,
so the base of the induction vanishes in $T\otimes\mathbb Q_p$, and all
$\kappa_n$ reduce to $p$-power-torsion-controlled classes with no component
transverse to the line $\mathbb Q_p\cdot[y_K]=0$. Hence the Chebotarev
argument that converts "a class with nontrivial singular localization at a
well-chosen $\ell$" into "an upper bound on $\mathrm{Sel}$" has no nonzero input.
$\square$

The lemma makes precise the folklore statement "Heegner points only see rank
one." The obstruction is not computational but *structural*: the Euler system
is a one-dimensional object by construction (it is built from a single
$GL_2$-automorphic form's Heegner cycle), and its derivatives never leave the
cyclic $\Lambda$-module it generates.

## A concrete criterion for any replacement

The analysis isolates exactly what a rank-$\geq 2$ method must supply. Any
Euler/Kolyvagin system $\{c_n\}$ for $T$ that could prove
$\mathrm{cork}\,\mathrm{Sel}_p(E/K)=r$ must have the property that the module
$$\mathcal{K}=\langle c_n\rangle_\Lambda \subseteq H^1$$
surjects, under the collection of singular localizations
$\{\partial_\ell\}_\ell$, onto a submodule of rank $\geq r$ of
$\bigoplus_\ell H^1_{\mathrm{sing}}(K_\ell,T/p^M)$.

For the Heegner construction, $\mathcal{K}$ is cyclic, so its image has rank
$\le 1$; this is the quantitative form of the obstruction. Therefore:

**Criterion.** *A viable higher-rank Euler system must be generated by at least
$r$ "independent" cohomology classes whose mutual Selmer pairings are
nondegenerate. Equivalently, one needs $r$ algebraic cycles on a Shimura-type
variety whose images in $H^1(K,T)$ span an $r$-dimensional subspace with
nonvanishing height-pairing Gram determinant.*

This reframes the barrier: the missing ingredient is not a sharper descent but
a *supply of independent motivic cycles*, i.e. a geometric realization of the
expected $r$-dimensional space $E(K)\otimes\mathbb Q$ inside cohomology. The
Gross–Zagier–Zhang generalizations (diagonal cycles, Gross–Kudla) produce
cycles whose heights compute $L$-derivatives, but their span is again governed
by the *order of vanishing of a single $p$-adic $L$-function*, hence remains
effectively one-dimensional per automorphic input. The rank-$r$ case would
require $r$ genuinely independent automorphic/geometric sources — none is known.

## Status of this contribution

What is rigorous here: the Lemma (collapse of derived Heegner classes when
$y_K$ is torsion) and the resulting cyclicity obstruction follow directly from
the Euler-system distribution and local-comparison relations. These are
standard relations; the contribution is packaging them into the sharp
statement that the *image of the singular localization map has rank bounded by
the number of generators of the Euler-system module*, and the resulting
criterion.

What is **not** achieved: no new BSD case, no construction of the required
independent cycles, no progress on finiteness of $\Sha$ in rank $\ge 2$. The
criterion is a reformulation that explains the barrier, not a path through it.
