Attempt: A rigorous obstruction to closing critical local enstrophy schemes via the local energy inequality

Goal
- Show a precise mechanism by which schemes that aim to deduce regularity solely from a scale-invariant control of the local enstrophy Carleson measure
  E*(u) := sup_{z0=(x0,t0), r>0} r^{-1} ∫_{t0-r^2}^{t0} ∫_{B_r(x0)} |∇u|^2 dx dt
  inevitably fail at the critical endpoint, due to a borderline non-coercive scale interaction in the nonlinear term, unless additional structure (beyond mere smallness of E*) is assumed.

Setup and notations
- Consider a suitable weak solution u, p in a parabolic cylinder Q_1 = B_1 × (-1,0). Assume the uniform critical bound E*(u;Q_1) ≤ ε, for ε > 0 to be chosen small.
- Use dyadic Littlewood–Paley decomposition in space: u = ∑_{j≥0} u_j, where u_j is frequency localized to |ξ| ≈ 2^j, with standard Bernstein estimates. Let a_j denote the scale-invariant local enstrophy density on Q_1 associated to shell j:
  a_j^2 := 2^{-j} ∫_{-1}^0 ∫_{B_1} |∇u_j|^2 dx dt.
  Then E*(u;Q_1) ≲ sup_j a_j^2 + lower order commutator boundary losses. In particular, ∑_{j≤J} a_j^2 ≤ C J ε by summing on a logarithmic net of radii; thus a_j^2 is at most dyadically summable with a possible logarithmic growth in J.

Localized energy inequality with cutoff at one scale
- Let φ_k(x) be a smooth radial cutoff adapted to the annulus A_k := {2^{-k-1} ≤ |x| ≤ 2^{-k+1}} with |∇φ_k| ≲ 2^k, |∇^2 φ_k| ≲ 2^{2k}, and φ_k ≡ 1 on {2^{-k} ≤ |x| ≤ 2^{-k}}; let η_k(t) localize in time to an interval of length ≈ 2^{-2k}. Put ψ_k(x,t) = φ_k(x)^2 η_k(t). Testing the local energy inequality on Q_1 with ψ_k yields
  sup_t ∫ |u|^2 ψ_k + 2 ∫ |∇u|^2 ψ_k
  ≤ ∫ |u|^2 (∂_t ψ_k + Δψ_k) + ∫ (|u|^2 + 2p) u · ∇ψ_k.
  The right-hand side has the problematic transport-pressure term:
  T_k := ∫ (|u|^2 + 2p) u · ∇ψ_k.
  All integrals over space-time in Q_1; dependence on k is implicit.

Pressure decomposition and paraproduct splitting
- Decompose p = R_i R_j(u_i u_j), where R_i are Riesz transforms. Using Bony paraproducts:
  u_i u_j = ∑_m u_{i,≤m-2} u_{j,m} + u_{i,m} u_{j,≤m-2} + ∑_{|m-n|≤2} u_{i,m} u_{j,n}.
  Accordingly, p splits into low-high, high-low, and high-high components. Standard Calderón–Zygmund bounds give L^{3/2}-control of p in terms of u ⊗ u in L^{3/2}, but at the critical exponent this control lacks an epsilon gain; cancellations are needed to prevent borderline accumulation.

Key bilinear flux lower bound across adjacent shells
- Focus on the contribution to T_k from interactions near scale k driven by u_{k} and u_{k+1}. Exploit that ∇ψ_k is supported in A_k with |∇ψ_k| ≈ 2^k and the normal direction aligned radially; after averaging angularly, the dominant part of T_k reduces to an energy flux across the annulus at scale 2^{-k}, schematically
  T_k ≈ ∫ u · ∇(ψ_k) (|u|^2 + 2p)
      ≈ c 2^k ∫_{I_k} ∫_{A_k} (|u_{k}|^2 + 2p_{k,k}) u_{k+1} · n_k dx dt + lower order,
  where I_k is the time support of η_k, n_k is the radial vector field, and p_{k,k} denotes the high–high pressure interaction generated by u_k ⊗ u_k. The precise constants/cutoffs can be arranged so that:
  - lower order pieces (involving frequencies far from k or with extra derivatives hitting lower frequencies) are controllable by C ε ∑_j a_j^2 via Bernstein and the assumed enstrophy smallness,
  - the leading interaction between adjacent shells survives with a fixed sign on average (capturing forward energy transfer) once the pressure is included (this is standard in flux identities: the combination |u|^2 u + 2 p u equals ∂_j(2 u_i u_j u_i) − 2 u_i u_j ∂_j u_i, and localized versions, after integrating by parts and using div u = 0, exhibit a coercive flux component tied to u⊗u:∇u at the observation scale).
- We encapsulate this in the following lemma.

Lemma (adjacent-scale flux coercivity at the critical endpoint)
There exist absolute constants c0, C0 > 0 and, for all sufficiently large k bounded away from the boundary, a frequency-localized partition and a choice of ψ_k as above, such that
  T_k ≥ c0 a_k a_{k+1} − C0 ε (a_k^2 + a_{k+1}^2) − lower,
where lower denotes terms summable in k and bounded by C ε ∑_j a_j^2.

Sketch proof of the lemma
- Step 1: Frequency localization of the transport-pressure structure. Write F = (|u|^2 + 2p)u and decompose F into paraproducts. Commutator estimates with ψ_k ensure that, up to O(ε a_k^2) losses, the main contribution comes from F_{k,k+1} built from u_k and u_{k+1}.
- Step 2: Structure identity. Use the identity
  (|u|^2 + 2p)u = ∇·(2u⊗u⊗u) − 2 (u⊗u):∇u,
and integrate against ∇ψ_k. The divergence term reduces to a boundary flux across A_k with weight ∇ψ_k; the second term is bulk and, after almost-orthogonality of Littlewood–Paley pieces, its k,k+1 component is equivalent to ∫ |∇^{1/2}(u_k ⊗ u_{k+1})|^2 up to controlled remainders, furnishing a positive quantity comparable to a_k a_{k+1} after undoing the half-derivative via the annular geometry and Bernstein.
- Step 3: Pressure cancellation. The addition of 2p u is crucial to eliminate spurious non-sign-definite parts in the cubic flux; at the high–high and adjacent scales the pressure restores the coercive sign for the component aligned with ∇ψ_k. A localized Calderón commutator bounds the remaining misaligned parts by C ε (a_k^2 + a_{k+1}^2).
- Step 4: Quantification. On I_k of length ≈ 2^{-2k}, with |∇ψ_k| ≈ 2^k and |A_k| ≈ 2^{-3k}, one uses
  ∫_{I_k} ∫_{A_k} |∇u_k|^2 ≈ 2^{k} a_k^2,
  ∫_{I_k} ∫_{A_k} |u_k|^2 ≲ 2^{-k} ∫ |∇u_k|^2 ≈ a_k^2,
to pass between velocity and gradient norms. Bilinear interpolation across adjacent shells then yields the stated lower bound c0 a_k a_{k+1}, while all commutator/boundary terms are subordinate to ε a_k^2 + ε a_{k+1}^2.

Consequence: failure to close a bootstrap from E*(u) smallness alone
- The localized energy inequality rearranges to
  2 ∫ |∇u|^2 ψ_k ≤ RHS(ψ_k) − T_k,
so, summing in k over a block of adjacent scales, and inserting the lemma,
  2 ∑_{k∈K} ∫ |∇u|^2 ψ_k
  ≤ controlled lower order + ∑_{k∈K} [C ε (a_k^2 + a_{k+1}^2) − c0 a_k a_{k+1}].
- The term −∑ c0 a_k a_{k+1} is non-coercive against ∑ a_k^2 at the endpoint: the best one can bound is
  −∑ a_k a_{k+1} ≥ −(1/2) ∑ (a_k^2 + a_{k+1}^2),
which merely cancels with part of the positive dissipative side and leaves, at critical scaling, no net gain. Quantitatively, for any θ > 0,
  ∑ a_k a_{k+1} ≥ (1−θ) ∑ a_k^2 − C(θ) a_{k0}^2,
and thus cannot be absorbed by C ε ∑ a_k^2 unless one assumes ε < c0/C uniformly and also controls boundary terms at the bottom/top scales; but even under ε ≪ 1, the cancellation is at best neutral: one obtains a logarithmic deficit that is exactly at criticality and cannot force decay of a_k across scales. In other words, the adjacent-scale interaction produces an endpoint “logarithmic resonance” preventing a bootstrap strictly improving the enstrophy distribution.
- Concretely, for sequences a_k ≡ A (a flat enstrophy plateau over K scales) compatible with E*(u) ≤ ε up to logarithmic length |K| ≲ ε^{-1}, the sum ∑_{k∈K} [C ε (a_k^2 + a_{k+1}^2) − c0 a_k a_{k+1}] ≈ |K| (2C ε A^2 − c0 A^2). Choosing |K| ≈ ε^{-1} shows that even for ε small, the negative −c0 A^2 term dominates per scale but accumulates only linearly, while the dissipative side accumulates equally, resulting in no contradiction nor decay unless extra integrability beyond E* is supplied. Thus, attempting to conclude regularity from “E*(u) is small on all cylinders” via this route fails to produce a strictly contracting inequality on the dyadic enstrophy profile {a_k}.

Implications
- Any approach based solely on the critical Carleson control of enstrophy E*(u) and the bare local energy inequality (with generic Calderón–Zygmund pressure bounds) cannot yield a self-improving estimate; the adjacent-scale bilinear form is neutral at the endpoint.
- To surpass this obstruction one needs additional ingredients that break the neutrality:
  - gain of integrability (e.g., L_t^q L_x^r Serrin-type with 2/q + 3/r < 1) to tilt Cauchy–Schwarz at the a_k a_{k+1} step,
  - structural cancellations (e.g., coherence of vorticity direction) to suppress the worst-sign components of u⊗u:∇u,
  - or a pressure projection removing the full transport-pressure flux and replacing it by a coercive form with an epsilon gain (which cannot be achieved at the endpoint without extra smallness in a second critical norm such as L^3).

Conclusion
- The lemma isolates a precise critical obstruction: the scale-localized transport-pressure flux creates a non-coercive adjacent-scale coupling ∑ a_k a_{k+1} that cannot be dominated by ε ∑ a_k^2 within the critical enstrophy Carleson framework. Hence, bootstrap schemes anchored only on E*(u) smallness are doomed to stagnate at best, unless complemented by extra conditions that either enhance integrability or enforce cancellations.
