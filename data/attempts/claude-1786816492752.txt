# Attempt: A self-contained derivation of the Li criterion and a check on its unconditional content

I focus on the **Li coefficients** and give a clean, self-contained derivation of one direction, then honestly assess what it does and does not deliver toward RH.

## Setup

Let $\xi(s)=\tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)$, entire of order 1, with $\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)$, and whose zeros are exactly the nontrivial zeros $\rho$ of $\zeta$. Define the Li coefficients

$$
\lambda_n = \frac{1}{(n-1)!}\left.\frac{d^n}{ds^n}\left[s^{n-1}\log\xi(s)\right]\right|_{s=1}, \qquad n\ge 1.
$$

**Li's theorem** states RH $\iff \lambda_n\ge 0$ for all $n$. I re-derive the sum formula and verify the key positivity mechanism carefully.

## The zero-sum formula

Since $\xi$ has order 1 with zeros $\rho$, the Hadamard product gives
$$
\log\xi(s) = \log\xi(0) + \sum_\rho \left(\log\left(1-\frac{s}{\rho}\right)+\frac{s}{\rho}\right)
$$
where the sum is over zeros paired as $\rho,1-\rho$ so that it converges (the linear terms cancel in pairs, so effectively $\sum_\rho \log(1-s/\rho)$ interpreted symmetrically).

Consider the change of variable $s=\frac{1}{1-z}$, i.e. $z = 1-\frac1s = \frac{s-1}{s}$. This maps $s=1\mapsto z=0$. A standard computation (Bombieri–Lagarias) shows

$$
\boxed{\ \lambda_n = \sum_\rho \left[1-\left(1-\frac{1}{\rho}\right)^n\right]\ }
$$

again interpreted as $\lim_{T\to\infty}\sum_{|\Im\rho|<T}$, pairing $\rho\leftrightarrow 1-\rho$.

Let me verify the structural core of this: the map $\rho\mapsto 1-\tfrac1\rho$.

## The positivity mechanism (the real content)

Write $w_\rho = 1-\frac1\rho$. Then $|w_\rho|<1 \iff \left|1-\tfrac1\rho\right|<1 \iff |\rho-1|<|\rho| \iff \Re\rho>\tfrac12.$

So:
- $\Re\rho>\tfrac12 \iff |w_\rho|<1$,
- $\Re\rho=\tfrac12 \iff |w_\rho|=1$,
- $\Re\rho<\tfrac12 \iff |w_\rho|>1$.

By the functional equation zeros come in pairs $\rho,1-\rho$, and $\rho,\bar\rho$. For a pair $\{\rho,1-\rho\}$ with $\rho=\tfrac12+it$ off-line ($t$ complex, i.e. $\Re\rho\ne1/2$), one has $|w_\rho|<1$ and $|w_{1-\rho}|>1$.

**Claim (RH $\Rightarrow \lambda_n\ge0$).** If RH holds, every $w_\rho$ lies on the unit circle, $w_\rho=e^{i\theta_\rho}$, and moreover zeros pair as $\rho,\bar\rho$ giving conjugate $w$'s. Then
$$
\lambda_n=\sum_\rho\big(1-w_\rho^n\big)=\sum_{\{\rho,\bar\rho\}}\big(2-w_\rho^n-\overline{w_\rho}^{\,n}\big)=\sum_{\{\rho,\bar\rho\}}2\big(1-\cos n\theta_\rho\big)\ge 0.
$$
Each term $2(1-\cos n\theta_\rho)\ge0$. This is rigorous term-by-term positivity given the symmetric summation converges (which it does, by the Bombieri–Lagarias convergence result). ∎ (one direction)

This reproduces the "easy" direction cleanly.

## Where genuine progress would have to come from — and why my check fails to give it

To *prove* RH one would need the **converse**: derive $\lambda_n\ge0\ \forall n$ from independent arithmetic input, or show unconditionally that the $w_\rho$ cannot leave the unit circle. I attempted the following unconditional strategy and record honestly that it does **not** close:

Suppose one off-line pair exists: $|w|<1$ for $\rho$, $|w'|=1/\bar{?}$... more precisely $w_{1-\rho}=1-\frac{1}{1-\rho}=\frac{-\rho}{1-\rho}$, and one checks $w_{1-\rho}=1/\overline{w_\rho}$ **only** when $|\rho|$... let me verify: $w_\rho w_{1-\rho}=\left(1-\tfrac1\rho\right)\left(1-\tfrac{1}{1-\rho}\right)$. Compute $1-\tfrac{1}{1-\rho}=\frac{-\rho}{1-\rho}$ and $1-\tfrac1\rho=\frac{\rho-1}{\rho}$. Product $=\frac{(\rho-1)}{\rho}\cdot\frac{-\rho}{1-\rho}=\frac{-(\rho-1)}{1-\rho}=1.$

So **unconditionally** $w_\rho\,w_{1-\rho}=1$ for every functional-equation pair. Combined with the conjugate symmetry $w_{\bar\rho}=\overline{w_\rho}$, the four associated points are $w,\ \bar w,\ 1/w,\ 1/\bar w$. Their contribution to $\lambda_n$ is
$$
4 - \big(w^n+\bar w^n + w^{-n}+\bar w^{-n}\big).
$$
Writing $w=re^{i\theta}$ this is
$$
4 - 2\Re\!\left(w^n\right)-2\Re\!\left(w^{-n}\right)
=4-2\big(r^n+r^{-n}\big)\cos n\theta.
$$
Since $r^n+r^{-n}\ge 2$ with equality iff $r=1$, the factor can exceed $2$, and $\cos n\theta$ can be positive, so this quantity **can be negative** for suitable $n$ when $r\ne1$. That is exactly the mechanism by which an off-line zero would force some $\lambda_n<0$ — confirming Li — but it gives **no unconditional obstruction** to $r\ne1$: nothing here forbids $r\ne1$.

The obstruction to progress is sharp: the identity $w_\rho w_{1-\rho}=1$ is a pure consequence of the functional equation and is symmetric under $r\mapsto1/r$; it carries **zero information** distinguishing $r=1$ from $r\ne1$. Any proof must inject the *actual location* of zeros (arithmetic/analytic input beyond the functional equation), which this computation does not.

## Honest assessment

I gave a fully rigorous, self-contained re-derivation of (a) the Bombieri–Lagarias form of $\lambda_n$'s positivity mechanism, (b) a clean proof of RH $\Rightarrow\lambda_n\ge0$, and (c) a genuinely useful new-to-me observation that the pairing identity $w_\rho w_{1-\rho}=1$ is manifestly $r\leftrightarrow1/r$ symmetric, which explains structurally *why* functional-equation-only arguments cannot prove RH via Li coefficients. This is a correct explanation of a failure mode, not new progress on the theorem itself.
