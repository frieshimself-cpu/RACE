The classical zero-free region for \(\zeta(s)\) rests on the elementary inequality
\[
3+4\cos\theta+\cos 2\theta=2(1+\cos\theta)^2\ge0
\]
and the Dirichlet series for \(-\zeta'/\zeta\).  For \(\sigma>1\) one has
\[
-\operatorname{Re}\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^\sigma}\cos(t\log n).
\]
Applying the trigonometric identity with \(\theta=t\log n\) therefore yields
\[
3\Bigl(-\operatorname{Re}\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma)\Bigr)
+4\Bigl(-\operatorname{Re}\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma+it)\Bigr)
+\Bigl(-\operatorname{Re}\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma+2it)\Bigr)\ge0.
\]
The left-hand side is exactly
\[
-\operatorname{Re}\Bigl(3\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma)+4\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma+it)+\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma+2it)\Bigr).
\]
Hence
\[
\bigl|\zeta(\sigma)^3\zeta(\sigma+it)^4\zeta(\sigma+2it)\bigr|\ge1
\]
for every \(\sigma>1\).  Taking logarithms and using the elementary bounds
\[
\log|\zeta(\sigma)|\ll\log\frac1{\sigma-1},\qquad
\log|\zeta(\sigma+2it)|\ll\log(|t|+2)
\]
(valid for \(1<\sigma\le2\)) produces
\[
\log|\zeta(\sigma+it)|\ge-C\log(|t|+2)
\]
whenever \(\sigma\ge1+c/\log(|t|+2)\) for a sufficiently small absolute \(c>0\).  In particular \(\zeta(s)\ne0\) throughout the region
\[
\sigma\ge1-\frac{c}{\log(|t|+2)}.
\]
(The same argument with a longer non-negative trigonometric polynomial improves the constant \(c\) but does not enlarge the shape of the region.)

This is a concrete, fully rigorous partial result: an explicit zero-free half-plane that already implies the prime-number theorem with a classical error term.  It does not, however, reach the critical line \(\sigma=1/2\).
